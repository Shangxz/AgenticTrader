# Value Investing Weekly Report - 2026-W30

Run dates: 2026-07-20, 2026-07-22, 2026-07-23, 2026-07-24, 2026-07-25

This report is research support only, not personal financial advice. Each run starts from a fresh quality-value review of the US public equity market, emphasizing durable business quality, cash generation, balance-sheet resilience, and valuation that appears reasonable relative to current earnings power and medium-term prospects. Point-in-time prices and valuation multiples can change quickly and should be refreshed before any investment decision.

## Market Context

The broad US market still does not look cheap in aggregate. Advisor Perspectives' July market-valuation update, using June 2026 monthly data, estimated the S&P 500 as 116% to 207% above long-term valuation norms across four indicators, with the average more than 3 standard deviations above its historical mean. That makes the opportunity set narrower: the best ideas are concentrated in de-rated individual franchises rather than in broad index exposure.

The latest market action reinforces the same point. On Friday, July 24, the S&P 500 rose less than 0.1%, the Dow gained 0.5%, the Nasdaq fell 0.6%, and all major US indexes finished the week lower. The pressure was especially visible in AI-linked large-cap technology after investors focused on heavy capital spending and weaker free-cash-flow conversion. This backdrop favors companies with visible cash generation, strong competitive positions, and valuations that already discount some bad news.

## Company Notes - 2026-07-25

### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Adobe remains the top idea after a fresh scan because the stock still trades at an unusually low earnings multiple for a recurring-revenue software franchise. The market appears to be pricing in a severe generative-AI disruption scenario, even though the latest operating results do not yet show that the business has broken.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Adobe reported record fiscal Q2 2026 results and raised fiscal 2026 revenue and non-GAAP EPS guidance. The company still has entrenched positions in creative, document, and marketing workflows, high margins, and meaningful pricing power if AI features deepen rather than cannibalize paid usage.

**Main risks:** This is not a simple mean-reversion idea. AI-native tools could pressure Adobe's creative franchise, reduce willingness to pay for legacy bundles, or force more spending to defend the platform. Recent management transition and packaging concerns also reduce confidence.

**Valuation signals:** At about 12.9x trailing earnings, Adobe's valuation is closer to a mature cyclical business than a high-margin software platform. The thesis needs continued ARR growth, stable margins, and evidence that AI monetization improves customer value.

### 2. Accenture (ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Accenture again screens as one of the cleanest quality-value opportunities in large-cap services. A roughly 11.7x trailing P/E looks too low for a global consulting, managed-services, cybersecurity, cloud, data, and AI implementation franchise unless enterprise technology demand is structurally impaired.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Accenture's fiscal Q3 2026 results showed solid revenue growth, profitability, EPS growth, and robust free cash flow. Management also continued to expect full-year fiscal 2026 free cash flow of $10.8 billion to $11.5 billion.

**Main risks:** Consulting and transformation projects can remain soft if clients keep delaying discretionary spend. AI may compress lower-value service work, and Accenture needs to keep proving that it can shift client demand toward higher-value implementation and managed services.

**Valuation signals:** The valuation is low, the balance-sheet risk appears manageable, and free-cash-flow guidance is strong. That combination supports the highest confidence score in today's shortlist, even though Adobe offers the deeper visible discount.

### 3. Lululemon Athletica (LULU)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Lululemon remains a high-upside but higher-risk value idea. At about 9.3x trailing earnings, the stock now prices in a long deterioration in brand heat, North American growth, or margin structure.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Lululemon remains a premium athletic-apparel brand with historically strong margins, direct-to-consumer economics, and international growth. Fiscal Q1 2026 results showed total revenue growth and strong international revenue growth, even though the Americas business weakened.

**Main risks:** The disconfirming evidence is real. Americas revenue and comparable sales have been weak, product cadence is under scrutiny, and competition from other premium athletic brands has intensified. If markdowns rise or the brand loses pricing power, the low multiple could be a value trap.

**Valuation signals:** The multiple is compelling enough to keep Lululemon in the top three. Confirmation requires Americas comps to stabilize, international growth to persist, and inventory/markdown discipline to remain intact.

### 4. The Cigna Group (CI)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Cigna is today's most notable addition. At roughly 12.3x trailing earnings, and closer to a single-digit multiple of management's 2026 adjusted EPS outlook of at least $30.35, the stock looks inexpensive for a diversified health-services and insurance platform.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Cigna reported first-quarter 2026 revenue growth, adjusted income from operations of $2.1 billion, adjusted EPS of $7.79, and raised its 2026 outlook. Evernorth provides scale in pharmacy benefit services, specialty care, and health-services operations, while Cigna Healthcare adds insurance exposure.

**Main risks:** Health care is politically and operationally complicated. PBM regulation, medical-cost trend, client losses, reimbursement pressure, and leverage all matter. The low multiple partly reflects real uncertainty rather than simple neglect.

**Valuation signals:** The adjusted earnings yield is attractive, and Q1 execution was solid. Cigna ranks below Lululemon because the regulatory and reimbursement risks are less easy to handicap, but the current valuation is strong enough for the core list.

### 5. Berkshire Hathaway (BRK.B)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Berkshire is not the cheapest stock in the market, but it remains a high-quality defensive value candidate. A roughly 14.3x trailing P/E is reasonable for a collection of operating businesses, insurance float, public equities, and large cash/T-bill reserves.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Berkshire's business mix gives it resilience across insurance, rail, energy, manufacturing, service, retail, and investment assets. In an expensive broad market, Berkshire's liquidity and patience are valuable attributes rather than idle decoration.

**Main risks:** Succession, capital allocation, insurance underwriting cycles, regulated utility returns, and limited attractive reinvestment opportunities are the key issues. A large cash balance can protect capital but also drag returns when markets remain expensive for long periods.

**Valuation signals:** Berkshire remains the shortlist's defensive anchor. It ranks below the highest-conviction discounts because the valuation gap is less dramatic.

### 6. PayPal (PYPL)

**Why it looks undervalued:** PayPal enters the shortlist because a roughly 10.5x trailing P/E appears low for a profitable global payments platform with meaningful free cash flow and ongoing share repurchases. The market is treating PayPal as a slow-growth legacy payments company with fading relevance.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** PayPal's Q1 2026 results showed 7% revenue growth, 11% total payment volume growth, 439 million active accounts, free cash flow of $0.9 billion, adjusted free cash flow of $1.7 billion, and $1.5 billion returned through repurchases during the quarter.

**Main risks:** Competition from Apple Pay, card networks, banks, Shopify-linked payment systems, and newer fintech platforms is intense. PayPal's operating margin contracted in Q1, active-account growth was limited, and full-year guidance was not a clean growth story.

**Valuation signals:** The valuation and cash return are attractive, but the business needs to prove durable transaction-margin growth and renewed customer engagement before confidence can move higher.

### 7. Constellation Brands (STZ)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Constellation remains on the list because the stock trades near a low-teens earnings multiple despite strong beer brands, high operating margins, and meaningful free cash flow.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Fiscal Q1 2027 results showed substantial operating income and free cash flow, with margin performance helping offset softer sales trends. The beer portfolio remains a valuable consumer franchise.

**Main risks:** Alcohol demand is less certain than it once looked. Consumer moderation, trade-down, category weakness, input costs, retailer inventory shifts, and leverage all deserve attention.

**Valuation signals:** The valuation is attractive, but the durability of demand is less clear than for Adobe, Accenture, Berkshire, or Cigna. That keeps confidence medium-low.

### 8. Zoetis (ZTS)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Zoetis remains a contrarian quality-value idea after a steep valuation reset. A roughly 12.4x trailing P/E is low for the leading animal-health company if companion-animal weakness proves cyclical rather than structural.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Zoetis benefits from specialized animal-health channels, attractive margins, pet-care resilience, and livestock-health demand. The business model remains higher quality than the current multiple implies if growth stabilizes.

**Main risks:** This is one of the more uncertain ideas. US companion-animal weakness, veterinary visit softness, competition in dermatology and parasiticides, and modest 2026 organic growth guidance all limit confidence.

**Valuation signals:** The stock deserves a place on the list because the valuation has reset materially, but it needs clearer evidence of recovery before ranking higher.

## Watchlist / Not Selected Today

- **Alphabet (GOOGL):** Still one of the highest-quality operating franchises in public markets, but today's fresh ranking moved it to the watchlist. The reported P/E of about 16.1x is distorted by investment gains, while negative quarterly free cash flow and sharply higher AI capex make the margin-of-safety case less clean.
- **Comcast (CMCSA):** At roughly 7.2x trailing earnings, Comcast is statistically cheap and generated $4.6 billion of free cash flow in Q2 2026. It stayed off the top list because broadband subscriber losses and cable-sector competition create a more structural value-trap risk.
- **Microsoft (MSFT):** World-class quality, but less valuation support than today's selected names. Upcoming results are important for AI capex and free-cash-flow conversion.
- **Danaher (DHR) and Thermo Fisher Scientific (TMO):** Strong businesses, but current multiples and recent rebounds make them less compelling than the sharper valuation resets above.
- **Target (TGT), Dollar General (DG), and Dollar Tree (DLTR):** Retail valuations are not unreasonable, but business quality and margin durability are less attractive than Lululemon's brand-recovery setup.
- **CVS Health (CVS), UnitedHealth (UNH), Pfizer (PFE), Nike (NKE), Gilead (GILD), and Amgen (AMGN):** Each has some value angle, but either current multiples, earnings quality, growth visibility, or business risk kept them below today's cutoff.

## Changes From Earlier Updates This Week

Today's independent scan produced several meaningful changes from the July 24 ranking:

- **Adobe remains rank 1** even after a Friday rebound because the valuation still looks unusually low relative to software margins, recurring revenue, and Q2 execution.
- **Accenture remains rank 2** and continues to have the cleanest free-cash-flow evidence among the top ideas.
- **Lululemon remains rank 3** because the discount is large, but the weak Americas evidence still prevents higher confidence.
- **Cigna was added at rank 4** after a fresh health-care review highlighted a low valuation versus 2026 adjusted earnings guidance and solid Q1 execution.
- **PayPal was added at rank 6** because the stock combines a low multiple, free cash flow, and aggressive buybacks, though competitive and margin risks keep confidence medium-low.
- **Berkshire moved from rank 4 to rank 5** because Cigna offered a stronger current valuation signal, not because Berkshire's quality thesis weakened.
- **Constellation moved from rank 5 to rank 7** and **Zoetis moved from rank 6 to rank 8** as Cigna and PayPal offered fresher quality-value signals.
- **Alphabet moved from the shortlist to the watchlist** because the latest AI capex and free-cash-flow concerns make the valuation signal too noisy despite exceptional business quality.

Earlier July 20, July 22, July 23, and July 24 conclusions remain preserved here as weekly history, but today's ranking was formed independently from current valuation, current prices, and the latest public evidence.

## Conclusion

The strongest current quality-value candidates are **Adobe**, **Accenture**, and **Lululemon**. Adobe has the most striking software-franchise valuation reset, Accenture has the cleanest free-cash-flow support, and Lululemon offers the biggest brand-recovery discount. **Cigna** is the most important new addition because its adjusted earnings yield looks compelling, while **Berkshire** remains the defensive anchor. PayPal, Constellation, and Zoetis are credible but require more confirmation because their disconfirming risks are clearer.
