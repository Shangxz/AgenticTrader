## Market Context

The broad US market still does not look cheap in aggregate. The S&P 500's estimated trailing P/E was 26.64 on July 24, 2026. That is near the high end of the last five-year range and well above 10-year and 20-year averages, so the quality-value opportunity set remains concentrated in individual de-rated franchises rather than broad-market exposure.

There was no new US regular-session trading on Sunday, July 26. Today's scan therefore uses the latest available Friday, July 24 quote snapshots plus current public releases and news available on July 26. The market backdrop remains defined by elevated index valuation, heavy AI capital-spending debate, and selective de-ratings in software, consumer brands, health care, payments, and cable/telecom.

## Company Notes - 2026-07-26

### 1. Accenture (ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Accenture moved to the top of today's independent ranking because the valuation is low for a scaled global consulting, cloud, cybersecurity, data, AI implementation, and managed-services franchise. A trailing multiple near 11.7x suggests the market is discounting a prolonged enterprise technology spending slowdown.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Fiscal Q3 2026 results showed solid revenue, strong profitability and EPS growth, and robust free cash flow. Management expected fiscal 2026 revenue growth of 3% to 4% in local currency, or 4% to 5% excluding the estimated U.S. federal business impact.

**Main risks:** Discretionary consulting demand can remain weak, and AI could pressure lower-value labor-intensive services. The thesis depends on Accenture turning AI adoption into higher-value transformation work rather than margin compression.

**Valuation signals:** The combination of low-teens earnings multiple, strong free-cash-flow profile, global client relationships, and manageable balance-sheet risk makes Accenture the cleanest quality-value candidate today.

### 2. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Adobe still looks unusually inexpensive for a recurring-revenue software platform. At about 12.9x trailing earnings, the market is assigning a mature-business multiple to a company that continues to report growth across creative, document, and customer-experience workflows.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Adobe reported record fiscal Q2 2026 results, including $6.62 billion of revenue, GAAP operating income of $2.24 billion, non-GAAP operating income of $2.95 billion, operating cash flow of $2.17 billion, and total ARR of $27.10 billion including Semrush.

**Main risks:** Generative AI could pressure Adobe's pricing power, product packaging, or customer retention. The company also needs to show that AI features expand paid usage rather than merely defend the installed base.

**Valuation signals:** Adobe remains a high-upside value candidate, but Accenture ranks higher today because Adobe's long-term moat is more directly under debate.

### 3. The Cigna Group (CI)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Cigna trades at roughly 12.3x trailing earnings and about 9.5x management's 2026 adjusted income-from-operations outlook of at least $30.35 per share. That is a meaningful discount for a scaled health-services platform if earnings quality holds.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** First-quarter 2026 results showed total revenue growth of 5% to $68.5 billion, adjusted income from operations of $2.1 billion, adjusted EPS of $7.79, and improved SG&A efficiency. Evernorth's pharmacy and specialty-care scale remains a durable asset.

**Main risks:** PBM regulation, medical-cost trend, client losses, reimbursement pressure, and leverage can all impair the thesis. The low multiple reflects real policy and operating uncertainty, not just neglect.

**Valuation signals:** Cigna screens better than UnitedHealth on current valuation support. UnitedHealth's business quality is high, but the current trailing and forward valuation evidence is less compelling after its rebound.

### 4. Lululemon Athletica (LULU)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Lululemon remains a sharp brand-recovery discount. A trailing P/E near 9.3x prices in a long deterioration in North American growth, brand momentum, or margin structure.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** The business still has a premium brand, direct-to-consumer economics, high gross-margin potential, and meaningful international runway. Fiscal Q1 2026 net revenue rose 4% to $2.5 billion, with international revenue up 22% even as Americas revenue fell 3%.

**Main risks:** The disconfirming evidence is important. Americas comparable sales declined, full-year 2026 revenue guidance implies flat-to-down sales, and gross margin pressure was severe in Q1. If brand heat and product cadence do not improve, the low multiple could be a value trap.

**Valuation signals:** Lululemon ranks below Accenture, Adobe, and Cigna because its recovery case needs more proof, but the current multiple is low enough to keep it in the top group.

### 5. PayPal (PYPL)

**Why it looks undervalued:** PayPal trades near 10.5x trailing earnings despite a global payments network, large active-account base, and substantial cash generation. The market is treating it as a legacy payments business with limited growth and eroding relevance.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Q1 2026 net revenue increased 7% to $8.4 billion, total payment volume increased 11% to $464 billion, and active accounts reached 439 million. Free cash flow was $0.9 billion, adjusted free cash flow was $1.7 billion, and the company returned $1.5 billion through buybacks in the quarter.

**Main risks:** Transaction margin growth is slower than volume growth, operating margins contracted, active-account growth is modest, and competition from wallets, card networks, banks, merchant platforms, and fintechs remains intense.

**Valuation signals:** PayPal is a credible cash-return value idea, but it needs sustained transaction-margin growth before confidence can move above medium-low.

### 6. Zoetis (ZTS)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Zoetis is a high-quality franchise that has been priced for a weak recovery. A trailing P/E near 12.4x and an adjusted 2026 EPS outlook of $6.85 to $7.00 imply a much lower valuation than the animal-health leader historically commanded.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Zoetis remains the leading animal-health company, with a diversified companion-animal and livestock portfolio, specialized channels, global scale, and a pipeline that management says includes more than 12 potential blockbuster candidates.

**Main risks:** The latest quarter was mixed. U.S. segment revenue fell 8%, companion-animal product sales fell 11%, veterinary visits softened, pet owners showed more price sensitivity, and competition intensified in dermatology and parasiticides.

**Valuation signals:** Zoetis is on the list because the valuation reset is material and the franchise quality is still visible. It ranks below PayPal because the near-term recovery evidence is thinner.

### 7. Berkshire Hathaway (BRK.B)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Berkshire is not a deep-value stock, but it remains a reasonable defensive value candidate in an expensive market. A trailing P/E near 14.3x is acceptable for a group of operating businesses, insurance float, public equities, and large liquidity reserves.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** First-quarter 2026 operating earnings were $11.3 billion versus $9.6 billion a year earlier. Insurance float was about $176.9 billion at March 31, 2026, and the operating mix spans insurance, BNSF, energy, manufacturing, service, retail, and investments.

**Main risks:** Succession, insurance cycles, regulated utility returns, limited reinvestment opportunities, and cash drag all matter. Berkshire's strength is resilience more than near-term mispricing.

**Valuation signals:** Berkshire is a useful anchor but ranks lower today because several other candidates offer clearer valuation gaps.

### 8. Comcast (CMCSA)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Comcast re-enters the top list because the valuation is extremely low and the company continues to generate large free cash flow. Q2 2026 free cash flow was $4.6 billion, and the company announced a separation of NBCUniversal and Sky that could create a cleaner valuation story.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Comcast still owns scaled connectivity, wireless, media, studios, and theme-park assets. Q2 results included $8.9 billion of adjusted EBITDA and $2.1 billion returned to shareholders through dividends and buybacks.

**Main risks:** This is the most obvious value-trap candidate in the shortlist. Domestic residential broadband net losses were 167,000 in Q2, domestic broadband revenue fell 5.5%, and fixed-wireless/fiber competition continues to pressure the cable model. The share repurchase pause during the separation process also reduces near-term capital-return support.

**Valuation signals:** The stock is cheap enough to include, but confidence remains medium-low because the core broadband pressure may be structural rather than cyclical.

- **Nike (NKE):** Brand quality and turnaround actions are real, but at about 27.6x trailing earnings the valuation is not as compelling as Lululemon, and fiscal 2026 revenue was flat reported and down 2% currency-neutral.
- **UnitedHealth Group (UNH):** Operational recovery evidence improved in Q2 and guidance was raised, but at $420.74 and adjusted 2026 EPS guidance of $19.50 to $20.00, the forward valuation is not obviously cheap enough for today's top list.

- **Microsoft (MSFT), Danaher (DHR), Thermo Fisher Scientific (TMO), Visa (V), and Mastercard (MA):** Strong businesses, but less attractive current valuation support than the selected ideas.
- **CVS Health (CVS), Pfizer (PFE), Target (TGT), Dollar General (DG), and Dollar Tree (DLTR):** Some value signals exist, but quality, balance-sheet, margin, or structural concerns kept them below the cutoff.

## Conclusion

The strongest current quality-value candidates are **Accenture**, **Adobe**, and **Cigna**. Accenture has the cleanest mix of low valuation, business quality, and free-cash-flow support. Adobe offers the most striking software valuation reset but carries heavier AI-disruption risk. Cigna has an attractive adjusted earnings yield but requires comfort with health-care regulation and reimbursement complexity. Lululemon, PayPal, Zoetis, Berkshire, and Comcast are credible secondary candidates, with Comcast carrying the clearest value-trap risk.
