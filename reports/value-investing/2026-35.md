### 1. Adobe (ADBE) - High conviction

**Why it looks undervalued.** At 16.7 times trailing GAAP earnings, Adobe remains below the broad market despite double-digit recurring growth. First-half FY2026 operating cash flow of $5.12 billion annualizes to about 8.7% of the current $117.3 billion market capitalization before modest capital spending. The discount appears to price in a significant loss of moat or pricing power to generative-AI tools.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal Q2 revenue increased 13% to $6.62 billion, subscription revenue rose 14%, total ARR increased 12.5% to $27.10 billion, remaining performance obligations grew 13%, and first-half operating cash flow rose 10%. Creative and document workflows, enterprise trust, distribution, proprietary formats, and a 97% subscription revenue mix support resilience. [Adobe Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000112/adbe-20260529.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Q2 operating expenses increased 17%, faster than revenue, while quarterly net income was roughly flat. AI-native tools could reduce switching costs, undermine seat-based pricing, or shift value away from Adobe applications. The thesis weakens if organic ARR moves below high-single-digit growth, AI monetization fails to offset cannibalization, or cash margins structurally decline. Adobe's 6.6% price rise since the August 27 snapshot also narrowed the margin of safety.

### 2. GoDaddy (GDDY) - High conviction

**Why it looks undervalued.** GoDaddy trades at 14.5 times trailing earnings. Management's approximately $1.8 billion 2026 free-cash-flow target equals about 14.1% of the current $12.8 billion equity value. Even allowing for roughly $3.8 billion of debt and adjustments embedded in the company's FCF definition, that is a strong owner-earnings yield for a recurring platform with positive growth.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 6.6%, bookings increased 5.7%, operating income grew 28.6%, net income rose 20.1%, and operating cash flow increased 16.5%. Applications and Commerce revenue grew about 11%, while ARR reached $4.42 billion. Shares outstanding declined from 134.7 million at year-end to 126.8 million at June 30, improving per-share economics. Domain scale, high renewal behavior, bundled software, and customer relationships form the durability case. [GoDaddy Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1609711/000160971126000088/gddy-20260630.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Core Platform revenue grew only 3.9%, debt is meaningful, and basic site creation could be commoditized by AI. GoDaddy also depends on registry pricing, third-party productivity products, cybersecurity, and continued renewal strength. The thesis would weaken if bookings flatten, Applications and Commerce growth falls below mid-single digits, or margin gains reflect underinvestment rather than durable efficiency.

### 3. Deckers Brands (DECK) - High conviction

**Why it looks undervalued.** The shares trade at 11.8 times the midpoint of management's FY2027 GAAP EPS guidance of $7.35 to $7.50. That multiple is unusually low for a company still growing both flagship brands and carrying net cash.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal Q1 revenue increased 5.7% to $1.02 billion, HOKA grew 7.7%, UGG grew 4.9%, DTC sales rose 13%, and international revenue grew 8.4%. Gross margin improved to 56.4%. Deckers ended June with $1.60 billion of cash, lower inventory, and no outstanding borrowings; management still expects operating margin above 21.5%. [Deckers Q1 FY2027 SEC release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910521/000091052126000015/deckex991pressrelease-6302.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Operating income declined 6% even as revenue and gross margin improved, showing that SG&A growth must be watched. HOKA and UGG create concentration, footwear demand is fashion-sensitive, and tariffs or promotion could pressure margins. First-quarter repurchases averaged $103.79, well above Friday's price, which is a caution on capital allocation. The thesis weakens if DTC comparable sales turn negative or inventory starts growing faster than sales.

### 4. The Cigna Group (CI) - High conviction

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $278.88, Cigna trades at about 9.2 times management's raised 2026 adjusted EPS outlook of at least $30.45 and 11.5 times trailing GAAP earnings. That is a substantial discount to the market despite current revenue and profit growth.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 7% to $71.7 billion and adjusted income from operations increased 6% to $2.05 billion. Adjusted EPS rose to $7.78, Cigna Healthcare pretax adjusted profit grew 17%, and the adjusted SG&A ratio improved to 4.6% from 4.9%. Scale across insurance, pharmacy benefits, specialty pharmacy, and care services provides multiple earnings drivers. [Cigna Q2 2026 SEC release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1739940/000114036126030135/ef20078875_ex99-1.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** The medical care ratio rose to 84.5% from 83.2%, pharmacy-benefit profitability is under pressure, and regulation of PBMs and drug pricing remains a large overhang. Medical-cost inflation, client losses, adverse policy changes, or leverage could justify the discount. The case weakens materially if Evernorth profit contracts for a sustained period or Cigna Healthcare margins miss the guided range.

### 5. Cognizant Technology Solutions (CTSH) - Medium-high conviction

**Why it looks undervalued.** Cognizant trades at 13.7 times trailing GAAP earnings and about 11.1 times the midpoint of raised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $5.70 to $5.82. That valuation leaves room for only modest growth even though revenue, margins, bookings, and guided EPS are moving higher.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue grew 4.5%, or 4.1% in constant currency; GAAP operating margin expanded 30 basis points to 15.9%; and GAAP EPS rose 3.8%. Trailing bookings increased 5% to $29.1 billion, a roughly 1.3x book-to-bill, while Financial Services revenue grew 12%. Cognizant also repurchased $1.15 billion of stock in Q2 and guides to 8%-10% adjusted EPS growth for 2026. Its global delivery network, long client relationships, domain expertise, and modernization capabilities support recurring demand. [Cognizant Q2 2026 results](https://investors.cognizant.com/news-and-events/news/news-details/2026/Cognizant-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Quarterly bookings declined 6%, overall revenue growth slowed from the prior year, and employee attrition increased. AI could both create modernization work and reduce the labor-arbitrage value of traditional IT services. Cognizant borrowed $1.0 billion while acquiring Astreya, so recent buybacks and acquisitions should be judged together. The thesis weakens if book-to-bill drops below 1x, margins retreat, or AI-led revenue fails to offset pricing and headcount pressure.

## Near misses and exclusions

- **Comcast (CMCSA):** The 8.7x trailing P/E and very high screen-implied FCF yield remain attractive, but residential broadband losses, cord-cutting, capital intensity, and separation uncertainty lower the durability score. Cognizant's positive organic growth and improving margins clear today's quality threshold more comfortably.
- **Qualcomm (QCOM):** The quality screen ranks Qualcomm well and the licensing moat remains valuable, but the current 18.7x trailing multiple offers less downside protection than the active five amid handset cyclicality and customer concentration.
- **Expedia (EXPE):** The FCF screen is optically compelling, but at 20.6x trailing earnings after Friday's gain, the earnings valuation and travel cyclicality provide less margin of safety than GoDaddy or Deckers.
- **Intuit (INTU) and NXP Semiconductors (NXPI):** Both remain quality franchises, but current valuation support is weaker than the selected candidates.
- **Centene, Charter, General Motors, legacy packaged-food companies, and highly cyclical commodity names:** Headline cash yields are offset by medical-cost volatility, leverage, capital intensity, cyclicality, or structural revenue pressure. They do not clear today's quality threshold.
- **Fiserv (FISV):** A high screen-implied FCF yield is outweighed by contracting organic revenue, margins, and first-half cash flow, plus debt and execution risk. It remains an unresolved turnaround rather than quality value.

## Conclusion

**Adobe and GoDaddy remain the strongest current quality-value candidates.** Adobe has the best blend of franchise durability and double-digit recurring growth, while GoDaddy offers the highest normalized owner-earnings yield among the selected non-cyclical companies. Deckers combines the lowest forward GAAP multiple with the cleanest balance sheet. Cigna has the clearest near-term earnings support but heavier regulatory and medical-cost risk. Cognizant is the balanced fifth idea: less dramatically cheap, but operating momentum and balance-sheet resilience make its discount more credible than the deeper structural bargains.

This shortlist is a starting point for deeper diligence, not a prediction of near-term performance or a claim of certain intrinsic value.
