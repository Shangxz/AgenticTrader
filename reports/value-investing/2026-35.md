# Value Investing Weekly Report - 2026-W35

_Last updated: August 25, 2026 (prices observed around 8:00 a.m. ET). Research support only; not personal financial advice._

## Market context and method

The broad U.S. market still offers a limited aggregate margin of safety. The S&P 500 is near 19.9 times forward earnings, while Morningstar's third-quarter work estimated the U.S. market at an 8% discount to its fair-value estimates and small-value stocks at a 21% discount. The implication is not that every low-multiple stock is attractive, but that selectivity and balance-sheet quality matter more than market direction.

Today's assessment began from a fresh broad-market screen of U.S.-listed large-, mid-, and small-cap operating companies. Candidate generation combined market-wide value screens with checks on earnings and free-cash-flow yield, returns and margins, leverage, competitive durability, and recent operating momentum. Financials and commodity producers were judged with sector-appropriate measures. This is a broad market screen, not a claim that every listed security received a full intrinsic-value model. Prior entries were consulted only after today's ranking was complete.

Screening context: [Kiplinger quality-value screen](https://www.kiplinger.com/investing/stocks/the-best-value-stocks-to-buy), [Morningstar Q3 2026 valuation outlook](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/33-undervalued-stocks-buy-before-market-catches), and [Morningstar wide-moat value screen](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/10-undervalued-wide-moat-stocks-worth-buying-right-now).

### 1. Adobe (ADBE) - High conviction

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $276.27, Adobe trades at roughly 15.4 times the midpoint of FY2026 GAAP EPS guidance of $17.90-$18.00 and 11.3 times the midpoint of non-GAAP guidance of $24.35-$24.45. Those multiples are unusually modest for a subscription software company still producing double-digit growth. The discount appears to reflect concern that generative AI will weaken Adobe's product moat or pricing power.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal Q2 revenue rose 13% to a record $6.62 billion; subscription revenue was $6.42 billion; GAAP operating income was $2.24 billion; and operating cash flow was $2.17 billion. Adobe raised full-year revenue and non-GAAP EPS targets, while AI-first annualized recurring revenue more than tripled and exceeded $500 million. The combination of entrenched creative workflows, high gross profit, recurring revenue, and strong cash conversion supports the quality case even if growth normalizes. [Adobe Q2 FY2026 results](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/11606202/a5543arefgt.pdf)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** AI-native creation tools may lower switching costs, compress pricing, or shift value away from Adobe's applications. The Semrush acquisition contributes to reported growth, stock-based compensation remains material, and the CFO departed in June. The thesis would weaken if organic ARR growth falls below guidance, AI-first products fail to offset cannibalization, or margins decline structurally.

### 2. The Cigna Group (CI) - High conviction

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $280.45, Cigna trades at about 9.2 times management's raised 2026 adjusted EPS outlook of at least $30.45 and about 11.6 times trailing GAAP earnings. The discount is large relative to the broad market even though management raised, rather than cut, its outlook.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Second-quarter revenue increased 7% to $71.7 billion, adjusted income from operations rose to $2.1 billion, and adjusted EPS increased to $7.78 from $7.20. Cigna Healthcare pretax adjusted income grew 17%, while Specialty and Care Services profit grew 22%. Diversification across insurance, pharmacy benefits, specialty pharmacy, and care services helps offset weakness in any one line. [Cigna Q2 2026 results](https://newsroom.thecignagroup.com/2026-07-30-The-Cigna-Group-Reports-Strong-Second-Quarter-2026-Results%2C-Raises-2026-Outlook)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Pharmacy Benefit Services pretax adjusted income fell 27%, Evernorth's margin remains thin, and the medical care ratio rose to 84.5%. PBM regulation, drug-pricing reform, adverse medical-cost development, or leverage could justify the low multiple. A sustained decline in Evernorth profit or failure to control medical costs would impair the thesis.

### 3. EOG Resources (EOG) - Medium-high conviction

**Why it looks undervalued.** EOG generated $6.65 billion of free cash flow over the latest four reported quarters. Against a market capitalization of about $79.5 billion, that is an indicative trailing free-cash-flow yield of roughly 8.4%. The shares also trade at about 11.7 times trailing GAAP earnings. These are attractive signals, but current commodity prices make trailing cash flow a poor stand-alone estimate of normalized value.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Second-quarter free cash flow was $2.80 billion, and EOG returned $1.8 billion through dividends and repurchases. Total production increased to 1.41 million barrels of oil equivalent per day, cash operating costs were better than guidance in key categories, and net debt fell to $3.0 billion, only 8.7% of total capitalization. A diversified, low-cost resource base and conservative balance sheet give EOG more resilience than the typical producer. [EOG Q2 2026 results](https://investors.eogresources.com/2026-08-04-EOG-Resources-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** The thesis is directly exposed to oil and gas prices, depletion, cost inflation, and capital discipline. Second-quarter record cash generation benefited from higher oil prices, while debt is higher than a year ago following the Encino acquisition. Value would be overstated if investors capitalize peak commodity cash flow or if the acquired inventory underperforms.

### 4. NXP Semiconductors (NXPI) - High conviction on business, medium-high on valuation

**Why it looks undervalued.** Current consensus screens place NXP near 14.1 times forward earnings, below the broad market. Trailing 12-month non-GAAP free cash flow was $2.81 billion, about a 5.0% yield on the current market capitalization. That is a reasonable entry valuation for a high-margin embedded semiconductor business, though not a deep-value price.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue grew 19% to $3.50 billion, GAAP gross margin was 57.3%, and GAAP operating margin reached 30.6%. Non-GAAP free cash flow was $791 million, or 22.6% of revenue. NXP's positions in automotive, industrial, connectivity, and secure-edge processing benefit from long product cycles and customer qualification requirements. The company also repaid $750 million of notes from available cash. [NXP Q2 2026 results](https://investors.nxp.com/news-releases/news-release-details/nxp-semiconductors-reports-second-quarter-2026-results)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Automotive and industrial chip demand is cyclical; China exposure and export controls add geopolitical risk; and current margins may be above mid-cycle levels. Rising inventories, weaker design-win momentum, or a sustained drop in free-cash-flow conversion would reduce the apparent discount.

### 5. Salesforce (CRM) - Medium-high conviction

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $209.06, Salesforce trades at about 14.8 times the midpoint of FY2027 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $14.06-$14.12. The market is assigning a modest multiple to a recurring-revenue software leader guiding to roughly 11% revenue growth and a 34.3% non-GAAP operating margin.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal Q1 revenue rose 13% to $11.1 billion; current remaining performance obligations increased 14% to $33.6 billion; GAAP operating margin reached 21.1%; and free cash flow was $6.6 billion. The installed base, integrated product suite, and high renewal friction remain important sources of durability. [Salesforce Q1 FY2027 results](https://investor.salesforce.com/news/news-details/2026/Salesforce-Delivers-Record-First-Quarter-Fiscal-2027-Results/default.aspx)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Informatica contributes about three percentage points to full-year revenue growth, so underlying growth is weaker than the headline. AI-native tools could pressure seat-based pricing, and the $25 billion debt-funded accelerated repurchase makes capital allocation more aggressive. Slower current-RPO growth, poor Agentforce monetization, or rising leverage would weaken the case.

## Near misses and exclusions

- **Zoetis (ZTS):** The long-term animal-health franchise remains valuable and the multiple is low, but 2026 organic revenue is now expected to decline 1%-3% and adjusted net income 5%-9%. U.S. companion-animal sales fell 11%. The estimate-reset and competitive risks are too high for today's top five. [Zoetis Q2 2026 results](https://investor.zoetis.com/news/news-details/2026/Zoetis-Announces-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)
- **GE HealthCare (GEHC):** Backlog and installed-base durability are attractive, but leverage, declining adjusted margin, and the Patient Care Solutions contraction leave less margin of safety than today's selections.
- **Intuit (INTU):** The business has excellent switching costs and growth, but full-year results are scheduled after today's market close. The imminent information event and recent 17% workforce reduction make it prudent to reassess after guidance is refreshed. [Intuit investor relations](https://investors.intuit.com/)
- **T-Mobile US (TMUS):** Scale, network quality, and free-cash-flow growth are strong, but the current valuation discount is narrower than Cigna's and leverage limits downside protection.
- **Nike (NKE):** Brand strength is real, but roughly 27 times trailing earnings alongside falling revenue and profit does not yet demonstrate a sufficient margin of safety.
- **Banks and alternative asset managers:** Several clear low-multiple candidates emerged, but rate, credit, funding, and mark sensitivity made their earnings quality less comparable with the selected operating businesses today.

## Conclusion

**Adobe and Cigna are today's strongest quality-value candidates.** Adobe offers the most unusual combination of double-digit recurring growth, high margins, and a mid-teens GAAP earnings multiple, while Cigna has the clearest near-term earnings support at a single-digit adjusted multiple. EOG is the strongest cash-yield idea but requires a conservative commodity-price normalization. NXP remains the best industrial-technology franchise in the group, and Salesforce remains attractive if AI disruption proves less severe than the current price implies.

This shortlist is a starting point for deeper diligence, not a prediction of near-term performance or a claim of certain intrinsic value.
