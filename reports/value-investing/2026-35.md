# Value Investing Weekly Report - 2026-W35

_Last updated: August 27, 2026 (prices observed around 7:30-8:00 a.m. ET). Research support only; not personal financial advice._

## Market context and method

The S&P 500 remains close to its early-August record and trades around 19.9 times forward earnings. That leaves a thin aggregate margin of safety even though earnings growth has broadened. The opportunity set is therefore concentrated in company-specific resets rather than obvious market-wide cheapness. Higher long-term Treasury yields also raise the return hurdle for equities.

Today's assessment restarted from broad, current screens of U.S.-listed large-, mid-, and small-cap companies. Candidate generation combined earnings and free-cash-flow valuation, returns and margins, leverage, competitive durability, and recent operating momentum. Banks, insurers, and commodity producers were evaluated with sector-appropriate measures. This is a broad market screen, not a claim that every U.S.-listed security received a full intrinsic-value model. Earlier weekly entries were read only after today's ranking was fixed and are retained below solely as a record.

### 1. Adobe (ADBE) - High conviction

**Why it looks undervalued.** Adobe trades at about 15.7 times trailing GAAP earnings, a substantial discount to the broad market. First-half FY2026 operating cash flow of $5.12 billion annualizes to roughly 9% of the current market capitalization before modest capital spending. The valuation assumes that generative AI will meaningfully erode Adobe's moat or pricing power.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal Q2 revenue rose 13% to a record $6.62 billion, subscription revenue grew 14%, total ARR reached $27.10 billion, and remaining performance obligations increased 13% to $22.27 billion. GAAP operating income was $2.24 billion and quarterly operating cash flow was $2.17 billion. Entrenched creative workflows, enterprise trust, recurring revenue, and high cash conversion remain durable advantages. [Adobe Q2 FY2026 SEC release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000109/adbeex991q226.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** AI-native creation tools could lower switching costs, compress pricing, or shift value away from Adobe applications. Semrush contributes to reported growth, stock compensation remains material, and Q2 included a goodwill impairment. The thesis weakens if organic ARR decelerates materially, AI monetization stays weak, or operating margins structurally decline.

### 2. GoDaddy (GDDY) - High conviction

**Why it looks undervalued.** At a market capitalization near $12.5 billion, management's approximately $1.8 billion 2026 free-cash-flow target implies a 14.4% equity FCF yield. The shares also trade around 14.2 times trailing GAAP earnings. Even after adding $2.7 billion of net debt, the cash yield remains attractive for a recurring platform still growing.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue grew 7%, operating income rose 29%, operating margin expanded 450 basis points to 26.4%, and free cash flow increased 13% to $443.5 million. Applications and Commerce revenue grew 11%, ARR reached $4.42 billion, and ARPU increased 9%. GoDaddy's registrar scale, bundled web tools, and customer relationships support retention, while year-to-date repurchases reduced fully diluted shares by 7% on a gross basis. [GoDaddy Q2 2026 results](https://investors.godaddy.net/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/GDDY-Q2-2026-Earnings-Release.pdf)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Customer count is nearly flat, Core Platform growth is only low single digits, and AI tools may commoditize basic site creation. Debt, cybersecurity, registry pricing, and poorly timed buybacks are additional risks. The thesis would weaken if bookings stall, A&C growth drops below mid-single digits, or margin gains require underinvestment.

### 3. Deckers Brands (DECK) - High conviction

**Why it looks undervalued.** The shares trade at about 12.1 times the midpoint of management's raised FY2027 GAAP EPS guidance of $7.35-$7.50. That is a value multiple despite positive brand growth and a debt-free balance sheet.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal Q1 revenue rose 5.7% to $1.02 billion; HOKA grew 7.7%, UGG grew 4.9%, direct-to-consumer sales rose 13%, and international revenue grew 8.4%. Gross margin improved to 56.4%. Deckers ended June with $1.60 billion of cash, lower inventory, and no outstanding borrowings, giving it ample resilience and repurchase capacity. [Deckers Q1 FY2027 SEC release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910521/000091052126000015/deckex991pressrelease-6302.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Footwear demand is fashion-sensitive, HOKA and UGG create brand concentration, and first-quarter operating income declined despite revenue growth. Tariffs, promotions, channel inventory, or loss of brand heat could compress margins. The thesis weakens if DTC comparable growth turns negative or inventory begins outpacing sales.

### 4. The Cigna Group (CI) - High conviction

**Why it looks undervalued.** Cigna trades at about 9.2 times management's raised 2026 adjusted EPS outlook of at least $30.45 and about 11.6 times trailing GAAP earnings. That discount is wide relative to the market despite continued earnings growth.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 7% to $71.7 billion, adjusted income from operations increased 6% to $2.05 billion, and adjusted EPS reached $7.78. Cigna Healthcare pretax adjusted profit grew 17%, Specialty and Care Services profit grew 22%, and the adjusted SG&A ratio improved. Diversification across insurance, pharmacy benefits, specialty pharmacy, and care services provides scale and multiple earnings drivers. [Cigna Q2 2026 SEC release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1739940/000114036126030135/ef20078875_ex99-1.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Pharmacy Benefit Services pretax adjusted profit fell 27%, the medical care ratio rose to 84.5%, and PBM regulation remains a major overhang. Drug-pricing reform, adverse medical costs, client losses, or leverage could justify the low multiple. Sustained Evernorth profit contraction would materially weaken the case.

### 5. Comcast (CMCSA) - Medium conviction

**Why it looks undervalued.** Comcast trades at about 8.7 times trailing GAAP earnings. A current S&P 500 cash-flow screen estimates $20.4 billion of trailing free cash flow, or roughly a 23% yield; because separation activity and working-capital timing can distort that figure, the reported Q2 free cash flow of $4.6 billion is the firmer reference point.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Comcast's broadband network, business connectivity, media assets, studios, and theme parks remain difficult to replicate. Q2 operating cash flow increased 3.5%, free cash flow rose 2.3%, wireless lines exceeded 10 million after a record quarter, Business Services EBITDA grew 5%, and Peacock achieved its first quarterly profit. [Comcast Q2 2026 results](https://cmcsa.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/comcast-reports-2nd-quarter-2026-results)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Consolidated revenue declined 1.2%, adjusted EPS fell 16.7%, residential broadband customers are still shrinking, and the planned NBCUniversal/Sky separation creates execution and allocation uncertainty. Fiber and fixed-wireless competition, cord-cutting, capital intensity, and theme-park cyclicality make this the lowest-confidence top-five idea. Continued broadband losses without ARPU or wireless offsets would invalidate the thesis.

## Near misses and exclusions

- **Intuit (INTU):** The franchise remains high quality and the post-results valuation is reasonable, but today's ranking found stronger current cash-yield support in GoDaddy and cleaner balance-sheet support in Deckers. Its removal is a relative-ranking change, not evidence of a one-day business deterioration.
- **EOG Resources (EOG):** Strong assets and capital discipline remain attractive, but today's quality-value score applies a larger normalization haircut to commodity-driven cash flow after the energy sector's strong July performance.
- **NXP Semiconductors (NXPI):** Still a quality embedded-chip business at a reasonable multiple, but its roughly 5% cash yield offers less valuation protection than today's selected operating companies.
- **Fiserv (FISV):** The 13.7% screen-estimated FCF yield is tempting, but Q2 organic revenue fell 5%, adjusted EPS fell 26%, adjusted margin contracted sharply, and first-half FCF declined. High debt and execution risk make the low multiple look more like an unresolved turnaround than quality value today. [Fiserv Q2 2026 SEC release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/798354/000079835426000028/fisvq226earningsrelease.htm)
- **Centene, Charter, General Motors, and legacy packaged-food names:** Very high screen-implied cash yields are offset by medical-cost volatility, leverage, cyclicality, capital intensity, or structural revenue pressure. They do not clear today's quality threshold.

## Conclusion

**Adobe and GoDaddy are today's strongest quality-value candidates.** Adobe offers the best combination of franchise durability, double-digit recurring growth, and a market multiple far below the index. GoDaddy has the highest current owner-earnings yield among the selected non-cyclical businesses, supported by margin expansion and share reduction. Deckers offers the cleanest balance sheet and lowest forward earnings multiple; Cigna has the clearest near-term earnings support but heavier regulatory risk. Comcast has the widest headline cash-flow discount, paired with the most significant structural uncertainty.

This shortlist is a starting point for deeper diligence, not a prediction of near-term performance or a claim of certain intrinsic value.
