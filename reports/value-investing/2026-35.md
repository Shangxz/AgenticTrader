# Value Investing Weekly Report — 2026-W35

_Last updated: August 24, 2026 (prices observed around 8:00 a.m. ET). Research support only; not personal financial advice._

## Market context and method

The broad U.S. market is not obviously cheap: the S&P 500 trades at nearly 20 times forward earnings, while mid- and small-cap groups are closer to 16 times. Morningstar's early-third-quarter work estimated the U.S. market at an 8% aggregate discount to its fair-value estimates, with small-value stocks the cheapest style segment. This makes company-specific quality and cash-flow evidence more important than a low headline multiple alone.

Today's ranking was built independently from a broad pass across U.S.-listed large-, mid-, and small-cap operating companies. The screen emphasized earnings/free-cash-flow yield, durable competitive position, margins and returns on capital, balance-sheet resilience, and current operating momentum. Financials were judged on earnings quality and capital sensitivity rather than conventional free cash flow. Prior weekly picks were not used; this is the first update of the week.

Reference screens: [Kiplinger market and valuation context](https://www.kiplinger.com/investing/stocks/best-undervalued-stocks), [Morningstar Q3 2026 valuation context](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/33-undervalued-stocks-buy-before-market-catches), and [Morningstar wide-moat screen](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/10-undervalued-wide-moat-stocks-worth-buying-right-now).

### 1. The Cigna Group (CI) — High conviction

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $277.51, the shares trade at roughly 9.1 times management's raised 2026 adjusted EPS outlook of at least $30.45. The current trailing P/E is about 11.5 times. That is a sizable discount to the broad market despite positive earnings revisions rather than a collapsing earnings base.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Second-quarter revenue rose 7% to $71.7 billion, adjusted income from operations increased 6% to $2.1 billion, and adjusted EPS rose to $7.78 from $7.20. Cigna Healthcare pretax adjusted income grew 17%, while Specialty and Care Services grew 22%. The mix provides diversification across insurance, pharmacy-benefit services, specialty pharmacy, and care services. [Cigna Q2 2026 results](https://newsroom.thecignagroup.com/2026-07-30-The-Cigna-Group-Reports-Strong-Second-Quarter-2026-Results%2C-Raises-2026-Outlook)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Pharmacy Benefit Services pretax adjusted income fell 27%, Evernorth margin remains thin, and the medical care ratio rose to 84.5%. PBM regulation, drug-pricing reform, adverse medical-cost development, or leverage could make the low multiple appropriate. A sustained fall in Evernorth profit or failure to hold medical margins would weaken the thesis.

### 2. NXP Semiconductors (NXPI) — High conviction

**Why it looks undervalued.** Current screens place NXP near 14 times forward earnings, below the broad market. In Q2 the company generated $791 million of non-GAAP free cash flow, equal to 22.6% of revenue. Annualizing that quarter implies a roughly 5.5% equity FCF yield at the current market capitalization, although a single semiconductor quarter should not be treated as normalized.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue grew 19% to $3.50 billion, GAAP gross margin was 57.3%, and GAAP operating margin reached 30.6%. NXP's entrenched automotive, industrial, communications, and secure-edge positions create long product cycles and customer qualification costs. The company also repaid $750 million of notes at maturity from available cash. [NXP Q2 2026 results](https://investors.nxp.com/news-releases/news-release-details/nxp-semiconductors-reports-second-quarter-2026-results/)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Automotive and industrial semiconductor demand is cyclical, China exposure and export controls can impair growth, and high current margins may be above mid-cycle levels. The thesis would weaken if inventories rise, design-win momentum slows, or FCF conversion falls materially below the Q2 level.

### 3. Salesforce (CRM) — Medium-high conviction

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $209.17, Salesforce trades at about 14.8 times the midpoint of FY2027 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $14.06-$14.12. That is a modest multiple for a recurring-revenue software leader still guiding to 11% revenue growth and a 34.3% non-GAAP operating margin.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** First-quarter revenue grew 13% to $11.1 billion; current remaining performance obligations rose 14% to $33.6 billion; GAAP operating margin reached 21.1%; and free cash flow was $6.6 billion. These figures support durable switching costs, a large installed base, and strong cash economics even before the AI strategy is fully proven. [Salesforce Q1 FY2027 results](https://investor.salesforce.com/news/news-details/2026/Salesforce-Delivers-Record-First-Quarter-Fiscal-2027-Results/default.aspx)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Organic growth is lower than headline growth because Informatica contributes roughly three percentage points to full-year growth. AI-native competition could reduce seat-based pricing or switching costs. The $25 billion debt issuance used for an accelerated repurchase also makes capital allocation more aggressive. Slower current-RPO growth or failure to monetize Agentforce would undermine the valuation case.

### 4. Zoetis (ZTS) — Medium conviction

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $77.73, Zoetis trades at roughly 12.5 times the midpoint of its revised 2026 adjusted EPS range of $6.15-$6.25. That is a low multiple for the global animal-health leader and embeds substantial skepticism after the guidance cut.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Zoetis remains the world's leading animal-health company, with a broad portfolio across companion animals, livestock, diagnostics, parasiticides, dermatology, vaccines, and pain products. Q2 international companion-animal sales grew 5% organically and livestock sales rose in both the U.S. and international segments, showing that the franchise is broader than the currently weak U.S. pet market. [Zoetis Q2 2026 results](https://investor.zoetis.com/news/news-details/2026/Zoetis-Announces-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** This is not a clean compounder today: management now expects organic revenue to decline 1%-3% and adjusted net income to decline 5%-9% in 2026. U.S. companion-animal sales fell 11% amid lower clinic visits, price sensitivity, competition, and generic pressure. Further guidance cuts or persistent dermatology/parasiticide share losses would make the stock a value trap.

### 5. GE HealthCare Technologies (GEHC) — Medium conviction

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $74.82, GE HealthCare trades at about 15.3 times the midpoint of 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $4.80-$5.00. Guided free cash flow of approximately $1.6 billion equates to about a 4.7% yield on the current equity value. Morningstar recently estimated a 30% discount to its $88 fair-value estimate, although that estimate is not a guarantee of value.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** The company is a top-three global medical-imaging player with a large hospital installed base. Q2 organic revenue grew 3.5%; organic orders rose 11.1%; book-to-bill was 1.15 times; and backlog reached $23.9 billion. Advanced Imaging and Pharmaceutical Diagnostics grew organically by 5.0% and 14.6%, respectively. [GE HealthCare Q2 2026 results](https://investor.gehealthcare.com/news-releases/news-release-details/ge-healthcare-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results) [Morningstar value-company screen](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/10-best-value-stocks-buy-long-term)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Total debt is $10.1 billion versus $2.1 billion of cash. Patient Care Solutions organic revenue fell 13.5% and the segment posted a loss; adjusted EBIT margin also declined 40 basis points. Inflation, hospital capital-spending weakness, or failure to repair/dispose of PCS could erase much of the apparent discount.

## Near misses and exclusions

- **Charles Schwab (SCHW):** A durable scaled platform and about 15 times forward earnings, but the present discount is smaller than Cigna's and the balance sheet remains sensitive to rates, client cash sorting, and funding mix.
- **Intuit (INTU):** Strong switching costs, double-digit growth, and attractive forward earnings, but the 17% workforce reduction and rapid AI-led reorganization add execution risk immediately ahead of the next full-year report.
- **Nike (NKE):** The brand moat is real and the share-price decline is large, but a roughly 27 times trailing P/E alongside falling revenue and profit does not yet provide enough evidence that the turnaround is priced with a margin of safety.
- **United Airlines and other airlines:** Low multiples are offset by high fixed costs, fuel sensitivity, and balance-sheet cyclicality; they do not clear today's quality threshold.
- **Neurocrine Biosciences (NBIX):** Attractive growth-adjusted valuation, but product concentration and emerging safety/regulatory uncertainty make the evidence less durable than the selected names.

## Conclusion

**Cigna and NXP are today's strongest quality-value candidates.** Cigna has the clearest near-term earnings support and the lowest guidance-based multiple; NXP offers the best combination of margins, free-cash-flow conversion, and a defensible technical franchise. Salesforce is the strongest higher-quality software idea if AI disruption proves less severe than the price implies. Zoetis offers the largest fallen-quality upside but also the clearest risk of another estimate reset, while GE HealthCare is a more leveraged, moderate-return opportunity supported by backlog and installed-base durability.

This shortlist is a starting point for deeper diligence, not a prediction of near-term performance or a claim of certain intrinsic value.
