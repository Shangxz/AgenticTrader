## Fresh screen and method

Today's ranking was built independently, without using prior reports. The starting set was a broad systematic scan of roughly 4,600 U.S.-traded equities, cross-checked against an all-market valuation database. I then excluded funds, shells, pre-revenue companies, most microcaps, and businesses whose apparent free cash flow is not economically comparable (especially lenders and insurers).

The second pass emphasized:

- valuation versus earnings and free cash flow;
- durable margins, returns on capital, and cash conversion;
- balance-sheet resilience;
- evidence that the franchise is stable or improving; and
- a penalty for concentration, leverage, cyclicality, accounting noise, or a plausible structural decline.

Broad-screen references: [Ticker Nerd U.S. value ranking, October 17](https://tickernerd.com/stock/best-value-stocks/), [Fair Value Calculator U.S. quality-value screen, October 19](https://www.fairvalue-calculator.com/undervalued-stocks/us), and [Intrinsiqq cash-yield screen](https://intrinsiqq.com/blog/best-value-stocks).

### 1. Deckers Outdoor (NYSE: DECK)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $85.71, Deckers trades at roughly 11.5x the midpoint of management's FY2027 EPS guidance of $7.35-$7.50. That is a low multiple for a debt-free branded consumer company still expecting growth from both core franchises. The company also plans to repurchase shares equal to about 80% of projected FY2027 free cash flow.

**Why it qualifies as quality.** First-quarter FY2027 revenue rose 5.7% to $1.02 billion; HOKA grew 7.7% and UGG 4.9%. Deckers had $1.60 billion of cash, no outstanding borrowings, and inventory declined year over year. Management still expects HOKA to grow at a low-double-digit rate, UGG at a mid-single-digit rate, and operating margin above 21.5%. These are unusually strong economics for an apparel-and-footwear company.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Growth has slowed sharply from prior years, operating leverage weakened in the latest quarter, and the thesis depends heavily on two brands. Tariffs, fashion risk, weather, and a consumer slowdown could compress margins. The thesis weakens if HOKA falls below mid-single-digit growth for several quarters or if full-price sell-through deteriorates.

**Evidence:** [Deckers Q1 FY2027 results](https://ir.deckers.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/661/deckers-brands-reports-first-quarter-fiscal-year-2027-financial-results) and [FY2026 annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910521/000091052126000020/deckfy26annualreport.pdf).

### 2. GoDaddy (NYSE: GDDY)

**Why it looks undervalued.** GoDaddy's $13.9 billion equity value is about 7.7x management's approximately $1.8 billion FY2026 free-cash-flow target, a roughly 13% equity FCF yield. The trailing P/E is about 15.7x. Buybacks are meaningful: through July 29 the company spent $852 million and reduced fully diluted shares by 7% from year-end.

**Why it qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 7%, operating income rose 29%, and free cash flow rose 13%. Annualized recurring revenue reached $4.42 billion, while Applications & Commerce revenue grew 11% and its segment EBITDA margin expanded 250 basis points to 46.8%. The installed base of 20.5 million customers and recurring domain/hosting relationships support durable cash generation.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Net debt was $2.7 billion, about 1.5x the annual FCF target, and customer count is nearly flat. AI-native site creation could weaken GoDaddy's historical position at the front door of small-business web presence. Airo's $50 million annualized bookings run rate is promising but still immaterial. The thesis weakens if Core revenue turns negative or buybacks merely offset business erosion.

**Evidence:** [GoDaddy Q2 2026 results filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1609711/000160971126000087/gddyex991-20260630xq2earni.htm).

### 3. Accenture (NYSE: ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $229.68, Accenture trades near 15.7x the midpoint of FY2027 GAAP EPS guidance of $14.39-$14.81. FY2026 free cash flow was $11.6 billion, equal to roughly 8.2% of the current market value. This is a reasonable price for a capital-light global leader with a net-cash balance sheet, even after the stock's sharp post-earnings rebound.

**Why it qualifies as quality.** FY2026 revenue grew 6% to $74.2 billion, adjusted operating margin expanded to 15.8%, and new bookings were $84.5 billion, or 1.1x revenue. Managed Services bookings were particularly resilient at a 1.2x book-to-bill. Accenture ended August with $12.8 billion of cash and serves a diversified global enterprise client base.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Generative AI may reduce labor hours and pressure traditional consulting economics faster than Accenture can monetize reinvention work. FY2027 revenue guidance of 3%-6% is solid, not exceptional, and acquisitions contribute to growth. The thesis weakens if consulting book-to-bill stays below 1.0 or margins fall despite AI-related productivity.

**Evidence:** [Accenture Q4 and FY2026 results](https://investor.accenture.com/~/media/Files/A/accenture-v4/investors/documents/accenture-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-fiscal-2026-resultss.pdf) and [FY2026 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1467373/000146737326000049/acn-20260831.htm).

### 4. PayPal (NASDAQ: PYPL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $56.70, PayPal trades at roughly 10.5x management's FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guide of about $5.38. The company repurchased $1.5 billion of stock in Q2 and $6.0 billion over the trailing year, approximately 12% of today's market capitalization. Cash and investments of $15.3 billion exceeded debt of $13.4 billion.

**Why it qualifies as quality.** Q2 payment volume grew 10% to $486 billion, revenue rose 5%, and adjusted free cash flow was $1.83 billion. PayPal still has 439 million active accounts, substantial global acceptance, and a balance sheet capable of funding product investment and capital returns.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** The low multiple is deserved in part: GAAP operating margin contracted 171 basis points, non-GAAP operating income fell 8%, active accounts were flat, and transaction-margin dollars grew only 1%. Competition in branded checkout and lower-margin unbranded processing remain the central trap risks. The thesis requires transaction-margin growth to improve without relying on interest income or perpetual buybacks.

**Evidence:** [PayPal Q2 2026 earnings release filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633917/000163391726000080/pypl2q-26earningsrelease.htm).

### 5. Exelixis (NASDAQ: EXEL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The headline trailing P/E of about 19.5x is not deep value, but the balance sheet held approximately $1.38 billion of cash and marketable securities at June 30, equal to about 8.5% of the current market capitalization, with no material financial debt. Revenue growth and the funded pipeline make the multiple more attractive than it first appears.

**Why it qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 10.6% to $629 million and U.S. cabozantinib product revenue rose 10.2% to $573 million, primarily on volume. Gross product economics are strong, the company is profitable while funding a broad oncology pipeline, and it has repurchased $2.9 billion of stock since 2023. The next-generation drug zanzalintinib provides a credible, though not certain, path to extend the franchise.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Revenue is highly concentrated in cabozantinib. Generic litigation, patent challenges, pricing pressure, or disappointing zanzalintinib trial/regulatory outcomes could impair value quickly. The prior guidance range was reduced, and the STELLAR-303 non-liver-metastases subgroup showed only a non-statistically significant survival trend. This is the most binary idea in the top five and merits the lowest position size/confidence.

**Evidence:** [Exelixis Q2 2026 results](https://ir.exelixis.com/node/27651), [Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/939767/000093976726000098/exel-20260703.htm), and [patent-risk disclosure](https://ir.exelixis.com/static-files/9858c84b-8e64-4428-ae4b-6883a0383161).

## Conclusion

**Deckers is today's strongest overall quality-value candidate:** a roughly 11.5x guided earnings multiple, net cash, two growing global brands, and aggressive buybacks provide the cleanest combination of valuation and business quality.

**GoDaddy and Accenture are the next-best candidates.** GoDaddy offers the highest current cash yield but carries more leverage and technology-disruption risk; Accenture offers the strongest diversification and balance-sheet quality but at a less dramatic discount. PayPal is a cash-return special situation that still needs margin proof. Exelixis has attractive economics and optionality, but drug concentration and regulatory risk make it less suitable as a core holding.

The shortlist is a research starting point, not a recommendation. Upcoming earnings and guidance should be checked before acting, especially because DECK, GDDY, PYPL, and several near-misses report within the next few weeks.
