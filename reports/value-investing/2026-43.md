## Fresh screen and method — October 21

Today's ranking was completed independently before reading the October 19-20 entries below. The first pass covered 3,527 U.S.-listed companies worth at least $200 million, then cross-checked the 515-company large-cap universe for free-cash-flow yield and a separate analyst-curated list of high-quality businesses trading below estimated fair value. Financials were evaluated with underwriting profitability, return on equity, capital strength, and book value rather than industrial-company free cash flow.

The ranking favored multi-year earning power, cash conversion, durable competitive position, balance-sheet resilience, and a current valuation that does not require optimistic growth. It penalized peak-cycle profits, acquisition-adjusted accounting, leverage, customer concentration, and secular revenue decline. Multiples are screening signals, not intrinsic-value estimates.

Broad-screen references: [Sector Alpha U.S. market screens, data through October 20](https://www.sectoralpha.in/us-stock-screener), [Sweet Value Lab large-cap FCF-yield screen, October 19](https://sweetvaluelab.com/lists/highest-free-cash-flow-yield), and [Morningstar's October quality-company value screen](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/10-best-companies-invest-now).

### 1. Chubb (NYSE: CB)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Chubb trades at about 12.7x trailing earnings. Using year-end 2025 book value of $188.59 and the 3.6% first-half increase reported through June gives an estimated Q2 book value near $195 per share, or roughly 1.8x book. That is reasonable for an insurer producing mid-teens operating returns with unusually strong underwriting.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 core operating EPS rose 18.2%, the P&C combined ratio was 83.8%, and core operating ROE was 14.5%. Net premiums written grew 3.6%, while life premiums grew 7.5%. Chubb's global spread by geography, product, and customer type reduces dependence on any single insurance line.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** Property-catastrophe losses, reserve mistakes, social inflation, or faster commercial pricing deterioration can quickly change normalized earnings. Fixed-income mark-to-market moves also affect book value. The thesis weakens if the current accident-year combined ratio moves materially toward the mid-90s without offsetting premium growth, or if reserve development turns adverse.

**Evidence:** [Chubb Q2 2026 results](https://investors.chubb.com/News--Events/news/news-details/2026/Chubb-Reports-Second-Quarter-Per-Share-Net-Income-of-7-30-and-Per-Share-Core-Operating-Income-of-7-26-Up-18-2-Consolidated-Net-Premiums-Written-of-14-7-Billion-Up-3-6-with-PC-and-Life-Insurance-Up-3-0-and-7-5-PC-Combined-Ratio-of-83-8/default.aspx) and [Chubb FY2025 results](https://investors.chubb.com/News--Events/news/news-details/2026/Chubb-Reports-Fourth-Quarter/default.aspx).

### 2. Berkshire Hathaway (NYSE: BRK.B)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The current quote provider shows a trailing multiple near 12.6x, while the broad screen shows 13.6x. GAAP P/E is imperfect for Berkshire because unrealized investment gains flow through earnings, so the stronger valuation argument is the combination of operating businesses, a large equity portfolio, and about $365.5 billion of cash and equivalents at Q2 against the equity value.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 operating earnings were about $13.0 billion, up roughly 16% year over year, supported by railroad, energy, manufacturing, service, and retail operations. The balance sheet offers exceptional shock absorption and capital-allocation flexibility; the company also resumed meaningful repurchases in Q2.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** A conglomerate discount is not guaranteed to close. Insurance catastrophe risk, equity-portfolio concentration, regulated-utility capital needs, and succession-era capital allocation all matter. Reported P/E can be misleading in either direction. The thesis weakens if operating earnings stagnate while large acquisitions or repurchases destroy per-share value.

**Evidence:** [Berkshire Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1067983/000119312526341032/brka-20260630.htm) and [Berkshire Q2 earnings summary](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1067983/000119312526344495/d159922dex991.htm).

### 3. Progressive (NYSE: PGR)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Progressive trades near 11.1x trailing earnings. The apparent bargain is less obvious on assets: the $221.93 share price is about 3.7x September book value of $59.51. The thesis therefore depends on sustained superior underwriting and profitable growth rather than simple mean reversion to book value.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** September's companywide combined ratio was 90.0%, monthly net income more than doubled year over year, and Q3 net premiums written rose 4% to $22.3 billion. Progressive's direct distribution, pricing data, telematics, and claims scale support a structural cost and selection advantage in auto insurance.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** The premium-to-book valuation leaves less margin for underwriting normalization than the P/E suggests. Claims severity, repair costs, regulation, catastrophe losses, or aggressive competitor pricing can compress returns. The thesis weakens if premium growth slows while the combined ratio settles above the mid-90s.

**Evidence:** [Progressive September and Q3 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/80661/000008066126000367/pgr202609ex99earningsrelea.htm).

### 4. Omnicom (NYSE: OMC)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Omnicom's $22.8 billion market value compares with roughly $2.8 billion of last-fiscal-year free cash flow in the current large-cap screen, an indicative cash yield near 12%. The headline trailing P/E is distorted by merger and integration items, so cash flow and adjusted operating results are more useful than unadjusted EPS.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 core-operations revenue grew 6.1% organically, adjusted EBITA margin reached 17.8%, and adjusted EPS rose 29%. Scale across media, creative, health, public relations, commerce, and data helps Omnicom win consolidated global mandates and fund technology investment.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** Integration of Interpublic adds debt, restructuring, client-conflict, and culture risk. AI may pressure labor-based agency economics, while major account reviews can produce abrupt losses. The thesis weakens if organic growth falls toward zero, promised synergies fail to reach cash flow, or leverage remains elevated after integration.

**Evidence:** [Omnicom Q2 2026 results](https://investor.omc.com/financials/quarterly-results/) and [SEC-filed Q2 investor presentation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/29989/000002998926000018/a2026q2investorpresentat.htm).

### 5. PepsiCo (NASDAQ: PEP)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At about 16.8x trailing earnings, PepsiCo trades at a restrained multiple for a branded global staples company. The valuation does not assume a rapid North American recovery, creating upside if volume and productivity normalize.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q3 net revenue rose 5.6%, organic revenue grew 3.1%, and organic volume grew across global beverages and convenient foods. The portfolio includes numerous billion-dollar brands, extensive distribution, and a large international business that can offset weaker regions.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** Q3 core operating margin fell 35 basis points, North American performance remains soft, input costs are rising, and price increases may hurt already pressured consumers. Leverage and acquisitions reduce financial flexibility. The thesis weakens if volume turns negative again or price/mix cannot cover inflation and reinvestment.

**Evidence:** [PepsiCo Q3 2026 SEC earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/77476/000007747626000050/q320268-kxexhibit991.htm) and [Q3 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/77476/000007747626000048/pep-20260905.htm).

### 6. Comcast (NASDAQ: CMCSA)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Comcast trades around 7.2x trailing earnings. The large-cap screen shows $21.9 billion of last-fiscal-year free cash flow against a market value near $79 billion, while Q2 alone produced $4.6 billion. Even after allowing for working-capital and portfolio-change noise, the market is discounting a substantial erosion of the current cash stream.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Broadband remains a high-margin infrastructure franchise, wireless lines reached 10.2 million after a record 448,000 Q2 additions, and broadband customer losses improved by 34,000 year over year. Media, studios, theme parks, and the planned separation create multiple sources of value and potential strategic focus.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** This is the lowest-confidence finalist because the discount may be structurally justified. Fixed-wireless and fiber competition are pressuring subscribers and ARPU; Q2 adjusted EBITDA fell 13.4% and adjusted EPS fell 16.7%. Debt, separation execution, and legacy-TV decline add risk. The thesis weakens if broadband losses accelerate or post-separation free cash flow proves materially below the screen figure.

**Evidence:** [Comcast Q2 2026 results](https://www.cmcsa.com/node/45906) and [Comcast Q2 earnings call](https://www.cmcsa.com/static-files/8a2ae730-efbd-41c5-a2a9-b96aef03ced2).

## Current conclusion

**Chubb is today's strongest overall candidate.** Its valuation is modest relative to underwriting quality, operating returns, and diversification. **Berkshire is the most balance-sheet-resilient candidate**, though investors should ignore the apparent precision of GAAP P/E. **Progressive has the strongest operating franchise signal**, but its price-to-book premium requires sustained underwriting superiority.

Omnicom and PepsiCo offer credible quality at more moderate discounts, with integration and North American execution as the key tests. Comcast has the widest apparent margin of safety on cash flow, but also the clearest risk that the market is discounting a lasting decline rather than a temporary problem.

#### 1. Deckers Outdoor (NYSE: DECK)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $87.24, Deckers trades at about 11.7x the midpoint of management's FY2027 EPS guidance of $7.35-$7.50. That valuation leaves room for slower growth without requiring a distressed-business outcome.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q1 FY2027 revenue rose 5.7% to $1.02 billion, with HOKA up 7.7% and UGG up 4.9%. Deckers held $1.60 billion of cash, had no outstanding borrowings, and continues to target an operating margin above 21.5%. Management also expects repurchases equal to about 80% of projected FY2027 free cash flow.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** Growth has slowed, two brands dominate the economics, and footwear demand is exposed to fashion, tariffs, weather, and consumer weakness. The thesis would weaken if HOKA growth stays below the mid-single digits, full-price sell-through deteriorates, or the operating-margin guide is cut.

**Evidence:** [Deckers Q1 FY2027 results](https://ir.deckers.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/661/deckers-brands-reports-first-quarter-fiscal-year-2027-financial-results) and [FY2026 annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910521/000091052126000020/deckfy26annualreport.pdf).

#### 2. T. Rowe Price (NASDAQ: TROW)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $100.10, T. Rowe Price trades near 10.1x trailing earnings. At June 30 the balance sheet held $3.23 billion of cash and $4.09 billion of investments, together equal to roughly one-third of the current $21.4 billion market value, with no material financial debt shown in the earnings-release balance sheet.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Quarter-end assets under management reached a record $1.893 trillion, up 12.9% year over year. Q2 adjusted EPS rose 14.7% to $2.57, and the company returned $441 million through dividends and repurchases. The retirement franchise, diversified strategies, and balance-sheet strength provide staying power while ETFs, separately managed accounts, and alternatives grow.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** Fundamental active equity remains under pressure: Q2 net outflows were $6.5 billion, first-half outflows were $20.2 billion, and the effective fee rate fell to 38.1 basis points from 39.6. Much of the AUM gain came from markets rather than organic flows. The thesis weakens if equity outflows accelerate, fee compression overwhelms cost control, or newer channels fail to offset legacy mutual-fund erosion.

**Evidence:** [T. Rowe Price Q2 2026 earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1113169/000162828026051211/earningsreleaseq22026.htm).

#### 3. Leidos (NYSE: LDOS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $126.05, Leidos trades at roughly 10.2x the midpoint of FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $12.20-$12.50 and about 11.7x trailing GAAP earnings. Management raised revenue, EPS, and operating-cash-flow guidance after Q2.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 7%, including 4% organically, and the company booked $5.0 billion of awards. Total backlog was $48.7 billion, including $10.2 billion funded, versus annual revenue guidance of $18.2-$18.4 billion. The portfolio spans defense, intelligence, homeland security, health, energy, and air-traffic systems, supporting visibility beyond a normal commercial cycle.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** Debt increased to $6.0 billion after acquisitions, while cash was $748 million. Q2 adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 13.8% from 15.2%, partly because the prior year included one-time gains. Government budget delays, contract losses, acquisition execution, or sustained margin compression could erase the apparent discount.

**Evidence:** [Leidos Q2 2026 results and guidance](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336920/000133692026000243/ldos070326q2pressreleaseex.htm).

#### 4. Cognizant Technology Solutions (NASDAQ: CTSH)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $66.79, Cognizant trades at about 11.6x the midpoint of FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $5.70-$5.82 and 14.3x trailing GAAP earnings. The company authorized substantial repurchases and had $2.34 billion remaining under its authorization at June 30.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue grew 4.5%, operating income grew 7.0%, and adjusted operating margin expanded 40 basis points to 16.0%. Trailing 12-month bookings rose 5% to $29.1 billion, and management expects 4.0%-5.5% constant-currency revenue growth with further margin expansion for 2026. Q2 free cash flow improved to $459 million from $331 million.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** The IT-services model faces AI-related pricing pressure, wage inflation, and clients shifting work in-house. Acquisitions have increased goodwill and pushed debt above cash by roughly $0.5 billion. First-half free cash flow remained below the prior year, and restructuring adds execution risk. The thesis weakens if bookings soften or margin gains come mainly from cuts rather than durable demand.

**Evidence:** [Cognizant Q2 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1058290/000105829026000030/exhibit9916302026.htm) and [Q2 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1058290/000105829026000031/ctsh-20260630.htm).

#### 5. AXIS Capital (NYSE: AXS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $99.71, AXIS trades at about 7.1x trailing earnings and 1.24x June 30 book value of $80.67 per diluted share. That is a modest book premium for an insurer currently producing mid-teens returns on equity.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** First-half operating return on average common equity was 15.9%, the combined ratio was 91.5%, and book value per diluted share rose 14.7% year over year. Q2 gross premiums written grew 6%, and the company returned $122 million through dividends and repurchases. The underwriting result, rather than investment gains alone, supports the quality case.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** Insurance earnings are inherently volatile. Catastrophe losses, social inflation, reserve development, or faster pricing deterioration could make trailing earnings look cyclically high. Q2's combined ratio worsened to 93.1% from 88.9% a year earlier. The thesis weakens if the current accident-year ex-cat ratio rises materially or book-value growth stalls after normalizing catastrophe experience.

**Evidence:** [AXIS Capital Q2 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1214816/000121481626000201/q22026pressrelease.htm).
