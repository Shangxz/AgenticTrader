# Quality-Value Weekly Report - 2026-W38

_Last updated: 2026-09-18 (America/Detroit). Research support only; not personal financial advice._

## Market context

The valuation backdrop remains selective rather than broadly cheap. As of September 16, the S&P 500 traded near 19.0x forward earnings and 22.4x trailing earnings. Morningstar estimated the US market was about 9% below its aggregate fair-value estimate at August 31, but said that discount was concentrated in seven mega-cap stocks; excluding them, its coverage was around fair value. Rising long-term rates and elevated AI capital spending raise the hurdle for long-duration equities, so today's ranking emphasizes visible cash generation, balance-sheet capacity, and business durability.

Today's selection was completed from a fresh market evaluation before this file's September 14-17 entries were read. The broad pass used three complementary screens: a 5,039-stock US quality/value/momentum universe, a daily value ranking of roughly 1,500 liquid companies from a 4,600-stock database, and a positive-free-cash-flow screen requiring at least an 8% FCF yield, a quality score of 65, sub-18x earnings, and no share-count growth. I then reviewed current prices and the latest company-reported results, penalizing single-product dependence, peak-cycle earnings, leverage, structural decline, and cash flows inflated by working capital or temporary capex timing.

Prices and market capitalizations below are September 18 snapshots. Ratios are approximate and move with price. Guided cash-flow yields use company guidance, not analyst consensus.

### 1. Accenture (ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Accenture trades at about 15.2x trailing GAAP earnings. Management's FY2026 free-cash-flow outlook of $10.8-$11.5 billion represents roughly 9.2%-9.8% of today's equity value, an unusually high cash yield for a capital-light global services franchise.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal Q3 revenue rose 6% in US dollars to $18.7 billion, operating margin expanded 20 basis points to 17.0%, diluted EPS rose 9%, and quarterly FCF was $3.6 billion. Accenture had $10.2 billion of cash at May 31 and 104 client bookings of at least $100 million year to date. Its global delivery network, broad enterprise relationships, and modest capital intensity support durable returns.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** FY2026 local-currency revenue growth guidance is only 3%-4%, Q3 bookings declined year over year, and US federal work is a drag. Generative AI could increase productivity but compress labor-based pricing. The thesis weakens if bookings contract for several quarters, guided FCF falls materially, or AI efficiencies accrue mostly to clients.

**Evidence:** [Accenture Q3 FY2026 results](https://investor.accenture.com/~/media/Files/A/accenture-v4/investors/earnings-reports/2026/accenture-3q-fy26-earnings-release.pdf)

### 2. GoDaddy (GDDY)

**Why it looks undervalued.** GoDaddy's reaffirmed 2026 FCF target of about $1.8 billion equates to approximately 14.0% of its current market value, while the shares trade near 14.6x trailing earnings. Management repurchased $852 million of stock through July 29, reducing fully diluted shares by 7% from year-end.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** The company has a subscription-heavy base of domains, hosting, payments, and web-presence tools sold to millions of small businesses. Recurring customer relationships, scale in domain distribution, and high cash conversion support an above-average quality profile. Full-year revenue is guided to about $5.24 billion at the midpoint, up roughly 6%, with a normalized EBITDA margin above 33%.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Growth is modest, Core revenue is expected to rise only in the low single digits, and AI-native website tools or bundled commerce platforms could erode the role of domains and hosting as the customer's starting point. Net debt was $2.7 billion at June 30, so buybacks compete with deleveraging. The thesis weakens if FCF misses $1.8 billion, customer retention deteriorates, or repurchases fail to raise per-share value.

**Evidence:** [GoDaddy Q2 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1609711/000160971126000087/gddyex991-20260630xq2earni.htm)

### 3. Leidos (LDOS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Leidos trades near 12.2x trailing earnings. Raised FY2026 operating-cash-flow guidance of about $1.85 billion equals roughly 11.3% of current equity value before capital spending; a separate market-wide cash-yield screen estimated a 12.9% trailing FCF yield as of September 6.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 7% to $4.56 billion, including 4% organic growth, while quarterly FCF was $761 million. The company booked $5.0 billion of awards and ended the quarter with $48.7 billion of backlog, including $10.2 billion funded. Long-lived defense, intelligence, health, cyber, and infrastructure programs create switching costs and revenue visibility.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Government procurement and budget changes can delay awards, and Q2 adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 13.8% from 15.2%, partly because the prior period benefited from one-time items. The $2.4 billion Entrust acquisition increases integration and capital-allocation risk. The thesis weakens if book-to-bill remains below one, funded backlog contracts, or cash conversion normalizes sharply below guidance.

**Evidence:** [Leidos Q2 2026 results](https://investors.leidos.com/news-releases/news-release-details/leidos-delivers-strong-second-quarter-and-enhances-full-year)

### 4. Bristol Myers Squibb (BMY)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $62.84, the shares trade at roughly 9.0x-9.3x raised 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $6.75-$7.00. The market is assigning a low multiple because of the patent cycle, but current operating momentum is better than the headline discount implies.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 6% to $13.0 billion, the Growth Portfolio increased 15% to $7.6 billion, and management raised full-year revenue guidance to $49-$50 billion. Products such as Reblozyl, Breyanzi, Opdualag, Camzyos, and Cobenfy provide multiple growth vectors, while a 71% GAAP gross margin and global commercial scale support continued R&D investment.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** This is a transition story, not a clean compounder. Revlimid and Pomalyst/Imnovid Q2 revenue fell 49% and 71%, respectively, and Eliquis exclusivity remains a major medium-term issue. Acquisitions have increased leverage, pipeline outcomes are uncertain, and adjusted EPS excludes meaningful items. The thesis weakens if Growth Portfolio gains cannot offset legacy erosion, debt reduction stalls, or pivotal launches disappoint.

**Evidence:** [Bristol Myers Squibb Q2 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/14272/000001427226000018/a2026q2ex991-filing.htm)

### 5. Exelixis (EXEL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Exelixis trades near 18.3x trailing earnings despite double-digit revenue growth, high current profitability, and continued share retirement. Q2 GAAP diluted EPS rose 26% to $0.82, faster than revenue.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue increased 10.6% to $629 million, with US cabozantinib product revenue up 10% to $573 million, primarily on higher volume. The company funds a broad zanzalintinib program from internal cash generation. Since March 2023, it has repurchased $2.9 billion of stock and retired 93.3 million shares, while authorizing another $750 million program through 2027.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** CABOMETYX still supplies almost all product revenue. Patent litigation, reimbursement, competitive therapies, or disappointing zanzalintinib clinical and regulatory outcomes could change intrinsic value quickly. A December 3 FDA decision adds binary risk. The thesis weakens if cabozantinib volume stalls before a second franchise is de-risked or if repurchases crowd out productive pipeline investment.

**Evidence:** [Exelixis Q2 2026 results](https://ir.exelixis.com/node/27651)

## Watchlist and rejected screen leaders

- **Salesforce (CRM), Qualcomm (QCOM), and HP Inc. (HPQ):** All were selected on September 17. They remain credible, but today's fresh ranking found a wider cash-yield or valuation margin in GoDaddy, Leidos, and Bristol Myers. No thesis break was identified.
- **Adobe (ADBE), Deckers (DECK), and Jazz Pharmaceuticals (JAZZ):** Prior-week finalists remain on the watchlist. Adobe and Deckers look optically inexpensive, while Jazz retains attractive growth; AI disruption, brand concentration, and leverage/product concentration, respectively, keep them below today's five.
- **SkyWest (SKYW):** A 9.5x trailing P/E and strong screen-level cash yield are attractive, but $2.3 billion of debt plus $2.2 billion of aircraft and engine commitments make the cash yield less owner-like than it first appears.
- **Alphabet (GOOGL) and Meta Platforms (META):** Both have outstanding franchises and rapid revenue growth, but current headline P/E and FCF measures are distorted by unrealized investment gains and a very large AI capex cycle. Alphabet's Q2 capex exceeded operating cash flow; Meta's Q2 FCF fell to $784 million. They do not clear today's cash-evidence hurdle despite plausible long-term value.
- **Teradata, cyclical transport, shipping, refining, metals, and tiny biotechnology leaders:** Several dominate raw value screens. Declining revenue, asset intensity, commodity exposure, or binary product risk make normalized owner earnings too uncertain for the top five.

## Conclusion

**Accenture is today's strongest quality-value candidate.** Its double-digit-adjacent guided FCF yield, modest earnings multiple, cash-rich balance sheet, and embedded enterprise relationships provide the clearest margin of safety. **GoDaddy** offers the highest guided FCF yield among today's finalists, but slower growth and leverage reduce confidence. **Leidos** adds backlog-backed visibility at a low multiple. **Bristol Myers** is the cheapest on adjusted earnings but carries the hardest patent-cycle transition. **Exelixis** offers strong current economics with the most concentrated product and regulatory risk.

The key disconfirmation tests are Accenture's bookings and FY2026 FCF, GoDaddy's customer retention and $1.8 billion FCF target, Leidos's funded backlog and cash conversion, Bristol Myers's Growth Portfolio offset versus legacy erosion, and Exelixis's December FDA decision plus cabozantinib volume.
