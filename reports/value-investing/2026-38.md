# Quality-Value Weekly Report - 2026-W38

_Last updated: 2026-09-15 (America/Detroit). Research support only; not personal financial advice._

## Market context

The US market is not uniformly cheap. Morningstar estimated its covered US universe was 7.5% below aggregate fair value on September 8, while a separate September screen found only 12% of US stocks in the top global quality-value-momentum band. Dispersion matters more than the index-level label: software, select branded consumer companies, and a few cash-generative healthcare names offer better quality-adjusted value than the statistically cheapest shipping, commodity, and highly leveraged businesses.

Today's ranking was completed from a fresh assessment before reviewing the September 14 entry. The broad pass used current US-listed screens covering 5,000-plus companies, then validated finalists against recent SEC filings and company releases. The ranking rewards earnings and free-cash-flow yield, durable margins or market positions, balance-sheet resilience, and credible medium-term catalysts. It penalizes excessive leverage, cyclically inflated profits, customer or product concentration, and structural decline.

Prices and market capitalizations are September 15, 2026 snapshots from the scheduled run. Ratios are approximate and move with price.

### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The shares trade at about 15.2x trailing GAAP earnings, a low multiple for a subscription software franchise still growing revenue at a double-digit rate. Q3 operating cash flow was $2.52 billion and capital spending was only $85 million, implying about $2.44 billion of quarterly free cash flow before acquisition and investment items. Adobe also repurchased 9.5 million shares in the quarter.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** FY2026 Q3 revenue rose 13% to a record $6.76 billion, subscription revenue rose 14%, and GAAP operating income reached $2.35 billion. Annualized recurring revenue exited the quarter at $27.50 billion and remaining performance obligations were $22.16 billion. These figures support high switching costs, strong cash conversion, and pricing power despite the AI disruption narrative.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Generative AI and lower-cost creative tools could weaken Adobe's professional standard, pricing power, or conversion funnel. The freemium strategy delays monetization, Q4 guidance was not strong enough to remove that concern, and leadership transition adds execution risk. The thesis weakens if subscription growth falls to sustained single digits, AI products fail to lift paid conversion, or stock-based compensation offsets repurchases.

**Evidence:** [Adobe FY2026 Q3 SEC-filed results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000147/adbeex991q326.htm)

### 2. Deckers Outdoor (DECK)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $80.54, Deckers trades at about 11.0x trailing earnings and close to 10.9x the midpoint of management's FY2027 diluted EPS guidance of $7.30-$7.45. The June balance sheet held $1.60 billion in cash, about 14% of market value, with no meaningful funded debt. First-quarter repurchases were executed above today's price, showing that the current quote is below management's recent capital-allocation reference point.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal Q1 revenue rose 5.7% to $1.02 billion; HOKA grew 7.7%, UGG grew 4.9%, direct-to-consumer revenue rose 13.0%, and international revenue rose 8.4%. Gross margin improved 60 basis points to 56.4%. Fiscal 2026 HOKA revenue had grown 15.9% to nearly $2.6 billion, while disciplined full-price selling kept annual operating margin above 23%.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Q1 operating income fell 6% as SG&A rose 12.7%, and unit volume slipped 1.4%. HOKA and UGG create brand concentration; footwear demand is discretionary and fashion-sensitive. Tariffs, promotional pressure, or a sustained loss of HOKA momentum could compress both earnings and the terminal multiple.

**Evidence:** [Deckers June 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910521/000091052126000022/deck-20260630.htm)

### 3. Exelixis (EXEL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The stock trades at roughly 17.6x trailing earnings, below the broad market's recent forward multiple despite current product growth and a balance sheet with $1.38 billion of cash and marketable securities. First-half operating cash flow was $560 million, up 115% year over year. Exelixis used $753 million for repurchases in the first half, reducing shares outstanding from 262.5 million at year-end to 248.9 million in June.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 total revenue was $628.7 million and GAAP diluted EPS was $0.82. US cabozantinib franchise product revenue rose to $573.0 million; CABOMETYX volume increased 6% and revenue increased 10% year over year. The established oncology commercial platform, high incremental margins, net-cash balance sheet, and zanzalintinib pipeline provide a plausible bridge beyond the current franchise.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** This is still a concentrated biotechnology business: CABOMETYX supplies nearly all product revenue, and patent, reimbursement, safety, or competitive setbacks could be material. Zanzalintinib regulatory and clinical outcomes are uncertain. Repurchases do not replace pipeline success, and the thesis weakens sharply if cabozantinib growth stalls before a second franchise is de-risked.

**Evidence:** [Exelixis Q2 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/939767/000093976726000096/exel20260630exhibit991.htm), [Exelixis June 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/939767/000093976726000098/exel-20260703.htm)

### 4. DaVita (DVA)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $189.32, DaVita trades at about 12.5x-13.4x management's 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $14.10-$15.20. Guided free cash flow of $1.0-$1.25 billion implies an equity FCF yield of roughly 8%-10% against the current $12.5 billion market value. The valuation is supported by an essential service rather than a short-cycle product.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** DaVita treated about 298,500 patients at 3,266 outpatient dialysis centers as of June, including 2,671 US centers. Scale, local density, clinical infrastructure, and payer relationships create meaningful operating barriers. Q2 adjusted EPS was $4.02, and management maintained $2.15-$2.25 billion of adjusted operating-income guidance.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** The balance sheet is the major reservation: long-term debt was $10.66 billion plus $117 million current, and equity was negative at June 30. Reimbursement policy, labor cost, treatment volumes, payer mix, integrated-care risk, and interest expense can overwhelm the cheap multiple. DaVita ranks below the net-cash ideas because buybacks have increased financial risk.

**Evidence:** [DaVita Q2 2026 results and guidance](https://newsroom.davita.com/2026-08-04-davita-inc-2nd-quarter-2026-results/), [DaVita June 2026 Form 10-Q](https://investors.davita.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2026/08/Form-10-Q-Q2-2026.pdf)

### 5. HP (HPQ)

**Why it looks undervalued.** HP trades at about 13.0x trailing GAAP earnings. Management raised FY2026 free-cash-flow guidance to $3.0-$3.2 billion, implying an equity FCF yield of roughly 9.5%-10.2%. Q3 free cash flow was $1.57 billion, and the company returned $574 million through dividends and repurchases.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q3 revenue rose 12.5% to $15.7 billion, operating cash flow reached $1.74 billion, and non-GAAP EPS rose 11%. Commercial Personal Systems revenue rose 22%, and HP's distribution, installed base, brand, supply-chain scale, and profitable supplies business still produce substantial recurring cash.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** This is the weakest quality profile in the top five. Q3 non-GAAP operating margin fell 60 basis points, Printing revenue declined 2%, supplies fell 3%, and PC units fell despite revenue growth. Tariff refunds boosted reported EPS, leadership is transitional, and printing remains structurally challenged. HP stays fifth until revenue growth converts into stable unit demand and margins without one-time benefits.

**Evidence:** [HP FY2026 Q3 results and outlook](https://investor.hp.com/news-events/news/news-details/2026/HP-Inc--Reports-Fiscal-2026-Third-Quarter-Results/default.aspx)

## Watchlist and rejected screen leaders

- **Paycom (PAYC):** A high-quality recurring-revenue payroll platform: Q2 revenue rose 9.8%, recurring revenue was 95% of sales, and GAAP net margin was 20%. At 23.7x trailing earnings after aggressive debt-assisted repurchases, the margin of safety is thinner than the current top five.
- **Harmony Biosciences (HRMY):** Screens exceptionally well at 13.5x earnings, and preliminary Q2 WAKIX revenue grew 30%. WAKIX concentration and pipeline/regulatory risk keep confidence below Exelixis.
- **Cricut (CRCT):** Net cash, a 13.5x P/E, 74.5% Q2 gross margin, and recurring subscription revenue are attractive. Total Q2 revenue fell 9% and product revenue fell 22%, while tariff refunds flattered gross profit.
- **Alphabet (GOOGL):** Business quality is exceptional, but the reported 17.5x P/E is distorted by a $99 billion unrealized equity-security gain. Normalized operating earnings imply a much less obvious discount, while AI infrastructure capital intensity has risen sharply.
- **Shipping, refining, and commodity screen leaders:** Several names show 4x-9x earnings and strong recent returns on capital, but peak-cycle profits, asset intensity, and commodity exposure make them weaker quality-value candidates than the selected group.

## Changes from the September 14 update

Today's selection was made before reading the prior entry.

- **Still selected:** Deckers remains a top-two idea; its valuation, net-cash balance sheet, and brand economics continue to offer the cleanest downside support.
- **Added:** Adobe, Exelixis, DaVita, and HP enter today's top five after the fresh screen. Adobe's September 10 results provide the strongest new fundamental evidence: 13% revenue growth and record quarterly operating cash flow at roughly 15x trailing earnings.
- **Moved out of the current top five:** GoDaddy, Accenture, Stride, and Leidos. This is a relative-ranking change, not a claim that their theses broke. GoDaddy and Accenture remain attractive cash generators; Stride and Leidos carry more policy, concentration, leverage, or execution risk than today's leaders.
- **Largest caution change:** DaVita's cheapness is explicitly offset by $10.8 billion of current and long-term debt. It is a medium-confidence idea, not a balance-sheet-quality compounder.

## Conclusion

**Adobe is the strongest current quality-value candidate**, pairing double-digit subscription growth, very high cash conversion, and a roughly 15x earnings multiple. **Deckers is a close second** and has the best balance sheet. **Exelixis** offers strong cash generation and a cleaner balance sheet but higher product concentration. **DaVita and HP** provide the largest explicit cash-yield support, with leverage and structural-industry risks that justify lower confidence.

The key disconfirmation tests are Adobe paid AI conversion and subscription growth, Deckers operating-margin and HOKA durability, Exelixis pipeline progress before CABOMETYX concentration becomes acute, DaVita leverage and reimbursement, and HP unit demand plus printing decline.
