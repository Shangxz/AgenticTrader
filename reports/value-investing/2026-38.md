# Quality-Value Weekly Report - 2026-W38

_Last updated: 2026-09-14 (America/Detroit). Research support only; not personal financial advice._

## Market context

A fresh screen of the US-listed equity universe produced relatively few businesses combining strong operating quality with a meaningful valuation cushion. Morningstar estimated the US market was 7.5% undervalued on September 8, while its Q3 work identified small-value as the cheapest style segment. In a separate filing-based universe, 1,283 of 3,805 model-valued US listings traded below median modeled fair value on September 11; only 54 of 5,867 covered listings scored at least 9.5/10 for quality. This argues for selectivity rather than treating low multiples alone as opportunity.

Today's ranking was completed independently of prior report content. The broad pass combined a 5,867-name quality/fair-value universe with an SEC-derived operating-company screen requiring an 8%+ free-cash-flow yield, a quality score of at least 65/100, sub-18x earnings, and no five-year share dilution. Banks and insurers were assessed separately in principle because industrial free cash flow is not a useful comparison metric for those businesses. Finalists were then checked against recent company disclosures and current quotes.

Price and market-cap snapshots below are from September 14, 2026 quotes available around the scheduled run. Ratios are approximate and can change with price.

### 1. Deckers Outdoor (DECK)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $81.27, the shares trade at roughly 10.9x the midpoint of management's FY2027 diluted EPS guidance of $7.35-$7.50. That is a modest multiple for two premium global brands that are still growing. The company also repurchased $338 million of stock in the first quarter at an average $103.79 per share, materially above today's quote.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal Q1 sales rose 5.7%; HOKA grew 7.7%, UGG grew 4.9%, direct-to-consumer sales rose 13.0%, and international sales rose 8.4%. Gross margin improved 60 basis points to 56.4%. The June balance sheet held $1.60 billion of cash and no borrowings, giving the company unusual resilience and repurchase capacity.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Quarterly operating income fell 6% as SG&A rose 12.7%. HOKA and UGG create brand concentration, footwear demand is discretionary, and tariffs or fashion shifts could impair both margins and the terminal multiple. A sustained decline in brand growth or gross margin would weaken the thesis.

**Evidence:** [Q1 FY2027 results and balance sheet](https://ir.deckers.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000910521-26-000015/deckex991pressrelease-6302.htm)

### 2. GoDaddy (GDDY)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At a $12.85 billion equity value, management's approximately $1.8 billion FY2026 free-cash-flow target implies a roughly 14% equity FCF yield, or about 7.1x FCF. The trailing P/E is about 14.6x. GoDaddy had repurchased $852 million through July 29, reducing fully diluted shares by 7% since year-end.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 7%, operating income rose 29%, operating margin expanded 450 basis points to 26.4%, and FCF rose 13% to $444 million. Applications and Commerce grew 11%, while 20.5 million customers and $4.42 billion of annualized recurring revenue support recurring economics and switching friction.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Net debt was $2.7 billion at June 30, so buybacks are not backed by a net-cash balance sheet. Core Platform growth was only 4%, customer count was nearly flat, and competition from bundled website, commerce, and AI tools could weaken the domain-centered funnel. The thesis fails if cash growth is mainly cost reduction while customer economics erode.

**Evidence:** [Q2 2026 earnings release](https://investors.godaddy.net/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/GDDY-Q2-2026-Earnings-Release.pdf)

### 3. Accenture (ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The $183.90 quote is about 14.7x trailing EPS. Against a $113.2 billion market value, management's unchanged FY2026 FCF outlook of $10.8-$11.5 billion implies a roughly 9.5%-10.2% FCF yield. That is attractive for a diversified, asset-light services leader with a long client roster.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q3 revenue rose 6% in US dollars, operating margin expanded 20 basis points to 17.0%, EPS rose 9%, and quarterly FCF reached $3.6 billion. Managed Services grew 8%, providing a steadier complement to consulting, and the company serves about 9,000 clients across industries and geographies.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Local-currency growth was only 3%, bookings fell 3% in local currency, and US federal work reduced the full-year growth outlook by about one percentage point. AI may create large transformation demand but can also compress labor-heavy delivery economics. Continued booking contraction or pricing pressure would invalidate the apparent bargain.

**Evidence:** [Q3 FY2026 earnings release](https://newsroom.accenture.com/content/3qfy26-earnings/accenture-reports-third-quarter-fiscal-2026-results.pdf)

### 4. Stride (LRN)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $82.00, the shares trade at about 11.5x FY2026 diluted EPS of $7.14. FY2026 operating cash flow of $433.8 million less $78.8 million of capitalized software, curriculum, and equipment spending produces an approximate $355 million FCF figure, about 9.4% of the current market value. Cash and marketable securities totaled $1.03 billion.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Full-year revenue grew 4.7%, operating income rose 25%, net income rose 18%, and Career Learning revenue grew 15%. Average enrollment increased 4.2%, including 13.9% growth in Career Learning enrollment. The mix shift toward career programs provides a concrete source of growth beyond general education.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Q4 revenue fell 2.7%, adjusted operating income fell 9.8%, General Education full-year revenue declined 2.1%, and Adult Learning revenue fell 29.6%. Public funding, school renewals, regulation, academic outcomes, and a recent CEO succession add event risk. This merits medium rather than high confidence until quarterly growth stabilizes.

**Evidence:** [FY2026 and Q4 results](https://investors.stridelearning.com/news/news-details/2026/Stride-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2026-financial-results/default.aspx)

### 5. Leidos (LDOS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The $128.86 quote is about 12.0x trailing EPS. An independent SEC-derived screen estimated a 12.9% FCF yield as of September 6. Management raised FY2026 operating cash-flow guidance to approximately $1.85 billion, while the current market value is about $16.2 billion.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 7%, including 4% organic growth, and quarterly FCF was $761 million. Bookings of $4.9 billion produced a 1.1x book-to-bill ratio; total backlog reached $48.7 billion and funded backlog grew 44% year over year. Mission-critical defense, intelligence, health, and infrastructure programs provide unusually good revenue visibility.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Q2 GAAP EPS fell 7%, adjusted EBITDA margin declined from 15.2% to 13.8%, and debt reached $6.0 billion against $748 million of cash after acquisitions. Government budgets, contract protests, fixed-price execution, integration, and security-clearance labor constraints matter. Leverage and recent margin pressure keep this fifth despite the low multiple.

**Evidence:** [Q2 2026 results and guidance](https://investors.leidos.com/news-releases/news-release-details/leidos-delivers-strong-second-quarter-and-enhances-full-year)

## Near misses and watchlist

- **Paylocity (PCTY):** Excellent recurring growth, a 24.2% FY2026 FCF margin, low debt, and active repurchases. At roughly 30x trailing earnings and about a 5.6% FCF yield, however, the current margin of safety is thinner than today's top five.
- **SkyWest (SKYW):** Screens at about 9.6x earnings with strong recent growth and cash generation, but aircraft capital intensity, customer concentration, financing, and pilot availability lower the quality-adjusted rank.
- **Tenet Healthcare (THC):** Roughly 10.2x earnings and strong reported FCF, with an attractive shift toward ambulatory surgery. Leverage, reimbursement exposure, and a cash-growth comparison flattered by a depressed base keep it outside the shortlist.

## Conclusion

**DECK is today's strongest overall quality-value candidate:** the combination of a roughly 11x forward earnings multiple, net cash, premium brands, positive growth, and aggressive repurchases offers the cleanest margin of safety. **GDDY and ACN** follow for their double-digit or near-double-digit FCF yields and durable recurring/client relationships. **LRN and LDOS** offer lower multiples but require more tolerance for policy, concentration, leverage, or execution risk.

These are research candidates, not guaranteed outcomes. The key monitoring tests are DECK brand and operating-margin durability, GDDY customer and Core Platform growth, ACN bookings, LRN enrollment and public-funding trends, and LDOS leverage plus EBITDA margin.
