# Quality-Value Weekly Report - 2026-W38

_Last updated: 2026-09-17 (America/Detroit). Research support only; not personal financial advice._

## Market context

The hurdle rate for equities rose this week. On September 16 the Federal Reserve raised its target range to 3.75%-4.00%, while the 10-year Treasury yield was recently near 4.7%-5.0%. That makes balance-sheet resilience and a genuine cash yield more important than a merely low P/E. At the same time, Morningstar estimated the US market was about 9% below its aggregate fair-value estimate at August 31, but emphasized that the discount was concentrated rather than market-wide.

Today's selection was completed from a fresh market evaluation before this file's September 14-16 entries were read. The primary market-wide screen scored 5,039 US-listed stocks and retained 3,959 above a $250 million market-cap floor; a separate positive-FCF value screen contained 918 companies above $300 million. I then emphasized cash yield, operating durability, returns on capital, balance-sheet capacity, and current business momentum, while penalizing single-product dependence, peak-cycle earnings, leverage, and structural decline. Small and micro-cap names were considered in the broad pass but did not survive the investability and evidence review.

Prices and market capitalizations below are intraday snapshots from September 17. Ratios are approximate and move with price. Guided cash-flow yields use company guidance, not analyst consensus.

### 1. Accenture (ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At about 15.1x trailing GAAP earnings, Accenture is priced more like a slow-growth consultant than a capital-light, globally scaled technology-services franchise. Management's FY2026 free-cash-flow outlook of $10.8-$11.5 billion equates to roughly 9.3%-9.9% of today's equity value. The guided FCF yield is unusually high for a business with a broad enterprise client base and modest capital requirements.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal Q3 revenue rose 6% in US dollars and 3% in local currency to $18.7 billion, operating margin expanded 20 basis points to 17.0%, diluted EPS rose 9%, and quarterly FCF was $3.6 billion. Accenture had $10.2 billion of cash at May 31 and had 104 client bookings of at least $100 million year to date, supporting the view that its scale, delivery network, and embedded client relationships remain durable.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** FY2026 local-currency revenue growth guidance is only 3%-4%, new bookings dipped year over year in Q3, and US federal work is a stated drag. AI could improve Accenture's productivity but also compress labor-based pricing. The thesis weakens if bookings contract for several quarters, FCF guidance falls materially, or AI-driven efficiencies accrue mainly to clients rather than shareholders.

**Evidence:** [Accenture Q3 FY2026 results](https://investor.accenture.com/~/media/Files/A/accenture-v4/investors/earnings-reports/2026/accenture-3q-fy26-earnings-release.pdf)

### 2. Salesforce (CRM)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Salesforce's FY2026 FCF was $14.4 billion, about 7.0% of today's market capitalization. Management still expects FY2027 FCF growth of roughly 4%-5%, despite recent acquisitions and currency pressure. The 22.9x trailing GAAP earnings multiple is not deep value, but it is reasonable for a subscription platform with double-digit backlog growth and expanding GAAP profitability.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 FY2027 revenue rose 11% to $11.3 billion, current remaining performance obligation grew 14% in constant currency, GAAP operating margin was 20.5%, and quarterly FCF rose 81% to $1.1 billion. Subscription economics, high switching costs, a large installed base, and $66.3 billion of remaining performance obligations support durable cash generation.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Informatica and pending acquisitions contribute to reported growth and increase integration and capital-allocation risk. Stock-based compensation means headline FCF overstates cash available to a long-term owner unless repurchases at least offset dilution. AI-native competitors could weaken pricing or seat growth. The thesis weakens if organic cRPO growth drops to sustained single digits, GAAP margins reverse, or acquisitions absorb cash without raising per-share value.

**Evidence:** [Salesforce Q2 FY2027 results](https://investor.salesforce.com/news/news-details/2026/Salesforce-Delivers-Record-Second-Quarter-Fiscal-2027-Results/default.aspx), [Salesforce FY2026 results](https://investor.salesforce.com/news/news-details/2026/Salesforce-Delivers-Record-Fourth-Quarter-Fiscal-2026-Results/)

### 3. Qualcomm (QCOM)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Qualcomm trades at about 21.1x trailing earnings, a moderate multiple for a licensing franchise plus a semiconductor platform with a growing non-handset mix. For the first nine months of FY2026, operating cash flow was $8.4 billion and capital spending was $1.6 billion, implying about $6.8 billion of FCF before acquisitions. The valuation is less optically cheap than Accenture or HP, so the case depends on sustained diversification rather than multiple expansion alone.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal Q3 revenue was $9.9 billion. Combined automotive and IoT revenue grew 28% year over year, and automotive posted its 23rd consecutive quarter of double-digit growth. Qualcomm's patent portfolio, high-margin licensing stream, systems expertise, and expanding presence in autos, edge AI, and data-center technology provide competitive advantages beyond smartphone chips.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Cash, equivalents, and marketable securities of $8.3 billion were below $15.3 billion of debt at June 28 after acquisitions, while inventory rose to $8.4 billion. Handset exposure, customer concentration, licensing disputes, China risk, and heavy investment in new categories can impair cash conversion. The thesis weakens if non-handset growth slows sharply, inventory remains elevated, or diversification requires persistently higher capex and acquisition spending.

**Evidence:** [Qualcomm Q3 FY2026 results](https://www.qualcomm.com/news/releases/2026/07/qualcomm-announces-third-quarter-fiscal-2026-results), [Qualcomm June 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/804328/000080432826000086/qcom-20260628.htm)

### 4. HP Inc. (HPQ)

**Why it looks undervalued.** HP trades at about 12.5x trailing earnings. Raised FY2026 FCF guidance of $3.0-$3.2 billion represents roughly 9.9%-10.6% of current equity value, leaving room for a satisfactory return even without a large terminal multiple. Q3 alone produced $1.6 billion of FCF and the company returned $0.6 billion through repurchases and dividends.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q3 revenue rose 12.5% to $15.7 billion, non-GAAP EPS increased 10.7%, and management raised full-year EPS and FCF guidance. HP retains global distribution, procurement scale, a large commercial installed base, and a high-margin supplies stream. Those advantages support cash generation even though the underlying categories are mature.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** This is the shortlist's clearest value-versus-quality tradeoff. Printing is structurally challenged, PC economics are cyclical and competitive, and $0.11 of Q3 EPS came from tariff refunds. Inventory was $10.3 billion and margin pressure remains possible as component costs change. The thesis weakens if normalized FCF falls below $3 billion, Printing revenue deterioration accelerates, or repurchases are funded by a weaker balance sheet rather than durable cash.

**Evidence:** [HP Q3 FY2026 results](https://www.hp.com/us-en/newsroom/press-releases/2026/hp-inc-reports-fiscal-2026-third-quarter-results.html)

### 5. Exelixis (EXEL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Exelixis trades at about 17.8x trailing earnings despite double-digit revenue growth, high current operating profitability, and continued share retirement. Q2 operating income was $248 million on $629 million of revenue, while diluted EPS rose to $0.82 from $0.65.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue grew 10.6%, US cabozantinib net product revenue reached $573 million, and the company continues funding a broad zanzalintinib clinical program from internal cash generation. Exelixis has repurchased $2.9 billion of shares since March 2023, retiring 93.3 million shares, and authorized a further $750 million program through 2027.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** CABOMETYX supplies almost all product revenue. Patent litigation, reimbursement, competition, or disappointing zanzalintinib clinical and regulatory outcomes could change intrinsic value quickly. Updated 2026 revenue guidance was slightly below the original range, and a December 3 FDA decision adds binary risk. The thesis weakens if cabozantinib volume stalls before a second franchise is de-risked.

**Evidence:** [Exelixis Q2 2026 results](https://ir.exelixis.com/node/27651)

## Conclusion

**Accenture is today's strongest quality-value candidate.** Its roughly 9.3%-9.9% guided FCF yield, 15x earnings multiple, cash-rich balance sheet, and embedded enterprise relationships provide the clearest current margin of safety. **Salesforce** ranks second on recurring revenue and a roughly 7% FCF yield, with stock compensation and acquisition execution the main caveats. **Qualcomm** offers higher cyclicality but a credible automotive and IoT diversification path. **HP** has the highest indicated cash yield but the weakest structural growth profile. **Exelixis** offers strong current economics with the most concentrated product and regulatory risk.

The key disconfirmation tests are Accenture's bookings and FY2026 FCF, Salesforce's organic cRPO growth and dilution, Qualcomm's non-handset growth and inventory, HP's normalized FCF and Printing trajectory, and Exelixis' December FDA decision plus cabozantinib volume.
