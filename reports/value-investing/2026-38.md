# Quality-Value Weekly Report - 2026-W38

_Last updated: 2026-09-19 (America/Detroit). Research support only; not personal financial advice._

## Market context

The valuation backdrop remains selective rather than broadly cheap. As of September 16, the S&P 500 traded near 19.0x forward earnings and 22.4x trailing earnings. Morningstar estimated the US market was about 9% below its aggregate fair-value estimate at August 31, but said that discount was concentrated in seven mega-cap stocks; excluding them, its coverage was around fair value. Rising long-term rates and elevated AI capital spending raise the hurdle for long-duration equities, so today's ranking emphasizes visible cash generation, balance-sheet capacity, and business durability.

Today's selection was completed from a fresh market evaluation before the September 14-18 entries in this file were read. The broad pass combined a current 500-stock valuation/quality/earnings-quality/financial-strength ranking, current wide-moat and sector valuation screens, and a review of market-wide fair-value dispersion. I then validated finalists against September 18 closing prices and the latest company-reported results, penalizing leverage, shrinking margins, structural decline, single-product dependence, and cash flow that relies on temporary working-capital or investment gains.

Prices and market capitalizations below are September 18 closing snapshots. Ratios are approximate and move with price. Forward multiples use company guidance where stated; third-party fair-value estimates are clearly identified.

### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $248.92, Adobe trades at about 13.7x management's raised FY2026 GAAP EPS target of $18.12-$18.17 and roughly 10.2x the $24.45-$24.50 non-GAAP target. That is a low multiple for a subscription software company still producing double-digit growth.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q3 revenue increased 13% to $6.76 billion, subscription revenue rose 14%, total ARR reached $27.50 billion, GAAP operating margin was about 35%, and operating cash flow set a quarterly record at $2.52 billion. AI-first ARR grew more than 150%, evidence that AI is currently extending the platform rather than simply displacing it.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Generative tools may commoditize parts of creative production, pressure pricing, or shift value toward model providers and new workflows. CEO succession adds execution risk, and non-GAAP results exclude material stock compensation and acquisition accounting. The thesis weakens if ARR growth falls below high single digits, AI adoption fails to monetize, or cash-flow growth separates negatively from revenue.

**Evidence:** [Adobe Q3 FY2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000147/adbeex991q326.htm)

### 2. PayPal (PYPL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** PayPal trades at about 9.9x trailing earnings and around 9.7x its approximately $5.38 FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance. Q2 free cash flow was $1.78 billion, and the company repurchased $6.0 billion of shares over the trailing 12 months, large relative to the current $46.2 billion equity value.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** The platform processed $486.4 billion of Q2 payment volume, up 10%, across 439 million active accounts. Revenue grew 5%, transaction margin dollars excluding interest on customer balances rose 3%, and the balance sheet held $15.3 billion of cash and investments against $13.4 billion of debt. Scale, merchant acceptance, consumer recognition, and risk-management data remain valuable network assets.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Q2 GAAP operating income fell 5%, non-GAAP margin contracted 248 basis points, active accounts were nearly flat, and FY2026 GAAP EPS is still expected to decline mid-single digits. Branded checkout faces wallets, card networks, banks, and platform-native payment options. The thesis weakens if transaction-margin growth turns negative, branded checkout loses share, or buybacks merely offset business deterioration.

**Evidence:** [PayPal Q2 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633917/000163391726000080/pypl2q-26earningsrelease.htm)

### 3. UnitedHealth Group (UNH)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The shares trade at roughly 18.8x-19.3x management's raised 2026 adjusted EPS range of $19.50-$20.00. That is not deep value, but it is reasonable for a scaled insurer and health-services platform showing a recovery in cash generation and guidance.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue was $112.0 billion, operating earnings were $8.0 billion, and operating cash flow was $11.1 billion, or 1.9x net income. The combination of UnitedHealthcare and Optum creates data, distribution, purchasing, and care-delivery advantages that smaller competitors cannot easily replicate. Debt-to-capital improved to 41.2%.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Medical-cost estimation, Medicare pricing, risk adjustment, DOJ actions, pharmacy-benefit regulation, cybersecurity, and reputational issues can all impair economics. The margin of safety is narrower than for Adobe or PayPal. The thesis weakens if medical costs again outrun pricing, cash conversion falls materially below earnings, or legal and regulatory remedies constrain vertical integration.

**Evidence:** [UnitedHealth Q2 2026 results](https://www.unitedhealthgroup.com/newsroom/2026/2026-07-16-uhg-reports-second-quarter-2026-results.html)

### 4. Nike (NKE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $35.51, Nike trades at about 16.9x FY2026 diluted EPS of $2.10, while cash and short-term investments were $9.0 billion. A June Morningstar wide-moat screen placed Nike at the deepest discount in that index, though that fair-value signal should be treated as an external estimate rather than fact.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Nike retains exceptional global brand awareness, athlete relationships, product-development scale, and wholesale distribution. FY2026 revenue held at $46.4 billion, wholesale revenue rose 6%, gross margin improved 20 basis points to 42.9%, and net income remained $3.1 billion despite a difficult product reset.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Currency-neutral revenue fell 2%, Nike Direct fell 8%, digital fell 12%, Converse fell 32%, and inventories were flat despite weak demand. Q4 margin and EPS benefited heavily from tariff recoveries. The thesis weakens if product innovation fails to restore full-price demand, China and direct sales continue shrinking, or inventory requires heavier discounting.

**Evidence:** [Nike FY2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000076/q4fy26exhibit991er.htm)

### 5. Alphabet (GOOGL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Morningstar estimated Alphabet at a 23% discount to fair value on August 31. The reported 17.6x trailing P/E is misleadingly low because Q2 included a $98.0 billion unrealized securities gain, so today's ranking relies more on operating economics and the external fair-value signal than on headline EPS.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 24% to $119.8 billion, operating income increased 30% to $40.8 billion, operating margin reached 34%, Search revenue grew 17%, and Cloud revenue grew 82%. Alphabet held $242.5 billion of cash and marketable securities at June 30. Search distribution, user intent data, YouTube scale, and an integrated AI/cloud stack remain formidable competitive assets.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Q2 capital expenditures exceeded operating cash flow, creating negative quarterly FCF; reported earnings were dominated by unrealized investment gains; and long-term debt doubled from year-end. AI may alter search economics, while antitrust remedies could weaken distribution advantages. The thesis weakens if core operating profit fails to outgrow AI depreciation and infrastructure costs or if Search monetization erodes.

**Evidence:** [Alphabet Q2 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000066/googexhibit991q22026.htm)

## Watchlist and rejected screen leaders

- **Accenture (ACN):** Friday's top idea remains attractive on guided cash yield and enterprise relationships. It narrowly missed today's list because bookings softness and AI-related pricing risk reduce near-term growth visibility; no thesis break was identified.
- **Autodesk (ADSK):** Q2 revenue grew 16%, operating margin reached 29%, and FCF rose 24% to $561 million. At about 28x trailing earnings, however, the valuation discount is less clear than Adobe's after accounting for MaintainX acquisition costs and integration risk.
- **GoDaddy (GDDY), Leidos (LDOS), Bristol Myers (BMY), and Exelixis (EXEL):** All remain credible prior selections. Today's independent screen favored higher recurring-revenue quality, stronger balance sheets, or a larger mismatch between franchise durability and price.
- **Meta (META) and Microsoft (MSFT):** Both are high-quality and externally assessed as undervalued, but at about 25x and 29x reported earnings, respectively, their margins of safety depend heavily on sustained AI growth and returns on elevated capital spending.
- **CVS, Disney, commodity producers, airlines, and small-cap statistical bargains:** Low multiples alone were insufficient where leverage, regulation, cyclicality, secular pressure, or binary outcomes made normalized owner earnings difficult to underwrite.

## Changes from earlier this week

Today's ranking was completed independently before earlier entries were used for comparison.

- **Adobe returns at No. 1.** It appeared on September 15-16 but not September 18. The latest reported evidence remains unusually strong relative to price: 13% revenue growth, record quarterly operating cash flow, and roughly 13.7x guided GAAP EPS.
- **PayPal, UnitedHealth, Nike, and Alphabet enter today's five.** PayPal offers the deepest cash-backed multiple but weaker margin momentum. UnitedHealth adds recovery evidence and diversified health-service scale. Nike is a higher-risk brand turnaround. Alphabet offers exceptional operating quality but requires confidence that AI infrastructure earns adequate returns.
- **Accenture, GoDaddy, Leidos, Bristol Myers, and Exelixis leave today's five.** They remain in the weekly record and watchlist. These are relative-ranking changes, not newly identified thesis breaks.
- **The center of gravity shifts toward software and platforms.** This reflects the current dispersion: value stocks have rerated while selected growth franchises have sold off. The shift increases AI execution and capital-spending risk, which is why reported cash conversion and disconfirmation tests are explicit.

## Conclusion

**Adobe is today's strongest quality-value candidate.** Its recurring revenue, record cash generation, double-digit growth, and roughly 13.7x guided GAAP earnings offer the cleanest combination of franchise quality and valuation. **PayPal** has the largest raw multiple and cash-return discount, but contracting margins reduce confidence. **UnitedHealth** offers a more defensive recovery at a reasonable rather than distressed valuation. **Nike** has meaningful brand and balance-sheet assets but requires a real demand turnaround. **Alphabet** has the strongest operating momentum of the group, yet its AI capex and investment-gain distortions make the margin of safety unusually dependent on future returns.

The key disconfirmation tests are Adobe's ARR and AI monetization, PayPal's transaction margins and branded-checkout share, UnitedHealth's medical-cost trend and cash conversion, Nike's full-price demand and inventory health, and Alphabet's operating profit after AI depreciation and capex.
