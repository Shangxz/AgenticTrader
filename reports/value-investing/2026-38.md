# Quality-Value Weekly Report - 2026-W38

_Last updated: 2026-09-20 (America/Detroit). Research support only; not personal financial advice._

## Market context

The US market remains selectively priced rather than broadly cheap. Morningstar estimated its US coverage at a 9% discount to fair value on August 31, but said that excluding seven mega-cap stocks the remainder was at fair value. Its value category had already rerated to fair value, while communications and technology remained the largest sector discounts. Friday's 25-basis-point Fed hike left the 10-year Treasury yield at 5.006%, raising the hurdle for leveraged and long-duration businesses.

Today's ranking was completed from a fresh market evaluation before earlier entries in this file were used for comparison. The broad pass combined a current 1,426-stock valuation screen derived from SEC filings, market-wide style and sector valuation work, and a cross-sector review of profitable US-listed companies. Finalists were checked against September 18 closing prices and recent company filings. I penalized leverage, weak cash conversion, shrinking economics, customer or product concentration, cyclicality, and earnings inflated by non-operating items.

Because September 20 is a Sunday, prices and market capitalizations use the September 18 close. Today's changes are therefore a fresh same-close reranking, not a claim that prices moved after Friday.

### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $248.92, Adobe trades at about 13.7x management's raised FY2026 GAAP EPS target of $18.12-$18.17. That is a low multiple for a subscription platform still delivering double-digit revenue growth.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q3 revenue rose 13% to $6.76 billion, subscription revenue rose 14%, ARR reached $27.50 billion, and operating cash flow set a Q3 record at $2.52 billion. GAAP operating income was $2.35 billion, and AI-first ARR grew more than 150%. The installed base, file standards, workflows, and distribution remain meaningful advantages.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Generative tools could commoditize creative work, pressure pricing, or shift value to model providers. CEO succession and rising stock compensation add risk. The thesis weakens if ARR growth drops below high single digits, AI adoption does not monetize, or cash-flow growth materially trails revenue.

**Evidence:** [Adobe Q3 FY2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000147/adbeex991q326.htm)

### 2. Deckers Outdoor (DECK)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The shares trade at about 10.7x trailing EPS, while June cash of $1.60 billion equals roughly 15% of market value. The company had no borrowings under its primary revolver, and the board's remaining repurchase authorization was $4.68 billion as of July 9. That authorization is capacity, not a forecast.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** June-quarter sales rose 5.7% to $1.02 billion; HOKA grew 7.7%, UGG grew 4.9%, direct-to-consumer revenue grew 13%, and gross margin improved 60 basis points to 56.4%. The two flagship brands have premium positioning, high full-price demand, and international growth runway.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Operating income fell 6% and operating margin contracted 190 basis points as SG&A rose 12.7%. Inventory increased sharply from fiscal year-end, and unit volume fell 1.4%. Brand concentration, fashion risk, tariffs, and wholesale normalization matter. The thesis weakens if HOKA and UGG growth stalls together, full-price selling fades, or inventory growth continues to outpace demand.

**Evidence:** [Deckers June 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910521/000091052126000022/deck-20260630.htm)

### 3. Accenture (ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $181.29, Accenture trades at about 13.5x the midpoint of FY2026 GAAP EPS guidance of $13.38-$13.50. Guided free cash flow of $10.8-$11.5 billion implies a roughly 9.7%-10.3% yield on the current equity value.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q3 revenue rose 6% in US dollars to $18.72 billion, operating margin expanded 20 basis points to 17.0%, EPS rose 9%, and free cash flow was $3.60 billion. Accenture's scale, approximately 9,000 clients, ecosystem relationships, delivery network, and process expertise make it a difficult partner to displace in large transformations.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** New bookings fell 2% in dollars and 3% in local currency, while full-year local-currency growth guidance is only 3%-4%. AI may reduce labor intensity and pricing before new work offsets it; US federal work and acquisitions add uncertainty. The thesis weakens if bookings remain below revenue, margins fall despite slower hiring, or AI work fails to replace pressured legacy services.

**Evidence:** [Accenture Q3 FY2026 results](https://newsroom.accenture.com/content/3qfy26-earnings/accenture-reports-third-quarter-fiscal-2026-results.pdf)

### 4. Arch Capital Group (ACGL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $96.89, Arch trades at about 7.6x trailing earnings and 1.42x June book value of $68.04 per share. The multiple is attractive for a specialty insurer that continues to earn mid-teens returns, although insurance earnings are cyclical and headline P/E can overstate normalized cheapness.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 net income was $1.05 billion, operating income was $893 million, annualized operating ROE was 15.3%, and the consolidated combined ratio was 83.5%. Book value per share rose 15% year over year. Specialty underwriting expertise across insurance, reinsurance, and mortgage insurance, plus disciplined capital allocation, supports durable value creation.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Net premiums earned fell 8.1%, underwriting income fell 19.7%, and the underlying combined ratio worsened 1.6 points. Catastrophe losses, reserve error, softer pricing, investment-market volatility, and mortgage credit losses can change results quickly. The thesis weakens if underlying loss ratios deteriorate persistently or book-value growth falls below the cost of equity.

**Evidence:** [Arch Capital Q2 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/947484/000094748426000118/ex-991release63026.htm)

### 5. PayPal (PYPL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** PayPal trades at about 9.9x trailing earnings and around 9.7x its approximately $5.38 FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance. Q2 free cash flow was $1.78 billion, while trailing-12-month repurchases were $6.0 billion versus a $46.2 billion equity value.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 payment volume grew 10% to $486.4 billion, payment transactions rose 8%, and revenue increased 5%. Cash and investments of $15.3 billion exceeded $13.4 billion of debt. Merchant acceptance, consumer recognition, risk data, and scale remain useful network assets.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** GAAP operating income fell 5%, GAAP margin contracted 171 basis points, and active accounts were nearly flat. Branded checkout faces wallets, card networks, banks, and platform-native options. The thesis weakens if transaction-margin dollars turn negative, branded checkout loses share, or buybacks mask declining owner earnings.

**Evidence:** [PayPal Q2 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633917/000163391726000080/pypl2q-26earningsrelease.htm)

## Watchlist and rejected screen leaders

- **Alphabet (GOOGL):** exceptional growth and balance-sheet quality, and Morningstar estimated a 23% fair-value discount at August 31. It missed today's five because Q2 earnings included a very large unrealized investment gain and AI capital spending makes normalized owner earnings harder to underwrite.
- **UnitedHealth (UNH):** scaled and cash-generative, but its roughly 19x adjusted-EPS guidance offers less valuation protection against medical-cost, legal, and regulatory risk than today's finalists.
- **Nike (NKE):** a valuable global brand with a solid liquidity position, but shrinking direct and digital sales keep the turnaround below the top five.
- **Comcast (CMCSA):** a 7.3x trailing P/E surfaced prominently in the broad screen, but broadband competition, legacy-TV decline, capital intensity, and debt make the low multiple less compelling than it first appears.
- **Coca-Cola Consolidated (COKE), Post (POST), and other statistical screen leaders:** valuation scores alone were insufficient where current multiples, leverage, liquidity, customer concentration, or cyclicality did not clear today's quality threshold.
- **Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, Nvidia, Broadcom, and Tesla:** external fair-value estimates support selected mega-cap opportunities, but outcomes depend heavily on sustained AI growth and adequate returns on very large capital commitments.

## Changes from earlier this week

Today's selection was completed independently before these comparisons were made.

- **Adobe remains No. 1.** Its combination of 13% revenue growth, record Q3 cash generation, and roughly 13.7x guided GAAP earnings remains the clearest quality-value mismatch.
- **Deckers, Accenture, and Arch Capital enter today's five.** Deckers adds balance-sheet strength and premium brands; Accenture adds a near-10% guided FCF yield after Friday's decline; Arch adds specialty underwriting quality and a mid-teens operating ROE at a low earnings multiple.
- **PayPal moves from No. 2 to No. 5.** The valuation and cash returns remain attractive, but margin contraction keeps confidence below the other finalists.
- **UnitedHealth, Nike, and Alphabet leave today's five.** This is a relative-ranking change, not a new thesis break. UnitedHealth offers less valuation protection; Nike needs stronger demand evidence; Alphabet's normalized owner earnings are unusually sensitive to AI capex and investment-accounting distortions.
- **No post-Friday price signal is implied.** The ranking changed because of a fresh quality-value weighting on the same September 18 close.

## Conclusion

**Adobe is the strongest current quality-value candidate.** It pairs recurring revenue, double-digit growth, and record cash generation with a mid-teens GAAP earnings multiple. **Deckers** offers the strongest balance sheet and brand economics, but its expense growth and inventory require watching. **Accenture** has the clearest cash-yield support after Friday's decline, though bookings are soft. **Arch Capital** is the lowest-multiple high-return business in today's five, with the important caveat that insurance earnings and reserves are cyclical. **PayPal** offers substantial cash returns, but declining margins make it the least certain thesis.

The most important disconfirmation tests are Adobe's ARR and AI monetization, Deckers' full-price demand and inventory, Accenture's bookings and AI-era pricing, Arch's underlying combined ratio and book-value growth, and PayPal's transaction-margin trajectory.
