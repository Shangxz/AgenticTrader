# Quality-Value Weekly Report - 2026-W38

_Last updated: 2026-09-16 (America/Detroit). Research support only; not personal financial advice._

## Market context

The US market's apparent discount is unusually concentrated. Morningstar estimated its covered US universe was about 9% below aggregate fair value on August 31, but also found that removing seven stocks left the rest of the market near fair value. That argues for company-level selection rather than a broad claim that US equities are cheap.

Today's ranking began with a fresh evaluation of the market, independent of earlier entries in this file. The broad pass used current screens spanning roughly 4,600-5,000 US listings, followed by primary-source review of the strongest candidates. The ranking rewards earnings and free-cash-flow yield, durable margins and market positions, manageable leverage, resilient demand, and evidence that the business is still compounding. It penalizes peak-cycle profits, single-product dependence, patent cliffs, excessive debt, and structural decline.

Prices and market capitalizations below are September 16 snapshots from the scheduled run. Ratios are approximate and move with price; guided multiples use company guidance rather than consensus estimates.

### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Adobe trades at about 14.8x trailing GAAP earnings after today's price decline. That is a modest multiple for a subscription software franchise that just produced record quarterly revenue and operating cash flow. Q3 operating cash flow was $2.52 billion and capital spending was $85 million, implying roughly $2.44 billion of quarterly free cash flow before acquisition and investment activity.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** FY2026 Q3 revenue rose 13% to $6.76 billion, subscription revenue rose 14%, and GAAP operating income reached $2.35 billion. Annualized recurring revenue exited the quarter at $27.50 billion, remaining performance obligations were $22.16 billion, and AI-first ARR grew more than 150%. The professional creative standard, integrated product suite, high switching costs, and very high cash conversion remain visible in the financial results.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Generative AI and lower-cost creative tools could weaken Adobe's professional standard, pricing power, or conversion funnel. The freemium strategy may expand usage without adequate monetization, and the CEO transition raises execution risk. The thesis weakens if subscription growth settles in sustained single digits, paid AI conversion disappoints, or stock-based compensation offsets the economic benefit of repurchases.

**Evidence:** [Adobe FY2026 Q3 SEC-filed results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000147/adbeex991q326.htm)

### 2. Deckers Outdoor (DECK)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $77.51, Deckers trades at about 10.6x trailing earnings and roughly 10.5x the midpoint of management's FY2027 diluted EPS guidance of $7.30-$7.45. The June balance sheet held $1.60 billion in cash, nearly 15% of current market value, with no meaningful funded debt. That balance sheet materially improves the downside support behind the headline multiple.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal Q1 revenue rose 5.7% to $1.02 billion; HOKA grew 7.7%, UGG grew 4.9%, direct-to-consumer revenue rose 13.0%, and international revenue rose 8.4%. Gross margin improved 60 basis points to 56.4%. In fiscal 2026, revenue rose 10% to about $5.5 billion and diluted EPS rose 11%, while disciplined full-price selling and strong brand economics supported an operating margin above 23%.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Q1 operating income fell 6% as SG&A rose 12.7%, and unit volume slipped 1.4%. HOKA and UGG create brand concentration; footwear demand is discretionary and fashion-sensitive. Tariffs, promotional pressure, or a sustained loss of HOKA momentum could compress earnings and the terminal multiple.

**Evidence:** [Deckers June 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910521/000091052126000022/deck-20260630.htm), [Deckers FY2026 annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910521/000091052126000020/deckfy26annualreport.pdf)

### 3. Jazz Pharmaceuticals (JAZZ)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Jazz trades at about 16.5x trailing GAAP earnings despite a significant acceleration in operating performance. First-half operating cash flow was $824 million; annualizing that figure would equal nearly 10% of the current equity value before capital spending. Management raised 2026 revenue guidance to $4.60-$4.75 billion. Cash and investments were $2.2 billion against $4.4 billion of debt at June 30, and the company repaid $1.0 billion of notes in June.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue reached a record $1.2 billion, up 16%. Xywav revenue rose 13% and Epidiolex revenue rose 16%, providing more than one growth engine. The FDA approved two Ziihera-containing regimens for first-line HER2-positive advanced gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma on August 25, and the Phase 3 study subsequently showed a statistically significant and clinically meaningful overall-survival benefit. High gross margins, specialty commercial infrastructure, and improving product diversification support the quality case.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Jazz remains dependent on a relatively small number of specialty medicines and carries meaningful debt. Regulatory, reimbursement, safety, or competitive setbacks could materially change the cash-flow outlook. The 2030 exchangeable notes may dilute shareholders, and pipeline spending must translate into durable revenue before existing product exclusivity erodes. The thesis weakens if Xywav or Epidiolex growth stalls, Ziihera uptake disappoints, or deleveraging reverses.

**Evidence:** [Jazz Q2 2026 results](https://investor.jazzpharma.com/news-releases/news-release-details/jazz-pharmaceuticals-announces-second-quarter-2026-financial), [Jazz June 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1232524/000123252426000045/jazz-20260630.htm), [Ziihera FDA approval and Phase 3 update](https://investor.jazzpharma.com/news-releases/news-release-details/ziiherar-zanidatamab-hrii-plus-chemotherapy-demonstrates)

### 4. Exelixis (EXEL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Exelixis trades at roughly 17.8x trailing earnings, below the broad market despite current double-digit revenue growth and a net-cash balance sheet. First-half operating cash flow was about $560 million. The company has repurchased $2.9 billion of stock since March 2023, retiring 93.3 million shares at an average $31.12, and $597.8 million remained under the current authorization at June 30.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 10.6% to $628.7 million, product revenue rose to $573.0 million from $520.0 million, and diluted EPS increased to $0.82 from $0.65. Exelixis combines an established oncology commercial platform, high incremental margins, strong cash generation, and a plausible second-franchise opportunity in zanzalintinib.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** CABOMETYX supplies nearly all product revenue, so patent, reimbursement, safety, or competitive setbacks would be material. Zanzalintinib regulatory and clinical outcomes remain uncertain. Repurchases do not substitute for successful pipeline diversification, and the thesis weakens sharply if cabozantinib growth stalls before a second franchise is de-risked.

**Evidence:** [Exelixis Q2 2026 SEC-filed results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/939767/000093976726000096/exel20260630exhibit991.htm), [Exelixis June 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/939767/000093976726000098/exel-20260703.htm)

### 5. Bristol Myers Squibb (BMY)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $63.73, Bristol Myers trades at about 14.0x trailing GAAP earnings and approximately 9.3x the midpoint of raised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $6.75-$7.00. The dividend yield was recently about 3.9%. First-half operating cash flow was $4.50 billion, while net debt fell $2.4 billion from year-end.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 6% to $13.0 billion. More importantly, the growth portfolio increased 15% to $7.6 billion, led by products including Opdivo Qvantig, Reblozyl, Camzyos, Breyanzi, and Opdualag. Scale in oncology, hematology, immunology, commercialization, and clinical development offers diversification that smaller biotechnology companies cannot match.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** This is a transition rather than a clean compounder. Legacy-product erosion and loss of exclusivity can outrun growth products, acquired pipeline assets may fail, and first-half operating cash flow fell from $5.87 billion a year earlier. Net debt was still $31.66 billion at June 30, and $830 million of first-half IPRD impairments underscores pipeline uncertainty. The thesis requires sustained double-digit growth from the new portfolio and continued deleveraging.

**Evidence:** [Bristol Myers Q2 2026 SEC-filed results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/14272/000001427226000018/a2026q2ex991-filing.htm), [Bristol Myers June 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/14272/000001427226000020/bmy-20260630.htm)

## Watchlist and rejected screen leaders

- **HP (HPQ):** About 12.9x trailing earnings and a roughly 9.5%-10.2% guided FCF yield remain attractive. Printing is structurally challenged, Q3 PC units fell despite revenue growth, and margin pressure keeps the quality score below today's top five.
- **DaVita (DVA):** An 8%-10% guided FCF yield and essential-service demand are appealing, but about $10.8 billion of current and long-term debt and negative equity weaken the balance-sheet quality case.
- **Harmony Biosciences (HRMY):** The screen is strong at roughly 14x earnings, but WAKIX concentration and pipeline/regulatory risk make the current evidence less diversified than Jazz or Bristol Myers.
- **Gilead Sciences (GILD):** Strong franchises and cash generation score well, but GAAP earnings are distorted and the current price offers a less transparent margin of safety than the selected names.
- **Cricut (CRCT):** Net cash, a roughly 14x multiple, and very high gross margins are attractive; falling product revenue and tariff-refund benefits leave business momentum too uncertain.
- **Shipping, refining, metals, and gold screen leaders:** Several names show 4x-9x earnings and high recent returns on capital. Their profits are more exposed to spot prices, freight rates, or peak-cycle conditions, so they rank below businesses with more durable pricing power and recurring demand.

## Changes from earlier this week

Today's selection was completed independently before earlier entries were used for comparison.

- **Still selected:** Adobe remains first, Deckers remains second, and Exelixis remains in the top five. Adobe and Deckers became modestly more attractive as their snapshot prices fell about 3% and 4%, respectively, while the underlying evidence was unchanged.
- **Added:** Jazz Pharmaceuticals enters at No. 3. Its 16% Q2 revenue growth, raised guidance, $1.0 billion debt repayment, August 25 Ziihera approval, and subsequent positive survival result improve both the growth runway and balance-sheet trajectory. Bristol Myers enters at No. 5 because the growth portfolio is expanding 15% while the shares trade near 9.3x guided adjusted EPS.
- **Moved out of today's top five:** DaVita and HP remain statistically cheap but have weaker quality profiles. DaVita's leverage and HP's printing decline plus margin pressure make their cash yields less dependable than the two additions.
- **No thesis break:** The removals are relative-ranking decisions, not claims that DaVita or HP became fundamentally impaired overnight.

## Conclusion

**Adobe is the strongest current quality-value candidate**, combining double-digit subscription growth, very high cash conversion, and a sub-15x trailing earnings multiple. **Deckers is a close second** and has the best balance sheet. **Jazz** offers the most meaningful positive change in today's evidence, while **Exelixis** provides strong cash generation with higher single-franchise risk. **Bristol Myers** has the lowest guided earnings multiple but also the most explicit patent-cycle and leverage burden.

The most important disconfirmation tests are Adobe's paid AI conversion and subscription growth, Deckers' HOKA momentum and operating margin, Jazz's Ziihera launch plus deleveraging, Exelixis' pipeline progress, and Bristol Myers' ability to replace legacy-product erosion with growth-portfolio cash flow.
