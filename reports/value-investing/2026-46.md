### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Adobe is one of the clearest intersections in today's screen: approximately 13.2x trailing earnings, an 11.4% screened free-cash-flow yield, and strong reported returns on equity. That valuation gives little credit for a recurring-revenue software franchise still expanding revenue and ARR at double-digit rates.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q3 revenue rose 13% to $6.76 billion, subscription revenue rose 14%, total ARR reached $27.50 billion, and quarterly operating cash flow reached a record $2.52 billion. Nine-month operating cash flow was $7.65 billion, up 11%. Adobe's installed workflows, distribution, and subscription model remain meaningful competitive advantages. [Adobe Q3 FY2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000147/adbeex991q326.htm) [Adobe Q3 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000156/adbe-20260828.htm)

**Main risks and disconfirming evidence.** Q3 GAAP net income grew only 3%, and AI-first ARR, despite rapid growth, remains small relative to total ARR. Generative AI could reduce switching costs or shift value away from Adobe's applications. ARR growth materially below 10%, sustained margin compression, or paid-seat cannibalization without offsetting AI revenue would weaken the thesis.

### 2. Deckers Brands (DECK)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $89.79, Deckers trades at about 11.4x trailing earnings. FY2027 EPS guidance of $7.80-$7.95 includes an estimated $0.52 tariff-refund benefit; stripping that out implies roughly 12.2x-12.4x normalized guidance. That is unusually inexpensive for a debt-free branded consumer company with high margins.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 revenue reached a record $1.48 billion, HOKA sales grew 6.8%, UGG grew 3.5%, and operating income rose to $389.5 million. The company held $1.21 billion of cash, had no outstanding borrowings, and repurchased $637.5 million of stock in the first half. HOKA and UGG segment operating margins were 34.9% and 41.4%, respectively, evidence of strong brand economics. [Deckers Q2 FY2027 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910521/000091052126000028/deckex991pressrelease-9302.htm) [Deckers Q2 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910521/000162828026068116/deck-20260930.htm)

**Main risks and disconfirming evidence.** Direct-to-consumer sales fell 1.8%, DTC comparable sales fell 6.4%, and reported EPS included a $0.28 tariff-refund benefit. Footwear brands can lose relevance quickly. Persistent DTC contraction, inventory growth ahead of sales, or heavier discounting would make the low multiple less trustworthy.

### 3. Progressive (PGR)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Progressive trades at about 11.7x trailing earnings despite underwriting profitability well ahead of break-even. The multiple appears modest for its pricing data, segmentation, distribution, and policy-growth advantages, though today's earnings should not be treated as fully mid-cycle.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q3 net premiums earned grew 5% to $21.9 billion, while the combined ratio improved to 88.7 from 89.5. September's combined ratio was 90.0 versus 100.4 a year earlier. The results support the inference that Progressive's underwriting discipline remains differentiated. [Progressive September and Q3 results](https://investors.progressive.com/financials/financial-news-releases/news-details/2026/Progressive-Reports-September-2026-Results/default.aspx)

**Main risks and disconfirming evidence.** Catastrophe losses, reserve development, claims inflation, and the pricing cycle can make earnings volatile. A combined ratio persistently above 96, adverse reserve development, or policy growth materially below peers would challenge the quality case.

### 4. Leidos (LDOS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Leidos trades at about 12.3x trailing earnings and 10.9x the midpoint of FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $12.50-$12.70. Year-to-date non-GAAP free cash flow of roughly $1.5 billion is substantial relative to a $16.7 billion market value, although working-capital timing and adjustments warrant caution.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q3 revenue grew 4% to $4.63 billion, non-GAAP EPS rose 19%, and quarterly free cash flow was $476 million. Backlog reached $49.6 billion, quarterly book-to-bill was 1.2, and funded backlog grew 22%. Mission-critical defense, intelligence, health, and air-traffic programs create switching costs and visibility. [Leidos Q3 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336920/000133692026000290/ldos100226q3pressreleaseex.htm) [Leidos Q3 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336920/000133692026000293/ldos-20261002.htm)

**Main risks and disconfirming evidence.** Leidos carried approximately $6.0 billion of debt against $630 million of cash, and its results depend heavily on U.S. government budgets, awards, and execution. A sustained book-to-bill below 1, fixed-price cost overruns, or weaker cash conversion would reduce the margin of safety.

### 5. PayPal (PYPL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** PayPal trades near 11.2x trailing earnings and about 10.5x FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance of roughly $5.60. The lower price increases the apparent discount, but the thesis depends on stabilization in branded checkout and transaction economics rather than the multiple alone.

**Why it still qualifies as a quality business.** Q3 payment volume rose 6% to $487.1 billion, transactions grew 7%, active accounts reached 440 million, and transaction-margin dollars increased 2%. Quarterly free cash flow was $1.43 billion, adjusted free cash flow was $2.05 billion, and cash plus investments exceeded debt. Scale, merchant acceptance, and a two-sided network still matter. [PayPal Q3 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633917/000163391726000086/pypl3q-26earningsrelease.htm) [PayPal Q3 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633917/000163391726000088/pypl-20260930.htm)

**Main risks and disconfirming evidence.** Revenue grew only 2%, GAAP operating income fell 8%, GAAP EPS fell 4%, and free cash flow declined 17%. Branded checkout faces intense competition, while lower-margin processing can expand volume without improving economics. Continued branded weakness, declining transaction margins, or buybacks that fail to lift per-share cash flow would invalidate the recovery case.

### Near misses and value-trap checks

- **Alphabet (GOOGL):** The low earnings multiple and Search/Cloud quality remain attractive, but exceptional AI capital spending leaves current free-cash-flow conversion unusually weak.
- **EOG Resources (EOG):** A low-cost asset base and sound balance sheet are attractive, but commodity prices drive a larger share of estimated value than company-specific execution.
- **Qualcomm (QCOM):** Licensing economics remain strong, but the current trailing multiple is less compelling, while handset demand, customer integration, and data-center execution add uncertainty.
- **Honeywell (HON):** The headline low multiple and high ROE screen well, but portfolio separation and one-time accounting effects make normalized earnings harder to read.
- **Micron, refiners, and shipping:** Very low multiples were discounted where current earnings or cash flow appear strongly cyclical.
- **Highly leveraged deep-value names:** Extreme FCF yields were rejected when debt, working-capital reversals, or structural erosion made the cash flow unlikely to be durable.

### Changes from the November 10 update

Today's ranking was completed independently before comparison with the earlier report.

- **Retained with unchanged ranking:** Adobe, Deckers, Progressive, Leidos, and PayPal remain the top five. The overlap of low valuation with business quality remains strongest for Adobe and Deckers.
- **Valuation changes:** Adobe and Deckers are modestly cheaper; PayPal's decline widens its apparent discount but does not improve its competitive trajectory. Progressive and Leidos are modestly higher, without enough change to alter their positions.
- **No additions or removals:** EOG and Qualcomm remain credible watchlist ideas, but commodity dependence and less-attractive current valuation, respectively, keep them below the selected group.

### Current conclusion

**Adobe, Deckers, and Progressive remain the strongest current candidates.** Adobe offers the best recurring cash-flow profile, Deckers combines a debt-free balance sheet with a normalized low-teens multiple, and Progressive pairs a modest multiple with outstanding underwriting. Leidos remains attractive but carries more leverage and government-contract exposure. PayPal has the widest turnaround risk and therefore the lowest confidence despite its apparent discount.

The most important monitoring points are Adobe's ARR and AI monetization, Deckers' DTC trend and inventory discipline, Progressive's combined ratio, Leidos' book-to-bill and cash conversion, and PayPal's branded-checkout and transaction-margin growth.
