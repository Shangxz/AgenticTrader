## Market context and method

This update starts from a fresh screen of the U.S.-listed market and does not carry forward prior picks. The initial screen covered 3,517 U.S.-listed companies above $200 million in market value using prices through November 6, then emphasized intersections among low earnings multiples, high returns on equity with low debt, and high free-cash-flow yield. Finalists were checked against the latest company releases and SEC filings. [Broad-market screen and rules](https://www.sectoralpha.in/us-stock-screener)

The setup is unusually selective: smaller-value stocks remain broadly discounted, while the recent technology selloff has created a few high-quality large-cap candidates. Higher-for-longer rate risk favors resilient balance sheets and cash generation. Reported P/E ratios are treated as signals rather than intrinsic-value estimates, especially where investment gains, tax items, or commodity prices distort earnings.

### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $239.30, Adobe trades at about 13.4 times trailing earnings. Nine-month operating cash flow of $7.65 billion implies roughly a 9x price-to-annualized-operating-cash-flow ratio before adjusting for working-capital seasonality. That is a modest price for a subscription software franchise still producing double-digit growth.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q3 revenue rose 13% to $6.76 billion, total ARR reached $27.50 billion, subscription revenue grew 14%, and operating cash flow reached a quarterly record of $2.52 billion. The recurring model, professional workflows, distribution, and one-billion-user reach provide meaningful advantages. [Adobe Q3 FY2026 release](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/01906202/au56y4ter.pdf)

**Disconfirming factors.** Generative AI may reduce switching costs or move value away from Adobe's applications. GAAP net income grew only 3% in Q3, and margin pressure would weaken the thesis. Evidence that ARR growth falls materially below 10% or that AI adoption cannibalizes paid seats without offsetting monetization would be a major negative.

### 2. Progressive (PGR)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Progressive trades at roughly 11.5 times trailing earnings despite underwriting economics that remain far better than break-even. The multiple gives limited credit to its pricing data, direct distribution, and policy-growth runway.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q3 net premiums earned grew 5% to $21.9 billion, and the combined ratio improved to 88.7 from 89.5. The September combined ratio was 90.0 versus 100.4 a year earlier. These results support the inference that Progressive's underwriting discipline and segmentation remain durable competitive advantages. [Progressive September and Q3 results](https://investors.progressive.com/financials/financial-news-releases/news-details/2026/Progressive-Reports-September-2026-Results/default.aspx)

**Disconfirming factors.** Insurance earnings are volatile, catastrophe losses can spike, and current margins may be above mid-cycle levels. Sustained premium growth below the industry, adverse reserve development, or a combined ratio persistently above 96 would challenge the quality case.

### 3. Alphabet (GOOGL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The screened trailing P/E is about 14.9, unusually low for a business with double-digit Search growth and rapidly expanding Cloud revenue. The multiple is not clean, however: investment marks affect reported earnings, while heavy AI infrastructure spending depresses free cash flow.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q3 revenue increased 25% to $127.6 billion, Search grew 12%, Cloud revenue grew 103% to $30.7 billion, and operating income increased 30% with a 31.8% margin. The company also screened at 44% ROE with debt below half of equity. [Alphabet Q3 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000080/googexhibit991q32026.htm)

**Disconfirming factors.** Q3 operating cash flow was $42.3 billion, but $51.4 billion of capex produced negative quarterly free cash flow. The thesis depends on AI spending earning attractive future returns while Search remains defensible. Continued capex growth without conversion into cash flow, or material Search monetization erosion, would make the apparent P/E discount misleading.

### 4. EOG Resources (EOG)

**Why it looks undervalued.** EOG trades near 9.5 times trailing earnings. Q3 free cash flow of $2.18 billion equaled about 2.8% of the current market value in one quarter, though commodity prices make annualizing that figure unsafe.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q3 volumes reached records, cash operating costs beat guidance, and net debt fell to $2.18 billion, only 6.2% of total capitalization. EOG returned $1.54 billion through dividends and repurchases and raised its regular dividend 8%. Its low-cost inventory and conservative financing distinguish it from more leveraged producers. [EOG Q3 2026 results](https://investors.eogresources.com/2026-11-05-EOG-Resources-Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Results-and-Raises-Regular-Dividend-8)

**Disconfirming factors.** This remains a commodity-price business, not a stable compounder. Current earnings may overstate mid-cycle value after strong realizations. A sustained oil and gas price decline, acquisition-driven leverage, or weakening well productivity would reduce the margin of safety.

### 5. Qualcomm (QCOM)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $165.41, the shares trade at about 15.7 times FY2026 non-GAAP EPS of $10.54. GAAP EPS of $12.70 includes a $5.32-per-share tax benefit and should not be used without adjustment. Fiscal 2026 dividends and repurchases totaled $11.7 billion, equivalent to about 6.6% of the current market value.

**Why it remains a quality business.** The licensing segment produced a 72% EBT margin in FY2026. Automotive revenue grew 52% for the year and 89% in Q4, partially offsetting handset revenue declines. Management targets more than 60% growth in non-handset revenue in FY2027, including a data-center ramp. [Qualcomm FY2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/804328/000080432826000127/qcom092726erex991.htm)

**Disconfirming factors.** FY2026 revenue declined 3%, non-GAAP EPS fell 12%, and Q4 handset revenue fell 26%. Customer vertical integration, China exposure, input-cost inflation, and execution risk in data center are material. The thesis weakens if automotive and data-center growth cannot offset handset and margin erosion.

## Conclusion

**Adobe and Progressive are the strongest current quality-value candidates** because their low-teens earnings multiples are supported by recurring cash generation or demonstrably strong underwriting. **Alphabet offers the largest strategic moat but requires confidence that record AI capex will convert into future cash returns.** EOG is the best cyclical value candidate because of its cost position and balance sheet, while Qualcomm is a lower-confidence recovery and diversification idea.

The most important items to monitor are Adobe's ARR and AI monetization, Progressive's combined ratio, Alphabet's free-cash-flow conversion, EOG's commodity-adjusted returns, and Qualcomm's non-handset growth.
