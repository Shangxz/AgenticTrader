# Value Investing Weekly Report - 2026-W31

_Run date: 2026-07-27_

This report is research support only, not personal financial advice. The shortlist below was built from a fresh quality-value screen of U.S.-listed public companies, emphasizing valuation, cash generation, balance-sheet/business quality, and identifiable reasons for mispricing.

## Market Context

U.S. equities remain expensive at the index level, so the better value setups are mostly in companies facing specific investor concerns rather than broad market bargains. MacroMicro showed the S&P 500 forward P/E at about 19.8 on 2026-07-24, and GuruFocus showed the S&P 500 trailing P/E at about 25.2 on the same date. Recent market commentary also points to stretched mega-cap/AI expectations, renewed attention to non-AI sectors, and pressure on companies with rising capital spending.

Today's screen favored cash-generative healthcare services, connectivity/media, autos, and select asset-light services. I avoided ranking companies highly when cheapness appears mainly tied to unresolved structural decline, overextended cyclicality, or heavy capital spending that is currently consuming free cash flow.

## Today's Top Ideas

### 1. The Cigna Group (CI)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Cigna screens as one of the clearest quality-value candidates today. StockAnalysis showed a trailing P/E near 12.3, forward P/E near 9.4, and price/free-cash-flow near 10.0. GuruFocus also showed CI price/free-cash-flow around 10.0, roughly 45% below the healthcare plans industry median cited on that page. The valuation is unusually low for a company with durable healthcare-services scale.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Cigna's Q1 2026 release reported revenue up 5% to $68.5 billion, shareholders' net income of $1.7 billion, adjusted EPS of $7.79, and a raised 2026 adjusted EPS outlook of at least $30.35. Evernorth remains a scaled pharmacy and care-services platform, while the insurance book gives the company recurring revenue and purchasing scale.

**Main risks:** Healthcare policy and pharmacy-benefit-manager scrutiny remain material. Medical-cost pressure, reimbursement changes, or customer losses could make the low multiple a justified discount. Cash flow is also seasonally weighted, so the full-year free-cash-flow case still needs confirmation later in 2026.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E about 9.4; P/FCF about 10.0; PEG about 0.9; 2026 adjusted EPS outlook at least $30.35.

### 2. Comcast (CMCSA)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Comcast is deeply out of favor, but the current valuation appears to discount a severe deterioration scenario. StockAnalysis showed a trailing P/E near 4.6, forward P/E near 6.6, and trailing free cash flow of about $20.4 billion. Comcast's Q2 2026 release reported $4.6 billion of free cash flow and $2.1 billion returned to shareholders.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Comcast still owns large-scale connectivity assets, generates substantial cash, and has a growing wireless business. In Q2, domestic wireless line net additions were 448,000, and Peacock revenue growth helped media results. The planned separation of NBCUniversal and Sky may also make the remaining connectivity and media assets easier to value.

**Main risks:** Broadband subscriber losses are the central disconfirming factor. Comcast lost 167,000 domestic residential broadband customers in Q2, and broadband revenue fell 5.5%. If fixed wireless, fiber, and satellite competition keep eroding the broadband profit pool, the low multiple may be a value trap.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E about 6.6; TTM FCF about $20.4 billion; Q2 FCF about $4.6 billion; recent share weakness tied to broadband erosion rather than near-term cash collapse.

### 3. General Motors (GM)

**Why it looks undervalued:** GM remains priced like a no-growth cyclical even after improving guidance. Barron's and MarketWatch coverage after Q2 noted that GM traded at less than six times forward earnings, while Finbox showed a latest-twelve-month free-cash-flow yield above 20%. The stock appears to offer a high current earnings yield if North American profitability proves more durable than the market assumes.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** GM has strong truck/SUV franchises, meaningful scale, and management is using excess cash for buybacks. Its Q2 release raised full-year 2026 guidance, including expected net income attributable to stockholders of $8.4 billion to $9.8 billion and automotive operating cash flow of $15.4 billion to $19.4 billion. Recent reports also highlighted continued share-count reduction.

**Main risks:** Autos are cyclical, capital-intensive, and exposed to pricing, incentives, rates, labor costs, and tariffs. GM also continues to absorb EV restructuring charges, with recent reporting pointing to large cumulative EV reset costs. A downturn in U.S. auto demand could quickly make the headline multiple less meaningful.

**Valuation signals:** Less than 6x forward earnings in recent post-earnings coverage; LTM FCF yield cited around 20.7%; 2026 automotive operating cash flow guidance of $15.4 billion to $19.4 billion.

### 4. UnitedHealth Group (UNH)

**Why it looks undervalued:** UnitedHealth is not statistically as cheap as CI or CMCSA, but it combines a discounted valuation with higher business quality. StockAnalysis showed a forward P/E around 19.8 and P/FCF around 16.2. GuruFocus showed UNH trading roughly 30% below its estimated GF Value and described it as significantly undervalued.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** UnitedHealth has unmatched scale across insurance and Optum health-services operations. Its Q2 2026 release reported $112.0 billion of revenue, $8.0 billion of operating earnings, cash flow from operations of $11.1 billion, and a raised full-year adjusted EPS outlook of $19.50 to $20.00. Cash flow was 1.9x net income in the quarter.

**Main risks:** The investment case depends on medical-cost discipline and regulatory/legal risk normalizing. The company has faced Department of Justice scrutiny over Medicare Advantage billing practices, and Medicare/Medicaid cost pressure remains a sector-wide concern. Because the multiple is higher than the deepest value names, disappointment would matter.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E about 19.8 to 21.2 depending on source; P/FCF about 16.2; Q2 operating cash flow $11.1 billion; 2026 adjusted EPS guidance $19.50 to $20.00.

### 5. Berkshire Hathaway (BRK.B)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Berkshire is only modestly undervalued, but it remains a high-quality defensive value candidate in a pricey market. StockAnalysis showed a P/B ratio near 1.47 and trailing P/E near 14.8, while GuruFocus showed BRK.B trading about 8% below its estimated GF Value on 2026-07-27.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Berkshire's insurance operations, operating subsidiaries, equity portfolio, and large Treasury/cash position give it unusual resilience. Its Q1 2026 filing showed net earnings of $10.2 billion and operating cash-flow detail consistent with a very liquid balance sheet. The company also restarted small repurchases in Q1 after several quarters without buybacks, suggesting valuation discipline remains intact.

**Main risks:** Berkshire is not a deep bargain. A large cash balance can drag returns if attractive reinvestment opportunities remain scarce, and succession under Greg Abel will remain closely watched. Insurance results and equity-market marks can also make reported earnings volatile.

**Valuation signals:** P/B about 1.47; trailing P/E about 14.8; roughly 8% below GuruFocus GF Value; strong balance-sheet optionality.

### 6. CBRE Group (CBRE)

**Why it looks undervalued:** CBRE is a lower-confidence value idea because headline valuation is not especially cheap, but it has an identifiable recovery path. StockAnalysis showed a forward P/E around 18.3, and recent third-party valuation work cited the stock as about 16% undervalued on cash flow. CBRE's own Q1 release cited nearly $1.7 billion of trailing-twelve-month free cash flow through March 31, 2026.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** CBRE is the largest global commercial real-estate services platform, with resilient outsourcing and facilities-management businesses offsetting more cyclical transaction activity. Its 2025 annual report noted record revenue above $40 billion, core EPS up 25% to $6.38, and free cash flow near $1.7 billion.

**Main risks:** Commercial real estate remains rate-sensitive, and transaction volumes can disappoint if rates stay high or property-market stress broadens. Reported ROIC can look modest, and today's valuation leaves less margin of safety than CI, CMCSA, or GM.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E about 18.3; TTM FCF cited near $1.7 billion in company materials; cash-flow undervaluation estimate around 16%; higher confidence depends on continued real-estate services recovery.

## Screened Out Or Lower-Ranked Today

- **Alphabet (GOOGL):** Still a very high-quality business, but today's value case is clouded by Q2 negative free cash flow and sharply higher AI capital spending. Recent reporting cited Q2 revenue of $119.8 billion and strong cloud growth, but also negative quarterly free cash flow and a raised 2026 capex outlook near $195 billion to $205 billion. I would revisit if capex visibility improves or the valuation compresses further.
- **PulteGroup (PHM):** A strong operator, but not clearly undervalued today. Current data showed respectable ROE/ROIC around the mid-teens, but GuruFocus characterized shares as roughly fairly valued to slightly over fair value.
- **Marathon Petroleum (MPC):** Low forward P/E, but less compelling on a quality-value basis today because GuruFocus characterized shares as materially above its GF Value despite the low multiple.

## Conclusion

The strongest current quality-value candidates are **Cigna (CI)**, **Comcast (CMCSA)**, and **General Motors (GM)**. CI has the cleanest blend of low valuation and durable business quality. CMCSA and GM offer deeper valuation discounts but require more confidence that broadband erosion and auto cyclicality, respectively, are manageable rather than structurally destructive. UNH and Berkshire are higher-quality but less statistically cheap, while CBRE is a watchlist-quality recovery idea with a narrower margin of safety.
