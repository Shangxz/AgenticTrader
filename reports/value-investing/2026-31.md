# Value Investing Weekly Report - 2026-W31

_Run dates: 2026-07-27, 2026-07-29, 2026-07-30_

This report is research support only, not personal financial advice. Each update starts from a fresh quality-value review of U.S.-listed public companies, emphasizing valuation, cash generation, balance-sheet and business quality, and identifiable reasons the market price may understate medium-term value. Prior entries from the same week are preserved only as comparison history after today's independent ranking is complete.

## Market Context

The broad U.S. market does not screen as deeply cheap. StreetStats showed the S&P 500 at 7,320.23 as of the July 29, 2026 close, with a forward P/E of 19.32 and trailing P/E of 23.22. MacroMicro's S&P 500 forward P/E series showed a latest reading of 19.52 for July 29. That makes today's strongest opportunities mostly company-specific dislocations rather than a broad market value call.

Today's fresh scan started from broad current value/quality screens, including Investing.com's July 2026 undervalued-stock list, Morningstar's July quality-value list, and low-multiple S&P 500 screens. The final ranking weighted valuation, returns on capital, cash conversion, balance-sheet risk, and current operating evidence. The top ideas remain concentrated in high-quality software/services that have sold off, healthcare services, animal health, payments, and select cash-flow-heavy defensive businesses. A new addition today is Progressive, which now screens as a high-quality insurer at a reasonable cash-flow multiple.

## Today's Top Ideas - 2026-07-30

### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Adobe remains a rare high-return software franchise trading at a value multiple. StockAnalysis, checked July 30, showed a trailing P/E of 15.07, forward P/E of 10.16, P/FCF of 10.19, EV/FCF of 10.33, PEG of 0.73, and FCF yield of 9.82%. The stock was still down about 28.7% over the prior 52 weeks despite a rally on July 29.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** The economics remain exceptional: ROIC of 60.74%, ROE of 62.95%, operating margin of 36.72%, FCF margin of 40.80%, debt/EBITDA of 0.72, and interest coverage of 34.83x. Adobe's Q2 FY2026 release reported record quarterly revenue of $6.62 billion, up 13% year over year, and operating cash flow of $2.17 billion.

**Main risks:** The central question is whether generative AI compresses Adobe's pricing power or weakens Creative Cloud's workflow moat. The stock is cheap because the market is discounting that risk, so the thesis requires monitoring subscription retention, AI monetization, and competitive pressure.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 10.16; P/FCF 10.19; EV/FCF 10.33; ROIC 60.74%; FCF margin 40.80%; 52-week price change -28.70%.

### 2. Accenture (ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Accenture still combines a severe drawdown with a strong free-cash-flow yield. StockAnalysis showed trailing P/E of 13.85, forward P/E of 12.08, P/FCF of 8.42, EV/FCF of 8.28, dividend yield of 3.77%, FCF yield of 11.87%, and a 37.49% 52-week price decline. Investing.com's July screen also ranked Accenture as the top large-cap undervalued idea by model-implied fair-value upside.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Accenture is an asset-light, scaled IT services and consulting platform with ROIC of 27.09%, ROE of 24.41%, net cash of about $1.78 billion, debt/FCF of 0.67, and interest coverage near 40x. Its fiscal Q3 2026 release said the company delivered solid revenue, strong profitability, EPS growth, robust free cash flow, and expected fiscal 2026 revenue growth of 3% to 4% in local currency, or 4% to 5% excluding the estimated federal-business drag.

**Main risks:** Consulting demand is cyclical, the U.S. federal business remains a near-term headwind, and AI could automate some service categories. The thesis depends on Accenture using AI to expand its relevance rather than seeing it erode pricing and utilization.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 12.08; P/FCF 8.42; EV/FCF 8.28; ROIC 27.09%; dividend yield 3.77%; debt/FCF 0.67.

### 3. The Cigna Group (CI)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Cigna's Q2 earnings now confirm the prior value case rather than leaving it dependent on a pending report. StockAnalysis showed trailing P/E of 12.56, P/FCF of 10.24, EV/EBITDA of 8.22, FCF yield of 9.77%, dividend yield of 2.11%, and shareholder yield of 6.93%. Using the company's newly raised 2026 adjusted EPS outlook of at least $30.45 and the July 29 close of $296.47 implies a forward adjusted earnings multiple below 10x.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Cigna reported Q2 2026 revenue up 7% to $71.7 billion, shareholders' net income of $1.7 billion, adjusted EPS of $7.78, and a raised 2026 adjusted EPS outlook. The healthcare-services platform remains scaled and diversified across Cigna Healthcare and Evernorth, with ROIC of 14.34% and debt/EBITDA of 2.50.

**Main risks:** Pharmacy-benefit-manager scrutiny, rebate-model changes, medical-cost pressure, and policy risk remain material. In Q2, total pharmacy customers decreased 4% from year-end 2025, and Cigna Healthcare's medical care ratio rose to 84.5% from 83.2% a year earlier. Those are not fatal to the thesis, but they keep confidence below the highest-quality software names.

**Valuation signals:** Implied forward adjusted P/E under 10x using guidance; P/FCF 10.24; EV/EBITDA 8.22; ROIC 14.34%; Q2 revenue +7%; 2026 adjusted EPS outlook at least $30.45.

### 4. The Progressive Corporation (PGR)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Progressive is not the statistically cheapest name, but it is one of today's better quality-value combinations. StockAnalysis showed trailing P/E of 11.04, forward P/E of 13.63, P/FCF of 7.79, EV/FCF of 8.30, and FCF yield of 12.84%. That valuation is reasonable for a leading auto insurer with strong underwriting discipline.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Progressive reported Q2 2026 net premiums written of $21.08 billion, up 5% year over year, net premiums earned of $21.57 billion, up 6%, net income of $3.31 billion, up 4%, and a combined ratio of 87.3. Policies in force rose 7% to 40.1 million. StockAnalysis showed ROIC of 28.70%, ROE of 34.94%, debt/equity of 0.24, debt/FCF of 0.51, and interest coverage of 50.76x.

**Main risks:** Insurance underwriting results can normalize quickly. The combined ratio worsened from 86.2 in Q2 2025 to 87.3 in Q2 2026, and analysts expect EPS pressure over the next three years. The value case depends on Progressive sustaining underwriting superiority as auto pricing competition increases.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 13.63; P/FCF 7.79; EV/FCF 8.30; ROIC 28.70%; Q2 combined ratio 87.3; policies in force +7%.

### 5. Zoetis (ZTS)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Zoetis still screens like a high-quality compounder priced for a more ordinary business. StockAnalysis showed trailing P/E of 12.76, forward P/E of 11.19, P/FCF of 14.60, EV/EBITDA of 10.03, dividend yield of 2.72%, and analyst price-target upside of about 45%. Investing.com's July undervalued-stock list ranked Zoetis second by fair-value upside, and Morningstar included it among July's undervalued Best Companies to Own.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Zoetis has a leading animal-health franchise with recurring demand, high margins, and strong returns. StockAnalysis showed gross margin of 71.84%, operating margin of 38.12%, FCF margin of 23.47%, ROIC of 27.80%, and interest coverage of 15.22x.

**Main risks:** Earnings are due August 6, 2026, so near-term information risk is elevated. The market is likely pricing in slower growth, product-cycle risk, or competitive pressure. Debt/equity of 2.86 is high, though debt/EBITDA of 2.32 looks manageable today.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 11.19; P/FCF 14.60; EV/EBITDA 10.03; ROIC 27.80%; operating margin 38.12%; next earnings August 6.

### 6. PayPal Holdings (PYPL)

**Why it looks undervalued:** PayPal remains a cheap turnaround rather than a pristine compounder. StockAnalysis showed trailing P/E of 11.00, forward P/E of 10.51, P/FCF of 7.58, EV/FCF of 8.03, FCF yield of 13.19%, and buyback yield of 7.83%.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** PayPal's Q2 2026 release reported net revenue up 5% to $8.7 billion, total payment volume up 10% to $486.4 billion, operating cash flow of $2.0 billion, free cash flow of $1.8 billion, and $1.5 billion of buybacks during the quarter. StockAnalysis showed ROE of 24.50%, ROIC of 22.27%, and manageable debt/FCF of 2.16.

**Main risks:** Branded checkout competition remains intense, active-account growth is still muted, and the investment case depends on management proving that transaction-margin-dollar and EPS improvement can become durable growth rather than temporary cost discipline.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 10.51; P/FCF 7.58; EV/FCF 8.03; FCF yield 13.19%; Q2 TPV +10%; Q2 free cash flow $1.8 billion.

### 7. Comcast (CMCSA)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Comcast remains one of the cheapest large-cap cash-flow names. StockAnalysis showed trailing P/E of 7.97, forward P/E of 7.00, P/FCF of 4.27, EV/FCF of 8.32, dividend yield of 5.36%, shareholder yield of 10.10%, and FCF yield of 23.40%.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Comcast still owns scaled connectivity, wireless, media, and studio assets that generate substantial cash. Its Q2 2026 release reported $4.6 billion of free cash flow, $2.1 billion returned to shareholders, record domestic wireless net additions of 448,000, business-services connectivity revenue growth of 3.7%, and Peacock's first profitable quarter.

**Main risks:** Comcast remains cheap for good reasons. Broadband erosion, fiber and fixed-wireless competition, high absolute debt, declining three-year growth forecasts, and the pause in repurchases during the separation process all lower confidence. This is a high-cash-flow value candidate, not a clean compounder.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 7.00; P/FCF 4.27; EV/FCF 8.32; dividend yield 5.36%; Q2 FCF $4.6 billion; 448,000 wireless net additions.

- **Still top ranked:** Adobe, Accenture, Cigna, Zoetis, PayPal, and Comcast remain on the list. Cigna's Q2 release is the main confirmation event since July 29; results were strong enough to keep it near the top, though PBM and medical-cost risks remain live.

Progressive adds a financially stronger insurer idea. Comcast and PayPal remain statistically cheap, but their theses require more tolerance for structural or competitive pressure.

## Conclusion

The strongest current quality-value candidates are **Adobe (ADBE)**, **Accenture (ACN)**, **Cigna (CI)**, and **Progressive (PGR)**. Adobe and Accenture offer the best mix of high returns on capital, free cash flow, and compressed valuation, though both carry AI-related uncertainty. Cigna's Q2 results support the healthcare-services value case at an implied forward adjusted P/E below 10x. Progressive is today's notable addition because it pairs high-quality underwriting economics with a reasonable cash-flow multiple. Zoetis, PayPal, and Comcast remain credible but carry either near-term earnings risk, turnaround risk, or structural pressure.
