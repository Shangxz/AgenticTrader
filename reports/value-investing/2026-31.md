# Value Investing Weekly Report - 2026-W31

_Run dates: 2026-07-27, 2026-07-29, 2026-07-30, 2026-07-31_

This report is research support only, not personal financial advice. Each update starts from a fresh quality-value review of U.S.-listed public companies, emphasizing valuation, cash generation, balance-sheet and business quality, and identifiable reasons the market price may understate medium-term value. Prior entries from the same week are preserved only as comparison history after today's independent ranking is complete.

## Market Context

The broad U.S. equity market still does not screen as generally cheap. FactSet's July 2026 Earnings Insight showed the S&P 500 at a forward 12-month P/E of 20.1, above both the 5-year average of 19.9 and 10-year average of 19.0. FactSet also noted strong Q2 earnings momentum, with blended S&P 500 earnings growth of 37.9% and revenue growth of 13.2%, but part of that growth was unusually concentrated in large technology and communication-services contributors.

Today's fresh scan used broad U.S. equity and large-cap screens, current value/quality lists, Morningstar's recent undervalued wide-moat and Best Companies to Own work, current StockAnalysis valuation/quality data, and company earnings releases. The strongest candidates remain company-specific dislocations rather than a broad value-market call. The final ranking weights valuation, free cash flow, return on invested capital, balance-sheet risk, business durability, and whether the current discount has a concrete medium-term explanation.

## Today's Top Ideas - 2026-07-31

### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Adobe continues to screen as the cleanest quality-value idea in the current U.S. large-cap universe. StockAnalysis, checked July 31, showed trailing P/E of 14.18, forward P/E of 9.56, P/FCF of 9.59, EV/FCF of 9.73, PEG of 0.68, and a 33.13% 52-week price decline. Those are value-stock multiples for a company that still has software-like margins and cash conversion.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Adobe's reported economics remain exceptional: ROIC of 60.74%, ROE of 62.95%, debt/FCF of 0.69, and interest coverage of 34.83x. Its financials show a trailing FCF margin of 40.80% and operating margin above 36%. Adobe's Q2 FY2026 release reported record quarterly revenue of $6.62 billion, up 13% year over year, and operating cash flow of $2.17 billion.

**Main risks:** The discount reflects a real debate over generative AI, creative-workflow disruption, and whether Adobe can sustain subscription pricing power. The thesis requires evidence that AI features deepen the suite's relevance rather than commoditizing parts of it.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 9.56; P/FCF 9.59; EV/FCF 9.73; ROIC 60.74%; FCF margin 40.80%; 52-week price change -33.13%.

### 2. Accenture (ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Accenture now trades at a larger discount after another price decline. StockAnalysis showed trailing P/E of 13.06, forward P/E of 11.39, P/FCF of 7.94, EV/FCF of 7.80, dividend yield of 3.99%, FCF yield of 12.59%, and a 41.45% 52-week price decline. For a global, asset-light professional-services franchise, that combination remains unusually inexpensive.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Accenture has high returns, low capital intensity, and a conservative balance sheet. StockAnalysis showed ROIC of 27.09%, ROE of 24.41%, debt/EBITDA of 0.64, debt/FCF of 0.67, interest coverage of 39.85x, and net cash of about $1.78 billion. Its fiscal Q3 2026 release said the company delivered solid revenue, strong profitability, EPS growth, robust free cash flow, and expected fiscal 2026 revenue growth of 3% to 4% in local currency, or 4% to 5% excluding the estimated federal-business drag.

**Main risks:** Consulting demand is cyclical, federal business remains a headwind, and AI may pressure parts of the services stack. The positive case depends on Accenture converting AI disruption into advisory, integration, and managed-services demand rather than simply seeing utilization and pricing erode.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 11.39; P/FCF 7.94; EV/FCF 7.80; FCF yield 12.59%; ROIC 27.09%; dividend yield 3.99%; net cash $1.78 billion.

### 3. The Cigna Group (CI)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Cigna remains a strong healthcare-services value candidate after its July 30 earnings report and subsequent stock weakness. StockAnalysis showed trailing P/E of 11.89, forward P/E of 9.08, P/FCF of 8.35, EV/EBITDA of 7.94, and PEG of 0.85. Using the company's raised 2026 adjusted EPS outlook of at least $30.45, the July 30 close of $287.66 implies a forward adjusted earnings multiple below 10x.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Cigna reported Q2 2026 revenue up 7% to $71.7 billion, shareholders' net income of $1.7 billion, adjusted EPS of $7.78, and a raised full-year adjusted EPS outlook. StockAnalysis showed ROIC of 13.39%, ROE of 16.76%, and a debt/EBITDA ratio of 2.51. The business remains scaled and diversified across Cigna Healthcare and Evernorth.

**Main risks:** Pharmacy-benefit-manager scrutiny, rebate-model changes, medical-cost inflation, and policy risk remain material. Cigna's medical care ratio rose to 84.5% from 83.2% a year earlier, and total pharmacy customers declined from year-end 2025. This is a cheap scaled platform, but not a controversy-free one.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 9.08; P/FCF 8.35; EV/EBITDA 7.94; implied forward adjusted P/E below 10x using 2026 guidance; Q2 revenue +7%.

### 4. The Progressive Corporation (PGR)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Progressive remains one of the best quality-value insurance ideas from today's fresh scan. It is not deep value on book value, but StockAnalysis showed trailing P/E of 10.70, forward P/E of 13.21, P/FCF of 7.55, EV/FCF of 8.06, and FCF yield of 13.24%. That is a reasonable cash-flow multiple for an insurer with a long record of underwriting discipline.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Progressive reported Q2 2026 net premiums written of $21.08 billion, up 5% year over year, net premiums earned of $21.57 billion, up 6%, net income of $3.31 billion, up 4%, and a combined ratio of 87.3. Policies in force rose 7% to 40.1 million. StockAnalysis showed ROIC of 28.70%, ROE of 34.94%, debt/equity of 0.24, debt/FCF of 0.51, and interest coverage of 50.76x.

**Main risks:** Auto-insurance underwriting profit can normalize quickly if pricing competition rises or loss-cost inflation reaccelerates. The Q2 combined ratio worsened from 86.2 in Q2 2025 to 87.3 in Q2 2026, so the quality case depends on Progressive sustaining underwriting superiority through the cycle.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 13.21; P/FCF 7.55; EV/FCF 8.06; FCF yield 13.24%; ROIC 28.70%; Q2 combined ratio 87.3.

### 5. Zoetis (ZTS)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Zoetis remains a high-quality animal-health franchise priced like growth has stalled permanently. StockAnalysis showed trailing P/E of 12.45, forward P/E of 10.91, P/FCF of 14.25, EV/EBITDA of 9.83, dividend yield of 2.79%, and a 49.65% 52-week price decline. Morningstar's July value list also included Zoetis among undervalued Best Companies to Own.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** The business has durable end-market demand, high margins, and strong returns. StockAnalysis showed gross margin of 71.84%, operating margin of 38.12%, FCF margin of 23.47%, ROIC of 27.80%, ROE of 67.75%, and interest coverage of 15.22x.

**Main risks:** Zoetis reports earnings on August 6, 2026, so near-term information risk is higher than for companies that have already reported. Debt/equity of 2.86 and debt/FCF of 4.14 are also worth watching. The market may be discounting slower product growth, competitive pressure, or animal-health demand normalization.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 10.91; P/FCF 14.25; EV/EBITDA 9.83; ROIC 27.80%; operating margin 38.12%; 52-week price change -49.65%.

### 6. PayPal Holdings (PYPL)

**Why it looks undervalued:** PayPal remains a cheap turnaround with strong cash generation. StockAnalysis showed trailing P/E of 10.87, forward P/E of 10.39, P/FCF of 7.49, EV/FCF of 7.94, and an 19.31% 52-week price decline. The company's share count has declined 7.83% year over year, supporting per-share value if operating results stabilize.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** PayPal's Q2 2026 release reported net revenue up 5% to $8.7 billion, total payment volume up 10% to $486.4 billion, operating cash flow of $2.0 billion, free cash flow of $1.8 billion, and $1.5 billion of buybacks during the quarter. StockAnalysis showed ROIC of 22.27%, ROE of 24.50%, debt/FCF of 2.16, and interest coverage of 12.90x.

**Main risks:** This is not a pristine compounder. Branded checkout competition remains intense, active-account growth is muted, and the turnaround depends on durable transaction-margin-dollar growth rather than temporary expense control and buybacks.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 10.39; P/FCF 7.49; EV/FCF 7.94; ROIC 22.27%; Q2 free cash flow $1.8 billion; shares outstanding -7.83% year over year.

### 7. Comcast (CMCSA)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Comcast is still one of the cheapest cash-flow-heavy large caps in the U.S. market. StockAnalysis showed trailing P/E of 7.66, forward P/E of 6.74, P/FCF of 4.11, EV/FCF of 8.16, dividend yield of 5.56%, and a 27.90% 52-week price decline. The valuation already reflects substantial pessimism.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Comcast still owns scaled connectivity, wireless, media, studio, and broadband infrastructure assets that generate significant cash. Its Q2 2026 release reported $4.6 billion of free cash flow, $2.1 billion returned to shareholders, record domestic wireless net additions of 448,000, business-services connectivity revenue growth of 3.7%, and Peacock's first profitable quarter.

**Main risks:** Comcast is cheap partly because the core broadband business faces real structural pressure from fiber and fixed wireless competition. Debt/FCF of 4.42 and interest coverage of 4.16x are less comfortable than the top-ranked names. The stock belongs on the list for cash-flow value, but confidence is lower than for Adobe, Accenture, Cigna, or Progressive.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 6.74; P/FCF 4.11; EV/FCF 8.16; dividend yield 5.56%; Q2 FCF $4.6 billion; ROIC 8.15%.

## Screened Out Or Lower-Ranked Today

- **Nvidia (NVDA):** Business quality is extraordinary, with StockAnalysis showing ROIC above 100%, minimal leverage, and a forward P/E below 20 after the recent selloff. Morningstar reportedly moved Nvidia into undervalued territory. It stays off the top quality-value list because P/FCF near 40x, very high expectations, hyperscaler spending risk, and high valuation uncertainty leave less margin of safety than the top-ranked names.
- **United Parcel Service (UPS):** UPS is improving and remains interesting after better Q2 results, but current StockAnalysis data showed P/FCF of 16.40, EV/FCF of 20.80, debt/FCF of 5.25, and debt/equity of 1.90. That makes the risk/reward less compelling than Progressive or Accenture despite the dividend and turnaround potential.
- **Berkshire Hathaway (BRK.B):** Berkshire remains a durable defensive compounder and still trades at a reasonable 1.51x book value, but current FCF multiples screen less attractive than the top ideas. It remains a watchlist anchor rather than a top weekly addition.
- **Nike (NKE):** Morningstar's wide-moat work still flags Nike as deeply undervalued, and the brand remains durable. It is lower-ranked today because the turnaround evidence is still mixed, China risk remains important, and near-term earnings quality is less clear than Adobe, Zoetis, or Progressive.
- **Bank of America (BAC):** Financials as a sector look cheaper than the market, and Morningstar has favored BAC among U.S. megabanks. It was lower-ranked today because rate sensitivity, credit-cycle uncertainty, and bank balance-sheet opacity make the quality-value case less clean than Cigna or Progressive.
- **Merck (MRK):** Merck looks inexpensive on some cash-flow measures, but the Keytruda 2028 patent cliff and pipeline replacement risk keep it below Cigna and Zoetis in healthcare.

## Conclusion

The strongest current quality-value candidates are **Adobe (ADBE)**, **Accenture (ACN)**, **Cigna (CI)**, and **Progressive (PGR)**. Adobe and Accenture offer the best blend of high returns on capital, free cash flow, and compressed valuation. Cigna's Q2 report supports the healthcare-services value case at a single-digit forward adjusted earnings multiple, though regulatory and medical-cost risks matter. Progressive adds a high-quality insurer with strong underwriting evidence at a reasonable cash-flow multiple. Zoetis, PayPal, and Comcast remain credible secondary candidates, but each carries more near-term earnings, turnaround, or structural pressure.
