This report is research support only, not personal financial advice. ## Market Context

The broad U.S. equity market still does not screen as generally cheap. FactSet's July 2026 Earnings Insight showed the S&P 500 at a forward 12-month P/E of 20.1, above both the 5-year average of 19.9 and 10-year average of 19.0. FactSet also noted strong Q2 earnings momentum, with blended S&P 500 earnings growth of 37.9% and revenue growth of 13.2%, but part of that growth was unusually concentrated in large technology and communication-services contributors.

The August 1 refresh re-ran the quality-value ranking from the latest broad U.S. equity dataset and company evidence available through today. Because August 1 is a Saturday, valuation inputs use the July 31 close; there is no newer market-price session. The review spans broad U.S. equity and large-cap screens, current value/quality lists, Morningstar's recent undervalued wide-moat and Best Companies to Own work, StockAnalysis valuation/quality data, and company earnings releases. The strongest candidates remain company-specific dislocations rather than a broad value-market call. The final ranking weights valuation, free cash flow, return on invested capital, balance-sheet risk, business durability, and whether the current discount has a concrete medium-term explanation.

## Today's Top Ideas - 2026-08-01

_August 1 is a Saturday. Prices and market-based multiples below therefore use the latest available close, July 31. Today's ranking was independently re-scored from the latest broad-market screen; prior weekly rankings were consulted only after the ranking was complete._

### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Adobe's latest available valuation snapshot shows a trailing P/E of 14.18, forward P/E of 9.56, P/FCF of 9.59, and EV/FCF of 9.73 after a 33.13% 52-week decline. Those multiples imply a severe deterioration that has not appeared in the latest operating results.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Q2 FY2026 revenue reached a record $6.62 billion, up 13% year over year, with $2.17 billion of operating cash flow. ROIC of 60.74%, a 40.80% FCF margin, debt/FCF of 0.69, and interest coverage of 34.83x support the view that this remains a high-quality software franchise.

**Main risks:** Generative AI may commoditize parts of the creative workflow, pressure pricing, or reduce switching costs. The thesis weakens if AI monetization fails to offset disruption or if recurring growth decelerates sharply.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 9.56; P/FCF 9.59; EV/FCF 9.73; ROIC 60.74%; FCF margin 40.80%.

### 2. Accenture (ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Accenture screens at a trailing P/E of 13.06, forward P/E of 11.39, P/FCF of 7.94, EV/FCF of 7.80, and a 12.59% FCF yield after a 41.45% 52-week decline. That is an unusually compressed valuation for a global, asset-light services platform.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** ROIC of 27.09%, debt/FCF of 0.67, interest coverage of 39.85x, and net cash of about $1.78 billion indicate strong economics and financial resilience. Fiscal Q3 2026 results still showed solid revenue, profitability, EPS growth, and free cash flow.

**Main risks:** Consulting is cyclical, U.S. federal work remains a headwind, and AI could reduce labor intensity or pricing in parts of the delivery model. The upside case depends on Accenture capturing enough AI advisory and implementation work to offset those pressures.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 11.39; P/FCF 7.94; EV/FCF 7.80; FCF yield 12.59%; ROIC 27.09%.

### 3. The Progressive Corporation (PGR)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Progressive is not deep value on book value, but its trailing P/E of 10.70, forward P/E of 13.21, and P/FCF of 7.55 are reasonable for a consistently superior underwriter. Its valuation offers a better quality adjustment than many lower-multiple financials.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Q2 2026 net premiums earned rose 6%, policies in force rose 7%, and the combined ratio remained excellent at 87.3. ROIC of 28.70%, ROE of 34.94%, debt/equity of 0.24, and interest coverage of 50.76x provide unusually clean balance-sheet support.

**Main risks:** Auto-insurance margins can normalize quickly if pricing competition rises, catastrophe losses worsen, or repair and bodily-injury inflation reaccelerate. The combined ratio also deteriorated from 86.2 a year earlier.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 13.21; P/FCF 7.55; ROIC 28.70%; debt/equity 0.24; Q2 combined ratio 87.3.

### 4. The Cigna Group (CI)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Cigna's trailing P/E of 11.89, forward P/E of 9.08, P/FCF of 8.35, and EV/EBITDA of 7.94 imply substantial skepticism. The July 30 close of $287.66 was below 10x the company's raised 2026 adjusted EPS outlook of at least $30.45.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Q2 revenue rose 7% to $71.7 billion, adjusted EPS was $7.78, and management raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance. The scaled Cigna Healthcare and Evernorth platforms produced ROIC of 13.39% and ROE of 16.76%.

**Main risks:** PBM regulation, rebate-model changes, medical-cost inflation, and policy risk are substantial. The medical care ratio rose to 84.5% from 83.2%, so the discount is not purely a market misunderstanding.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 9.08; P/FCF 8.35; EV/EBITDA 7.94; implied adjusted P/E below 10x; Q2 revenue +7%.

### 5. Zoetis (ZTS)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Zoetis screens at a trailing P/E of 12.45, forward P/E of 10.91, P/FCF of 14.25, and EV/EBITDA of 9.83 after a 49.65% 52-week decline. The market appears to be pricing a prolonged growth slowdown.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Gross margin of 71.84%, operating margin of 38.12%, FCF margin of 23.47%, and ROIC of 27.80% reflect durable animal-health economics and a strong franchise.

**Main risks:** Earnings are due August 6, creating near-term information risk. Debt/FCF of 4.14, slower product growth, competition, or demand normalization could justify part of the discount.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 10.91; P/FCF 14.25; EV/EBITDA 9.83; ROIC 27.80%; operating margin 38.12%.

### 6. PayPal Holdings (PYPL)

**Why it looks undervalued:** PayPal's trailing P/E of 10.87, forward P/E of 10.39, P/FCF of 7.49, and EV/FCF of 7.94 leave room for value creation if operating trends merely stabilize. Shares outstanding fell 7.83% year over year.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Q2 2026 revenue rose 5%, payment volume rose 10%, free cash flow reached $1.8 billion, and ROIC was 22.27%. The platform retains scale and the cash capacity to repurchase shares at a low multiple.

**Main risks:** Branded-checkout competition, muted active-account growth, and uncertain transaction-margin durability make this a turnaround rather than a pristine compounder. Buybacks do not repair a weakening franchise on their own.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 10.39; P/FCF 7.49; EV/FCF 7.94; ROIC 22.27%; Q2 FCF $1.8 billion.

### 7. Comcast (CMCSA)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Comcast's trailing P/E of 7.66, forward P/E of 6.74, P/FCF of 4.11, and 5.56% dividend yield price in substantial pessimism. Q2 free cash flow of $4.6 billion demonstrates that the asset base still generates large absolute cash flows.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Comcast retains scaled broadband, wireless, business-connectivity, studio, and media assets. Q2 included record domestic wireless net additions, business-services connectivity growth, and Peacock's first profitable quarter.

**Main risks:** Core broadband faces structural pressure from fiber and fixed wireless. Debt/FCF of 4.42 and interest coverage of 4.16x make this the lowest-confidence balance sheet in the top seven.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 6.74; P/FCF 4.11; dividend yield 5.56%; Q2 FCF $4.6 billion; ROIC 8.15%.

## Conclusion

The strongest current quality-value candidates are **Adobe (ADBE)**, **Accenture (ACN)**, **Progressive (PGR)**, and **Cigna (CI)**. Adobe and Accenture offer the most attractive combination of high returns on capital, strong free cash flow, manageable balance sheets, and compressed valuations. Progressive ranks just ahead of Cigna because its underwriting evidence and leverage profile are cleaner, while Cigna's regulatory and medical-cost risks demand a larger uncertainty discount despite a lower earnings multiple. Zoetis, PayPal, and Comcast remain credible secondary candidates with more event, turnaround, or structural risk. This is a research shortlist, not a claim of precise intrinsic value or a recommendation tailored to any investor.
