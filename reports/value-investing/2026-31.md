# Value Investing Weekly Report - 2026-W31

_Run dates: 2026-07-27, 2026-07-29_

This report is research support only, not personal financial advice. Each update starts from a fresh quality-value review of U.S.-listed public companies, emphasizing valuation, cash generation, balance-sheet and business quality, and identifiable reasons the market price may understate medium-term value. Prior entries from the same week are preserved only as comparison history after today's independent ranking is complete.

## Market Context

U.S. equities remain expensive enough that the best value ideas are still concentrated in company-specific dislocations rather than broad market bargains. StreetStats showed the S&P 500 at 7,428.16 as of the July 28, 2026 close, with a forward P/E of 19.61 and trailing P/E of 23.56. MacroMicro's S&P 500 forward P/E series also showed a latest reading near 19.82 for July 28.

Today's screen used broad value/quality sources as a starting point, including July 2026 undervalued-stock lists and low-multiple screens, then rechecked company-specific valuation, free cash flow, returns on capital, balance-sheet risk, and recent operating results. The strongest current setups are no longer only healthcare services, media/connectivity, and autos. Several high-return software and services businesses now screen as unusually cheap after large drawdowns, so today's list adds Adobe and Accenture near the top and moves some prior defensive names lower.

## Today's Top Ideas - 2026-07-29

### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Adobe now screens as a rare high-quality software company at a value multiple. StockAnalysis showed a trailing P/E of 14.25, forward P/E of 9.61, P/FCF of 9.64, EV/FCF of 9.78, and a 10.38% FCF yield as of July 29, 2026. The stock was down about 33% over the prior 52 weeks, suggesting the market is heavily discounting AI disruption and execution risk.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Adobe's Q2 FY2026 release reported record quarterly revenue of $6.62 billion, up 13% year over year, operating cash flow of $2.17 billion, and updated FY2026 targets for $26.50 billion to $26.60 billion of revenue and non-GAAP EPS of $24.35 to $24.45. StockAnalysis showed ROIC above 60%, operating margin above 36%, and an FCF margin above 40%.

**Main risks:** Adobe faces a real AI-disruption test. If generative-AI tools reduce pricing power in Creative Cloud or weaken the moat around professional workflows, the low multiple may be deserved. The CFO transition announced with Q2 results also adds some near-term governance and execution uncertainty.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 9.61; P/FCF 9.64; EV/FCF 9.78; ROIC 60.74%; Q2 revenue up 13%; FY2026 non-GAAP EPS target $24.35 to $24.45.

### 2. Accenture (ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Accenture combines a large drawdown with strong economics. StockAnalysis showed a trailing P/E of 13.17, forward P/E of 11.48, P/FCF of 8.01, EV/FCF of 7.87, and a stock price down about 42% over the prior 52 weeks. Investing.com's July undervalued-stock screen also flagged Accenture with a high model-implied fair-value upside.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Accenture remains one of the world's leading IT services and consulting platforms, with scale, customer relationships, and an asset-light cash model. Its fiscal Q3 2026 release said the company delivered solid revenue, strong profitability, EPS growth, robust free cash flow, and expected full-year fiscal 2026 revenue growth of 3% to 4% in local currency, or 4% to 5% excluding an estimated federal-business drag. StockAnalysis showed ROE of 24.41%, ROIC of 27.09%, debt/equity of 0.25, and interest coverage near 40x.

**Main risks:** Consulting demand can slow quickly if enterprise spending weakens. The U.S. federal business remains a headwind, AI automation may pressure some services categories, and a large workforce makes utilization critical.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 11.48; P/FCF 8.01; EV/FCF 7.87; ROIC 27.09%; debt/equity 0.25; 52-week price decline about 41.85%.

### 3. The Cigna Group (CI)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Cigna remains one of the cleaner healthcare quality-value ideas. StockAnalysis showed a trailing P/E of 12.76, forward P/E of 9.75, P/FCF of 10.40, EV/EBITDA of 8.32, PEG of 0.96, and FCF yield of 9.62%. The valuation is modest for a scaled healthcare-services and insurance platform.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Cigna's Q1 2026 release reported revenue up 5% to $68.5 billion, shareholders' net income of $1.7 billion, adjusted EPS of $7.79, and a raised 2026 adjusted EPS outlook of at least $30.35. StockAnalysis showed ROE of 16.27%, ROIC of 14.34%, and debt/EBITDA of 2.50.

**Main risks:** Cigna reports Q2 2026 results on July 30, so today's view may change quickly. Healthcare policy risk, pharmacy-benefit-manager scrutiny, medical-cost pressure, and reimbursement changes remain material. The low current ratio is common in the sector but still worth monitoring alongside cash flow.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 9.75; P/FCF 10.40; PEG 0.96; ROIC 14.34%; 2026 adjusted EPS outlook at least $30.35.

### 4. Zoetis (ZTS)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Zoetis now screens like a quality compounder priced closer to a cyclical disappointment. StockAnalysis showed a trailing P/E of 12.69, forward P/E of 11.13, P/FCF of 14.53, EV/EBITDA of 9.98, and a share-price decline of about 49% over the prior 52 weeks. Its next confirmed earnings date is August 6, 2026.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Zoetis has a leading animal-health franchise with high margins, recurring demand characteristics, and strong returns on capital. StockAnalysis showed gross margin of 71.84%, operating margin of 38.12%, profit margin of 28.03%, FCF margin of 23.47%, ROIC of 27.80%, and interest coverage above 15x.

**Main risks:** The market is likely discounting slower growth, competitive pressure, or product-cycle concerns. Debt/equity is elevated at 2.86, though debt/EBITDA of 2.32 and interest coverage above 15x look manageable today. With earnings due next week, position sizing and confidence should reflect near-term information risk.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 11.13; P/FCF 14.53; EV/EBITDA 9.98; ROIC 27.80%; operating margin 38.12%; 52-week decline about 49.05%.

### 5. PayPal Holdings (PYPL)

**Why it looks undervalued:** PayPal remains a turnaround candidate, but today's Q2 results make the value case more concrete. StockAnalysis showed a trailing P/E of 10.99, forward P/E of 10.51, P/FCF of 7.58, EV/FCF of 7.90, FCF yield of 13.20%, and a 25% 52-week price decline even after the recent rally.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** PayPal's Q2 2026 release reported net revenue up 5% to $8.7 billion, total payment volume up 10% to $486.4 billion, operating cash flow of $2.0 billion, free cash flow of $1.8 billion, and $1.5 billion of buybacks during the quarter. The company also raised full-year non-GAAP transaction-margin-dollar and EPS guidance. StockAnalysis showed ROE of 24.50%, ROIC of 22.98%, and manageable debt/FCF of 2.03.

**Main risks:** Branded checkout remains a competitive battlefield, active accounts were essentially flat sequentially, non-GAAP operating margin contracted year over year, and the investment case still depends on management proving the turnaround can restore durable growth rather than merely cut costs. Recent buyout speculation may also distort short-term pricing.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 10.51; P/FCF 7.58; EV/FCF 7.90; FCF yield 13.20%; Q2 TPV up 10%; Q2 free cash flow $1.8 billion.

### 6. Comcast (CMCSA)

**Why it looks undervalued:** Comcast remains one of the cheapest large-cap cash-flow names. StockAnalysis showed a trailing P/E of 7.83, forward P/E of 6.88, P/FCF of 4.20, EV/FCF of 8.25, dividend yield of 5.46%, shareholder yield of 10.20%, and FCF yield of 23.81%. The stock was down about 28% over the prior 52 weeks.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Comcast still owns scaled connectivity, media, and studio assets that generate substantial cash. Its Q2 2026 release reported $4.6 billion of free cash flow, $2.1 billion returned to shareholders, record domestic wireless net additions of 448,000, business-services connectivity revenue growth of 3.7%, and Peacock's first profitable quarter.

**Main risks:** Broadband erosion remains the central concern. Residential connectivity weakness, competition from fiber and fixed wireless, high debt, and the pause in repurchases during the separation process all reduce confidence. This is statistically very cheap, but quality is less pristine than Adobe, Accenture, Cigna, or Zoetis.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 6.88; P/FCF 4.20; EV/FCF 8.25; dividend yield 5.46%; Q2 FCF $4.6 billion; 448,000 wireless net additions.

### 7. General Motors (GM)

**Why it looks undervalued:** GM remains inexpensive on forward earnings and free cash flow. StockAnalysis showed a forward P/E of 6.61, P/FCF of 5.50, P/B of 1.28, FCF yield of 18.17%, and a buyback yield of 10.37%. GM also reduced its share count by more than 10% year over year.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** The quality case is less about stability and more about scale, franchise strength, and current execution. GM's Q2 2026 release reported revenue of $48.0 billion, EBIT-adjusted of $3.9 billion, GM North America EBIT-adjusted margin of 8.6%, and adjusted automotive free cash flow of $5.0 billion in the quarter. Management raised full-year 2026 guidance to EBIT-adjusted of $14.0 billion to $16.0 billion and adjusted automotive free cash flow of $9.5 billion to $11.5 billion.

**Main risks:** GM is cyclical, capital-intensive, and leveraged. StockAnalysis showed debt/equity of 2.02, debt/FCF of 8.94, and ROIC of only 6.11%. The stock has already risen about 69% over the prior 52 weeks, so the margin of safety is narrower than it was earlier in the year. Auto pricing, incentives, rates, labor, tariffs, and EV restructuring remain key risks.

**Valuation signals:** Forward P/E 6.61; P/FCF 5.50; FCF yield 18.17%; Q2 adjusted automotive FCF $5.0 billion; 2026 adjusted automotive FCF guide $9.5 billion to $11.5 billion.

## Screened Out Or Lower-Ranked Today

- **UnitedHealth Group (UNH):** Still a high-quality healthcare platform, but less compelling on today's quality-value ranking after the stock's recovery. StockAnalysis showed forward P/E of 20.14 and P/FCF of 16.49, while the stock was up about 53% over the prior 52 weeks. That is no longer as clearly discounted as CI, Adobe, Accenture, or Zoetis.
- **Berkshire Hathaway (BRK.B):** Remains a durable defensive value holding, but today's screen found stronger discounts elsewhere. Berkshire is more of a fair-to-modestly-undervalued quality anchor than a top mispricing candidate.
- **CBRE Group (CBRE):** Still a useful recovery watchlist idea, but the valuation is not as compelling as the newly surfaced high-return software/services candidates. Commercial real estate cyclicality and rate sensitivity keep confidence moderate.
- **Alphabet (GOOGL):** Business quality remains exceptional, but current value evidence is clouded by AI capital-spending intensity and recent free-cash-flow pressure. It stays on the revisit list rather than today's top value shortlist.
- **Everest Group (EG), AIG, and Global Payments (GPN):** Low-multiple screens surfaced these names, but today's highest-confidence quality-value blend favored companies with stronger current cash-flow evidence, clearer business durability, or better return profiles.

## Changes From Earlier Updates This Week

- **New additions to the top list:** Adobe, Accenture, Zoetis, and PayPal. The major change from July 27 is that high-return software/services and payments names now screen as sufficiently cheap to compete with the previously favored healthcare, media/connectivity, and auto names.
- **Still highly ranked:** Cigna, Comcast, and GM remain on the list, though Comcast and GM carry more obvious value-trap risk than the top four because of broadband erosion and auto cyclicality.
- **Moved lower or removed from top ideas:** UnitedHealth, Berkshire Hathaway, and CBRE. They remain investable watchlist names, but today's fresh ranking found a stronger quality-value mix elsewhere.
- **Confidence adjustment:** Adobe and Accenture now look like the cleanest combination of high returns on capital, manageable leverage, strong free cash flow, and unusually low current valuation. CI remains very compelling, but its Q2 earnings are due July 30, making near-term confirmation important.

## Conclusion

The strongest current quality-value candidates are **Adobe (ADBE)**, **Accenture (ACN)**, and **Cigna (CI)**. Adobe and Accenture offer the most attractive mix of high returns on capital, free-cash-flow strength, and compressed valuation. Cigna remains a low-multiple healthcare-services compounder, but tomorrow's earnings report is a key near-term check. Zoetis and PayPal are also credible additions: Zoetis offers high-quality animal-health economics at a much lower multiple than usual, while PayPal's Q2 results give its turnaround case more evidence. Comcast and GM are statistically cheaper, but both require more tolerance for structural and cyclical risk.
