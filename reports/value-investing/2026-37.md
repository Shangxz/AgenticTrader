> Research support only, not personal financial advice. Multiples are screening signals, not estimates of certain intrinsic value.

## Market context

The broad U.S. market looks less cheap than the headline suggests. Morningstar estimated its covered U.S. market at a 9% discount to fair value on August 31, but said the apparent discount was concentrated in seven mega-cap technology companies; excluding those names, its coverage was around fair value. That argues for selectivity rather than treating the whole market as inexpensive.

Today's selection was made independently before checking this weekly file. The initial screen used a current 2,278-name U.S.-listed scored universe and cross-checked dedicated quality-value, free-cash-flow, and quality-at-52-week-low screens. Finalists were then reviewed against the latest company or SEC disclosure. Ranking favors cash-backed earnings, durable margins or market position, balance-sheet resilience, and a credible discount. It does not simply choose the lowest P/E stocks.

### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $266.51, Adobe trades at roughly 15.3x trailing GAAP earnings. Against management's FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $24.35–$24.45, the price is about 10.9x the midpoint. That is an unusually low starting multiple for a subscription software franchise still reporting double-digit growth. The September 4 selloff followed a CEO-transition announcement rather than a new earnings release, creating a potentially useful mismatch between price and the latest operating evidence.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue increased 13% to $6.62 billion, total ARR rose 12.5% to $27.10 billion, and operating cash flow was $2.17 billion. Subscription revenue was $6.39 billion, or about 97% of quarterly revenue. Creative workflows, document standards, enterprise integrations, and accumulated user skill create meaningful switching costs; current results do not yet show wholesale AI-driven erosion.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Generative AI could commoditize parts of creation and pressure seat growth or pricing. FY2026 includes Semrush, so reported growth is not wholly organic. CEO and CFO transitions increase execution risk. Adobe reports Q3 on September 10; ARR growth, organic customer-group growth, AI monetization, and any change to guidance are immediate thesis checks. A material drop to mid-single-digit organic growth without offsetting margin or buyback benefits would weaken the case.

### 2. Deckers Brands (DECK)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $85.81, the shares are about 11.6x the midpoint of raised FY2027 EPS guidance of $7.35–$7.50. June-quarter cash was $1.60 billion with no outstanding borrowings, so enterprise value is materially below market capitalization. The board had approximately $4.7 billion of repurchase authorization remaining, though authorization is not a promise to spend.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal Q1 revenue rose 5.7% to $1.02 billion. HOKA grew 7.7%, UGG grew 4.9%, direct-to-consumer sales rose 13.0%, and gross margin expanded 60 basis points to 56.4%. The balance sheet provides room to invest through a consumer slowdown and retire shares at a depressed valuation.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Growth has slowed and operating income fell 6% in the latest quarter as SG&A rose. HOKA and UGG carry nearly the whole economic load, leaving fashion, execution, and brand-concentration risk. Tariffs, discretionary demand, wholesale inventory, and product-cycle misses could compress margins. Watch full-price sell-through, HOKA growth, international growth, and inventory; sustained low-single-digit brand growth would make the low multiple less exceptional.

### 3. Accenture (ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $186.72, Accenture trades at about 13.5x the midpoint of FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $13.78–$13.90, or about 13.9x the GAAP guidance midpoint. Guided FY2026 free cash flow of $10.8–$11.5 billion equates to roughly a 9.4%–10.0% yield on the current equity value before considering the $10.2 billion cash balance.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q3 revenue rose 6% in U.S. dollars, operating margin expanded 20 basis points to 17.0%, diluted EPS rose 9%, and the quarter generated $3.6 billion of free cash flow. Its scale, industry expertise, embedded client relationships, and ability to execute complex transformations remain difficult to replicate. Large AI programs can add work even as automation pressures lower-value services.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Local-currency growth was only 3%, bookings declined modestly to $19.3 billion, and U.S. federal business is a roughly one-point drag on FY2026 growth. AI could reduce labor-heavy implementation revenue faster than new transformation work replaces it. Acquisitions also add integration risk. Watch consulting bookings, organic growth excluding federal work, utilization, pricing, and whether margins hold.

### 4. The Cigna Group (CI)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $282.52, Cigna trades at about 11.7x trailing GAAP earnings and 9.3x management's raised 2026 adjusted EPS outlook of at least $30.45. Q2 adjusted income from operations rose 6%, and the company increased its full-year outlook.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Cigna combines employer health insurance with Evernorth's pharmacy, specialty, and care services. Q2 revenue rose 7% to $71.7 billion. Cigna Healthcare adjusted pre-tax income increased 17%, and its margin improved to 10.9%. Scale, broad client relationships, and specialty capabilities support recurring demand.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Pharmacy-benefit economics are politically and competitively exposed. Pharmacy Benefit Services adjusted pre-tax income fell 27% in Q2, and Evernorth's total adjusted pre-tax income fell 2%. Medical costs, contract renewals, reimbursement regulation, and $31.9 billion of total debt can overwhelm the cheap multiple. First-half operating cash flow was only $0.7 billion because of working-capital, Inflation Reduction Act, and liability timing effects; cash conversion must normalize. Watch PBM profit, medical-care ratio, debt reduction, and full-year cash flow.

### 5. Mueller Industries (MLI)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $63.74, Mueller trades around 16.3x trailing earnings. Its $1.42 billion of cash and short-term investments is about 10% of the $14.1 billion equity value, providing a meaningful valuation cushion and acquisition capacity.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 sales increased to $1.43 billion from $1.14 billion. Net income edged up to $249.7 million, while operating income increased 15.7% after adjusting the prior-year insurance gain. The current ratio was 4.8x. Mueller has a long record of profitable operation across industrial cycles and maintains a conservatively financed balance sheet.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** A 30.6% rise in average copper prices boosted reported sales, so headline growth overstates volume progress. Construction, HVAC, refrigeration, and industrial demand are cyclical; copper spreads and acquisition execution can move earnings sharply. This is the least obvious bargain in the top five and needs continued evidence that margins and returns are sustainable rather than peak-cycle.

## Near misses and exclusions

- **Alphabet (GOOGL):** The reported 17.0x trailing P/E is misleading because Q2 included a $99.0 billion net gain on equity securities. The core business is exceptional, but Q2 free cash flow was negative $5.9 billion after $44.9 billion of capex, and trailing free cash flow fell to $53.3 billion. The AI investment may prove highly valuable, but the current cash-return profile does not clear this report's margin-of-safety threshold.
- **Doximity (DOCS):** Excellent 47.7% adjusted EBITDA margin, net cash, and strong network economics, but roughly 31x trailing earnings and guidance implying about 1% Q2 revenue growth leave too little valuation support today.
- **GoDaddy (GDDY):** Strong free cash flow and margin expansion at about 15x trailing earnings, but financial leverage and a less certain long-term moat keep it below the five selected names.
- **Mueller's ranking versus Cigna:** Mueller has the stronger balance sheet; Cigna has the cheaper normalized earnings multiple. Both remain medium-confidence because cyclicality and regulation, respectively, can make headline value deceptive.

## Current conclusion

**Adobe is the strongest current quality-value candidate**, with double-digit recurring-revenue growth and heavy cash generation available at a low-teens forward cash-earnings multiple. **Deckers is the cleanest balance-sheet value**, while **Accenture offers the most established service franchise and a high guided free-cash-flow yield**. Cigna and Mueller add sector diversification but require a larger risk discount.

The next update should start from a fresh market ranking. After that ranking is complete, compare any changes against this list, with special attention to Adobe's September 10 earnings.
