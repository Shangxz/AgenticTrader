## Market context

The September 11 ranking was rebuilt from a fresh market evaluation before comparing it with earlier weekly selections. The starting set combined current screens covering roughly 4,600-5,055 U.S. stocks, a separate high-ROIC screen, and current quotes; finalists then received focused checks against recent SEC filings and company disclosures. This is a broad quantitative screen followed by fundamental review, not a claim that every listed security received a full appraisal.

### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $248.83, Adobe trades at about 13.7x the midpoint of updated FY2026 GAAP EPS guidance of $18.12-$18.17 and 10.2x the $24.45-$24.50 non-GAAP range. The multiple is low for a subscription software franchise still producing double-digit growth and substantial cash flow.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q3 revenue rose 13% to a record $6.76 billion, subscription revenue rose 14%, ending ARR reached $27.50 billion, and operating cash flow reached a quarterly record of $2.52 billion. AI-first ARR grew more than 150%, Adobe reached one billion monthly active users across creativity and productivity products, and management raised full-year revenue and EPS targets. File standards, installed workflows, enterprise integrations, and accumulated user skill support switching costs.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** The stock's premarket decline despite the beat suggests investors remain concerned about the pace and quality of future growth. FY2026 ending ARR growth is targeted at 10.2%, slower than current revenue growth, and freemium AI adoption still must convert into durable monetization. Generative AI can commoditize parts of content creation, while Anil Chakravarthy's December 1 CEO transition adds execution risk. Organic ARR, paid conversion, retention, and margin are the key tests.

### 2. Deckers Brands (DECK)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $79.88, Deckers trades at roughly 10.8x the midpoint of raised FY2027 EPS guidance of $7.35-$7.50. The company held $1.60 billion of cash and had no outstanding borrowings at June 30, so enterprise value is meaningfully below equity value.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal Q1 revenue grew 5.7%, HOKA grew 7.7%, UGG grew 4.9%, direct-to-consumer sales rose 13.0%, international sales rose 8.4%, and gross margin expanded to 56.4%. Strong brands, premium positioning, and a debt-free balance sheet provide resilience and room to reinvest.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** HOKA and UGG carry nearly all the economics, leaving the company exposed to fashion cycles, tariffs, channel inventory, and discretionary demand. Q1 operating income fell 6% as expenses increased, and the $338 million of quarterly repurchases occurred at an average $103.79, well above today's quote. The thesis weakens if full-price sell-through, brand growth, or gross margin deteriorates.

### 3. Accenture (ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $177.91, Accenture trades at about 12.9x the midpoint of FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $13.78-$13.90. Guided free cash flow of $10.8-$11.5 billion implies a 9.9%-10.5% yield on the current $109.5 billion market value.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q3 revenue rose 6% in U.S. dollars, operating margin expanded 20 basis points to 17.0%, EPS rose 9%, and quarterly free cash flow was $3.6 billion. Accenture's scale, industry expertise, ecosystem ties, and deeply embedded delivery relationships are difficult to replicate, while large transformation programs give it a credible route to participate in enterprise AI spending.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Local-currency growth was only 3%, bookings fell 3% in local currency, and book-to-bill was 1.0. AI may reduce labor-heavy implementation work faster than transformation demand replaces it, and U.S. federal weakness remains a drag. Organic bookings, utilization, pricing, and margin should determine whether the discount is opportunity or structural reset.

### 4. Halozyme Therapeutics (HALO)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $107.00, Halozyme trades at about 12.1x the midpoint of raised 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $8.65-$9.00. Adding approximately $1.92 billion of June net debt to the $12.97 billion equity value produces an enterprise value near $14.9 billion, or about 11.9x the $1.225-$1.280 billion adjusted EBITDA guidance range.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 48% to $481.0 million, royalty revenue rose 50% to $307.7 million, and operating income increased 42% to $287.7 million. ENHANZE is embedded in ten commercialized products across more than 100 markets, with a broad set of major pharmaceutical partners. The licensing model produces high incremental margins, while Hypercon and Surf Bio expand the technology platform beyond the original enzyme franchise.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Halozyme had $2.15 billion of gross debt against only $232 million of cash, restricted cash, and marketable securities at June 30, reflecting acquisition-funded expansion. Core ENHANZE patents begin expiring in 2027-2029 in key jurisdictions; later partner-product and new-platform patents may extend economics, but litigation and royalty durability remain central uncertainties. Partner-product concentration, integration execution, and the wide gap between GAAP and non-GAAP earnings also warrant a lower confidence rating than the top three.

### 5. Bristol Myers Squibb (BMY)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $63.75, Bristol Myers trades at about 9.3x the midpoint of raised 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $6.75-$7.00. The valuation leaves room for substantial erosion in legacy products, while the current earnings outlook moved higher rather than lower.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue increased 6% to $13.0 billion, Growth Portfolio revenue rose 15% to $7.6 billion, and non-GAAP EPS increased 40% to $2.04. Growth products now represent a larger share of the business, management raised full-year revenue guidance to $49-$50 billion, and June net debt fell $2.4 billion from year-end to $31.7 billion.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** This is the most obvious potential value trap in the top five. Revlimid already faces unrestricted U.S. generic volume, Eliquis has European exclusivity pressure, and additional major products approach loss of exclusivity later this decade. Net debt remains high, drug pricing policy can compress returns, and pipeline success must more than replace legacy erosion. Growth Portfolio trajectory and deleveraging are the decisive checks.

## Changes from earlier updates this week

Today's shortlist was selected independently, then compared with the September 10 report.

- **Adobe remains rank 1 after reporting Q3.** Revenue, EPS, cash flow, and AI-first ARR all strengthened, and full-year targets rose. The immediate earnings event risk is gone, but the shares fell about 2.4% from yesterday's snapshot and forward ARR growth remains the key debate; confidence is therefore high-medium rather than unqualified high.
- **Halozyme enters at rank 4.** Its roughly 12x forward non-GAAP earnings and EV/adjusted EBITDA multiples, paired with 48% quarterly revenue growth and 50% royalty growth, now outweigh its leverage and patent-duration risks.
- **Bristol Myers enters at rank 5.** A 9.3x guidance-based multiple, 15% Growth Portfolio expansion, higher guidance, and debt reduction earn it a place, but patent cliffs keep confidence at medium.
- **GoDaddy and Leidos move to near misses.** Both remain inexpensive cash generators. Halozyme offers materially faster growth, while Bristol Myers' raised outlook and screen strength provide a larger explicit earnings cushion.
- **Deckers and Accenture hold ranks 2 and 3.** Each requalified on today's fresh screen; their prices and thesis evidence changed only modestly.

## Near misses and exclusions

- **GoDaddy (GDDY):** about 7.0x its roughly $1.8 billion 2026 free-cash-flow target at today's $12.62 billion market value remains compelling. Material debt, nearly flat customer count, slower Core growth, and AI-native website competition keep it just outside the five.
- **Leidos (LDOS):** roughly 10.5x the midpoint of adjusted EPS guidance and a large backlog offer value and visibility. Acquisition debt, weaker margins, government budget risk, and a changed backlog policy reduce comparability.
- **Exelixis (EXEL):** an 18.0x trailing P/E, $1.38 billion of cash and marketable investments, and 10% Q2 product-revenue growth are attractive. CABOMETYX supplies nearly all product revenue, while patent litigation and zanzalintinib regulatory outcomes make the durable cash-flow case less settled.
- **Cirrus Logic (CRUS):** a 14.2x trailing P/E, record Q1 revenue, 52.6% gross margin, and a net-cash balance clear the quantitative quality/value bar. Apple represented approximately 90% of quarterly sales, an unusually high single-customer risk that keeps it below the finalists.
- **Harmony Biosciences (HRMY):** approximately 13.3x trailing earnings and a net-cash balance remain attractive, but reliance on WAKIX and generic entry generally permitted in July 2030 cap durability.
- **SAIC (SAIC):** approximately 12.0x raised adjusted EPS guidance and an 11%+ guided free-cash-flow yield are inexpensive, but trailing-twelve-month book-to-bill of 0.8 and flat-to-negative organic growth guidance weaken the forward-quality signal.
- **Financials, energy, shippers, and miners:** many ranked highly on headline factors, but sector-specific balance sheets or commodity-cycle earnings make raw P/E and cash-yield comparisons unreliable. None displaced the more durable finalists after normalization.

## Current conclusion

**Adobe remains the strongest current quality-value candidate** after a solid Q3 and a further valuation decline, though ARR deceleration and the CEO transition still matter. **Deckers has the cleanest balance sheet and lowest straightforward GAAP earnings multiple**, while **Accenture offers the best combination of franchise durability and double-digit guided free-cash-flow yield**. Halozyme brings the strongest growth at a modest forward multiple but carries leverage and patent risk. Bristol Myers offers the largest earnings discount, paired with the highest value-trap risk.
