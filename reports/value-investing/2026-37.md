> Research support only, not personal financial advice. Multiples are screening signals, not claims of certain intrinsic value.

## Market context

U.S. stocks rebounded Friday after four down sessions: the S&P 500 rose 0.9%, the Dow gained 1.0%, the Nasdaq rose 1.0%, and the Russell 2000 added 0.4%. Even after the rebound, the four indexes finished the week down 0.8%, 1.6%, 0.7%, and 2.4%, respectively. Brent crude settled 2.8% lower at $104.61, but August CPI was still 3.4% year over year and the 10-year Treasury yield edged up to 4.97%. High discount rates therefore remain an important stress test for long-duration equities.

The September 12 ranking was built independently before reading earlier selections. The starting set combined current screens spanning roughly 4,600 U.S. stocks, a separate 100-name low-P/E/high-Piotroski screen updated September 11, a 204-name high-ROIC screen, and a quality/value/momentum screen extending down to $250 million market capitalization. I then normalized obvious sector distortions and checked the finalists against recent filings and company disclosures. This is a broad systematic screen followed by focused fundamental review, not a claim that every listed company received a full intrinsic-value appraisal.

### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $252.23, Adobe trades at about 13.9x the midpoint of updated FY2026 GAAP EPS guidance of $18.12-$18.17 and 10.3x the $24.45-$24.50 non-GAAP range. That is a modest price for a subscription software franchise still producing double-digit growth and substantial cash flow.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q3 revenue rose 13% to a record $6.76 billion, subscription revenue rose 14%, ending ARR reached $27.50 billion, and operating cash flow reached a quarterly record $2.52 billion. AI-first ARR grew more than 150%, Adobe reported one billion monthly active users across creativity and productivity products, and management raised full-year revenue and EPS targets. File standards, installed workflows, enterprise integrations, and accumulated user skill support switching costs.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** FY2026 ending ARR growth is targeted at 10.2%, slower than current revenue growth, and freemium AI adoption still must convert into durable monetization. Generative AI can commoditize parts of content creation, while Anil Chakravarthy's December 1 CEO transition adds execution risk. Organic ARR, paid conversion, retention, and margin are the key tests.

### 2. Deckers Brands (DECK)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $81.27, Deckers trades at roughly 10.9x the midpoint of raised FY2027 EPS guidance of $7.35-$7.50. The company held $1.60 billion of cash and had no outstanding borrowings at June 30, so enterprise value is meaningfully below equity value.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Deckers also appeared on the current low-P/E/high-quality screen with a 7/9 Piotroski F-Score and 51.9% ROIC. Fiscal Q1 revenue grew 5.7%, HOKA grew 7.7%, UGG grew 4.9%, direct-to-consumer sales rose 13.0%, international sales rose 8.4%, and gross margin expanded to 56.4%. Strong brands, premium positioning, and a debt-free balance sheet provide resilience and room to reinvest.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** HOKA and UGG carry nearly all the economics, leaving the company exposed to fashion cycles, tariffs, channel inventory, and discretionary demand. Q1 operating income fell 6% as expenses increased, and $338 million of quarterly repurchases occurred at an average $103.79, well above Friday's quote. The thesis weakens if full-price sell-through, brand growth, or gross margin deteriorates.

### 3. Accenture (ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $183.90, Accenture trades at about 13.3x the midpoint of FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $13.78-$13.90. Guided free cash flow of $10.8-$11.5 billion implies a 9.5%-10.2% yield on the current $113.2 billion market value, even after Friday's 3.3% share-price rebound.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q3 revenue rose 6% in U.S. dollars, operating margin expanded 20 basis points to 17.0%, EPS rose 9%, and quarterly free cash flow was $3.6 billion. Accenture's scale, industry expertise, ecosystem ties, and deeply embedded delivery relationships are difficult to replicate, while large transformation programs offer a credible route to participate in enterprise AI spending.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Local-currency growth was only 3%, bookings fell 3% in local currency, and book-to-bill was 1.0. AI may reduce labor-heavy implementation work faster than transformation demand replaces it, and U.S. federal weakness remains a drag. Organic bookings, utilization, pricing, and margin should determine whether the discount is opportunity or structural reset.

### 4. Halozyme Therapeutics (HALO)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $107.20, Halozyme trades at about 12.1x the midpoint of raised 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $8.65-$9.00. Adding approximately $1.92 billion of June net debt to the $12.99 billion equity value produces enterprise value near $14.9 billion, or about 11.9x the $1.225-$1.280 billion adjusted EBITDA guidance range.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 48% to $481.0 million, royalty revenue rose 50% to $307.7 million, and operating income increased 42% to $287.7 million. ENHANZE is embedded in ten commercialized products across more than 100 markets with major pharmaceutical partners. The licensing model produces high incremental margins, while Hypercon and Surf Bio broaden the technology platform.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Halozyme had about $2.15 billion of gross debt against $232 million of cash, restricted cash, and marketable securities at June 30, reflecting acquisition-funded expansion. Core ENHANZE patents begin expiring in 2027-2029 in key jurisdictions; later partner-product and new-platform patents may extend economics, but litigation and royalty durability remain central uncertainties. Partner-product concentration, integration execution, and the wide gap between GAAP and non-GAAP earnings warrant lower confidence than the top three.

### 5. Bristol Myers Squibb (BMY)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $63.64, Bristol Myers trades at about 9.3x the midpoint of raised 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $6.75-$7.00. The current systematic value screen ranks BMY first on value among roughly 4,600 U.S. stocks and gives it a 92nd-percentile quality score. The price leaves room for substantial legacy-product erosion while the current earnings outlook has moved higher.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue increased 6% to $13.0 billion, Growth Portfolio revenue rose 15% to $7.6 billion, and non-GAAP EPS increased 40% to $2.04. Management raised full-year revenue guidance to $49-$50 billion, and June net debt fell $2.4 billion from year-end to $31.7 billion.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** This is the clearest potential value trap in the top five. Revlimid already faces unrestricted U.S. generic volume, Eliquis has European exclusivity pressure, and additional major products approach loss of exclusivity later this decade. Net debt remains high, drug-pricing policy can compress returns, and pipeline success must more than replace legacy erosion. Growth Portfolio trajectory and deleveraging are the decisive checks.

## Near misses and exclusions

- **GoDaddy (GDDY):** roughly 7.1x its approximately $1.8 billion 2026 free-cash-flow target at Friday's $12.85 billion market value remains compelling. Material debt, nearly flat customer count, slower Core growth, and AI-native website competition keep it just outside the five.
- **Leidos (LDOS):** a 12.0x trailing P/E, a large backlog, and durable mission demand offer value and visibility. Acquisition debt, weaker margins, government budget risk, and a changed backlog policy reduce comparability.
- **Exelixis (EXEL):** a 17.6x trailing P/E, strong cash resources, and continuing product growth are attractive. CABOMETYX supplies nearly all product revenue, while patent litigation and zanzalintinib regulatory outcomes leave durability less settled.
- **Cirrus Logic (CRUS):** a 14.5x trailing P/E, strong margins, and net cash clear the quantitative quality/value bar. Apple concentration remains unusually high.
- **PayPal (PYPL) and Cognizant (CTSH):** both surfaced on the September 11 low-P/E/high-Piotroski screen, but competitive pressure and slower organic growth make their forward quality less convincing than the finalists.
- **Financials, energy, shippers, and miners:** many ranked highly on headline factors, but sector-specific balance sheets or commodity-cycle earnings make raw P/E and ROIC comparisons unreliable. None displaced the finalists after normalization.

## Current conclusion

**Adobe remains the strongest current quality-value candidate**, though ARR deceleration, AI monetization, and the CEO transition still matter. **Deckers has the cleanest balance sheet and lowest straightforward earnings multiple**, while **Accenture offers the best combination of franchise durability and a near-double-digit guided free-cash-flow yield**. Halozyme brings the strongest growth at a modest adjusted multiple but carries leverage and patent risk. Bristol Myers offers the largest explicit earnings discount, paired with the highest value-trap risk.
