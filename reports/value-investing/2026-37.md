# Quality-Value Report — 2026 Week 37

_Last updated: September 8, 2026, before the U.S. market open. Prices are September 4 closing snapshots because September 7 was Labor Day._

> Research support only, not personal financial advice. Multiples are screening signals, not estimates of certain intrinsic value.

## Market context

The opportunity set remains selective. Morningstar estimated its covered U.S. market at a 9% discount to fair value on August 31, but said that discount was concentrated in seven mega-cap technology companies; excluding them, its coverage was near fair value. Vanguard separately described broad U.S. valuations as extremely elevated relative to history at June 30. Before Tuesday's open, higher oil prices and geopolitical tension were pressuring index futures and increasing the importance of upcoming inflation data.

Today's selection was made from a fresh evaluation, without using Monday's picks to form the shortlist. The screen covered a current 2,278-name U.S.-listed scored universe and emphasized valuation, cash generation, returns and margins, balance-sheet resilience, and competitive durability. Financial firms were treated separately because conventional operating-company quality metrics are not directly comparable. Finalists were checked against current company or SEC disclosures. This is broad-market screening, not a claim that every security received a full fundamental appraisal.

### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $266.51, Adobe trades at roughly 15.3x trailing GAAP earnings and 10.9x the midpoint of FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $24.35–$24.45. Even using the $17.90–$18.00 GAAP guidance range, the forward multiple is about 14.8x. The starting valuation is unusually low for a recurring-revenue software franchise that raised full-year revenue and non-GAAP EPS targets in June.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 13% to $6.62 billion, operating cash flow was $2.17 billion, and subscription revenue was $6.39 billion, about 97% of total revenue. Ending ARR was $27.10 billion and AI-first ARR exceeded $500 million. Adobe's file standards, creative workflows, enterprise integrations, and accumulated user skill create real switching costs.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Semrush contributed about $480 million of ARR, so headline growth is not wholly organic. Generative AI may commoditize parts of creation, pressure seats or pricing, or require expensive investment before monetization catches up. CEO and CFO transitions add execution risk. Q3 results on September 10 are the immediate test: weaker organic ARR, poor paid conversion of AI users, or lower guidance would reduce confidence.

### 2. Deckers Brands (DECK)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $85.81, Deckers trades at about 11.6x the midpoint of raised FY2027 EPS guidance of $7.35–$7.50. At June 30 it held $1.60 billion of cash and had no outstanding borrowings, making enterprise value meaningfully lower than market capitalization.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal Q1 revenue rose 5.7% to $1.02 billion. HOKA grew 7.7%, UGG grew 4.9%, direct-to-consumer sales rose 13.0%, international sales rose 8.4%, and gross margin expanded 60 basis points to 56.4%. The net-cash balance sheet provides room to invest and repurchase shares through a softer consumer cycle.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Operating income fell 6% as SG&A increased, and HOKA plus UGG carry almost all of the economics. Fashion, product-cycle, tariff, and discretionary-demand risks are material. The $338 million repurchased in Q1 was at an average $103.79, well above the current price, which is a reminder that buybacks create value only when allocation is disciplined. Watch full-price sell-through, inventories, brand growth, and margin.

### 3. Accenture (ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $186.72, Accenture is about 13.5x the midpoint of FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $13.78–$13.90. Guided free cash flow of $10.8–$11.5 billion implies roughly a 9.4%–10.0% yield on the current equity value.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q3 revenue rose 6% in U.S. dollars, operating margin expanded 20 basis points to 17.0%, EPS rose 9%, and free cash flow was $3.6 billion. Accenture's scale, industry knowledge, ecosystem ties, and embedded client relationships are difficult to reproduce. It has recorded 104 quarterly bookings of at least $100 million year to date, up 13%, suggesting large clients still entrust it with complex transformations.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Local-currency revenue growth was only 3%, bookings declined to $19.3 billion, and U.S. federal work is an estimated one-point drag on full-year growth. AI could reduce labor-heavy implementation revenue faster than new transformation work replaces it. A raised acquisition-spending target also adds integration and capital-allocation risk. Watch organic consulting bookings, utilization, pricing, and margins.

### 4. GoDaddy (GDDY)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $101.31 and a $13.27 billion market value, GoDaddy trades at about 7.4x its approximately $1.8 billion 2026 free-cash-flow target, a 13.6% equity FCF yield. Adding June net debt of $2.7 billion produces an enterprise-value-to-guided-FCF multiple of about 8.9x. That valuation leaves room for modest growth rather than requiring a heroic forecast.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue grew 7%, operating income rose 29%, operating margin expanded 450 basis points to 26.4%, and free cash flow increased 13% to $443.5 million. ARR reached $4.42 billion, Applications and Commerce revenue grew 11%, and ARPU rose 8.7%. Domains, hosting, payments, and business tools form recurring relationships with more than 20 million customers; Airo's annualized bookings run rate rose to $50 million from $10 million one quarter earlier.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Growth is modest, Core revenue rose only 3.9%, and total customers were nearly flat. The company has $3.8 billion of debt, and aggressive repurchases reduced book equity to only $6.7 million; buybacks magnify downside if the franchise weakens. Competition, domain regulation, cybersecurity, and AI-enabled site-building alternatives remain threats. The thesis needs sustained ARR and ARPU growth, stable renewal behavior, and prudent leverage.

### 5. Neurocrine Biosciences (NBIX)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $155.64, Neurocrine trades at about 22.8x trailing EPS and about 4.0x an annualized Q2 revenue run rate. That is not statistically cheap, but it is reasonable relative to current product growth, high gross economics, and a longer commercial runway than many profitable biotechnology peers. The valuation earns only medium confidence because acquisition accounting and R&D needs make current earnings an imperfect proxy for normalized cash value.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue grew 39% to $959 million. INGREZZA sales rose 15% to $716 million, CRENESSITY sales rose 247% to $184 million, and acquired VYKAT XR contributed $54 million from May 18. Management raised 2026 INGREZZA guidance to $2.825–$2.875 billion. INGREZZA patent settlements generally permit U.S. generic entry beginning in March 2038, while CRENESSITY patents run into 2035–2041, supporting a meaningful runway.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** INGREZZA still supplies most revenue. The $2.9 billion Soleno acquisition reduced cash and investments to about $482 million and created $2.23 billion of acquired intangibles, raising integration and capital-allocation risk. R&D and SG&A are rising sharply, pipeline readouts can fail, reimbursement may tighten, and generic entry can occur earlier under some settlement conditions. Watch prescription volume, launch persistence, operating cash generation, and 2027 Phase 3 data.

## Changes from earlier updates this week

The September 8 ranking was completed independently and then compared with the September 7 report.

- **Added GoDaddy at rank 4.** Fresh screening gave more weight to its 13.6% guided equity FCF yield, 29% Q2 operating-income growth, and 7% year-to-date gross share-count reduction. Leverage and customer stagnation keep confidence below the top three.
- **Added Neurocrine at rank 5.** Its 39% Q2 revenue growth, improving product diversification, and patent runway outweighed its higher multiple, but acquisition and product-concentration risks cap confidence.
- **Removed Cigna from the top five.** The earnings multiple remains low, but first-half cash conversion, PBM profit pressure, regulation, and leverage make the apparent discount harder to underwrite than today's additions.
- **Removed Mueller Industries from the top five.** Its balance sheet remains excellent, but the current multiple offers less margin for copper-price and construction-cycle normalization.

## Near misses and exclusions

- **Cigna (CI):** About 9.3x management's 2026 adjusted EPS outlook remains attractive, but PBM earnings pressure, regulation, $31.9 billion of debt, and weak first-half operating cash flow reduce the quality of the apparent discount.
- **Mueller Industries (MLI):** Strong liquidity and a 4.8x current ratio are positives, but a 30.6% increase in average copper prices boosted reported sales and the 16.3x trailing multiple is less compelling if earnings are near a cyclical high.
- **Autodesk (ADSK):** The franchise and recent selloff deserve monitoring, but roughly 28x trailing earnings does not offer as much valuation support as the selected software and services names.
- **Alphabet (GOOGL):** The moat is exceptional, but reported earnings are distorted by a large securities gain and the current AI capital-intensity makes near-term cash returns difficult to normalize.
- **Cyclical energy refiners and many banks:** Low headline multiples were not enough to overcome commodity, credit, duration, or capital-structure sensitivity without a clearer company-specific edge.

## Current conclusion

**Adobe remains the strongest current quality-value candidate**, combining double-digit recurring-revenue growth with a low-teens forward earnings multiple, though September 10 results are a near-term thesis test. **Deckers offers the cleanest balance sheet**, and **Accenture offers the most established services franchise with a high guided free-cash-flow yield**. GoDaddy has the largest explicit cash-flow discount but more leverage and moat uncertainty. Neurocrine has the strongest growth in the group but the highest product and clinical risk.

The next update should begin with another independent market ranking and only then compare against this weekly record.
