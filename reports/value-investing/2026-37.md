# Quality-Value Report - 2026 Week 37

_Last updated: September 9, 2026, before the U.S. market open. Prices are the latest available quotes at approximately 8:00 a.m. ET; most reflect premarket trading and can be volatile._

> Research support only, not personal financial advice. Multiples are screening signals, not estimates of certain intrinsic value.

## Market context

U.S. equities returned from the holiday with a risk-off session on September 8: the S&P 500 fell 0.6%, the Dow fell 1.2%, and the Nasdaq fell 0.3% as Brent crude briefly approached $99.50 and renewed inflation concerns moved to the foreground. Valuation remains selective rather than broadly cheap. Morningstar estimated its covered U.S. market at a 9% discount to fair value on August 31, but said the discount was concentrated in seven mega-cap technology companies; excluding them, its coverage was near fair value.

Today's ranking was built independently, before reviewing the September 7 and 8 selections. The latest available broad screen covered 2,286 U.S.-listed stocks and scored quality, growth, and value; financial firms were evaluated separately because operating-company metrics do not transfer cleanly. Current quotes were then applied to the strongest candidates, and finalists were checked against recent company and SEC disclosures. This is a broad-market screen followed by focused fundamental review, not a claim that all 2,286 securities received a full appraisal.

### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $257.26, Adobe trades at roughly 14.3x the midpoint of FY2026 GAAP EPS guidance of $17.90-$18.00 and 10.5x the $24.35-$24.45 non-GAAP range. The valuation is unusually low for a subscription software franchise that raised full-year revenue and non-GAAP EPS targets in June.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 13% to $6.62 billion, operating cash flow was $2.17 billion, and subscription revenue was $6.39 billion, approximately 97% of total revenue. Ending ARR reached $27.10 billion. Adobe's file standards, creative workflows, enterprise integrations, and accumulated user skill create meaningful switching costs.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Semrush contributed about $480 million of ARR, so headline growth is not wholly organic. Generative AI could commoditize parts of creation, pressure pricing, or require heavy investment before monetization catches up. CEO and CFO transitions add execution risk. September 10 results are the immediate test: weak organic ARR, poor paid conversion of AI users, or lower guidance would reduce confidence.

### 2. Deckers Brands (DECK)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $82.60, Deckers trades at about 11.1x the midpoint of raised FY2027 EPS guidance of $7.35-$7.50. It held $1.60 billion of cash and no outstanding borrowings at June 30, so enterprise value is meaningfully lower than market capitalization.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal Q1 revenue rose 5.7% to $1.02 billion. HOKA grew 7.7%, UGG grew 4.9%, direct-to-consumer sales rose 13.0%, international sales rose 8.4%, and gross margin expanded 60 basis points to 56.4%. The net-cash balance sheet provides room to invest through a softer consumer cycle.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Operating income fell 6% as SG&A increased, and HOKA plus UGG carry nearly all the economics. Fashion, product-cycle, tariff, and discretionary-demand risks are material. Q1 repurchases averaged $103.79 per share, well above today's price, highlighting capital-allocation risk. Watch full-price sell-through, inventories, brand growth, and margin.

### 3. Accenture (ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $179.03, Accenture is about 12.9x the midpoint of FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $13.78-$13.90. Guided free cash flow of $10.8-$11.5 billion implies roughly a 9.8%-10.4% yield on the current equity value.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q3 revenue rose 6% in U.S. dollars, operating margin expanded 20 basis points to 17.0%, EPS rose 9%, and free cash flow was $3.6 billion. Accenture's scale, industry knowledge, ecosystem ties, and embedded client relationships are difficult to reproduce.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Local-currency growth was only 3%, bookings fell 3% in local currency to $19.3 billion, and U.S. federal work is an estimated one-point drag on full-year growth. AI could reduce labor-heavy implementation work faster than new transformation demand replaces it. Watch organic consulting bookings, utilization, pricing, and margins.

### 4. GoDaddy (GDDY)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $92.88 and a $12.17 billion market value, GoDaddy trades at about 6.8x its approximately $1.8 billion 2026 free-cash-flow target, a 14.8% equity FCF yield. Adding June net debt of $2.7 billion produces an enterprise-value-to-guided-FCF multiple of roughly 8.3x.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue grew 7%, operating income rose 29%, operating margin expanded 450 basis points to 26.4%, and free cash flow increased 13% to $443.5 million. ARR reached $4.42 billion, Applications and Commerce revenue grew 11%, and ARPU rose 8.7%. Domains, hosting, payments, and business tools support recurring relationships with 20.5 million customers.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Core revenue grew only 3.9%, customers were nearly flat, and the company carried $3.8 billion of debt. Aggressive repurchases can magnify downside if the franchise weakens. Competition, domain regulation, cybersecurity, and AI-enabled site-building alternatives remain threats. The thesis needs sustained ARR and ARPU growth, stable renewals, and disciplined leverage.

### 5. Harmony Biosciences (HRMY)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $42.07, Harmony trades at roughly 13.7x trailing EPS. At June 30 it held $962.5 million of cash, cash equivalents, and investments against $155.0 million of debt, leaving about $807.5 million of net cash and investments, roughly one-third of its current market capitalization. That balance-sheet cushion makes the low-teens multiple more meaningful.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 WAKIX revenue rose 30% to $261.3 million, average patients increased to about 8,950, and net income rose to $75.4 million from $39.8 million. Management reiterated $1.0-$1.04 billion of 2026 WAKIX revenue. The business is profitable, cash generative, and has the resources to develop a broader rare-neurology pipeline.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** This is a concentrated pharmaceutical franchise: WAKIX is the only commercial product, and settled generic licenses generally permit entry in July 2030 if pediatric exclusivity is obtained, or earlier under specified circumstances. One ANDA case remains contested. Royalties raised Q2 cost of product sold to 24.2% of revenue from 19.0%, while pipeline and regulatory outcomes remain binary. Evidence that patient growth is stalling, adverse patent outcomes, or weak Phase 3 data would materially impair the thesis.

## Changes from earlier updates this week

The September 9 ranking was completed independently and only then compared with the prior entries.

- **Added Harmony Biosciences at rank 5.** Its 30% product-revenue growth, low-teens earnings multiple, and approximately $808 million net cash and investments produced a stronger current risk-adjusted value signal than the prior fifth-ranked name. Product concentration and the 2030 WAKIX horizon cap confidence at medium.
- **Moved Neurocrine Biosciences to near misses.** Neurocrine's growth and longer patent runway remain attractive, but its approximately 22.7x trailing multiple, acquisition integration risk, and R&D intensity offer less immediate valuation support than Harmony's balance-sheet-backed discount.
- **Retained Adobe, Deckers, Accenture, and GoDaddy.** Their quoted prices fell about 3%-8% from the September 8 snapshots, improving valuation without a new company disclosure that changed the underlying operating thesis. This is a price-driven ranking update, not a new fundamental catalyst.
- **GoDaddy's cash-flow discount widened most.** Its approximately 14.8% guided equity FCF yield is the highest explicit cash yield in the shortlist, but leverage and low customer growth still keep it below the cleaner top-three franchises.

## Near misses and exclusions

- **Neurocrine Biosciences (NBIX):** Q2 revenue growth of 39% and a long product runway remain compelling, but the approximately 22.7x trailing multiple and Soleno integration risk leave less margin of safety than today's fifth selection.
- **dLocal (DLO):** Q2 TPV rose 92%, revenue rose 56%, and adjusted FCF rose 41%, while corporate cash was $369 million. It narrowly missed because gross margin fell seven points, payment take rates are compressing, adjusted FCF definitions require care, and emerging-market regulatory and currency risks complicate normalized value.
- **Cigna (CI):** The earnings multiple remains low, but PBM profit pressure, regulation, leverage, and weak first-half cash conversion reduce the quality of the apparent discount.
- **Mueller Industries (MLI):** The balance sheet is excellent, but recent earnings benefited from higher copper prices and the multiple offers less protection if margins normalize.
- **FICO (FICO):** Its scorecard franchise and pricing power are exceptional, but roughly 27x trailing earnings is less compelling than the selected names.
- **Cyclical energy refiners and many banks:** Low headline multiples were not enough to overcome commodity, credit, duration, or capital-structure sensitivity without a clearer company-specific advantage.

## Current conclusion

**Adobe remains the strongest current quality-value candidate**, combining recurring double-digit growth with a low-teens GAAP forward multiple, though September 10 results are a near-term thesis test. **Deckers has the cleanest balance sheet**, and **Accenture offers the most established services franchise with a double-digit guided FCF yield at the low end of guidance**. GoDaddy has the widest explicit cash-flow discount but more leverage and moat uncertainty. Harmony is the most asymmetric smaller-cap idea, supported by net cash and current profitability but constrained by single-product and patent-cliff risk.

The next update should begin with another independent market ranking and only then compare against this weekly record.
