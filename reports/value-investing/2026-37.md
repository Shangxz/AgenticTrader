U.S. index futures were modestly positive early September 10, but the backdrop still argues for a real valuation cushion and resilient cash generation rather than low multiples alone.

The September 10 ranking was completed from a fresh broad-market evaluation before this weekly file was read. The process combined current whole-market quality/value screens with separate treatment for financial firms, current quotes, and focused checks of recent SEC and company disclosures. It is a broad screen followed by fundamental review, not a claim that every U.S.-listed security received a full appraisal.

### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $254.86, Adobe trades at about 14.2x the midpoint of FY2026 GAAP EPS guidance of $17.90-$18.00 and 10.4x the $24.35-$24.45 non-GAAP range. That is a modest valuation for a subscription software franchise still posting double-digit revenue growth.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 13% to $6.62 billion, subscription revenue was $6.39 billion, ending ARR reached $27.10 billion, and quarterly operating cash flow was $2.17 billion. Adobe's file standards, installed workflows, enterprise integrations, and accumulated user skill support switching costs.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Generative AI can commoditize parts of content creation, pressure pricing, and demand continued investment. Acquired Semrush ARR makes headline growth less clean, while CEO and CFO changes add execution risk. Adobe reports after the September 10 close; weak organic ARR, poor AI monetization, or lower guidance would require prompt reassessment.

### 2. Deckers Brands (DECK)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $80.23, Deckers trades at roughly 10.8x the midpoint of raised FY2027 EPS guidance of $7.35-$7.50. It had $1.60 billion of cash and no outstanding borrowings at June 30, so enterprise value is below equity value.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal Q1 revenue grew 5.7%, HOKA grew 7.7%, UGG grew 4.9%, direct-to-consumer sales rose 13.0%, international sales rose 8.4%, and gross margin expanded to 56.4%. The debt-free balance sheet gives management room to invest through a weak consumer cycle.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** HOKA and UGG carry nearly all the economics; fashion, tariffs, channel inventory, and discretionary demand can change quickly. Operating income fell 6% as expenses increased, and recent repurchases occurred above today's price. The thesis weakens if full-price sell-through, brand growth, or gross margin deteriorates.

### 3. Accenture (ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $175.80, Accenture trades at about 12.7x the midpoint of FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $13.78-$13.90. Guided FCF of $10.8-$11.5 billion implies a 10.0%-10.6% yield on the current $108.2 billion market value.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q3 revenue rose 6% in U.S. dollars, operating margin expanded 20 basis points to 17.0%, EPS rose 9%, and quarterly FCF was $3.6 billion. Scale, industry expertise, ecosystem ties, and deeply embedded delivery relationships remain meaningful advantages.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Local-currency growth was only 3%, bookings fell 3% in local currency, and book-to-bill was 1.0. AI may reduce labor-heavy implementation work faster than transformation demand replaces it. Organic bookings, utilization, pricing, and margin are the key checks.

### 4. GoDaddy (GDDY)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $93.60 and a $12.26 billion market value, GoDaddy trades at about 6.8x its approximately $1.8 billion 2026 FCF target, or a 14.7% equity FCF yield. Even after adding roughly $2.7 billion of June net debt, the implied cash-flow multiple remains low.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue grew 7%, operating income rose 29%, operating margin expanded 450 basis points to 26.4%, and FCF rose 13% to $443.5 million. ARR reached $4.42 billion, Applications and Commerce revenue grew 11%, and ARPU increased 8.7%. Domains, hosting, payments, and business tools create recurring relationships with 20.5 million customers.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Core revenue grew only 3.9%, customer count was nearly flat, and debt remains material. Aggressive buybacks amplify outcomes if the franchise weakens. Competition, cybersecurity, domain regulation, and simpler AI-native site-building tools deserve close attention.

### 5. Leidos (LDOS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $128.14, Leidos trades at about 10.4x the midpoint of raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $12.20-$12.50. Management's approximately $1.85 billion operating-cash-flow outlook is 11.5% of current market value; capital spending was only $63 million in the first half, although working-capital timing makes a one-year cash yield imperfect.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 7% to $4.56 billion, including 4% organically, and quarterly FCF was $761 million. Total backlog was $48.7 billion, trailing-twelve-month book-to-bill was 1.1, total backlog grew 5%, and funded backlog grew 44%. Mission-critical defense, intelligence, health, and infrastructure work provides better visibility than most companies at this multiple.

**Risks and disconfirming factors.** Debt rose to $6.0 billion after the Entrust acquisition, while Q2 operating margin and net income declined against unusually strong prior-year comparisons. Government budget delays, fixed-price execution, contract concentration, integration risk, and a revised backlog policy all reduce confidence. Debt paydown and organic book-to-bill should be monitored.

## Changes from earlier updates this week

Today's shortlist was selected independently, then compared with the September 9 report.

- **Leidos enters at rank 5.** Its low-teens earnings multiple, rising guidance, 1.1 book-to-bill, and sharply higher funded backlog now offer a stronger durability-adjusted value case than the prior fifth-ranked Harmony Biosciences.
- **Harmony moves to near misses.** Its growth, profitability, and net cash remain attractive, but reliance on one commercial product and generic entry generally permitted in July 2030 make the cash-flow runway more concentrated.
- **Adobe, Deckers, Accenture, and GoDaddy remain.** All four requalified in today's fresh ranking. Prices moved by roughly -1% to +1% from the September 9 snapshots, so the ordering is fundamentally unchanged rather than driven by a large quote move.
- **Adobe has an immediate event risk.** Q3 FY2026 results are due after today's close; the current high-confidence label is conditional on organic ARR, AI monetization, and guidance holding up.

## Near misses and exclusions

- **Harmony Biosciences (HRMY):** approximately 13.7x trailing earnings and about $808 million of June net cash and investments are compelling, but single-product dependence and the 2030 WAKIX horizon cap durability.
- **PayPal (PYPL):** roughly 9.9x trailing earnings, an approximately 13% screening FCF yield, net cash/investments above debt, and heavy buybacks provide value support. It misses the top five because Q2 operating margin contracted 171 basis points, EPS declined, active accounts were flat, and the business still needs to prove durable branded-checkout economics.
- **Cigna (CI):** approximately 9.1x 2026 adjusted EPS guidance is cheap, but PBM regulation, medical-cost risk, leverage, and the special cash-flow accounting of an insurer make the apparent discount harder to compare with operating companies.
- **Alphabet (GOOGL):** the headline 16.6x trailing P/E is distorted by a very large unrealized investment gain. Q2 core operations were strong, but negative quarterly FCF and $195-$205 billion of planned 2026 capital spending leave too much uncertainty around normalized owner earnings.
- **SkyWest (SKYW):** approximately 9.5x earnings and strong cash-flow screening metrics are attractive. Aircraft intensity, major-airline customer concentration, pilot availability, and cyclical travel exposure keep it below the higher-durability finalists.
- **Teradata and other extreme cash-yield names:** declining or low-growth revenue and cash improvement driven largely by margin expansion raise value-trap risk. Cyclical energy, airlines, and many banks likewise failed to clear the quality threshold despite low multiples.

## Current conclusion

**Adobe remains the strongest quality-value candidate**, but tonight's earnings are a real test rather than a formality. **Deckers has the cleanest balance sheet and lowest straightforward earnings multiple**, while **Accenture offers the best combination of a mature global franchise and double-digit guided FCF yield**. GoDaddy has the widest explicit cash-flow discount but more leverage and moat uncertainty. Leidos rounds out the list with contracted demand and improving backlog, offset by acquisition debt and government-customer risk.
