# Quality-Value Weekly Report — 2026 Week 34

## Update: 2026-08-19

> Research support only; not personal financial advice. Valuation is uncertain, and none of these companies is a guaranteed winner.

### Market context and method

Today's work began with a fresh cross-sector review of U.S.-listed stocks, independent of the August 18 selections. The discovery set included profitable large-, mid-, and smaller-cap companies and emphasized free-cash-flow yield, normalized earnings, returns on capital, recurring or resilient revenue, balance-sheet capacity, and a credible reason for the discount. Commodity producers, banks, automakers, and other cyclicals were normalized rather than ranked on headline peak-cycle multiples; highly leveraged and structurally declining businesses were penalized.

The broad market still offers a demanding hurdle: an August 12 value screen placed the S&P 500 near **19.9x forward earnings**. Today's best opportunities again cluster in durable franchises facing AI, policy, or near-term growth skepticism. Prices and market values below are approximate August 19 snapshots and will move.

#### 1. Accenture (ACN) — highest conviction

**Why it looks undervalued.** At roughly $173 and a market value near $106.5 billion, Accenture's FY26 free-cash-flow outlook of **$10.8-$11.5 billion** implies a simple equity FCF yield of about **10.1%-10.8%**. Management's GAAP EPS outlook of $13.38-$13.50 also puts the shares near 12.8x-12.9x that range, unusually low for this franchise.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q3 revenue rose 6% in U.S. dollars, operating margin expanded 20 basis points to 17.0%, EPS rose 9%, and quarterly FCF was $3.6 billion. Accenture serves about 9,000 clients and combines industry expertise, delivery scale, ecosystem relationships, and embedded managed-services work. Cash was $10.2 billion at May 31, providing meaningful resilience and acquisition capacity.

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** Local-currency growth was only 3%; new bookings fell 3% in local currency; U.S. federal work remains a drag; and AI could pressure labor-based delivery economics even as it creates consulting demand. The thesis weakens if bookings and organic growth deteriorate or if acquisitions absorb the cash discount without improving per-share value.

**Evidence:** [Accenture Q3 FY26 results](https://newsroom.accenture.com/content/3qfy26-earnings/accenture-reports-third-quarter-fiscal-2026-results.pdf), [August 19 price snapshot](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/ACN/)

#### 2. Adobe (ADBE) — exceptional economics at a disruption discount

**Why it looks undervalued.** At roughly $263, Adobe trades near **14.6x-14.7x** its raised FY26 GAAP EPS target of $17.90-$18.00. That multiple embeds a sharp loss of future pricing power even though current revenue and recurring revenue remain healthy.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 revenue was a record $6.62 billion, up 13%; subscription revenue rose 14%; operating cash flow was $2.17 billion; and AI-first ARR more than tripled to over $500 million. Creative Cloud and Acrobat remain deeply embedded in professional workflows, while high recurring revenue and margins fund continued repurchases.

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** AI-native tools may reduce retention or pricing, Semrush adds integration risk, and the CFO transition raises execution uncertainty. Reported AI usage must become durable net-new ARR rather than merely defend the installed base.

**Evidence:** [Adobe Q2 FY26 results](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/11606202/a5543arefgt.pdf), [August 19 price snapshot](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/ADBE/)

#### 3. Intuit (INTU) — premium franchise at a newly reasonable price

**Why it looks undervalued.** At about $350, Intuit trades near **14.7x** its FY26 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $23.80-$23.85, versus roughly 22.2x GAAP EPS guidance. The gap is material, so this is attractive on adjusted earnings but not a classic deep-value stock on fully expensed GAAP economics.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q3 revenue grew 10%, Global Business Solutions revenue rose 15%, Online Ecosystem revenue grew 19%, and GAAP EPS increased 11%. QuickBooks and TurboTax benefit from proprietary financial data, regulatory complexity, workflow integration, and switching costs. Intuit also held $6.8 billion of cash and investments against $6.2 billion of debt at quarter-end.

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** FY26 non-GAAP guidance excludes about $2.1 billion of stock compensation plus amortization and restructuring, so adjusted EPS can overstate owner earnings. The 17% workforce reduction, reliance on price/mix, Credit Karma cyclicality, Mailchimp execution, and AI competition all require monitoring.

**Evidence:** [Intuit Q3 FY26 results](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1312/intuit-reports-strong-third-quarter-results-and-raises-full-year-revenue-guidance), [August 19 price snapshot](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/INTU/)

#### 4. Salesforce (CRM) — strong cash yield with a capital-allocation caveat

**Why it looks undervalued.** At roughly $196 and a market value near $170.8 billion, FY26 FCF of $14.4 billion represents a simple trailing yield of about **8.4%**. This is compelling for a leader in recurring enterprise software, though stock compensation and acquisition-related economics reduce the clean owner-earnings yield.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q1 FY27 cRPO grew 14%, revenue grew 13%, GAAP operating margin reached 21.1%, and FCF was $6.6 billion. The installed base, workflow depth, and data integration create switching costs, while Agentforce ARR has passed $1 billion.

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** Informatica contributed to headline growth, and organic growth may be weaker. The $25 billion accelerated repurchase was debt-funded, increasing capital-allocation and balance-sheet risk. Agentforce monetization, dilution, and true cash growth must justify that choice.

**Evidence:** [Salesforce Q1 FY27 results](https://investor.salesforce.com/news/news-details/2026/Salesforce-Delivers-Record-First-Quarter-Fiscal-2027-Results/default.aspx), [August 19 price snapshot](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/CRM/)

#### 5. The Cigna Group (CI) — deepest earnings multiple, highest policy risk

**Why it looks undervalued.** The roughly $279 share price is only about **9.1x** management's raised 2026 adjusted EPS floor of $30.45. The discount compensates investors for substantial pharmacy-benefit-manager and reimbursement uncertainty.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 revenue rose 7% to $71.7 billion and adjusted operating income rose 6% to $2.1 billion. Cigna Healthcare adjusted pre-tax income increased 17%, while Specialty and Care Services income grew 22%. Scale across employer health plans, pharmacy services, and specialty care supports purchasing power and diversification.

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** Pharmacy Benefit Services adjusted income fell 27%, pharmacy customer relationships declined, and policy changes could structurally compress economics. Medical-cost estimation, customer losses, and meaningful debt keep this below the software and services names despite the lower multiple.

**Evidence:** [Cigna Q2 2026 results](https://newsroom.thecignagroup.com/2026-07-30-The-Cigna-Group-Reports-Strong-Second-Quarter-2026-Results%2C-Raises-2026-Outlook), [August 19 price snapshot](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/CI/)

#### 6. T-Mobile US (TMUS) — attractive cash economics with leverage

**Why it looks undervalued.** At roughly $183 and a market value near $197.8 billion, the 2026 adjusted FCF guide of **$18.4-$18.8 billion** implies a simple yield around **9.3%-9.5%**. That is attractive given its stronger growth profile versus traditional national carriers.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 service revenue grew 9%, Core Adjusted EBITDA rose 12%, adjusted FCF grew 4% to $4.8 billion, and management raised cash-flow guidance. Spectrum scale, network quality, and a growing customer base are difficult to replicate.

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** Debt and continuing spectrum/network requirements limit financial flexibility. Postpaid account additions fell year over year, competition can intensify, and UScellular integration benefits are not guaranteed.

**Evidence:** [T-Mobile Q2 2026 results](https://www.t-mobile.com/news/business/t-mobile-q2-2026-earnings), [August 19 price snapshot](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/TMUS/)

### Changes from the August 18 update

Today's ranking was completed before comparing it with the prior update.

- **Accenture enters at #1.** Its approximately 10%-11% guided FCF yield, cash-rich balance sheet, and embedded enterprise relationships produced the best overall quality-value balance in today's screen.
- **Intuit enters at #3.** The share-price compression created a much more reasonable adjusted valuation while operating growth remained double digit; the large GAAP/non-GAAP gap caps conviction.
- **Adobe remains a top candidate but moves from #1 to #2** because Accenture offers a higher current cash yield and less product-level disruption risk.
- **Salesforce, Cigna, and T-Mobile remain selected** but move from #2, #3, and #4 to #4, #5, and #6, respectively, because the two new entrants ranked higher today.
- **NXP and WTW move to the watchlist.** This is a relative-ranking change rather than evidence of sudden fundamental deterioration. NXP's cyclical exposure and roughly 4.8% trailing non-GAAP FCF yield at today's market value were less compelling than the finalists; WTW's integration and restructuring execution requirements left less valuation cushion.

### Conclusion

**Accenture and Adobe are today's strongest current candidates.** Accenture offers the best combination of current cash yield, balance-sheet resilience, and embedded client relationships; Adobe offers higher growth and equally unusual valuation, but with more direct AI disruption risk. **Intuit** is the most notable new opportunity, though investors should anchor on GAAP economics and treat adjusted EPS cautiously. **Salesforce** remains cash-generative but its debt-funded repurchase raises the monitoring burden. **Cigna** and **T-Mobile** offer the lowest earnings or cash-flow valuations in the final group, paired with policy and leverage risks.

Key disconfirmation signals are weaker Accenture bookings, deteriorating Adobe retention or AI monetization, Intuit growth that depends mainly on price or adjustments, Salesforce organic cRPO deceleration, further Cigna PBM compression, and weaker T-Mobile account economics.

#### 1. Adobe (ADBE) — highest conviction

**Why it looks undervalued.** At roughly $254, Adobe trades near **14.5x trailing earnings**, a strikingly low multiple for a subscription software franchise still growing double digits. The discount reflects the market's fear that generative AI will commoditize creative tools and weaken pricing power.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Fiscal Q2 revenue reached a record **$6.62 billion, up 13%**, subscription revenue grew 14%, operating cash flow was $2.17 billion, and AI-first ARR more than tripled year over year to over $500 million. Creative Cloud and Acrobat remain embedded in professional workflows, while recurring revenue and high margins support large repurchases; Adobe bought back about 8.5 million shares in Q2.

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** The thesis weakens if AI-native rivals materially reduce retention or pricing, if Adobe must spend away its margin advantage to defend the franchise, or if reported AI adoption fails to translate into durable net-new ARR. Recent acquisitions also introduce execution and capital-allocation risk.

**Evidence:** [Adobe Q2 FY26 results](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/11606202/a5543arefgt.pdf), [August 18 price snapshot](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/ADBE/)

#### 2. Salesforce (CRM) — highest cash-yielding software idea

**Why it looks undervalued.** At about $191 and a market value near $166 billion, FY26 free cash flow of **$14.4 billion** equates to a simple trailing FCF yield of roughly **8.7%**. That is attractive for a leader in mission-critical enterprise software, even allowing for stock compensation and acquisition risk.

**Why it remains a quality business.** FY26 revenue rose 10% to $41.5 billion, FCF rose 16%, and the company ended the year with $72.4 billion of remaining performance obligation. In Q1 FY27, cRPO grew 14%, GAAP operating margin reached 21.1%, and revenue grew 13% (partly from Informatica). The installed base, data integration, and workflow switching costs support durable recurring revenue.

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** Organic growth could be materially weaker than headline growth after acquisitions; Agentforce monetization may disappoint; stock-based compensation remains economically meaningful; and aggressive buybacks can destroy value if growth or margins fade.

**Evidence:** [Salesforce quarterly results](https://investor.salesforce.com/financials/quarterly-results/), [August 18 price snapshot](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/CRM/)

#### 3. The Cigna Group (CI) — cheapest normalized earnings

**Why it looks undervalued.** The roughly $275 share price is only about **9.0x** management's raised 2026 adjusted EPS floor of $30.45 and about 11.4x trailing GAAP earnings. The valuation prices in substantial policy and reimbursement risk.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 revenue grew 7% to $71.7 billion and adjusted operating income grew 6% to $2.1 billion. Cigna Healthcare's adjusted pre-tax income rose 17%, while Specialty and Care Services income rose 22%. Scale across employer health plans, pharmacy services, specialty care, and data creates purchasing power and diversified earnings streams.

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** Pharmacy-benefit-manager regulation, rebate reform, drug-pricing changes, medical-cost underestimation, and customer losses can impair economics. Pharmacy customer relationships declined, and Pharmacy Benefit Services adjusted income fell 27% in Q2; these are real warnings, not cosmetic volatility. Debt is also meaningful.

**Evidence:** [Cigna Q2 2026 results](https://newsroom.thecignagroup.com/2026-07-30-The-Cigna-Group-Reports-Strong-Second-Quarter-2026-Results%2C-Raises-2026-Outlook), [August 18 price snapshot](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/CI/)

#### 4. T-Mobile US (TMUS) — superior telecom economics at a reasonable price

**Why it looks undervalued.** At roughly $180 and a market value near $195 billion, the 2026 adjusted FCF guide of **$18.4-$18.8 billion** implies a simple equity FCF yield around **9.4%-9.6%**. The stock also screened near 14.3x forward earnings, below the broad market despite better growth than traditional carriers.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 service revenue grew 9%, Core Adjusted EBITDA grew 12%, and adjusted FCF grew 4% to $4.8 billion. Postpaid ARPA rose 2%, network tests continued to support the company's differentiation claims, and management raised cash-flow guidance. Scale and spectrum assets are difficult to replicate.

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** T-Mobile carries substantial debt and must keep investing heavily in spectrum and network assets. Postpaid account additions fell 13% year over year, competition can intensify, and the UScellular integration may cost more or deliver less than expected. Deutsche Telekom's controlling interest also limits minority-holder influence.

**Evidence:** [T-Mobile Q2 2026 results](https://www.t-mobile.com/news/business/t-mobile-q2-2026-earnings), [August 18 price snapshot](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/TMUS/)

#### 5. NXP Semiconductors (NXPI) — quality cyclical at a fair discount

**Why it looks undervalued.** NXP screened at about **14.1x forward earnings**, versus roughly 19.9x for the S&P 500. That multiple does not require heroic growth, although investors must normalize semiconductor-cycle earnings rather than extrapolate one quarter.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 revenue increased 19% to $3.50 billion, GAAP gross margin was 57.3%, and non-GAAP FCF was $791 million, or 22.6% of revenue. Automotive revenue grew 9%, and the company has differentiated positions in software-defined vehicles, industrial edge computing, secure connectivity, and identification. NXP also repaid $750 million of notes at maturity.

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** Automotive and industrial inventory cycles can reverse quickly; China exposure, tariffs/export controls, customer concentration, and capital intensity add volatility. Non-GAAP FCF should not be treated as identical to GAAP cash economics, and acquisition execution remains a watch item.

**Evidence:** [NXP Q2 2026 results](https://investors.nxp.com/news-releases/news-release-details/nxp-semiconductors-reports-second-quarter-2026-results/), [August 18 price snapshot](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/NXPI/)

#### 6. WTW (WTW) — durable brokerage economics with execution upside

**Why it looks undervalued.** WTW screened near **16.3x forward earnings**, below the broad market and below richer peer valuations common in insurance brokerage. The discount leaves room for value creation if margin and cash conversion continue to improve.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 revenue grew 9%, organic revenue grew 5%, adjusted EPS rose 17%, and adjusted operating margin expanded 100 basis points to 19.5%. First-half FCF rose to $360 million from $217 million. Risk broking and benefits consulting are relationship-driven, recurring, capital-light services, and management increased repurchase authority by $1.5 billion.

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** GAAP EPS fell 27% in Q2, leverage increased after acquisitions, and the Newfront integration plus the new Propel efficiency plan create execution risk. Management's 2028 margin target requires substantial restructuring spend and should be treated as an aspiration, not a fact.

**Evidence:** [WTW Q2 2026 results](https://investors.wtwco.com/news-releases/news-release-details/wtw-reports-second-quarter-2026-earnings), [August 18 price snapshot](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/WTW/)

### Conclusion

**Adobe and Salesforce are the strongest current candidates**: both combine deeply embedded recurring-revenue franchises with valuations that imply much weaker economics than their latest results show. **Cigna** offers the widest earnings-multiple discount, but its regulatory and PBM risks make the required monitoring burden higher. **T-Mobile** has the best telecom cash-growth profile but meaningful leverage. **NXP** and **WTW** round out the list as high-quality franchises whose cyclicality or execution requirements justify somewhat lower conviction.

The clearest disconfirmation signals for the next update are weakening Adobe retention/AI monetization, Salesforce organic cRPO growth below headline growth, further Cigna PBM profit compression, slowing T-Mobile account economics, a renewed NXP inventory correction, or WTW failing to convert margin improvement into cash.
