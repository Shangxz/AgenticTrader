## How the October 7 screen was run

Today's ranking was completed independently before reading the October 5-6 entries below.

The first pass covered the current 5,614-stock U.S.-listed universe, then cross-checked a 930-company value subset requiring positive free cash flow and price/free cash flow below 25x. Candidates were ranked qualitatively on valuation (earnings and FCF yields, balance sheet, and comparison with their own business economics) and quality (cash conversion, recurring revenue, margins/returns, competitive position, and resilience). The final step reviewed recent company filings and earnings releases and penalized leverage, cyclicality, accounting noise, and plausible structural disruption. Screen references: [Stock Analysis U.S. screener](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/screener/), [MoatMint value screen](https://moatmint.com/stocks/value), and [MoatMap quality/value methodology](https://moatmap.ai/best-stocks/usa).

Prices and market-based multiples below use the October 6 close. Forward multiples are third-party estimates unless explicitly tied to company guidance. They are screening signals, not intrinsic-value conclusions.

### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Adobe's 13.3x trailing P/E and 8.8x price/FCF imply unusually high yields for a subscription software franchise. The shares outstanding fell 6.2% year over year, adding a meaningful per-share tailwind. Current valuation data: [Stock Analysis statistics](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/adbe/statistics/).

**Why it remains a quality business.** Fiscal Q3 revenue grew 13% to $6.76 billion, ARR reached $27.50 billion, GAAP operating income was $2.35 billion, and operating cash flow was $2.52 billion. Subscription revenue rose 14%, while AI-first ARR grew more than 150% from a smaller base. Those results suggest that AI has not yet broken the economics of Adobe's installed creative and document workflows. Source: [Adobe Q3 FY2026 release filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000147/adbeex991q326.htm).

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** Generative AI could commoditize parts of content creation, pressure pricing, or move value to new platforms. Stock compensation, the Semrush acquisition, AI infrastructure costs, and the CEO transition complicate owner-earnings analysis. The thesis would weaken if ARR growth falls to low single digits or cash generation stops translating into lower share count.

### 2. Accenture (ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The shares trade at 14.3x FY2026 GAAP earnings and 9.9x FY2026 FCF, versus materially higher valuation levels in recent years. Accenture's $11.6 billion of FY2026 FCF equates to roughly a 10% yield on the current market value. Current ratios: [Stock Analysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/acn/financials/ratios/).

**Why it remains a quality business.** FY2026 revenue grew 6% to $74.2 billion, GAAP EPS rose 12%, and full-year bookings increased to $84.5 billion with a 1.1 book-to-bill ratio. Managed-services bookings carried a 1.2 book-to-bill, supporting revenue visibility, while the balance sheet held $12.8 billion of cash. The breadth of industry relationships and ability to execute large transformations remain durable advantages. Source: [Accenture FY2026 results filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1467373/000146737326000037/q4fy26earnings8-kexhibit.htm).

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** FY2027 revenue growth guidance is only 3%-6% in local currency. AI could reduce labor intensity faster than Accenture replaces it with higher-value work, and federal/public-sector weakness may persist. The valuation is attractive partly because the market doubts that historical headcount-based economics survive; bookings and revenue per employee must validate the reinvention thesis.

### 3. Booking Holdings (BKNG)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Booking trades at 17.5x trailing earnings, 14.2x forward earnings, and 12.4x trailing FCF after a roughly one-third decline in market value over the past year. That is a reasonable price for a global, asset-light marketplace that is still growing. Current ratios: [Stock Analysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/bkng/financials/ratios/).

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 gross bookings rose 9%, revenue rose 8%, and adjusted EPS rose 15%. Adjusted EBITDA margin reached 36.0%, and operating expenses grew more slowly than revenue. The direct channel remains in the mid-50% range of room nights, and $3.7 billion of Q2 repurchases show substantial cash-return capacity. Source: [Booking Q2 2026 release filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1075531/000107553126000036/q2-26bkngearningsrelease.htm).

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** Travel is cyclical and exposed to war, oil prices, currencies, and consumer confidence. AI assistants and Google could alter customer acquisition, while marketing already absorbs 4.7% of gross bookings. Negative book equity, debt, and aggressive buybacks reduce balance-sheet flexibility. The thesis requires direct traffic and accommodation supply to remain defensible.

### 4. Salesforce (CRM)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Salesforce trades at 12.2x trailing FCF and 20.9x trailing GAAP earnings. TTM FCF was $15.15 billion, and the share count fell 7.4% year over year, giving the current price a credible cash-yield and capital-return case rather than merely a non-GAAP earnings story. Current financials: [Stock Analysis statistics](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/crm/statistics/).

**Why it remains a quality business.** Fiscal Q2 revenue rose 11%, subscription and support revenue rose 12%, current RPO grew 14% in constant currency, and total RPO reached $66.3 billion. GAAP operating margin was 20.5%; the installed CRM/data workflow base and contract backlog support recurring economics. Source: [Salesforce Q2 FY2027 results](https://investor.salesforce.com/financials/quarterly-results/).

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** About four points of Q2 revenue growth came from Informatica, so organic growth is slower than the headline. Acquisition financing lifted net debt to about $31 billion; stock compensation and repeated large deals can dilute the apparent FCF yield. Agentic AI may either deepen the platform moat or reduce seat-based pricing. The thesis needs sustained cRPO growth and deleveraging.

### 5. UnitedHealth Group (UNH)

**Why it looks undervalued.** UNH trades at about 17.7x forward earnings and 14.3x trailing FCF, below its 2024 valuation while the company works through a margin reset. That is not deep value, so the idea ranks fifth, but the price offers potential upside if earnings normalize without another major reserve or policy shock. Current valuation: [Stock Analysis statistics](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/unh/statistics/).

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 revenue was $112.0 billion, earnings from operations were $8.0 billion, and operating cash flow was $11.1 billion, or 1.9x net income. Management raised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $19.50-$20.00. Scale across UnitedHealthcare and Optum, data, provider relationships, and breadth of services remain significant competitive assets. Source: [UnitedHealth Q2 2026 results](https://www.unitedhealthgroup.com/newsroom/2026/2026-07-16-uhg-reports-second-quarter-2026-results.html).

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** Medical-cost trends, Medicare Advantage reimbursement, PBM regulation, litigation, cyber risk, and integration complexity can overwhelm scale advantages. Debt-to-capital was 41.2%, and historical cash flow includes working-capital timing. Q3 results are due October 13, making this the list's highest near-term event risk. A failure to confirm margin recovery would invalidate the rerating case.

## Near misses and traps to watch

- **Deckers (DECK), GoDaddy (GDDY), and Intuit (INTU):** All remain credible quality-value candidates from October 6. They missed today's top five only on relative ranking, not because of a new adverse fact.
- **Arch Capital (ACGL):** Strong underwriting and a low P/E remain attractive, but softening pricing and reserve uncertainty make normalized earnings harder to estimate than today's top choices.
- **CF Industries (CF):** The cash yield is high, but current earnings depend heavily on nitrogen pricing and energy inputs; it remains the most cyclical prior selection.
- **Alphabet (GOOGL):** A 17.5x headline P/E appears cheap, but Q2 earnings included a very large investment gain and Q2 capex exceeded operating cash flow. The clean operating valuation is less compelling than the headline multiple.
- **CVS Health (CVS):** Raised 2026 guidance and strong cash generation are encouraging, but medical-cost volatility, leverage, and complex segment economics keep it below UNH on quality.
- **Qualcomm (QCOM):** Automotive and IoT diversification is real, but handset/customer concentration and weaker near-term margins leave less margin of safety at roughly 21x trailing earnings.

## Conclusion

**Adobe and Accenture are today's clearest quality-value candidates.** Both offer roughly double-digit FCF yields with established customer workflows and solid current operating evidence. **Booking Holdings** follows closely, with a strong marketplace and capital returns at a compressed multiple. **Salesforce** has a persuasive cash-yield and backlog case, but acquisition leverage lowers confidence. **UnitedHealth** offers recovery upside at a reasonable rather than exceptional price and requires the most caution.

The key disconfirming checks are Adobe's ARR and owner-earnings durability through the AI transition, Accenture's bookings conversion and revenue per employee, Booking's direct-channel mix and marketing efficiency, Salesforce's organic growth and deleveraging, and UnitedHealth's medical-cost trend at the October 13 report.
