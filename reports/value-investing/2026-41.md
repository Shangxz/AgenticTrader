# Quality-Value Report - 2026-W41

_Last updated: 2026-10-06, before the U.S. regular session_

> Research support only, not personal financial advice. Prices and valuation multiples are snapshots, estimates can change, and none of the companies below is a certain bargain.

## Market context

The valuation backdrop remains demanding. The S&P 500 closed October 5 at 7,773.95, within 0.3% of its record, while the 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.31%. A near-record equity market and a high risk-free rate raise the required return from any "cheap" stock, so today's ranking emphasizes current cash generation, defensible economics, and balance-sheet resilience over low multiples alone. Source: [AP market recap, October 5](https://apnews.com/article/7f89624b604f25f313502c77d4d0b010).

## How the October 6 screen was run

Today's assessment began from a fresh ranking and did not use the October 5 selections. The market-wide pass combined:

- a 2,226-name U.S.-listed quality, growth, and value universe, scored October 5;
- a 57-name deep-value screen requiring P/E of 12 or less and free-cash-flow yield of at least 6%; and
- a 100-name cash-flow screen limited to companies with positive free cash flow in at least seven of the last ten years.

The quantitative pass was followed by company-level review of the latest earnings releases, guidance, balance sheets, and material disconfirming evidence. Screen sources: [Bull Rankings full universe](https://bullrankings.com/screener), [The Stocks School deep-value screen](https://www.thestocksschool.com/ideas/deep-value), and [Intrinsic Terminal long-history FCF screen](https://intrinsicterminal.com/stocks/fcf-yield).

Prices are the October 6 pre-market or latest available snapshot. Forward multiples are simple price-to-company-guidance calculations, not consensus estimates. FCF yields use current market capitalization and company-reported or guided FCF and should not be treated as normalized intrinsic-value estimates.

### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $238.79, Adobe trades at about 13.2 times management's raised FY2026 GAAP EPS target of $18.12-$18.17. That is a modest multiple for a subscription franchise still producing double-digit top-line growth.

**Why it is still a quality business.** Q3 revenue grew 13% to $6.76 billion, subscription revenue grew 14%, GAAP operating income was $2.35 billion, and operating cash flow reached a record $2.52 billion. ARR ended the quarter at $27.5 billion. The scale of Adobe's installed base and its integration into creative and document workflows remain real advantages; AI-first ARR growth above 150% is encouraging, although still off a smaller base. Source: [Adobe Q3 FY2026 release](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/01906202/au56y4ter.pdf).

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Generative AI lowers content-creation costs and may shift value to new tools or platforms. Adobe must sustain ARR growth while absorbing AI infrastructure costs. Stock-based compensation remains meaningful, and the December CEO transition adds execution risk. The thesis weakens materially if ARR growth falls to low single digits without a commensurate expansion in owner earnings.

### 2. Deckers Outdoor (DECK)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The $80.21 share price is about 10.8 times the midpoint of FY2027 EPS guidance of $7.35-$7.50. June cash of $1.60 billion equals roughly 14% of the current $11.1 billion market value, and the company reported no outstanding borrowings.

**Why it is still a quality business.** Fiscal Q1 revenue grew 5.7% to $1.02 billion. HOKA sales rose 7.7%, UGG rose 4.9%, DTC sales grew 13%, and gross margin expanded to 56.4%. Management still expects HOKA to grow at a low-double-digit rate for FY2027, an operating margin slightly above 21.5%, and it repurchased $338 million of shares during the quarter. Source: [Deckers fiscal Q1 2027 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910521/000091052126000015/deckex991pressrelease-6302.htm).

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Operating income fell 6% in Q1 as SG&A grew 12.7%, so the valuation is partly pricing a genuine deceleration. HOKA and UGG create brand concentration, footwear demand is discretionary, and tariffs or sourcing changes can pressure margins. Buybacks only create value if brand relevance and full-price sell-through persist.

### 3. GoDaddy (GDDY)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Management's approximately $1.8 billion FY2026 FCF target implies a roughly 13.8% yield on the current $13.1 billion market value. The shares also trade around 14.8 times trailing GAAP earnings despite improving margins and a shrinking share count.

**Why it is still a quality business.** Q2 revenue grew 7%, operating income rose 29%, and GAAP operating margin expanded 450 basis points to 26.4%. FCF rose 13% to $443.5 million; annualized recurring revenue reached $4.42 billion. Domains, hosting, and commerce tools are recurring and embedded in small-business operations. Through July 29, buybacks reduced fully diluted shares by 7% from year-end. Source: [GoDaddy Q2 2026 release filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1609711/000160971126000087/gddyex991-20260630xq2earni.htm).

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Customer count was almost flat, Core revenue grew only 4%, and management expects just 6% full-year revenue growth. Net debt was $2.66 billion at June 30. The FCF measure adds back certain restructuring and other payments, so it is somewhat more generous than plain operating cash flow less capex. The thesis depends on ARPU and Applications & Commerce growth offsetting slow customer growth.

### 4. Intuit (INTU)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $284.68, Intuit trades at about 14.1 times the midpoint of FY2027 GAAP EPS guidance of $20.12-$20.36. The multiple remains low for a business guiding 9%-10% revenue growth and 22%-24% GAAP EPS growth.

**Why it is still a quality business.** FY2026 revenue grew 14% to more than $21 billion. For FY2027, management guides Global Business Solutions growth of 13%-14%, Credit Karma growth of 11%-13%, and GAAP operating income growth of 26%-27%. Tax and accounting workflows are recurring, data-rich, and costly for customers to replace. Buybacks more than offset stock compensation in FY2026, reducing weighted-average diluted shares by 2%. Source: [Intuit FY2026 results and FY2027 guidance](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1320/intuit-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-fiscal-2026-results-sets-fiscal-2027-guidance).

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** TurboTax growth is guided to only 2%-3% and Mailchimp to flat or slightly down. Cash and investments of $7.2 billion were slightly below total debt of $7.7 billion. Credit Karma adds credit-cycle sensitivity, while AI-native accounting tools could lower switching costs over time. The thesis requires Global Business Solutions to remain a durable double-digit grower.

### 5. Arch Capital Group (ACGL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $94.36, Arch trades around 7.4 times trailing earnings and 1.39 times June book value of $68.04 per share. That is attractive if the company can earn even a mid-teens return on equity through the insurance cycle.

**Why it is still a quality business.** Q2 annualized operating ROE was 15.3%, the consolidated combined ratio excluding catastrophe activity and prior-year development was 82.5%, and book value per share rose 2.8% sequentially. The business is diversified across specialty insurance, reinsurance, and mortgage insurance. Arch also repurchased $1.2 billion of stock in the quarter. Source: [Arch Capital Q2 2026 results](https://ir.archgroup.com/news/news-details/2026/Arch-Capital-Group-Ltd--Reports-2026-Second-Quarter-Results/default.aspx).

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Net premiums written fell 6.9% and underwriting income fell 19.7% year over year as market conditions softened and catastrophe losses rose. Reserve estimates can prove wrong years later, and book value is exposed to both underwriting and investment-market shocks. The low P/E should not be capitalized as if peak insurance earnings are permanent.

### 6. CF Industries (CF)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The $116.02 share price is about 8.6 times trailing earnings. TTM FCF of $1.82 billion equates to roughly a 10.2% yield on the current $17.9 billion market value, while the quarterly dividend was raised 20%.

**Why it is still a quality business.** CF operated at 98% of available ammonia capacity in the first half, earned $1.34 billion, and generated $2.18 billion of adjusted EBITDA. Its large North American plants benefit structurally when regional natural-gas feedstock costs remain below marginal global production economics. Management has continued repurchases while funding the Blue Point low-carbon ammonia project. Source: [CF Industries first-half 2026 results](https://investor.cfindustries.com/Investors/news/news-details/2026/CF-Industries-Holdings-Inc--Reports-First-Half-2026-Net-Earnings-of-1-34-Billion-Adjusted-EBITDA-of-2-18-Billion/default.aspx).

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Nitrogen is a commodity business. Current earnings benefit from constrained global supply and geopolitical disruption, while first-half natural-gas costs rose. New capacity, lower crop economics, weaker fertilizer demand, or higher U.S. gas prices could reduce normalized FCF sharply. Blue Point also adds construction and capital-allocation risk. This is the most cyclical top-six idea and therefore carries the lowest confidence.

## Near misses and value traps to watch

- **H&R Block (HRB):** At $42, the shares are only about 6.8 times the midpoint of FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance, and FY2026 revenue grew 4.9% while adjusted EPS grew 13.9%. It narrowly misses the top six because tax preparation is seasonal, assisted filing faces long-run digital and policy pressure, and buybacks are doing significant work in per-share growth. [FY2026 results](https://investors.hrblock.com/news-releases/news-release-details/hr-block-reports-fiscal-2026-results-and-enters-fiscal-2027).
- **Fiserv (FISV):** A roughly 7.4 times trailing P/E and high screen-reported FCF yield look compelling, but Q2 organic revenue fell 5%, adjusted EPS fell 26%, and first-half FCF fell 12%. Until the One Fiserv program demonstrates stabilization, the low multiple is more warning than opportunity. [Q2 2026 results](https://investors.fiserv.com/news-releases/news-release-details/fiserv-reports-second-quarter-2026-results).
- **Accenture (ACN):** Still inexpensive near 15.6 times trailing earnings with excellent client relationships and bookings, but the fresh screen gives better price-to-growth and cash-yield combinations elsewhere. It remains a high-quality watchlist name rather than a rejected thesis.
- **Comcast (CMCSA):** The very low multiple and cash generation are offset by declining residential economics, capital intensity, and separation complexity. It remains a possible sum-of-the-parts situation, but not a clean quality-value compounder.

## Conclusion

**Adobe, Deckers, and GoDaddy are the strongest current candidates.** Adobe offers the best franchise-quality and valuation combination; Deckers offers the widest balance-sheet margin of safety; GoDaddy offers the highest guided cash yield among the top three. **Intuit** remains a high-quality bargain but ranks lower today because parts of its portfolio are slowing. **Arch Capital** is the best financial-sector candidate, while **CF Industries** offers the most raw cash yield and the most cyclical risk.

The most important checks before committing capital are Adobe's ARR durability through the AI transition, Deckers' brand momentum and expense discipline, GoDaddy's customer growth and definition of FCF, Arch's reserve development as pricing softens, and CF's earnings power under normalized nitrogen prices.
