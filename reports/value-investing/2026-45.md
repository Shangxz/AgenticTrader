## Market context

Today's list was selected from a fresh ranking before reviewing the November 2-5 entries. The primary universe screen covered 3,531 U.S.-listed companies above $200 million in market value, supplemented by a 501-company S&P 500 value-and-quality screen and current prices after the November 5 selloff. I ranked valuation against cash generation, returns on capital or underwriting quality, balance-sheet risk, competitive position, and the durability of current earnings; cyclical peak earnings and one-time benefits were penalized.

The opportunity set widened sharply. On November 5 the S&P 500 fell 4.4%, the Nasdaq fell 5.5%, and the 10-year Treasury yield declined to 4.84%. The selloff improved entry prices, but the catalyst was concern about AI capital spending and financing conditions, so lower prices do not automatically mean lower fundamental risk. Today's list therefore favors established cash generators and businesses with visible demand or recurring revenue.

Prices and market multiples below are snapshots from approximately 8:00 a.m. Eastern on November 6 and can move materially.

## Today's top ideas - November 6

### 1. Adobe (ADBE) - High confidence

**Valuation signal:** Approximately $246.44, $97.5 billion market value, and 13.8x trailing GAAP earnings. The S&P 500 screen assigns Adobe a 91 value score and 99 quality score, with an estimated 10.9% trailing free-cash-flow yield.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The price implies a rapid erosion of Adobe's franchise, yet the latest operating evidence still shows double-digit recurring growth and exceptional cash generation. The market is appropriately discounting generative-AI disruption, but the current multiple appears to price in a much faster deterioration than reported results show.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Fiscal Q3 revenue rose 13% to $6.76 billion, subscription revenue rose 14%, ARR reached $27.50 billion, and operating cash flow was a record $2.52 billion. Nine-month operating cash flow rose 11% to $7.65 billion. Adobe's embedded professional workflows, file formats, enterprise relationships, and large recurring-revenue base remain meaningful advantages.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Generative AI could reduce switching costs, weaken pricing, or move value toward model and distribution platforms. Stock compensation and repurchase intensity matter. Sustained ARR deceleration, margin erosion, weaker cash conversion, or evidence that AI products cannibalize more revenue than they create would undermine the thesis.

Sources: [Adobe Q3 FY2026 release](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/01906202/au56y4ter.pdf), [Adobe Q3 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000156/adbe-20260828.htm)

### 2. Deckers Brands (DECK) - Medium-high confidence

**Valuation signal:** Approximately $87.74, $11.9 billion market value, and 11.2x trailing earnings. Against management's FY2027 EPS range of $7.80-$7.95, the price is about 11.1x; after subtracting the approximately $0.52 tariff-refund benefit, it is roughly 11.9x normalized guidance. The S&P 500 screen gives Deckers a 99 value score and 98 quality score.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The valuation now assumes that HOKA and UGG are close to maturity or face a lasting demand problem. Growth has slowed, but an approximately 12x normalized multiple is modest for a debt-free branded consumer company with high gross margins and two global franchises.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Fiscal Q2 sales rose 3.5% to a record $1.48 billion; HOKA grew 6.8%, UGG grew 3.5%, and international sales grew 9.0%. Even excluding the one-time tariff refund, the business retains unusually strong profitability. Deckers held $1.21 billion in cash, had no borrowings, and repurchased $637.5 million of shares in the fiscal first half.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Direct-to-consumer sales fell 1.8% and comparable DTC sales fell 6.4%; domestic sales also declined slightly. Fashion and footwear demand can reverse quickly, and HOKA and UGG concentration is high. Continued DTC weakness, inventory growth above sales, promotional activity, or brand-level revenue contraction would make the low multiple more consistent with a value trap.

Sources: [Deckers Q2 FY2027 results filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910521/000091052126000028/deckex991pressrelease-9302.htm), [current valuation screen](https://www.siriusinvestors.com/stock/screener/value-stocks)

### 3. Progressive (PGR) - High confidence

**Valuation signal:** Approximately $233.18, $135.8 billion market value, and 11.7x trailing earnings.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The multiple is modest for an insurer that is still growing policies and premiums while delivering excellent underwriting results. Some profit normalization is likely, but the price leaves a reasonable margin if Progressive continues to compound policies without sacrificing underwriting discipline.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Third-quarter net premiums earned rose 5%, total policies in force rose 6%, and the quarterly combined ratio improved to 88.7 from 89.5. September's combined ratio was 90.0 versus 100.4 a year earlier. Progressive's pricing data, direct distribution, scale, and long underwriting record remain durable advantages.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Current underwriting margins may be above through-cycle levels. Catastrophe losses, reserve development, repair-cost inflation, regulatory constraints, or aggressive competitor pricing can reduce earnings quickly. A sustained combined ratio above the mid-90s without compensating policy growth would weaken the case.

Sources: [Progressive September and Q3 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/80661/000008066126000367/pgr202609ex99earningsrelea.htm), [current price reference](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/PGR/)

### 4. Leidos (LDOS) - Medium-high confidence

**Valuation signal:** Approximately $135.81, $16.6 billion market value, and 12.1x trailing GAAP earnings. The price is about 10.8x the midpoint of management's FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $12.50-$12.70. The S&P 500 screen estimates an 11.9% trailing free-cash-flow yield and gives Leidos an 84 quality score.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The valuation is low for a mission-critical technology and government-services contractor with improving margins, a large backlog, and raised earnings and cash-flow guidance. The market appears to discount federal budget risk and the company's $6.0 billion debt more heavily than current execution warrants.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Q3 revenue rose 4% to $4.63 billion, GAAP EPS rose 16%, adjusted EPS rose 19%, and adjusted EBITDA margin increased to 13.9%. Quarterly free cash flow was $476 million. Total backlog reached $49.6 billion, including $11.1 billion funded, and management raised EPS and operating-cash-flow guidance for the third consecutive quarter.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Revenue depends heavily on U.S. government priorities, appropriations, and contract awards. Debt is meaningful, some growth came from acquisition, and quarterly free cash flow fell from the prior-year period. Budget cuts, contract losses, weaker organic growth, integration problems, or poor cash conversion would narrow the margin of safety.

Sources: [Leidos Q3 2026 results and guidance](https://leidos.gcs-web.com/node/36321/html), [current valuation screen](https://www.siriusinvestors.com/stock/screener/value-stocks)

### 5. The Cigna Group (CI) - Medium-high confidence

**Valuation signal:** Approximately $301.48, $79.4 billion market value, 13.3x trailing GAAP earnings, and about 9.9x management's raised 2026 adjusted EPS floor of $30.60. The S&P 500 screen estimates an 11.7% free-cash-flow yield.

**Why it looks undervalued:** Post-earnings evidence now supports a low adjusted multiple rather than requiring an untested forecast. Revenue is growing, medical membership increased, leverage declined, and guidance rose. The valuation still reflects PBM regulation, customer attrition, and medical-cost uncertainty.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Q3 revenue rose 6% to $73.7 billion, adjusted EPS increased to $7.98 from $7.83, and Cigna Healthcare adjusted operating income rose 15%. Medical customers grew 2% from year-end, the debt-to-capitalization ratio improved to 41.8% from 44.9% a year earlier, and management raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance to at least $30.60.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Total customer relationships fell 6% from year-end, pharmacy customers fell 5%, and Pharmacy Benefit Services operating income fell 22%. PBM reform, client losses, drug-pricing changes, medical-cost inflation, reserve error, and leverage remain material. Continued customer attrition or deterioration in Evernorth profit would reduce confidence.

Sources: [Cigna Q3 2026 results and raised outlook](https://newsroom.thecignagroup.com/2026-11-05-The-Cigna-Group-Reports-Strong-Third-Quarter-2026-Results,-Raises-2026-Outlook), [current valuation screen](https://www.siriusinvestors.com/stock/screener/value-stocks)

### 6. Comcast (CMCSA) - Medium confidence

**Valuation signal:** Approximately $24.95, $88.6 billion market value, and 7.8x trailing earnings. The S&P 500 screen estimates a 20.3% trailing free-cash-flow yield, the highest cash-yield signal among today's finalists.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The stock discounts prolonged broadband erosion and difficult separation work. A single-digit earnings multiple and very high trailing cash yield provide room for weak growth if the remaining connectivity, wireless, business-services, media, and parks assets continue to produce substantial cash.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Q3 generated $4.21 billion of free cash flow. Wireless lines reached 10.6 million, business-services revenue grew 2.7% with a 55.9% EBITDA margin, and Peacock delivered a second consecutive profitable quarter with $261 million of EBITDA and 17% subscriber growth. Local network scale and bundled customer relationships remain valuable.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Q3 adjusted EBITDA fell 5.8% and free cash flow fell 14.8%; residential connectivity is under structural pressure from fiber and fixed wireless. Separation costs, debt, capital intensity, and cyclical parks and studios add risk. Accelerating broadband losses or continued double-digit cash-flow decline would outweigh the low headline multiple.

Sources: [Comcast Q3 2026 results](https://www.cmcsa.com/static-files/14d5a2cc-cfdb-41f5-8b41-85fb055bc695), [current valuation screen](https://www.siriusinvestors.com/stock/screener/value-stocks)

## Changes from the November 5 update

- **Adobe remains number one.** It was selected independently again because double-digit recurring growth, high margins, and strong cash conversion remain unusually attractive at roughly 14x GAAP earnings.
- **Deckers, Leidos, and Cigna enter today's top group.** Deckers offers a debt-free balance sheet and two premium brands at about 12x normalized guidance; Leidos pairs a roughly 11x adjusted multiple with raised guidance and a $49.6 billion backlog; Cigna's November 5 results validated the low adjusted multiple and improved balance-sheet trend.
- **Progressive and Comcast remain.** Progressive retains the best underwriting-quality signal, while Comcast remains the strongest cash-yield opportunity despite greater value-trap risk.
- **CVS Health and Qualcomm move to the watchlist.** CVS remains inexpensive but its planned separation raises execution and debt-allocation uncertainty. Qualcomm's lower post-results price is more interesting, but fiscal Q4 revenue and adjusted EPS declines leave less current operating confirmation than today's finalists.
- These are ranking changes from a fresh daily screen, not sell recommendations.

## Conclusion

**Strongest overall candidate:** Adobe combines recurring double-digit growth, high margins, and strong cash conversion with a valuation that assumes substantial franchise erosion.

**Best balance-sheet-backed contrarian:** Deckers offers two scaled premium brands, no borrowings, and substantial repurchases at approximately 12x normalized guidance, but slowing DTC demand needs close monitoring.

**Best financial compounder at a value price:** Progressive pairs excellent underwriting and policy growth with a roughly 12x earnings valuation, though current margins should not be treated as permanent.

**Best visibility at a low multiple:** Leidos has raised guidance, a large backlog, and strong cash conversion at about 11x adjusted earnings, offset by government-budget and leverage risk.

**Best post-earnings validation:** Cigna's raised outlook and improving leverage support the value thesis, while customer attrition and PBM regulation cap confidence.

**Highest cash yield, highest value-trap risk:** Comcast is exceptionally cheap, but broadband decline and falling free cash flow require a larger margin of safety.

This shortlist is research support, not a claim of precise intrinsic value, a guarantee of returns, or personal financial advice.
