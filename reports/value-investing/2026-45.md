## Market context

Today's selection was built from a fresh market-wide ranking, without reference to the November 2-4 picks until after the ranking was complete. The primary screen covered 3,531 U.S.-listed companies above $200 million in market value using prices through November 4. It found 207 companies above $10 billion trading below 15x trailing earnings and 311 companies above $2 billion with trailing free-cash-flow yield above 8%. I cross-checked valuation against profitability, cash conversion, leverage, recurring revenue or competitive position, then penalized cyclically inflated earnings and balance-sheet risk.

The opportunity set remains selective. The S&P 500 forward P/E was recently about 18.5x while the 10-year Treasury yield was near 5%, so a low multiple is not enough by itself. Many of the screen's cheapest results were commodity, shipping, retail, or leveraged turnaround businesses. Today's shortlist favors companies where current operating evidence supports the quality side of the thesis.

Prices and market multiples below are snapshots from approximately 8:00 a.m. Eastern on November 5 and can move materially.

## Today's top ideas - November 5

### 1. Adobe (ADBE) - High confidence

**Valuation signal:** $248.48, $98.3 billion market value, and about 13.9x trailing GAAP earnings. Against Adobe's FY2026 GAAP EPS target of $18.12-$18.17, the price is also about 13.7x.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The valuation implies material franchise erosion, but current results still show double-digit growth, recurring revenue, high margins, and strong cash generation. This is the best current balance of quality and price in the screen.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Fiscal Q3 revenue rose 13% to $6.76 billion, subscription revenue rose 14%, ARR reached $27.50 billion, and operating cash flow set a Q3 record at $2.52 billion. GAAP operating margin was about 35%, while AI-first ARR grew more than 150% and the product base exceeded one billion monthly active users.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Generative AI could reduce switching costs or move value toward model and distribution platforms. Stock compensation is material, repurchases consume substantial cash, and aggressive new entrants could pressure pricing. Sustained ARR deceleration, margin erosion, or weaker cash conversion would undermine the thesis.

Sources: [Adobe Q3 FY2026 release](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/01906202/au56y4ter.pdf), [Adobe Q3 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000156/adbe-20260828.htm)

### 2. Progressive (PGR) - High confidence

**Valuation signal:** $234.82, $136.7 billion market value, and about 11.8x trailing earnings. The market-wide screen also shows 33% trailing return on equity with debt below half of equity.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The multiple is modest for an insurer that is still growing policies and premiums while producing unusually strong underwriting results. The price appears to discount meaningful normalization from today's profitability, leaving a reasonable margin if underwriting remains disciplined.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Third-quarter net premiums earned rose 5%, total policies in force rose 6%, and the quarterly combined ratio improved to 88.7 from 89.5. September's combined ratio was 90.0 versus 100.4 a year earlier. Progressive's direct distribution, pricing data, scale, and long record of underwriting discipline are durable advantages.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Current margins may be above through-cycle levels. Catastrophe losses, reserve development, repair-cost inflation, regulatory constraints, or aggressive competitor pricing could quickly reduce earnings. A sustained combined ratio above the mid-90s without compensating policy growth would weaken the case.

Sources: [Progressive September and Q3 2026 results](https://investor.progressive.com/financials/financial-news-releases/news-details/2026/Progressive-Reports-September-2026-Results/default.aspx), [market-wide quality and valuation screen](https://www.sectoralpha.in/us-stock-screener)

### 3. Comcast (CMCSA) - Medium-high confidence

**Valuation signal:** $24.85, $88.2 billion market value, about 7.8x trailing earnings, and a screen-estimated trailing free-cash-flow yield of roughly 22.5%.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The price discounts continuing broadband erosion and complicated corporate separation work, but the remaining connectivity, wireless, business-services, media, and parks assets still produce substantial cash. A single-digit earnings multiple provides room for weak growth if free cash flow proves durable.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Q3 generated $4.21 billion of free cash flow. Wireless lines reached 10.6 million, business-services revenue grew 2.7% with a 55.9% EBITDA margin, and Peacock delivered a second consecutive profitable quarter with $261 million of EBITDA and 17% subscriber growth. These assets benefit from local network scale, bundled customer relationships, and valuable content and parks franchises.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Residential connectivity revenue fell 3.5% and EBITDA fell 5.8%; broadband losses and fixed-wireless competition may be structural. The company has paused buybacks during its separation work, carries meaningful debt, and parks and studios are cyclical. Falling free cash flow or accelerating broadband losses would turn the low multiple into a value trap.

Sources: [Comcast Q3 2026 results](https://cmcsa.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/comcast-reports-3rd-quarter-2026-results), [Comcast Q3 Form 10-Q](https://www.cmcsa.com/static-files/d10c1512-56b6-4290-a4b6-ee0a88897c27)

### 4. CVS Health (CVS) - Medium confidence, event-sensitive

**Valuation signal:** $79.96, $103.2 billion market value, about 9.3x trailing earnings, and about 9.8x management's new 2026 adjusted EPS floor of $8.20. The screen estimates a 15.2% trailing free-cash-flow yield.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The valuation reflects medical-cost pressure, regulatory risk, and skepticism about the integrated model. Yet operating momentum improved and management raised earnings and cash-flow guidance. Today's sharp selloff also appears to price substantial execution risk around the newly announced plan to create two public companies.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Q3 revenue rose 5.4% to $108.4 billion, adjusted operating income rose 21.2%, and adjusted EPS rose to $2.09 from $1.60. Year-to-date operating cash flow reached $15.3 billion; full-year guidance is now at least $15.5 billion. Aetna's quarterly medical-benefit ratio improved 150 basis points to 91.3%, and CVS retains scale across insurance, pharmacy benefits, specialty pharmacy, and retail distribution.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Separation execution, debt allocation, stranded costs, reimbursement changes, PBM regulation, Medicare Advantage economics, and medical-cost inflation are material. Cash flow includes working-capital effects and should not be treated as a stable run rate. Renewed deterioration in the medical-benefit ratio or a loss of investment-grade strength would lower confidence.

Sources: [CVS Q3 2026 results and guidance](https://investors.cvshealth.com/news/news-details/2026/CVS-HEALTH-CORPORATION-REPORTS-THIRD-QUARTER-2026-RESULTS-AND-RAISES-FULL-YEAR-2026-GUIDANCE/default.aspx), [CVS separation announcement](https://investors.cvshealth.com/news/news-details/2026/CVS-Health-Announces-Intent-to-Create-Two-Distinct-Leaders-in-Healthcare/default.aspx)

### 5. Qualcomm (QCOM) - Medium confidence

**Valuation signal:** $173.19, $185.1 billion market value, about 13.6x FY2026 GAAP EPS of $12.70 and 16.4x FY2026 non-GAAP EPS of $10.54. The GAAP figure benefited from a large tax item, so the adjusted multiple is the more conservative anchor.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The market is focused on handset weakness and the loss of Apple modem content. At roughly a mid-teens adjusted earnings multiple, investors are paying little for the possibility that automotive, IoT, edge AI, and data-center products reduce handset concentration.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Qualcomm owns foundational wireless intellectual property and renewed long-term licensing agreements with Apple, Huawei, and Transsion. In fiscal Q4, combined automotive and IoT revenue grew 36%; automotive achieved its first $2 billion quarter and grew 89%. The company returned $11.7 billion through repurchases and dividends during fiscal 2026.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Fiscal Q4 GAAP revenue fell 8% and non-GAAP EPS fell 28%. Smartphone demand, memory constraints, customer concentration, Apple insourcing, litigation, and large spending commitments for new AI businesses create real downside. Non-handset growth must become profitable cash flow, not merely replace shrinking handset revenue at lower returns.

Sources: [Qualcomm FY2026 results filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/804328/), [Qualcomm earnings release notice](https://investor.qualcomm.com/news-events/press-releases/news-details/2026/Qualcomm-Earnings-Release-Available-on-Companys-Investor-Relations-Website-c5258267a/default.aspx), [Qualcomm Q3 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/804328/000080432826000086/qcom-20260628.htm)

## Conclusion

**Strongest current candidate:** Adobe remains the best overall quality-value combination: recurring growth, high margins, strong cash conversion, and a valuation that assumes much worse deterioration than current results show.

**Best financial compounder at a value price:** Progressive pairs disciplined underwriting and policy growth with a roughly 12x earnings valuation, though normalized catastrophe and pricing conditions must be considered.

**Best cash-yield opportunity:** Comcast is exceptionally cheap on earnings and trailing free cash flow, but broadband decline and separation execution make it less certain than Adobe or Progressive.

**Most event-sensitive value:** CVS offers improving operating results and a low multiple, while the planned separation materially raises execution and balance-sheet uncertainty.

**Diversification option:** Qualcomm's licensing franchise and automotive growth are attractive, but the thesis depends on non-handset businesses offsetting a real smartphone and Apple headwind.

This shortlist is research support, not a claim of precise intrinsic value, a guarantee of returns, or personal financial advice.
