# Value Investing Weekly Report — 2026-W45

**Latest update:** 2026-11-03  
**Universe:** U.S.-listed public equities  
**Purpose:** Research support only; not personal financial advice.

## Market context

The market remains expensive enough to demand selectivity. The S&P 500 trades near 19 times forward earnings while the 10-year Treasury yield is about 5%, so a low multiple is useful only when paired with durable cash generation and a sound balance sheet. At the same time, the opportunity set below the index is broad: a rules-based screen of 3,536 U.S.-listed companies with market values above $200 million found 198 companies above $10 billion trading below 15 times trailing earnings, 99 companies combining return on equity above 20% with debt below half of equity, and 319 companies above $2 billion with trailing free-cash-flow yield above 8%. Prices are through November 2.

Today's ranking was built independently of Monday's report. The broad screen was followed by company-level review of current filings and releases, with explicit penalties for cyclically elevated earnings, deteriorating end markets, leverage, and cash flow that does not support reported profit.

Sources: [broad U.S. stock screen](https://www.sectoralpha.in/us-stock-screener), [Goldman Sachs market valuation discussion](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/can-the-s-and-p-500-rally-as-treasury-yields-rise)

## Today's top ideas — November 3

### 1. Adobe (ADBE) — High confidence

**Valuation signal:** About 14.0x trailing earnings and a 10.9% trailing free-cash-flow yield at roughly $250 per share.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The market is pricing Adobe as a mature or structurally impaired software vendor even though revenue, subscriptions, recurring revenue, and cash flow are still growing at healthy rates. The combination of a mid-teens earnings multiple and double-digit cash yield provides the best balance of price and business quality in today's screen.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Fiscal Q3 revenue rose 13% to $6.76 billion, subscription revenue rose 14% to $6.58 billion, ARR reached $27.50 billion, and operating cash flow reached a quarterly record of $2.52 billion. GAAP operating margin was about 35%, and Adobe repurchased 9.5 million shares in the quarter. Entrenched creative workflows, recurring revenue, high margins, and internally funded innovation remain real advantages.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Generative AI could reduce switching costs, pressure pricing, or shift value toward model and distribution platforms. Leadership transition adds execution risk, and stock-based compensation is material. Sustained ARR deceleration, falling retention, or AI products failing to support paid growth would weaken the thesis.

Sources: [Adobe Q3 FY2026 results](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/01906202/au56y4ter.pdf), [broad U.S. stock screen](https://www.sectoralpha.in/us-stock-screener)

### 2. lululemon athletica (LULU) — Medium confidence

**Valuation signal:** About 8.8x trailing earnings and an 11.3% trailing free-cash-flow yield at roughly $107 per share; the company has net cash rather than funded debt.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The price discounts a prolonged brand decline. That may prove too severe if new leadership can stabilize product relevance and North American traffic. The stock combines the lowest multiple in the top five with a clean balance sheet, a 31% screened return on equity, and an estimated double-digit cash yield.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Even in a weak quarter, gross margin was 60.5% and operating margin was 18.8%. The company ended Q2 with $1.39 billion of cash, inventory dollars down 1% year over year and inventory units down 7%, while repurchasing $330 million of stock. Those margins and the debt-free balance sheet provide time for a repair.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** The deterioration is not cosmetic: Q2 revenue fell 4%, comparable sales fell 9%, Americas revenue fell 8%, and management expects FY2026 revenue to decline 5%-7%. Q2 profit also benefited materially from tariff refunds. Continued double-digit comparable-sales declines or margin erosion would make the low multiple a value trap rather than an opportunity.

Sources: [lululemon Q2 FY2026 results](https://corporate.lululemon.com/newsroom/press-releases/2026/09-03-2026-210528733), [broad U.S. stock screen](https://www.sectoralpha.in/us-stock-screener)

### 3. Universal Health Services (UHS) — Medium confidence

**Valuation signal:** About 7.5x trailing earnings and a 10.5% trailing free-cash-flow yield at roughly $176 per share.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The market appears to capitalize near-term margin pressure as though it were permanent. Demand is still growing across acute and behavioral care, while the equity trades at a single-digit earnings multiple and a double-digit cash yield.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Nine-month revenue rose 7.7% to $13.87 billion. Operating cash flow increased to $1.48 billion and capital spending declined to $660 million, implying roughly $822 million of nine-month cash flow after capital expenditures. Same-facility Q3 revenue grew 4.7% in acute care and 4.0% in behavioral care, with positive patient-volume growth in both segments.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Q3 adjusted EBITDA fell 6.9%, consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin declined to 13.2% from 14.9%, and debt rose to 2.2x adjusted EBITDA. Labor costs, reimbursement changes, exchange-plan losses, California staffing rules, litigation reserves, and acquisition execution can keep returns below expectations. This is a cash-generative operator, but not a low-risk balance-sheet story.

Sources: [UHS Q3 2026 supplemental results](https://ir.uhs.com/static-files/ee9e776d-c82b-446c-baa0-e3942b532e3d), [UHS Q3 earnings release](https://ir.uhs.com/node/20916/pdf), [broad U.S. stock screen](https://www.sectoralpha.in/us-stock-screener)

### 4. Alphabet (GOOGL) — Medium confidence

**Valuation signal:** About 14.6x trailing earnings at $278.99, only 3.1% above its 52-week low as of November 2.

**Why it looks undervalued:** Revenue and operating income growth are far above what a mid-teens earnings multiple usually implies. The post-earnings decline offers an unusual entry valuation for a franchise with leading positions in Search, YouTube, Cloud, Android, and AI infrastructure.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Q3 revenue rose 25% to $127.6 billion, operating income rose 30% to $40.6 billion, and operating margin reached 31.8%. Search revenue grew 12%, Cloud revenue grew 103% to $30.7 billion, and Cloud operating margin reached 36.2%. Alphabet ended the quarter with $265.1 billion of cash and marketable securities.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** The P/E overstates present cash economics. Q3 capital expenditures reached $51.4 billion, Q3 free cash flow was negative $9.1 billion, trailing-12-month free cash flow was only $19.7 billion, and management expects capital spending to rise significantly again in 2027. Future purchase commitments are very large, Search faces AI disruption and regulatory pressure, and investment gains or losses make reported earnings noisy.

Sources: [Alphabet Q3 2026 earnings call and results](https://abc.xyz/investor/events/event-details/2026/2026-Q3-Earnings-Call-2026-Ujou16CTw9/default.aspx), [broad U.S. stock screen](https://www.sectoralpha.in/us-stock-screener)

### 5. CF Industries (CF) — Medium confidence

**Valuation signal:** About 9.0x trailing earnings and a 10.5% trailing free-cash-flow yield; the screen values the equity near $18.2 billion.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The current price offers a double-digit cash yield and a single-digit P/E despite strong operating execution and capital returns. North American natural-gas economics and a concentrated asset base can support attractive cash generation when nitrogen markets are balanced.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** First-half 2026 net earnings were $1.34 billion and adjusted EBITDA was $2.18 billion. Available ammonia capacity ran at 98%, trailing-12-month operating cash flow was $2.98 billion, and cash ended Q2 at $2.48 billion. The company repurchased $245 million of shares in the first half and raised its quarterly dividend 20%.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** This is the most cyclical selection. Nitrogen pricing, natural-gas costs, crop economics, trade policy, plant reliability, and new global capacity can rapidly compress earnings. Capital needs are rising, including Blue Point One, and Q3 results are due November 4, so today's ranking relies on Q2 financials plus current market data. Through-cycle earnings are lower-confidence than the headline multiple suggests.

Sources: [CF Industries Q2 2026 results](https://ir.cfindustries.com/Investors/news/news-details/2026/CF-Industries-Holdings-Inc--Reports-First-Half-2026-Net-Earnings-of-1-34-Billion-Adjusted-EBITDA-of-2-18-Billion/default.aspx), [CF Industries quarterly financials](https://cfindustries.q4ir.com/Investors/financials/quarterly-results/default.aspx), [broad U.S. stock screen](https://www.sectoralpha.in/us-stock-screener)

## Conclusion

**Strongest current candidate:** Adobe has the best overall mix of recurring revenue, margin durability, free-cash-flow generation, and valuation.

**Best higher-risk value setup:** lululemon offers the widest clean-balance-sheet valuation cushion, but the thesis requires stabilization in comparable sales and product relevance.

**Cash-generative diversifiers:** UHS and CF Industries screen cheaply and produce substantial cash, but UHS carries reimbursement and margin risk while CF carries commodity-cycle risk. Alphabet remains the highest-quality franchise in the list, yet its present free-cash-flow yield is poor because of extraordinary AI capital spending.

This ranking is a research shortlist, not a claim of precise intrinsic value or a guarantee of returns.
