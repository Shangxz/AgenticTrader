## Market context

Today's selection was completed from a fresh ranking before the November 2-6 report history was reviewed. The primary screen covered 3,516 U.S.-listed companies with market values above $200 million using prices through November 6, supplemented by a separate S&P 500 quality-value screen. Candidates were ranked on valuation, recurring or defensible earnings, cash generation, returns or margins, leverage, competitive position, and the durability of current results. Highly cyclical cash flow, one-time gains, structural decline, and weak balance sheets were penalized.

The market remains expensive in aggregate and cheapness is concentrated: a current S&P 500 cash-flow survey puts the median company at about 22x free cash flow, while only 49 of 501 S&P 500 members clear a value score of 80 with a minimum quality floor. That favors selectivity over buying the lowest multiple. Today's highest-ranked names combine double-digit or near-double-digit cash yields or modest earnings multiples with evidence that the underlying franchise remains intact.

Prices and market multiples are snapshots from the November 6 close and can move materially.

## Today's top ideas - November 7

### 1. Adobe (ADBE) - High confidence

**Valuation signal:** $239.30, about $94.6 billion in market value, and 13.4x trailing GAAP earnings. The current S&P 500 screen estimates a 10.9% trailing free-cash-flow yield and assigns Adobe a 99 quality score.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The share price discounts substantial generative-AI disruption even though Adobe is still producing double-digit subscription growth and exceptional cash flow. At roughly 13x earnings, the market appears to require a much faster franchise erosion than the latest results demonstrate.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Fiscal Q3 revenue rose 13% to $6.76 billion, subscription revenue rose 14%, ARR reached $27.50 billion, and quarterly operating cash flow set a record at $2.52 billion. Adobe's professional workflows, file standards, installed base, and recurring enterprise relationships remain material competitive advantages.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Generative AI may reduce switching costs, weaken pricing, or shift value to model and distribution platforms. Stock-based compensation and aggressive repurchases also deserve scrutiny. Sustained ARR deceleration, falling margins, weaker cash conversion, or evidence that AI offerings cannibalize more revenue than they create would invalidate the thesis.

Sources: [Adobe Q3 FY2026 release](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/01906202/au56y4ter.pdf), [current valuation screen](https://www.siriusinvestors.com/stock/screener/value-stocks), [price reference](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/ADBE/)

### 2. Deckers Brands (DECK) - Medium-high confidence

**Valuation signal:** $86.71, about $11.7 billion in market value, and 11.0x trailing earnings. The price is about 11.8x management's FY2027 EPS midpoint after removing the approximately $0.52 per-share tariff-refund benefit. The current screen assigns Deckers a 99 value score and 98 quality score.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The valuation assumes HOKA and UGG are near maturity or face a lasting demand problem. Growth has slowed, but approximately 12x normalized guidance is modest for a debt-free branded consumer company with two scaled franchises, strong margins, and international runway.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Fiscal Q2 sales rose 3.5% to a record $1.48 billion; HOKA grew 6.8%, UGG grew 3.5%, and international sales grew 9.0%. Gross margin was 60.5%, though 310 basis points came from the tariff refund. Deckers held $1.21 billion in cash, had no borrowings, and repurchased $637.5 million of shares in the fiscal first half.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Direct-to-consumer sales fell 1.8%, comparable DTC sales fell 6.4%, and domestic sales declined slightly. Brand concentration and fashion risk are high. Continued DTC weakness, inventories rising faster than sales, heavier promotion, or contraction at HOKA or UGG would make the low multiple more consistent with a value trap.

Sources: [Deckers Q2 FY2027 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910521/000091052126000028/deckex991pressrelease-9302.htm), [Deckers Q2 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910521/000162828026068116/deck-20260930.htm), [price reference](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/DECK/)

### 3. Leidos (LDOS) - Medium-high confidence

**Valuation signal:** $132.68, about $16.2 billion in market value, and 11.9x trailing GAAP earnings. The price is about 10.5x management's FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance midpoint of $12.60; the current screen estimates an 11.9% trailing free-cash-flow yield and assigns an 84 quality score.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The valuation is low for a mission-critical government technology contractor with improved margins, strong cash conversion, and guidance raised for a third consecutive quarter. The market is pricing meaningful federal-budget, acquisition, and leverage risk, but current execution provides a buffer.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Q3 revenue rose 4% to $4.63 billion, GAAP EPS rose 16%, adjusted EPS rose 19%, and adjusted EBITDA margin improved to 13.9%. Quarterly free cash flow was $476 million. Management now expects approximately $1.90 billion in 2026 operating cash flow.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Leidos depends heavily on U.S. government priorities, appropriations, and contract awards. It also carried $6.0 billion of debt against $630 million of cash at quarter-end, and some growth came from acquisition. Budget cuts, major contract losses, weak organic growth, integration problems, or poor cash conversion would narrow the margin of safety.

Sources: [Leidos Q3 2026 results](https://investors.leidos.com/news-releases/news-release-details/leidos-delivers-strong-third-quarter-and-enhances-full-year), [current valuation screen](https://www.siriusinvestors.com/stock/screener/value-stocks), [price reference](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/LDOS/)

### 4. Expedia Group (EXPE) - Medium-high confidence

**Valuation signal:** $259.24, about $31.7 billion in market value, and 15.2x trailing earnings. The current S&P 500 screen assigns Expedia a 95 quality score and estimates a 14.3% trailing free-cash-flow yield.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The multiple and cash yield are modest for a scaled travel marketplace that is growing bookings, expanding margins, and shrinking its share count. The valuation leaves room for slower consumer travel while giving limited credit to the faster-growing B2B platform.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Q3 gross bookings rose 11%, revenue rose 12%, operating income rose 28%, and adjusted EBITDA rose 17% with 135 basis points of margin expansion. B2B revenue grew 23%. Nine-month free cash flow increased to $3.63 billion from $2.99 billion, and the company repurchased roughly $576 million of shares during Q3.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Travel demand is cyclical, online acquisition costs are substantial, and competition from Booking, Google, suppliers, and AI-driven discovery is intense. Quarterly cash flow is seasonal and Q3 itself was negative. Slowing room-night growth, rising marketing expense relative to bookings, margin reversal, or weakening B2B growth would undermine the case.

Sources: [Expedia Q3 2026 results](https://ir.expediagroup.com/news-and-events/news/news-details/2026/Expedia-Group-Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx), [current valuation screen](https://www.siriusinvestors.com/stock/screener/value-stocks), [price reference](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/EXPE/)

### 5. Zoetis (ZTS) - Medium confidence

**Valuation signal:** $79.40, about $32.7 billion in market value, and 13.4x trailing GAAP earnings. The price is about 12.6x management's 2026 adjusted EPS guidance midpoint of $6.30. The current screen assigns Zoetis an 87 quality score and estimates a 6.8% trailing free-cash-flow yield.

**Why it looks undervalued:** A historically premium animal-health franchise now trades at a low-teens earnings multiple because investors expect current U.S. companion-animal weakness and competitive pressure to persist. The discount is attractive if product innovation and international growth can stabilize the business, but this is the least confirmed thesis in today's top five.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Zoetis remains the world's largest animal-health company, with diversified medicines, vaccines, diagnostics, and biologics sold in more than 100 countries. Q3 international revenue grew 4%, livestock revenue grew in both segments, and the company raised adjusted EPS guidance to $6.25-$6.35. The share count fell roughly 7% year over year, helping adjusted EPS grow despite lower profit.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Q3 revenue fell 1%, U.S. revenue fell 6%, adjusted net income fell 5%, and full-year organic revenue guidance remains negative. Companion-animal price sensitivity, generic and branded competition in dermatology, pipeline execution, and acquisition adjustments are material. Continued U.S. contraction or failure of new products to restore organic growth would make the apparent bargain a value trap.

Sources: [Zoetis Q3 2026 results](https://investor.zoetis.com/news/news-details/2026/Zoetis-Announces-Third-Quarter-2026-Results-Highlights-Progress-Advancing-Innovation-Across-Diversified-Portfolio-and-Narrows-Full-Year-2026-Revenue-Guidance-Raises-Full-Year-2026-Adjusted-EPS-Guidance/default.aspx), [current valuation screen](https://www.siriusinvestors.com/stock/screener/value-stocks), [price reference](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/ZTS/)

## Changes from the November 6 update

- **Adobe remains number one.** It was independently selected again because recurring double-digit growth and strong cash generation remain unusually attractive at about 13x GAAP earnings.
- **Deckers and Leidos remain in the top group.** Deckers retains the cleanest balance sheet and strongest valuation-quality screen scores; Leidos continues to pair raised guidance and cash conversion with a roughly 10.5x adjusted multiple.
- **Expedia enters at number four.** Its November 4 report added fresh confirmation: double-digit bookings and revenue growth, margin expansion, faster B2B growth, and higher nine-month free cash flow.
- **Zoetis enters at number five, with lower confidence.** Its low-teens multiple and durable animal-health position are compelling, but today's selection explicitly treats shrinking U.S. companion-animal sales and negative organic guidance as unresolved.
- **Progressive, Cigna, and Comcast move to the watchlist.** They remain credible value candidates, but Expedia's current growth and cash-flow evidence and Zoetis's unusually compressed franchise valuation ranked higher today. These are fresh-ranking changes, not sell recommendations.

## Conclusion

**Strongest overall candidate:** Adobe offers the best combination of recurring growth, margins, cash conversion, and valuation, though AI disruption is a genuine rather than cosmetic risk.

**Best balance-sheet-backed contrarian:** Deckers has two scaled brands, no borrowings, and a low normalized multiple, but DTC weakness requires close monitoring.

**Best visible cash-flow value:** Leidos combines raised guidance and strong cash conversion with a low multiple, offset by federal-budget exposure and leverage.

**Best growth-at-a-value-price:** Expedia's bookings, B2B revenue, margins, and cash flow are advancing at a valuation that does not demand exceptional growth.

**Most asymmetric but least confirmed:** Zoetis offers a premium franchise at a low-teens multiple, but current U.S. and organic trends make it the highest-risk thesis in today's shortlist.
