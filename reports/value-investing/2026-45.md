# Value Investing Weekly Report — 2026-W45

**Update date:** 2026-11-02  
**Universe:** U.S.-listed public equities  
**Purpose:** Research support only; not personal financial advice.

## Market context

The opportunity set remains unusually split. The U.S. market is highly concentrated in its largest technology companies, while a broad screen of 3,528 U.S.-listed companies (minimum market value $200 million) still found 198 companies above $10 billion trading below 15 times trailing earnings, 98 companies combining return on equity above 20% with debt below half of equity, and 324 companies above $2 billion with trailing free-cash-flow yield above 8%. Prices in that screen are through October 30, 2026. Meanwhile, the Morningstar Wide Moat Focus Index remains underweight the most momentum-driven AI names and overweight consumer defensive, consumer cyclical, healthcare, and industrial companies.

This report starts from a fresh ranking. The initial screen covered the broad U.S.-listed universe, then cross-checked candidates against a 4,325-company SEC-filing database, a 530-company large-cap free-cash-flow screen, Morningstar's moat universe, current prices, and recent company filings. The final ranking emphasizes cash generation, returns on capital, balance-sheet resilience, competitive durability, and valuation. It deliberately penalizes cyclically inflated earnings, leverage, deteriorating end markets, and thin evidence.

## Today's top ideas

### 1. Adobe (ADBE) — High confidence

**Price / valuation snapshot:** $250.23; approximately 14.0x trailing earnings; approximately 10.9% trailing free-cash-flow yield.

**Why it looks undervalued:** Adobe is priced like a mature or structurally impaired software vendor even though its latest quarter still showed double-digit growth. The broad screen places it simultaneously below 15x earnings, above a 10% free-cash-flow yield, and at 28% return on equity. That combination offers the clearest quality-value balance in today's ranking.

**Why it remains a quality business:** Fiscal Q3 revenue rose 13% to $6.76 billion, subscription revenue rose 14% to $6.56 billion, annualized recurring revenue reached $27.50 billion, and operating cash flow hit a quarterly record of $2.52 billion. The recurring subscription base, entrenched creative workflows, high margins, and ability to fund product development and buybacks internally remain substantial advantages.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Generative AI may reduce switching costs, pressure pricing, or shift value toward model and distribution platforms. CEO succession adds execution risk. A sustained slowdown in ARR, weaker net retention, or AI products failing to monetize would challenge the thesis.

**What would improve confidence:** Continued double-digit subscription growth, stable margins, and evidence that AI-first usage expands paid seats or pricing rather than cannibalizing the core franchise.

Sources: [Adobe Q3 FY2026 results](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/01906202/au56y4ter.pdf), [broad U.S. stock screen](https://www.sectoralpha.in/us-stock-screener)

### 2. Deckers Brands (DECK) — Medium-high confidence

**Price / valuation snapshot:** $81.99; approximately 10.4x trailing earnings; approximately 10.2% trailing free-cash-flow yield.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The valuation now assumes sharply weaker growth or meaningful brand erosion. Deckers appears on all three useful broad-screen dimensions: below 15x earnings, 44% return on equity with low debt, and free-cash-flow yield above 10%.

**Why it remains a quality business:** Fiscal Q2 2027 revenue reached a record $1.48 billion, HOKA sales increased 6.8%, UGG increased 3.5%, international sales increased 9%, and operating income increased to $389.5 million. The company ended September with $1.21 billion of cash and no borrowings, while repurchasing $637 million of shares during the first half.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Growth is slowing: direct-to-consumer comparable sales fell 6.4% and domestic sales declined slightly. The quarter's margin and EPS benefited from tariff refunds; excluding that benefit, underlying improvement was less dramatic. HOKA and UGG concentration, fashion risk, inventory growth, tariffs, and a promotional consumer environment could make the low multiple deserved.

**What would improve confidence:** Reacceleration in HOKA, positive DTC comparable sales, disciplined inventory, and margins that remain strong without tariff-refund benefits.

Sources: [Deckers fiscal Q2 2027 SEC exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910521/000091052126000028/deckex991pressrelease-9302.htm), [broad U.S. stock screen](https://www.sectoralpha.in/us-stock-screener)

### 3. Monster Beverage (MNST) — Medium confidence

**Price / valuation snapshot:** $47.34; approximately 21.9x trailing earnings.

**Why it looks undervalued:** This is the least statistically cheap top-five name, but its multiple is reasonable relative to current growth and balance-sheet quality. A separate quality/fair-value screen estimates roughly 31% upside to fair value, while the broad universe screen records 26% return on equity with low debt.

**Why it remains a quality business:** Second-quarter net sales increased 20.2% to $2.54 billion, net income increased 19.6%, and international sales grew 34.6% to about 46% of total sales. Gross margin held near 56%, supporting the case that Monster's brand, distribution reach, and energy-drink category position remain durable.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** The valuation offers less margin of safety than Adobe or Deckers. Marketing costs are rising, the alcohol segment is shrinking, category competition is intense, and health or regulatory pressure could affect long-term demand. Q3 results are scheduled for November 5, so near-term evidence is incomplete.

**What would improve confidence:** Q3 growth that confirms the second-quarter acceleration, stable gross margin, and continued international gains without disproportionate marketing spend.

Sources: [Monster Q2 2026 results](https://monsterenergycompany.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/monster-beverage-reports-2026-second-quarter-financial-results), [Monster Q3 reporting date](https://investors.monsterbevcorp.com/node/18251/pdf), [quality/fair-value screen](https://www.stocksxray.com/value/quality?f%5B0%5D=lowdebt&sort=growth)

### 4. Alphabet (GOOGL) — Medium confidence

**Price / valuation snapshot:** $279.84; approximately 14.6x trailing earnings.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The broad screen combines a sub-15x earnings multiple with 44% return on equity and low debt relative to equity. Revenue growth remains far above what that earnings multiple normally implies. The post-earnings selloff improves the entry valuation, but the cash-flow picture makes the apparent cheapness less clean than the P/E suggests.

**Why it remains a quality business:** Q3 revenue increased 25% to $127.6 billion, Google Services revenue increased 11%, Search revenue increased 12%, Cloud revenue increased 103% to $30.7 billion, and consolidated operating income increased 30% with a 31.8% margin. Alphabet also held about $265 billion of cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities at quarter-end.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Q3 free cash flow was negative after $51.4 billion of capital expenditure, and long-term debt increased to about $125 billion. Search growth decelerated, AI may disrupt the core advertising economics, and management expects capital spending to rise significantly again in 2027. The trailing P/E also includes volatile investment gains and should not be treated as normalized earning power.

**What would improve confidence:** A credible path from AI infrastructure spending to durable free cash flow, stable Search economics, and moderation in debt-funded capital intensity.

Sources: [Alphabet Q3 2026 SEC exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000080/googexhibit991q32026.htm), [Alphabet Q3 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000084/goog-20260930.htm), [broad U.S. stock screen](https://www.sectoralpha.in/us-stock-screener)

### 5. Newmont (NEM) — Medium confidence

**Price / valuation snapshot:** $106.61; approximately 12.4x trailing earnings; net-cash balance sheet at quarter-end.

**Why it looks undervalued:** Newmont screens at 31% return on invested capital, a 9/9 Piotroski score, net cash, and roughly 37% modeled fair-value upside. At about 12.4x earnings, the market is discounting the cyclicality of current commodity-driven cash flow.

**Why it remains a quality business:** Q3 free cash flow was $1.9 billion, adjusted EBITDA was $4.0 billion, and the company ended the quarter with $8.8 billion of cash and $3.2 billion of net cash before a planned $1.95 billion payment. Newmont improved full-year cost guidance, remains on track for 5.3 million attributable gold ounces, and has reduced share count by about 10% since February 2024.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** This is a cyclical value idea, not a steady compounder. Earnings and free cash flow are highly sensitive to gold and copper prices, costs, taxes, mine execution, reserve replacement, and large-project capital needs. Current ROIC is unlikely to represent through-cycle economics. The planned Barrick payment will reduce net cash.

**What would improve confidence:** Stable production, continued cost control, disciplined Red Chris spending, and robust free cash flow under more conservative commodity-price assumptions.

Sources: [Newmont Q3 2026 results](https://www.newmont.com/investors/news-release/news-details/2026/Newmont-Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Results-with-Record-Third-Quarter-Adjusted-Net-Income-Positioned-to-Achieve-Full-Year-Guidance-Provides-Update-on-Red-Chris-Block-Cave-Project/default.aspx), [quality/fair-value screen](https://www.stocksxray.com/value/quality?f%5B0%5D=lowdebt&sort=growth)

## Watchlist, not top five

- **Comcast (CMCSA):** At about 7.3x earnings and a reported 26.6% trailing free-cash-flow yield, the stock is statistically cheaper than every top-five name. It stays off the shortlist because leverage and structural pressure on cable video and broadband make the quality leg of the thesis materially weaker.
- **Booking Holdings (BKNG):** Strong marketplace economics and a roughly 15.8x earnings multiple are attractive, but the current screen's split-adjusted data require additional normalization before a high-conviction ranking.
- **Corteva (CTVA):** The large-cap cash-flow screen shows an extraordinary 36.2% trailing free-cash-flow yield, but the price and cash-flow series appear affected by corporate-action or data-normalization issues. It is excluded pending reconciliation with filings.

## Conclusion

**Strongest current candidates:** Adobe offers the best overall blend of recurring growth, cash generation, and depressed valuation. Deckers offers the largest clean balance-sheet margin of safety, but slowing brand momentum requires close monitoring. Monster is the highest-quality growth-at-a-reasonable-price idea, while Alphabet and Newmont are more conditional: Alphabet depends on AI capital intensity converting back into free cash flow, and Newmont depends on through-cycle commodity economics.

The ranking is a research shortlist, not a claim of precise intrinsic value. Position sizing, tax circumstances, portfolio concentration, and individual risk tolerance are outside this report's scope.
