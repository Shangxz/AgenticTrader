## Market context

Today's work began with a fresh, market-wide quality-value sweep, independent of the November 2 and November 3 selections. The primary screen covered 3,536 U.S.-listed companies above $200 million in market value; additional quality, fair-value, and beaten-down screens were used to check smaller names and reduce single-screen bias. The first pass favored positive free cash flow, reasonable leverage, defensible margins or returns on capital, and low earnings or cash-flow multiples. The final ranking then penalized cyclically elevated earnings, deteriorating end markets, and accounting profit unsupported by cash.

The broad market is close enough to fair value that the discounts are concentrated rather than universal. A November 2 screen still found 198 companies above $10 billion below 15x trailing earnings and 319 companies above $2 billion with trailing free-cash-flow yield above 8%, but many of the cheapest results were commodity, shipping, retail, or leveraged turnaround businesses. Morningstar likewise describes Q4 undervaluation as concentrated. That makes business durability and balance-sheet quality more important than the cheapest headline multiple.

Prices and trailing P/E ratios below are a market-data snapshot from approximately 8:00 a.m. Eastern on November 4. They can move materially.

## Today's top ideas - November 4

### 1. Adobe (ADBE) - High confidence

**Valuation signal:** $246.14, $97.3 billion market value, and about 13.7x trailing earnings.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The multiple implies stagnation or material franchise erosion, yet the latest quarter still showed double-digit revenue and recurring-revenue growth. Adobe offers the strongest current combination of a depressed multiple, recurring revenue, high margins, and demonstrated cash generation.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Fiscal Q3 revenue rose 13% to $6.76 billion, total ARR rose 11.2% to $27.50 billion, remaining performance obligations reached $22.16 billion, and quarterly operating cash flow was a record $2.52 billion. Nine-month operating cash flow was $7.65 billion. Creative and document workflows remain deeply embedded, while the subscription model makes revenue more resilient than the valuation suggests.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Generative AI may lower switching costs, weaken pricing, or move value toward model and distribution platforms. Operating expenses grew faster than revenue in Q3, stock compensation is material, and aggressive repurchases should be judged after dilution. Sustained ARR deceleration or weaker cash conversion would undermine the thesis.

Sources: [Adobe Q3 FY2026 release](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/01906202/au56y4ter.pdf), [Adobe Q3 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000156/adbe-20260828.htm)

### 2. Deckers Brands (DECK) - Medium-high confidence

**Valuation signal:** $84.09, $11.4 billion market value, and about 10.7x trailing earnings. The price is also about 10.7x the midpoint of FY2027 EPS guidance of $7.80-$7.95, although that guidance includes about $0.52 of tariff-refund benefit.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The market is treating slower HOKA and UGG growth as the start of durable brand decline. At roughly 11x earnings, little growth is required for a satisfactory outcome if the brands retain pricing power and management continues protecting margins.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Fiscal Q2 revenue reached a record $1.48 billion, EPS rose 27% to $2.31, and the company repurchased $637 million of shares in the first half. Fiscal 2026 revenue grew 10%, operating margin remained above 23%, HOKA grew 16%, and the company ended that fiscal year with a debt-free balance sheet. This is unusually strong profitability and financial flexibility for an apparel and footwear company.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Q2 sales growth slowed to 3.5%, full-year sales guidance was reduced, direct-to-consumer trends weakened, and both major brands could be closer to maturity than the valuation case assumes. Tariff refunds flatter current EPS. Continued weak comparable sales, discounting, or gross-margin erosion would signal a value trap.

Sources: [Deckers fiscal Q2 2027 SEC release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910521/000091052126000028/deckex991pressrelease-9302.htm), [Deckers FY2026 annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910521/000091052126000020/deckfy26annualreport.pdf)

### 3. Cognizant Technology Solutions (CTSH) - Medium-high confidence

**Valuation signal:** $63.83, $28.7 billion market value, and about 11.6x trailing earnings.

**Why it looks undervalued:** Cognizant is priced as a no-growth legacy outsourcer even though revenue is still growing, margins are improving, and cash returns are meaningful. The multiple leaves room for modest execution rather than requiring an AI-led growth acceleration.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Q3 revenue rose 4.6% to $5.66 billion, adjusted operating margin reached 16.3%, and quarterly free cash flow was $925 million. Through nine months, free cash flow was $1.6 billion and dividends plus repurchases totaled $2.3 billion. Its scale, entrenched enterprise relationships, and broad delivery footprint support recurring demand.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** AI could commoditize labor-intensive services faster than Cognizant can move into higher-value work. Growth remains modest, the balance sheet now carries roughly neutral net debt rather than a large net-cash cushion, and immigration or labor-compliance disputes could affect delivery. Organic growth below peers or falling bookings would weaken the case.

Sources: [Cognizant Q3 2026 results](https://investors.cognizant.com/news-and-events/news/news-details/2026/Cognizant-Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx), [Cognizant Q3 SEC presentation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1058290/000105829026000039/exhibit9929302026.htm)

### 4. T. Rowe Price (TROW) - Medium confidence

**Valuation signal:** $106.23, $22.7 billion market value, and about 10.8x trailing earnings. Cash and investments attributable to T. Rowe Price were $7.52 billion at September 30, a substantial cushion relative to the equity value.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The price discounts persistent active-management outflows and fee compression, but gives limited credit to the balance sheet, brand, distribution, and earnings power of $1.86 trillion in assets under management.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Q3 advisory fees rose 5.8%, net revenue rose 5.9%, adjusted operating income rose 4.5%, and adjusted EPS rose 1.8% to $2.86. Ending AUM was 5.4% above the prior year. The company generates strong operating cash flow and can fund dividends, seed products, acquisitions, and buybacks internally.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Q3 net client outflows were $28.3 billion, the effective fee rate continued to decline, and passive products remain a structural threat. Market appreciation can conceal weak organic flows. Persistent outflows that overwhelm market gains or sharper fee compression would invalidate the thesis.

Sources: [T. Rowe Price Q3 2026 results](https://investors.troweprice.com/static-files/63ae59c6-2eaf-45e2-9347-1004c462ba7a), [T. Rowe Price Q3 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1113169/000111316926000005/trow-20260930.htm)

### 5. The Cigna Group (CI) - Medium confidence, event-sensitive

**Valuation signal:** $295.44, $78.1 billion market value, and about 12.2x trailing earnings. The price is about 9.7x management's current 2026 adjusted EPS outlook of at least $30.45.

**Why it looks undervalued:** The valuation assumes sustained medical-cost, regulatory, and pharmacy-benefit pressure. Cigna's diversified Evernorth services franchise and ongoing capital returns may deserve a higher through-cycle multiple if adjusted earnings prove durable.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Cigna raised its full-year adjusted EPS outlook after Q2 and operates at scale across pharmacy benefits, specialty services, and employer health benefits. These businesses have recurring demand, large purchasing networks, and meaningful switching friction.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Q3 results are due November 5, so this is the least timely financial read in the shortlist and should be reassessed immediately after the release. Medical-cost trends, PBM regulation, rebate reform, customer concentration, debt, and the gap between adjusted and GAAP earnings all warrant caution. A guidance cut or weakening Evernorth economics would quickly change the ranking.

Sources: [Cigna Q2 2026 results and raised outlook](https://newsroom.thecignagroup.com/2026-07-30-The-Cigna-Group-Reports-Strong-Second-Quarter-2026-Results%2C-Raises-2026-Outlook), [Cigna Q3 reporting date](https://newsroom.thecignagroup.com/2026-10-09-The-Cigna-Groups-Third-Quarter-2026-Earnings-Release-Details)

### 6. SkyWest (SKYW) - Medium confidence

**Valuation signal:** $77.70, $3.0 billion market value, and about 7.5x trailing earnings.

**Why it looks undervalued:** A single-digit P/E appears to discount a sharp normalization in regional-airline profitability. Capacity-purchase agreements reduce direct ticket-pricing exposure, while fleet investment and scarce regional lift support demand from major-airline partners.

**Why it still qualifies as quality:** Q3 revenue rose 6.4% to $1.12 billion and EPS increased to $3.08 from $2.81. SkyWest held $569 million of cash and marketable securities at quarter-end and repurchased $225 million of shares through nine months. Contracted flying provides more visibility than a conventional airline model.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors:** Total debt was $2.4 billion, operating cash flow fell 20% through nine months, and aircraft purchases make free cash flow lumpy. Customer concentration, pilot availability, labor costs, fuel exposure in prorate flying, and residual-value risk remain material. Debt rising faster than contracted cash generation would break the thesis.

Sources: [SkyWest Q3 2026 earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/793733/000110465926120182/skyw-ex99d1.htm), [SkyWest Q3 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/793733/000110465926120651/skyw-20260930x10q.htm)

## Changes from the November 3 update

- **Adobe remains number one.** Its latest growth and cash-flow evidence still offers the cleanest quality-value balance in today's independent ranking.
- **Deckers returns at number two.** It was on the November 3 watchlist. Today's ranking gives more weight to its roughly 11x earnings valuation, debt-free balance sheet, and margin durability, while retaining the prior warning about slowing direct-to-consumer demand.
- **Cognizant, T. Rowe Price, Cigna, and SkyWest are new to the current shortlist.** They passed today's cross-sectional valuation and cash-generation checks, with distinct risks rather than a shared macro bet.
- **lululemon, UHS, Alphabet, and CF Industries leave the current list.** lululemon's operating deterioration is more severe than Deckers'; UHS has margin and leverage pressure; Alphabet's low P/E currently overstates cash economics because Q3 free cash flow was negative amid extraordinary capital spending; and CF's earnings remain too cycle-sensitive for the strongest quality-value group.
- These are ranking changes, not sell recommendations. Earlier selections remain below as a dated research record.

## Conclusion

**Strongest current candidate:** Adobe has the best overall mix of recurring revenue, margin durability, cash generation, and valuation.

**Best consumer-franchise value:** Deckers offers a rare debt-free, high-margin brand portfolio at roughly 11x earnings, but sales stabilization is essential.

**Best steady cash generators:** Cognizant and T. Rowe Price combine low-teens earnings multiples with substantial cash generation; each faces a credible secular threat that the investor must monitor.

**Higher-risk ideas:** Cigna is cheap but reports tomorrow, and SkyWest's contracted revenue does not eliminate balance-sheet and capital-intensity risk.

This ranking is a research shortlist, not a claim of precise intrinsic value or a guarantee of returns.
