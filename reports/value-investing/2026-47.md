## Today's top ideas

### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At about 13.6 times trailing earnings, Adobe is priced more like a slow-growth mature software vendor than a high-margin subscription platform. A separate multi-factor large-cap screen ranked Adobe second on value, with a 7/9 Piotroski F-Score. The multiple offers a meaningful margin of safety if growth merely settles into the high-single or low-double digits.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal Q3 revenue reached a record $6.76 billion, up 13% year over year; operating cash flow was a record $2.52 billion. Total ARR was $27.50 billion, up 11.2%, and nine-month operating cash flow rose 11% to $7.65 billion. Those figures support the inference that the subscription base remains sticky and cash generative despite generative-AI competition. [Adobe Q3 FY2026 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000147/adbeex991q326.htm) · [Adobe Q3 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000156/adbe-20260828.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Generative AI could reduce the scarcity value of creative tools, raise product-development expense, or shift value to model and distribution layers. The thesis weakens materially if ARR growth drops into the mid-single digits, cash margins contract, or AI features fail to support pricing and retention.

**Valuation signal.** Strongest current combination of franchise quality and undemanding headline valuation in this review.

### 2. Leidos (LDOS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The shares trade at roughly 11.7 times trailing earnings. That is modest for a contractor with long-duration, mission-critical government work and unusually visible demand. A separate S&P 500 screen assigned Leidos a quality score of 84 and an indicated FCF yield of 11.9%.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q3 revenue rose 4% to $4.6 billion; net income and diluted EPS increased 12% and 16%, respectively. Non-GAAP free cash flow was $476 million in the quarter and $1.5 billion year to date, already above the company's original full-year target. The combination of backlog, low capital intensity, and government customer relationships supports durable cash conversion. [Leidos Q3 2026 results](https://investors.leidos.com/news-releases/news-release-details/leidos-delivers-strong-third-quarter-and-enhances-full-year) · [Leidos Q3 summary](https://www.leidos.com/insights/leidos-delivers-strong-q3-enhances-2026-outlook-third-straight-quarter)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Federal budget delays, contract losses, fixed-price execution problems, and customer concentration can quickly change the economics. Non-GAAP FCF should be reconciled carefully to working-capital timing. A shrinking funded backlog or book-to-bill persistently below 1 would weaken the case.

**Valuation signal.** A low-teens earnings multiple appears conservative if current cash generation and backlog conversion persist.

### 3. Cognizant Technology Solutions (CTSH)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Cognizant trades at about 11.5 times trailing earnings. The market appears to be pricing a structurally slow IT-services business, leaving limited credit for its improving financial-services demand, margin discipline, and capital returns.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q3 revenue was $5.7 billion, up 4.6% reported and 4.8% in constant currency. Adjusted operating margin expanded 30 basis points to 16.3%, adjusted EPS rose 7.2%, and trailing 12-month bookings grew 4%. The company generated $1.58 billion of FCF in the first nine months and returned $2.3 billion through repurchases and dividends. [Cognizant Q3 2026 results](https://investors.cognizant.com/news-and-events/news/news-details/2026/Cognizant-Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx) · [Cognizant SEC exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1058290/000105829026000039/exhibit9919302026.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Revenue growth is still modest; AI may compress labor-based pricing faster than it creates higher-value work. Nine-month FCF fell from $1.88 billion a year earlier, so capital returns should not be mistaken for organic improvement. The thesis weakens if bookings decelerate or adjusted margins fail to convert into GAAP cash flow.

**Valuation signal.** An attractive cash-yield setup, but with less moat strength than Adobe or Alphabet.

### 4. Universal Health Services (UHS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At roughly 8.1 times trailing earnings, UHS is the cheapest finalist on earnings. A broad S&P 500 value screen ranked it first, showing a 10.7% FCF yield and a quality score of 67.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q3 net revenue increased 5.3% to $4.73 billion. UHS operates one of the largest U.S. hospital and behavioral-health networks, where scale, licenses, payer relationships, and local density are meaningful barriers. It repurchased $689.6 million of stock during the first nine months at an average $176 per share, below today's indicated price. [UHS Q3 2026 SEC release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/352915/000119312526434414/uhs-ex99_1.htm) · [UHS investor release](https://ir.uhs.com/news-releases/news-release-details/universal-health-services-inc-announces-financial-results-4)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Labor costs, reimbursement changes, adverse patient mix, malpractice reserves, and regulatory scrutiny can all overwhelm volume growth. Q3 included a net $21 million favorable pre-tax impact from items outside the prior forecast, so headline earnings should not be annualized mechanically.

**Valuation signal.** The discount is large enough to justify deeper work, but healthcare-policy and reserve risks keep confidence below the top three.

### 5. Alphabet (GOOGL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The reported trailing P/E is about 15.4 times, unusually low for a business growing revenue and operating income at double-digit rates. However, the ratio is flattered by large mark-to-market investment gains earlier in 2026, so it should not be compared mechanically with normalized operating earnings.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q3 revenue rose 25% to $127.6 billion, operating income increased 30%, and operating margin reached 31.8%. Google Services produced a 40.7% operating margin, while Cloud revenue more than doubled. Q3 operating cash flow was $42.3 billion. Search distribution, data, products, and infrastructure remain formidable competitive assets. [Alphabet Q3 2026 SEC release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000080/googexhibit991q32026.htm) · [Alphabet Q3 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000084/goog-20260930.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Q3 free cash flow was negative $9.1 billion as AI-related capex surged, and management expects capex to rise significantly again in 2027. Search disruption, antitrust remedies, and uncertain returns on infrastructure are central risks. The thesis requires operating growth to translate back into substantial owner cash flow; until then, the apparent P/E discount is lower confidence.

**Valuation signal.** Outstanding business quality, but currently the least clean valuation case in the top five.
