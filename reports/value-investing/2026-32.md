## Market context

The S&P 500 closed August 3 at 7,600.50, just 0.1% below its record, while the 10-year Treasury yield remained elevated at 4.68%. A broad index near a high and a relatively demanding risk-free rate make free-cash-flow yield, balance-sheet resilience, and evidence of durable returns especially important. The current screen therefore penalizes companies whose low headline P/E is caused by cyclical peak earnings, one-off accounting gains, weak cash conversion, or excessive leverage.

This update began from a fresh market-wide ranking and did not use the August 3 selections as inputs. The earlier entry was read only after today's shortlist was fixed.

**Screening approach.** The first pass used profitability, return on capital, leverage, earnings yield, and free-cash-flow yield across the US stock universe. The second pass reviewed current filings and company releases, normalized sector-specific accounting, and tested the bear case. Financial-company cash flows are not treated as directly comparable with industrial free cash flow.

### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Adobe trades at about 9.7 times trailing free cash flow despite revenue still growing. Fiscal Q2 revenue rose 13% year over year to a record $6.62 billion, total ARR rose 12.5% to $27.10 billion, and management raised full-year revenue and non-GAAP EPS targets. The market is pricing a sharp deterioration in the franchise, giving the stock a credible margin of safety if AI expands rather than destroys Adobe's monetization.

**Why it is quality.** Subscription revenue, high switching costs in professional creative workflows, an 89% gross margin, a 41% trailing FCF margin, and 61% ROIC are rare at this valuation. Adobe generated $10.28 billion of trailing FCF, has debt/FCF below 1x, and reduced its share count 5.8% over the past year.

**Risks and disconfirming evidence.** Generative-AI tools could compress pricing, reduce seat growth, or weaken Creative Cloud's distribution advantage. A sustained fall in ARR growth, lower cash conversion, or AI adoption that benefits competitors more than Adobe would invalidate the thesis. Management transition risk and stock-based compensation also deserve monitoring.

Sources: [current statistics](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/adbe/statistics/); [Adobe Q2 FY2026 release](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/11606202/a5543arefgt.pdf); [Adobe Q2 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000112/adbe-20260529.htm).

### 2. Ameriprise Financial (AMP)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At 13.3 times trailing earnings and 11.3 times forward estimates, Ameriprise is priced like a mature financial even though its advice and wealth platform continues to compound client assets and productivity. The share count fell 5.2% year over year, adding per-share value at a reasonable multiple.

**Why it is quality.** Q1 adjusted operating EPS increased 19% to $11.26, assets under management, administration and advisement grew 12% to $1.7 trillion, and adjusted operating ROE excluding AOCI was 54.3%. The advice network, recurring fee base, insurance cash generation, and disciplined capital return form a durable combination. Ameriprise returned 88% of Q1 operating earnings and raised its dividend 6%.

**Risks and disconfirming evidence.** Earnings are sensitive to equity markets, interest rates, adviser retention, and asset-management outflows. Insurance guarantees and market-risk benefits add accounting and tail-risk complexity. Persistent organic outflows, falling adviser productivity, or capital return funded by weakening balance-sheet strength would lower conviction.

Sources: [current statistics](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/amp/statistics/); [Ameriprise Q1 2026 results](https://ir.ameriprise.com/news/news-details/2026/Ameriprise-Financial-Reports-First-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx); [quarterly results](https://ir.ameriprise.com/financials/quarterly-results/default.aspx).

### 3. PayPal (PYPL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** PayPal trades at 10.9 times earnings and 7.5 times trailing FCF, with an 8.8% combined dividend-and-buyback yield. Q2 revenue of $8.68 billion exceeded expectations and management raised its profit outlook. The price implies little durable growth even though the platform remains profitable and cash generative.

**Why it is quality.** The two-sided network, PayPal and Venmo brands, merchant integrations, and large installed base remain defensible assets. Trailing FCF was $6.59 billion, ROIC was 22.3%, interest coverage was 12.9x, and the share count declined 7.8% year over year. Those figures provide room for imperfect execution.

**Risks and disconfirming evidence.** Branded checkout faces intense competition, lower-margin processing can dilute economics, and transaction-margin pressure may prove structural. New leadership must show that buybacks are accompanied by stable branded growth and better execution. Continued margin contraction or customer engagement erosion would make the low multiple deserved.

Sources: [current statistics](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/pypl/statistics/); [PayPal Q1 2026 filing and release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633917/000163391726000065/pypl1q-26earningsrelease.htm); [PayPal news releases](https://newsroom.paypal-corp.com/news?l=100).

### 4. The Cigna Group (CI)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Cigna trades at 8.9 times forward earnings and 8.2 times trailing FCF. First-quarter adjusted EPS rose 16% to $7.79, and management raised its 2026 adjusted EPS outlook to at least $30.35. The valuation discounts a material amount of regulatory and medical-cost risk.

**Why it is quality.** Evernorth's pharmacy, specialty, and care services diversify Cigna away from pure underwriting. Q1 total revenue rose 5% to $68.5 billion; specialty and care services adjusted profit rose 20%. Trailing ROIC was 13.4%, FCF was $9.11 billion, and the company continues to reduce its share count.

**Risks and disconfirming evidence.** PBM reform, drug-pricing scrutiny, customer concentration, medical-cost inflation, reserve errors, and $31.9 billion of debt are meaningful. Cash flow includes working-capital timing, so normalized conversion must be checked. A loss of large Evernorth clients or sustained compression in pharmacy-benefit economics would disconfirm the thesis.

Sources: [current statistics](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ci/statistics/); [Cigna Q1 2026 results](https://newsroom.thecignagroup.com/2026-04-30-The-Cigna-Group-Reports-Strong-First-Quarter-2026-Results%2C-Raises-2026-Outlook); [Cigna Q1 SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1739940/000114036126017971/ef20071317_ex99-1.htm).

### 5. H&R Block (HRB)

**Why it looks undervalued.** H&R Block trades at 8.0 times earnings and 7.5 times FCF, while returning capital through a 3.7% dividend yield and a 5.7% buyback yield. Fiscal Q3 revenue increased 5.3%. The valuation appears to assume a stagnant or shrinking filing franchise.

**Why it is quality.** Tax preparation is recurring, deadline-driven, brand-sensitive, and supported by a large physical and digital distribution network. The capital-light model produced a 19.5% FCF margin and 36.1% ROIC. Debt/FCF of 2.7x is manageable, though not trivial.

**Risks and disconfirming evidence.** Free and AI-assisted filing, government tax simplification, pricing pressure, and seasonality could erode the moat. Debt and negative book equity reflect aggressive capital returns. Full-year results are due August 11; weaker assisted-filing volumes or cash generation would warrant immediate reassessment.

Sources: [current statistics](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/hrb/statistics/); [H&R Block fiscal Q3 2026 results](https://investors.hrblock.com/news-releases/news-release-details/hr-block-reports-fiscal-2026-third-quarter-results).

### 6. Comcast (CMCSA)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Comcast trades near book value, at 8.0 times earnings and 8.3 times enterprise value to trailing FCF. Q2 generated $4.6 billion of FCF. Broadband losses improved year over year, wireless additions reached a record 448,000, and Peacock achieved its first quarterly profit.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Comcast retains valuable last-mile connectivity, a growing wireless bundle, a high-margin business-services unit, major media franchises, and theme-park assets. Business-services EBITDA grew 5% with a 56.7% margin in Q2. The mix provides more resilience than the collapsing-cable narrative suggests.

**Risks and disconfirming evidence.** This is the least clean quality idea in the group. Domestic broadband revenue fell 5.5%, residential platform EBITDA fell 8%, total debt was $90.4 billion, and business separations add execution risk. Management paused buybacks during the planned NBCUniversal/Sky separation. Continued broadband erosion or a weak post-separation balance sheet could turn apparent value into a trap.

Sources: [current statistics](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/cmcsa/statistics/); [Comcast Q2 2026 results](https://www.cmcsa.com/node/45906).

## Conclusion

**Adobe is the strongest current quality-value candidate**: its valuation implies severe franchise erosion that is not yet visible in ARR, revenue, margins, or cash flow. **Ameriprise** is the best financial compounder at a reasonable price, while **PayPal** offers the highest combination of cash yield and balance-sheet flexibility among the turnaround candidates. **Cigna** is the preferred defensive value, **H&R Block** is a compact cash-return idea pending August 11 results, and **Comcast** is attractive only with a larger margin of safety because its core broadband economics are under pressure.
