### 1. Chubb (CB)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At 12.4 times trailing earnings, Chubb is priced well below the broad market despite producing strong underwriting and investment results. The shares equal about 1.79 times June 30 book value of $195.45 per share. That is not deep book-value cheapness, but it is reasonable for an insurer earning mid-teens ROE and compounding tangible book value at a double-digit rate.

**Why it is quality.** Q2 core operating income rose 14.6% to $2.84 billion. The P&C combined ratio improved to 83.8%, annualized ROE was 15.3%, core operating ROTE was 21.2%, and tangible book value per share rose 17.1% year over year. Global diversification, disciplined underwriting, and a $175 billion invested asset base support durable compounding.

**Risks and disconfirming evidence.** Catastrophe losses, reserve development, social inflation, pricing cycles, and investment-market volatility can make current profitability look better than normalized results. North America commercial premiums declined 2.3% in Q2. A sustained combined ratio above the mid-90s, adverse reserve development, or weakening book-value growth would undermine the thesis.

### 2. lululemon athletica (LULU)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Lululemon trades at 10.4 times trailing earnings and about 11.6 times the midpoint of fiscal 2026 EPS guidance of $10.95-$11.15. That multiple discounts a durable North American brand impairment and margin reset. Even a partial stabilization in full-price selling could produce material upside without requiring a return to peak growth.

**Why it is quality.** The company still owns a premium global brand, controls much of its distribution, and ended Q1 with $1.5 billion of cash. Fiscal 2025 revenue reached $11.1 billion and the company ended that year with no debt. International growth was 17% in fiscal Q4, providing a second growth engine while the Americas business is repaired.

**Risks and disconfirming evidence.** Fiscal 2026 revenue guidance calls for flat to down 1% sales. Q1 operating income fell to $276.9 million from $438.6 million and operating margin fell to 11.2% from 18.5%. Fashion missteps, discounting, competition, tariffs, inventory risk, and leadership transition could make the lower earnings base persistent. Continued negative Americas sales plus further guidance cuts would turn this into a value trap.

### 3. Zoetis (ZTS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Zoetis closed at $72.66, or 11.9 times trailing earnings, after another 6% decline on August 7. That is an unusually low multiple for the leading global animal-health company and appears to discount prolonged weakness in US companion-animal demand and a significant loss of franchise economics.

**Why it is quality.** Zoetis has nearly 75 years of animal-health experience, a broad medicines, vaccines, diagnostics, and biopharmaceutical portfolio, and sales reach in more than 100 countries. Q1 revenue still rose 3% to $2.3 billion and adjusted EPS rose 9% to $1.53 despite the US slowdown. Its specialized R&D, veterinary channels, regulated products, and global scale remain meaningful competitive assets.

**Risks and disconfirming evidence.** This is the least settled thesis in the top five. In Q1, US revenue fell 8% and companion-animal sales fell 11% amid fewer veterinary visits, price sensitivity, generics, and greater competition in dermatology and parasiticides. The company held its Q2 call on August 6, but the detailed release was not reliably retrievable during this run; that evidence gap lowers confidence. Further guidance cuts, continued US share loss, or weaker cash conversion would invalidate the apparent bargain.

### 4. Comcast (CMCSA)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Comcast trades at 8.2 times trailing earnings. Q2 free cash flow was $4.60 billion, equal to about 5.1% of the current $90.0 billion market capitalization in a single quarter. The price assumes continuing erosion in broadband and content economics and gives limited credit to wireless, business services, studios, and parks.

**Why it is quality.** Comcast retains valuable last-mile infrastructure, a high-margin business-services franchise, a growing wireless bundle, and differentiated media and entertainment assets. Q2 wireless net additions reached a record 448,000, business-services EBITDA rose 5% with a 56.7% margin, and Peacock recorded its first quarterly profit.

**Risks and disconfirming evidence.** Adjusted EPS fell 16.7% in Q2, broadband remains under pressure, capital expenditures increased 8.3%, and the planned separation of NBCUniversal and Sky adds execution and balance-sheet uncertainty. The large debt load limits flexibility. Persistent broadband revenue decline, weakening FCF after separation, or poor capital allocation would make the low P/E deserved.

Sources: [current price and valuation](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/cmcsa/statistics/); [Comcast Q2 2026 results](https://www.cmcsa.com/node/45906).

### 5. GE HealthCare Technologies (GEHC)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $71.77, GE HealthCare trades at 16.5 times trailing earnings and about 14.6 times the midpoint of full-year adjusted EPS guidance of $4.80-$5.00. Management's approximately $1.6 billion FCF outlook implies a 4.9% yield on the current market value. The discount is modest, but current order evidence makes the valuation more credible than many deeper statistical bargains.

**Why it is quality.** Q2 revenue grew 5.7%, organic orders grew a record 11.1%, book-to-bill was 1.15 times, and backlog reached $23.9 billion. The installed base, regulated clinical workflows, service relationships, imaging technology, and recurring demand for diagnostics provide durable customer ties. Management reaffirmed 2026 guidance.

**Risks and disconfirming evidence.** Q2 FCF was only $68 million and first-half FCF was $180 million, so achieving the full-year target requires a heavily back-end-weighted cash result. Patient Care Solutions remains challenged, and inflation, supply constraints, tariffs, hospital capital budgets, and spin-off debt add risk. Weak order conversion or a material FCF miss would lower conviction.
