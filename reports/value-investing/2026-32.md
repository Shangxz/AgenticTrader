### 1. Accenture (ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Accenture trades at 13.7 times trailing earnings and 8.3 times trailing FCF after a roughly 34% one-year decline. The resulting 12% FCF yield prices in a durable growth and margin impairment even though fiscal 2026 local-currency revenue is still expected to grow 3%-4%, or 4%-5% excluding the estimated one-point drag from the US federal business.

**Why it is quality.** Accenture has scale, embedded enterprise relationships, a broad delivery network, and a recurring role in complex technology transformations. It generated $12.58 billion of trailing FCF at a 17.2% margin, earned 27.1% ROIC, held $1.8 billion of net cash, and reduced shares 1.5% year over year. Those economics provide room for slower consulting demand and continued AI investment.

**Risks and disconfirming evidence.** Generative AI may automate parts of software implementation and knowledge work, pressure billing rates, or enable clients to bring more work in-house. New bookings of $19.3 billion in fiscal Q3 were below the prior-year quarter, and US federal spending is already a drag. Sustained negative organic growth, falling bookings, weaker cash conversion, or margin compression despite restructuring would weaken the thesis.

Sources: [current valuation and operating statistics](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/acn/statistics/); [Accenture fiscal Q3 2026 results](https://newsroom.accenture.com/news/2026/accenture-reports-third-quarter-fiscal-2026-results).

### 2. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Adobe trades near 10 times trailing FCF and 14.8 times trailing earnings while fiscal Q2 revenue grew 13% and total ARR grew 12.5%. The stock still discounts substantial franchise erosion despite roughly $10.3 billion of trailing FCF and continued double-digit operating growth.

**Why it is quality.** Creative Cloud's professional workflows, file formats, distribution, and switching costs support a durable subscription model. Trailing ROIC is 60.7%, FCF margin is 40.8%, debt is less than one year's FCF, and the share count fell 5.8% year over year.

**Risks and disconfirming evidence.** Generative AI may lower creation costs, weaken seat-based pricing, or shift value to model and platform vendors. The current ratio is below 1, management transition adds execution risk, and stock-based compensation remains material. Falling ARR growth, sustained margin compression, or weak monetization of AI features would undermine the thesis.

Sources: [current valuation and operating statistics](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/adbe/statistics/); [Adobe Q2 FY2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000109/adbeex991q226.htm); [Adobe Q2 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000112/adbe-20260529.htm).

### 3. Deckers Outdoor (DECK)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Deckers trades at 14.0 times earnings and 12.1 times FCF despite both HOKA and UGG continuing to grow. Fiscal Q1 2027 revenue rose 5.7%, management raised full-year EPS guidance to $7.35-$7.50, and the enterprise value is reduced by a substantial net-cash position.

**Why it is quality.** HOKA has a credible performance-running position and UGG has shown unusual brand longevity. Deckers produced a 22.7% trailing operating margin, 20.2% FCF margin, and very high returns on capital. It held $1.60 billion of cash with no outstanding borrowings at quarter-end and repurchased $338 million of shares in Q1; the trailing share count is down 5.7%.

**Risks and disconfirming evidence.** Footwear is fashion-sensitive, the company relies heavily on two brands, tariffs and freight can pressure margins, and HOKA growth slowed to 7.7% in Q1. SG&A growth caused quarterly operating income to decline even as sales rose. Negative HOKA growth, weaker full-price sell-through, inventory growth ahead of sales, or sustained operating-margin contraction would invalidate the quality case.

Sources: [current valuation and operating statistics](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/deck/statistics/); [Deckers fiscal Q1 2027 results](https://ir.deckers.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000910521-26-000015/deckex991pressrelease-6302.htm).

### 4. lululemon athletica (LULU)

**Why it is quality.** The company retains a premium global brand, direct-to-consumer distribution, a 55.7% gross margin, 27.7% ROIC, and a strong Altman Z-score. The share count declined 3.9% over the past year.

### 5. PayPal (PYPL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** PayPal trades near 11 times earnings and roughly 7.5 times trailing FCF, offering about a 13% FCF yield. Q2 revenue grew to $8.68 billion, results exceeded expectations, and management raised its profit outlook. The price still assumes little durable growth and continued pressure on transaction economics.

Q1 total revenue rose 5% to $68.5 billion; specialty and care services adjusted profit rose 20%. Trailing ROIC was 13.4%, FCF was $9.11 billion, and the company continues to reduce its share count.

**Risks and disconfirming evidence.** PBM reform, drug-pricing scrutiny, customer concentration, medical-cost inflation, reserve errors, and $31.9 billion of debt are meaningful. Cash flow includes working-capital timing, so normalized conversion must be checked. A loss of large Evernorth clients or sustained compression in pharmacy-benefit economics would disconfirm the thesis.

### 5. H&R Block (HRB)

**Why it looks undervalued.** H&R Block trades at 8.0 times earnings and 7.5 times FCF, while returning capital through a 3.7% dividend yield and a 5.7% buyback yield. Fiscal Q3 revenue increased 5.3%. The valuation appears to assume a stagnant or shrinking filing franchise.

**Why it is quality.** Tax preparation is recurring, deadline-driven, brand-sensitive, and supported by a large physical and digital distribution network. The capital-light model produced a 19.5% FCF margin and 36.1% ROIC. Debt/FCF of 2.7x is manageable, though not trivial.

**Risks and disconfirming evidence.** Free and AI-assisted filing, government tax simplification, pricing pressure, and seasonality could erode the moat. Debt and negative book equity reflect aggressive capital returns. Full-year results are due August 11; weaker assisted-filing volumes or cash generation would warrant immediate reassessment.

### 6. Comcast (CMCSA)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Comcast trades near book value, at 8.0 times earnings and 8.3 times enterprise value to trailing FCF. Q2 generated $4.6 billion of FCF. Broadband losses improved year over year, wireless additions reached a record 448,000, and Peacock achieved its first quarterly profit.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Comcast retains valuable last-mile connectivity, a growing wireless bundle, a high-margin business-services unit, major media franchises, and theme-park assets. Business-services EBITDA grew 5% with a 56.7% margin in Q2. The mix provides more resilience than the collapsing-cable narrative suggests.

**Risks and disconfirming evidence.** This is the least clean quality idea in the group. Domestic broadband revenue fell 5.5%, residential platform EBITDA fell 8%, total debt was $90.4 billion, and business separations add execution risk. Management paused buybacks during the planned NBCUniversal/Sky separation. Continued broadband erosion or a weak post-separation balance sheet could turn apparent value into a trap.
