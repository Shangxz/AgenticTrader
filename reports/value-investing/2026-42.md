Today's screen therefore required both a valuation cushion and evidence that the business can still compound book value, recurring revenue, cash flow or unit economics. #### 1. Yum China (YUMC)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The shares trade near 15.8x trailing earnings and about 56% of Morningstar's $77 fair-value estimate. That is a restrained price for a consumer franchise still producing positive same-store sales, rapid unit growth and double-digit reported profit growth. Management's planned $1.5 billion of 2026 capital returns is roughly 10% of the market value cited at the time of the July release. ([current quote](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/YUMC/); [Morningstar valuation](https://www.morningstar.com.au/stocks/10-best-us-companies-invest-now-2))

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 system sales grew 6%, same-store sales rose 1%, operating profit increased 14%, and operating margin expanded for a ninth consecutive quarter. The company opened a record 560 net new stores in the quarter, with 41% franchised, while same-store transactions increased for the 14th consecutive quarter. Scale in sourcing, delivery and restaurant operations supports KFC and Pizza Hut in a fragmented market. ([Yum China Q2 results](https://ir.yumchina.com/news-releases/news-release-details/yum-china-reports-second-quarter-2026-results/))

**Why the market may be wrong.** The valuation appears to extrapolate weak Chinese consumer spending and price-sensitive ordering. Continued transaction growth, franchise-led expansion and ownership of the Pizza Hut brand in mainland China can support per-share cash-flow growth even if the macro recovery remains slow.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** This is China exposure: consumer weakness, food safety, regulation, currency translation and geopolitical risk can overwhelm company execution. Average ticket declined as value offerings and smaller orders increased. The thesis weakens if same-store transactions turn negative, new-store returns lengthen materially beyond the stated two-to-three-year target, or capital returns consume cash needed for operations.

#### 2. Arch Capital Group (ACGL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At about 7.6x trailing earnings and 1.42x June book value of $68.04 per share, Arch's price requires neither a heroic terminal multiple nor sustained peak profitability. The relevant anchor is through-cycle book-value growth, not free cash flow.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 operating ROE was 15.3%; consolidated combined ratio was 83.5%, or 82.5% excluding catastrophe activity and prior-year development; and book value per share rose 2.8% sequentially. Insurance, reinsurance and mortgage insurance diversify the underwriting engine, and management repurchased $1.2 billion of shares. ([Arch Q2 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/947484/000094748426000118/ex-991release63026.htm); [Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/947484/000094748426000124/acgl-20260630.htm))

**Why the market may be wrong.** Investors appear to be capitalizing current results as the end of a favorable specialty-insurance cycle. Arch's underwriting discipline should allow acceptable book-value compounding even as pricing and reserve releases normalize.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Q2 underwriting income fell almost 20%, insurance net premiums written declined 5.1%, favorable reserve development helped results, and catastrophes can overwhelm any quarter. The thesis weakens if the normalized combined ratio moves persistently toward the mid-90s or book value stops compounding.

#### 3. Broadridge Financial Solutions (BR)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At roughly 18.0x trailing earnings, Broadridge is not statistically deep value, but the multiple is modest relative to its recurring-revenue durability and 8%-12% fiscal 2027 adjusted EPS growth target. Fiscal 2026 FCF of $1.23 billion represents about a 6.3% yield on the current equity value, while the current price is roughly one-third below Morningstar's $250 estimate. ([current quote](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/BR/); [Morningstar valuation](https://www.morningstar.com.au/stocks/10-best-us-companies-invest-now-2))

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal 2026 recurring revenue grew 8%, adjusted EPS grew 12%, FCF conversion was 110%, and closed sales reached $305 million. Its regulated proxy-processing position and securities-processing platforms are embedded in customer workflows, creating switching costs and highly recurring demand. Management raised the annual dividend 12%, the 20th consecutive increase. ([Broadridge FY2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1383312/000138331226000022/ex991earningsrelease4q2026.htm))

**Why the market may be wrong.** The market may be underestimating the persistence of regulated communications and the ability to grow adjacent capital-markets infrastructure. Mid-single-digit recurring growth plus margin discipline can compound value without requiring a premium software multiple.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Client concentration, acquisition integration, market-volume sensitivity and technology modernization can pressure returns. The adjusted figures exclude recurring acquired-intangible amortization. The thesis weakens if recurring-revenue growth falls below mid-single digits or closed sales fail to convert to revenue and cash.

#### 4. Otis Worldwide (OTIS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Otis trades at about 17.4x trailing earnings and roughly 64% of Morningstar's $105 fair-value estimate. Management's $1.50-$1.55 billion adjusted FCF outlook implies a roughly 5.9% yield at the midpoint, reasonable for a service-heavy industrial franchise. ([current quote](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/OTIS/); [Morningstar valuation](https://www.morningstar.com.au/stocks/10-best-us-companies-invest-now-2))

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Otis maintains about 2.5 million customer units, the industry's largest service portfolio. Q2 Service organic sales rose 9%, including 24% modernization growth; total company organic sales grew 6%; and first-half adjusted FCF rose to $562 million from $429 million. The installed base, safety requirements and technician network support recurring service revenue and switching costs. ([Otis Q2 results](https://www.otisinvestors.com/news/news-details/2026/OTIS-REPORTS-SECOND-QUARTER-2026-RESULTS/default.aspx))

**Why the market may be wrong.** China new-equipment weakness obscures a larger, more profitable service and modernization engine. Aging elevator stock and digital diagnostics can extend that recurring runway.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** New-equipment orders fell 5% in constant currency, including a high-teens decline in China, and Service margin contracted 170 basis points. Adjusted EPS was down 4%. The thesis weakens if Service organic growth falls below mid-single digits or labor and mix prevent margin recovery.

#### 5. Clorox (CLX)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The stock trades around 13.7x trailing earnings and about 55% of Morningstar's $154 estimate. The $5.00 annualized dividend is a roughly 5.9% yield at today's price, although dividend coverage must improve as operations normalize. ([current quote](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/CLX/); [Morningstar valuation](https://www.morningstar.com.au/stocks/10-best-us-companies-invest-now-2))

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Clorox owns leading household brands with entrenched retail distribution and continues to invest heavily behind advertising, innovation and digital capabilities. Its ERP transition is complete, GOJO adds Purell, and management expects fiscal 2027 adjusted EPS of $5.70-$6.00, up 3%-8%. The branded portfolio and historically mid-40s gross margin support a recovery case rather than a purely low-multiple case. ([Clorox FY2026 results and FY2027 outlook](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/21076/000002107626000028/ex991-pressreleasedatedaug.htm))

**Why the market may be wrong.** Fiscal 2026 comparisons were distorted by the retailer inventory pull-forward and subsequent ERP-related drawdown. Lapping that effect, integrating GOJO and realizing productivity savings can reveal steadier underlying demand.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Fiscal 2026 sales fell 5%, gross margin declined 290 basis points to 42.3%, net earnings fell 28%, and operating cash flow dropped to $612 million. Private label, promotion and commodity inflation remain real. The thesis weakens if organic growth excluding the ERP lap is negative or gross margin stays near 42% after temporary items fade.

#### 6. Comcast (CMCSA)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Comcast trades near 6.7x trailing earnings. First-half FCF of $8.5 billion equals about 11.5% of the current $74.1 billion market value before considering the year's second half; this is not a forecast, but it shows how much deterioration the price already discounts. ([current quote](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/CMCSA/); [Comcast Q2 results](https://www.cmcsa.com/node/45906))

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 FCF was $4.6 billion, wireless additions hit a record 448,000, total lines exceeded 10 million, and Peacock achieved quarterly profitability. Business Services revenue grew 3.7% with a 56.7% EBITDA margin. Broadband scale, local infrastructure, wireless bundling and premium media assets still produce substantial cash.

**Why the market may be wrong.** The planned separation of NBCUniversal and Sky can make the connectivity cash engine easier to value and give both businesses clearer capital-allocation mandates. Better broadband churn and wireless penetration offer a path to stabilization.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Q2 pro forma EBITDA fell 5.3%, adjusted EPS fell 16.7%, residential connectivity revenue declined, leverage is meaningful, and the separation adds execution and allocation uncertainty. Repurchases are paused. The thesis weakens if broadband losses reaccelerate, post-separation debt is unfavorable, or the media assets require sustained cash support.

### Changes from earlier updates this week

- **Arch Capital returns at No. 2.** It was the October 12 No. 2 and remains the week's most persistent high-conviction idea; current underwriting and book-value evidence still support the thesis.
- **Comcast moves from near-miss to No. 6.** The same structural risks remain, but the extreme valuation, first-half cash generation and separation catalyst now clear the threshold at medium confidence.

### October 14 conclusion

**Yum China and Arch Capital are today's strongest candidates.** Yum China combines current profit growth, scale-driven unit expansion and large capital returns with the widest operating momentum among the discounted franchises. Arch provides the lowest valuation against demonstrated through-cycle underwriting quality. **Broadridge and Otis** offer the most dependable recurring-revenue economics, with less raw yield but clearer durability. **Clorox and Comcast** provide larger apparent discounts but require evidence that margin pressure and structural erosion, respectively, are stabilizing.

No estimate here is a certainty about intrinsic value. This report is research support, not personal financial advice; prices, operating conditions and the evidence behind each thesis can change quickly.

### 1. GoDaddy (GDDY)

Current ARPU, margin and cash-flow trends instead suggest GoDaddy can monetize its installed base and retire shares faster than the core business grows.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Customer growth is nearly flat; Core revenue is expected to grow only in the low single digits; $2.7 billion of net debt reduces flexibility; and AI-native discovery or commerce tools could bypass domains and standalone websites. The thesis weakens if ARR/ARPU growth stalls or buybacks are funded with rising leverage.

### 2. Arch Capital Group (ACGL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At roughly 7.5x trailing earnings and about 1.40x June 30 book value of $68.04 per share, the price does not demand heroic assumptions for an insurer generating a 15.3% annualized operating ROE.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2's consolidated combined ratio was 83.5%; even excluding catastrophe activity and prior-year development, it was 82.5%. Book value per share rose 2.8% sequentially, and the company repurchased $1.2 billion of shares. Its insurance, reinsurance and mortgage platforms diversify the underwriting engine. ([Q2 results](https://ir.archgroup.com/news/news-details/2026/Arch-Capital-Group-Ltd--Reports-2026-Second-Quarter-Results/default.aspx); [Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/947484/000094748426000124/acgl-20260630.htm))

**Mispricing thesis.** Investors appear to be capitalizing current earnings as if specialty-insurance pricing and reserve releases are near a sharp peak. Arch's underwriting discipline and diversified franchise should allow acceptable book-value compounding even as the cycle normalizes.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Premiums and underwriting income declined year over year, favorable reserve development helped results, and catastrophe losses can overwhelm a quarter. The thesis weakens if the normalized combined ratio moves persistently toward the mid-90s or book-value growth stalls. For an insurer, P/B and through-cycle ROE deserve more weight than FCF.

### 3. Leidos (LDOS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The shares trade around 11.3x trailing earnings and about 9.9x the midpoint of management's $12.20-$12.50 adjusted 2026 EPS guidance. Guided operating cash flow of approximately $1.85 billion equals about 12.1% of the current market capitalization before capital spending.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue grew 7%; the company produced $761 million of quarterly FCF; total backlog was $48.7 billion; funded backlog rose 44% year over year; and trailing book-to-bill was 1.1x. Government mission, defense, intelligence and health contracts provide unusually high revenue visibility. ([Q2 results and guidance](https://investors.leidos.com/news-releases/news-release-details/leidos-delivers-strong-second-quarter-and-enhances-full-year))

**Mispricing thesis.** The stock seems priced as a no-growth government contractor despite growing backlog and credible defense, cyber and energy-infrastructure demand. Modest execution plus debt reduction could produce an adequate return without multiple expansion.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Debt was $6.0 billion after acquisition activity; federal budget shifts, contract losses or shutdowns can delay awards and cash; Q2 cash conversion benefited from working-capital timing; and adjusted EPS excludes acquisition and restructuring items. The thesis weakens if book-to-bill falls below 1.0 for several quarters or debt does not decline.

### 4. SkyWest (SKYW)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The stock trades around 9.6x trailing earnings. A September quality-value screen estimated a 23.2% FCF yield, but aircraft deliveries and financing make any single-year airline cash-flow yield unusually noisy; the earnings multiple is the more conservative anchor. ([Cash-yield screen](https://intrinsiqq.com/blog/best-value-stocks))

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Unlike a conventional airline, most capacity-purchase flying is compensated at contracted rates based on block hours, departures and aircraft, while major-airline partners control fares and seat inventory. Q2 revenue grew 7%, production rose 5%, the company secured 11 additional E175s for American, and the board added $250 million to repurchase authorization. ([Q2 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/793733/000110465926086251/skyw-20260723xex99d1.htm); [Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/793733/000110465926086641/skyw-20260630x10q.htm))

**Mispricing thesis.** The market applies an airline discount even though contracted flying shifts much of the fare risk to larger partners. Higher utilization and new E175 placements can support earnings and cash generation.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** The customer base is concentrated, aircraft ownership is capital-intensive, pilot supply and maintenance can constrain production, and prorate routes retain fuel and fare risk. Q2 net income declined year over year. The thesis weakens if contract economics deteriorate or new-aircraft capex fails to earn adequate returns.

### 5. lululemon athletica (LULU)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At about $94.50, the stock is roughly 9.8x the midpoint of 2026 EPS guidance of $9.48-$9.73, or about 10.8x after subtracting the stated $0.86 tariff-refund benefit. That valuation embeds a substantial permanent impairment to a once-premium brand.

**Why it still qualifies, with lower confidence.** The company retained $1.4 billion of cash, inventory dollars fell 1% and units fell 7%, and it repurchased $330 million of stock in Q2. The brand still supports a high reported gross margin and a global store base, while a new CEO and leadership structure create a concrete reset path. ([Q2 results](https://corporate.lululemon.com/newsroom/press-releases/2026/09-03-2026-210528733); [Oct. 7 leadership update](https://corporate.lululemon.com/newsroom/press-releases))

**Mispricing thesis.** The price assumes recent Americas weakness and product fatigue will persist. Even partial stabilization, supported by clean inventory and disciplined repurchases, could justify a materially higher normalized multiple.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** This is the closest name to a value trap: Q2 revenue fell 4%, comparable sales fell 9%, Americas revenue fell 8%, and management expects Q3 revenue down 10%-11%. Q2 EPS included a non-recurring tariff benefit. Confidence should rise only after sequential sales and underlying margin stabilization.

## Conclusion

**Strongest current candidates: GoDaddy and Arch Capital.** GoDaddy offers the clearest combination of recurring revenue, improving margins, a double-digit FCF yield and aggressive per-share value creation. Arch offers strong through-cycle underwriting economics at a restrained earnings and book-value multiple. **Leidos** is the best lower-volatility follow-up, supported by backlog and government demand. **SkyWest** is attractive but more capital-cycle sensitive. **lululemon** has the widest apparent upside and the weakest evidence of stabilization, so it belongs in a smaller, watch-for-confirmation category.

No estimate here is a certainty about intrinsic value. Prices, guidance and operating conditions can change quickly; each thesis includes explicit evidence that would invalidate it.
