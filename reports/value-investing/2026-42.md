### 1. GoDaddy (GDDY)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At a market capitalization of about $13.7 billion, management's reaffirmed 2026 free-cash-flow target of approximately $1.8 billion implies an equity FCF yield near 13.2%. The stock also trades near 15.5x trailing earnings. This is a much better valuation than is typical for a recurring-revenue software and web-services platform producing mid-single-digit growth.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 7%, operating income rose 29%, FCF rose 13%, annualized recurring revenue reached $4.42 billion and ARPU rose 8.7%. Applications & Commerce revenue grew 11%. Management had reduced fully diluted shares by 7% gross year to date through July 29. Net debt of $2.7 billion is meaningful but only about 1.5x the 2026 FCF target. ([Q2 filing and release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1609711/000160971126000087/gddyex991-20260630xq2earni.htm))

**Mispricing thesis.** The market appears to be discounting mature domain/hosting growth and the risk that AI platforms weaken the traditional website funnel. Current ARPU, margin and cash-flow trends instead suggest GoDaddy can monetize its installed base and retire shares faster than the core business grows.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Customer growth is nearly flat; Core revenue is expected to grow only in the low single digits; $2.7 billion of net debt reduces flexibility; and AI-native discovery or commerce tools could bypass domains and standalone websites. The thesis weakens if ARR/ARPU growth stalls or buybacks are funded with rising leverage.

### 2. Arch Capital Group (ACGL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At roughly 7.5x trailing earnings and about 1.40x June 30 book value of $68.04 per share, the price does not demand heroic assumptions for an insurer generating a 15.3% annualized operating ROE.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2's consolidated combined ratio was 83.5%; even excluding catastrophe activity and prior-year development, it was 82.5%. Book value per share rose 2.8% sequentially, and the company repurchased $1.2 billion of shares. Its insurance, reinsurance and mortgage platforms diversify the underwriting engine. ([Q2 results](https://ir.archgroup.com/news/news-details/2026/Arch-Capital-Group-Ltd--Reports-2026-Second-Quarter-Results/default.aspx); [Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/947484/000094748426000124/acgl-20260630.htm))

**Mispricing thesis.** Investors appear to be capitalizing current earnings as if specialty-insurance pricing and reserve releases are near a sharp peak. Arch's underwriting discipline and diversified franchise should allow acceptable book-value compounding even as the cycle normalizes.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Premiums and underwriting income declined year over year, favorable reserve development helped results, and catastrophe losses can overwhelm a quarter. The thesis weakens if the normalized combined ratio moves persistently toward the mid-90s or book-value growth stalls. For an insurer, P/B and through-cycle ROE deserve more weight than FCF.

### 3. Leidos (LDOS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The shares trade around 11.3x trailing earnings and about 9.9x the midpoint of management's $12.20-$12.50 adjusted 2026 EPS guidance. Guided operating cash flow of approximately $1.85 billion equals about 12.1% of the current market capitalization before capital spending.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue grew 7%; the company produced $761 million of quarterly FCF; total backlog was $48.7 billion; funded backlog rose 44% year over year; and trailing book-to-bill was 1.1x. Government mission, defense, intelligence and health contracts provide unusually high revenue visibility. ([Q2 results and guidance](https://investors.leidos.com/news-releases/news-release-details/leidos-delivers-strong-second-quarter-and-enhances-full-year))

**Mispricing thesis.** The stock seems priced as a no-growth government contractor despite growing backlog and credible defense, cyber and energy-infrastructure demand. Modest execution plus debt reduction could produce an adequate return without multiple expansion.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Debt was $6.0 billion after acquisition activity; federal budget shifts, contract losses or shutdowns can delay awards and cash; Q2 cash conversion benefited from working-capital timing; and adjusted EPS excludes acquisition and restructuring items. The thesis weakens if book-to-bill falls below 1.0 for several quarters or debt does not decline.

### 4. SkyWest (SKYW)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The stock trades around 9.6x trailing earnings. A September quality-value screen estimated a 23.2% FCF yield, but aircraft deliveries and financing make any single-year airline cash-flow yield unusually noisy; the earnings multiple is the more conservative anchor. ([Cash-yield screen](https://intrinsiqq.com/blog/best-value-stocks))

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Unlike a conventional airline, most capacity-purchase flying is compensated at contracted rates based on block hours, departures and aircraft, while major-airline partners control fares and seat inventory. Q2 revenue grew 7%, production rose 5%, the company secured 11 additional E175s for American, and the board added $250 million to repurchase authorization. ([Q2 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/793733/000110465926086251/skyw-20260723xex99d1.htm); [Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/793733/000110465926086641/skyw-20260630x10q.htm))

**Mispricing thesis.** The market applies an airline discount even though contracted flying shifts much of the fare risk to larger partners. Higher utilization and new E175 placements can support earnings and cash generation.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** The customer base is concentrated, aircraft ownership is capital-intensive, pilot supply and maintenance can constrain production, and prorate routes retain fuel and fare risk. Q2 net income declined year over year. The thesis weakens if contract economics deteriorate or new-aircraft capex fails to earn adequate returns.

### 5. lululemon athletica (LULU)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At about $94.50, the stock is roughly 9.8x the midpoint of 2026 EPS guidance of $9.48-$9.73, or about 10.8x after subtracting the stated $0.86 tariff-refund benefit. That valuation embeds a substantial permanent impairment to a once-premium brand.

**Why it still qualifies, with lower confidence.** The company retained $1.4 billion of cash, inventory dollars fell 1% and units fell 7%, and it repurchased $330 million of stock in Q2. The brand still supports a high reported gross margin and a global store base, while a new CEO and leadership structure create a concrete reset path. ([Q2 results](https://corporate.lululemon.com/newsroom/press-releases/2026/09-03-2026-210528733); [Oct. 7 leadership update](https://corporate.lululemon.com/newsroom/press-releases))

**Mispricing thesis.** The price assumes recent Americas weakness and product fatigue will persist. Even partial stabilization, supported by clean inventory and disciplined repurchases, could justify a materially higher normalized multiple.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** This is the closest name to a value trap: Q2 revenue fell 4%, comparable sales fell 9%, Americas revenue fell 8%, and management expects Q3 revenue down 10%-11%. Q2 EPS included a non-recurring tariff benefit. Confidence should rise only after sequential sales and underlying margin stabilization.

## Quality guardrails and near-misses

- **Teradata (TDC):** exceptional reported cash yield, but shrinking revenue and competitive pressure from cloud-native data platforms make the durability test fail.

## Conclusion

**Strongest current candidates: GoDaddy and Arch Capital.** GoDaddy offers the clearest combination of recurring revenue, improving margins, a double-digit FCF yield and aggressive per-share value creation. Arch offers strong through-cycle underwriting economics at a restrained earnings and book-value multiple. **Leidos** is the best lower-volatility follow-up, supported by backlog and government demand. **SkyWest** is attractive but more capital-cycle sensitive. **lululemon** has the widest apparent upside and the weakest evidence of stabilization, so it belongs in a smaller, watch-for-confirmation category.

No estimate here is a certainty about intrinsic value. Prices, guidance and operating conditions can change quickly; each thesis includes explicit evidence that would invalidate it.
