### Market context

The S&P 500 remains near a record while long rates above 5% and renewed Fed-tightening expectations raise the hurdle rate for equities. That makes this a stock-picker's market: a low multiple matters only when current cash generation, competitive durability and balance-sheet capacity survive a higher discount rate. ([AP, Oct. 12](https://apnews.com/article/a9a26b9706963afe9bdeda1b2eb1f51d); [Federal Reserve minutes coverage, Oct. 7](https://apnews.com/article/b7ed20e31e1a6fa5da6d78e5fe35b0c3))

Candidate generation spanned 4,325 U.S.-listed companies using filing-based value, long-run ROIC and persistent-FCF screens, plus a separate 75-name mid-cap cash-conversion screen and large-cap cross-checks. I then removed obvious cyclically inflated, development-stage and highly levered names and re-ranked the survivors on valuation, cash conversion, returns on capital, leverage, moat durability and identifiable disconfirming evidence. #### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** A roughly $94 billion equity value against $10.59 billion of trailing FCF implies an approximately 11% FCF yield. The shares trade near 13.3x trailing GAAP earnings and 8.9x FCF despite current double-digit revenue growth. Net debt is only about $1.2 billion, so leverage does not explain the discount. ([valuation and cash-flow data](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/adbe/statistics/))

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal Q3 revenue rose 13% to a record $6.76 billion, customer-group subscription revenue rose 14%, operating cash flow reached $2.52 billion and ARR ended at $27.50 billion. GAAP operating margin was about 35%, and the installed base spans creative professionals, documents and enterprise marketing workflows. Those economics support high switching costs and exceptional cash conversion. ([Adobe Q3 release](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/01906202/au56y4ter.pdf))

**Why the market may be wrong.** The price appears to capitalize AI-native creative tools as a permanent impairment. Current subscription and AI-first ARR growth instead indicate that Adobe can remain a distribution and workflow layer even if individual content-generation features commoditize.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Generative tools could reduce pricing power, freemium users may not convert, and the December CEO transition adds execution risk. Heavy repurchases only create value if the moat survives. The thesis weakens if ARR growth falls below mid-single digits, renewal economics deteriorate, or cash flow decouples from reported revenue.

#### 2. Cirrus Logic (CRUS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Cirrus trades near 13.0x earnings, 9.3x TTM FCF and about 7.5x EV/FCF. Cash exceeds debt by roughly $1 billion, and current ROIC is about 28%. This is an unusually conservative price for a profitable analog/mixed-signal designer. ([current ratios and returns](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/crus/financials/ratios/))

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal Q1 2027 revenue was a record $459.7 million, GAAP gross margin was 52.6%, and next-quarter revenue guidance was $510-$570 million. Trailing operating cash flow was $598.6 million, while the asset-light model requires little capital spending. Design expertise in low-power, high-precision components and deep integration with flagship devices create real technical switching costs. ([Cirrus Q1 results](https://investor.cirrus.com/news-and-events/investor-news/news-details/2026/Cirrus-Logic-Reports-Record-Fiscal-First-Quarter-Revenue-of-460-Million/default.aspx))

**Why the market may be wrong.** The valuation largely treats Cirrus as a no-growth single-customer component supplier. Continued content gains plus early PC and general-market designs could produce acceptable growth without requiring a richer multiple.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Customer and product concentration are extreme, smartphone cycles are volatile, and a lost socket or customer insourcing decision could impair earnings quickly. The thesis weakens if diversification efforts remain immaterial or gross margin falls structurally below 50%.

#### 3. The Cigna Group (CI)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Cigna trades at roughly 11.7x earnings and 10.9x enterprise value to trailing FCF. TTM FCF is approximately $9.11 billion against a market value near $75 billion; current ROIC is about 13.4% and net debt/EBITDA about 1.9x. ([current valuation and operating data](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ci/financials/ratios/); [cash-flow data](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ci/statistics/))

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** The combination of Evernorth's pharmacy and health-services scale with employer-focused insurance produces recurring demand, broad purchasing data and substantial cash generation. Q2 revenue was about $71.7 billion, and management raised its 2026 outlook after solid growth and earnings. ([Cigna quarterly-results hub](https://investors.thecignagroup.com/financials/quarterly-results/))

**Why the market may be wrong.** Investors appear to be applying a persistent policy and PBM-regulation discount. Even modest per-share growth and continued debt reduction can support an adequate return at the present cash yield without a large re-rating.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** PBM reform, reimbursement pressure, medical-cost surprises and client concentration can alter economics rapidly. Debt is manageable but not trivial. The thesis weakens if Evernorth retention falls, medical costs outrun pricing, or regulatory changes structurally reduce retained spread.

#### 4. Capital One Financial (COF)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Capital One trades around 11.7x trailing earnings. At approximately 1.91x June tangible book value of $105.21 per share, it is not deep value on book alone, but the earnings multiple gives limited credit to owning a scaled proprietary payment network after the Discover acquisition. ([current quote](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/cof/statistics/); [Q2 presentation](https://investor.capitalone.com/static-files/ace3324c-ceec-42c0-b0a1-841e3db2e267))

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 adjusted EPS was $5.81, net interest margin reached 8.01%, and the CET1 ratio was a strong 13.7%. Deposits were 85% insured. The card franchise has strong analytics and funding scale, while Discover adds network economics and a credible path to vertical integration. ([Capital One Q2 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927628/000092762826000083/ex991q22026earningsrelease.htm))

**Why the market may be wrong.** The market is emphasizing near-term integration costs and consumer-credit cyclicality. If Discover integration remains on track, the combined funding, issuing and network platform may earn structurally better through-cycle returns.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Consumer credit can deteriorate nonlinearly; Discover and Brex integrations increase operational risk; regulatory capital rules may limit distributions; and the current price already exceeds tangible book materially. The thesis weakens if charge-offs exceed normalization assumptions, CET1 trends down, or promised network synergies fail to appear.

#### 5. PayPal (PYPL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** PayPal trades near 10.6x earnings and 7.3x trailing FCF. TTM FCF is about $6.59 billion, a roughly 13.7% equity yield, while the share count fell 7.8% year over year. ROIC is about 22%, although debt increased and net debt is now roughly $3 billion. ([current valuation and cash-flow data](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/pypl/statistics/))

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 payment volume grew 10% and revenue grew 5%. The platform remains accepted across roughly 200 markets and produced $1.78 billion of quarterly FCF. Its two-sided consumer and merchant footprint remains valuable even though the competitive advantage is narrower than it once was. ([PayPal Q2 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633917/000163391726000080/pypl2q-26earningsrelease.htm))

**Why the market may be wrong.** The price assumes that wallet commoditization and branded-checkout pressure will permanently offset the cash engine. Stable transaction economics plus buybacks could deliver attractive per-share returns even with only low-single-digit revenue growth.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Q2 GAAP operating income fell 5%, operating margin contracted 171 basis points, and management still guided to a mid-single-digit decline in full-year GAAP EPS. Competition from device wallets, card networks and merchant platforms is intense. The thesis weakens if transaction-margin dollars decline, branded checkout loses share, or repurchases merely mask deteriorating operating profit.

### Changes from the October 12 update

Today's independently generated top five is entirely new. **Adobe and Cirrus Logic** move to the front because they combine double-digit cash yields or near-double-digit FCF multiples with current operating growth and strong balance sheets. **Cigna, Capital One and PayPal** add diversified sector exposure and statistically stronger starting yields, with explicit regulatory, credit and moat discounts.

The October 12 ideas — GoDaddy, Arch Capital, Leidos, SkyWest and lululemon — are retained below as the prior-day record. Their absence today is a relative-ranking change, not evidence that all five theses broke overnight. ### October 13 conclusion

**Adobe and Cirrus Logic are today's strongest candidates.** Adobe offers the rare combination of a mature software moat, current double-digit growth and an approximately 11% FCF yield. Cirrus has the cleaner balance sheet and lower enterprise multiple, offset by much greater customer concentration. **Cigna** is the best defensive cash-value candidate, while **Capital One** offers the most identifiable integration catalyst. **PayPal** has the largest raw cash yield but the lowest confidence in competitive durability.

No estimate here is a certainty about intrinsic value. These are research candidates, not portfolio instructions; position sizing, tax circumstances and risk tolerance are outside this report.

### 1. GoDaddy (GDDY)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At a market capitalization of about $13.7 billion, management's reaffirmed 2026 free-cash-flow target of approximately $1.8 billion implies an equity FCF yield near 13.2%. The stock also trades near 15.5x trailing earnings. This is a much better valuation than is typical for a recurring-revenue software and web-services platform producing mid-single-digit growth.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 7%, operating income rose 29%, FCF rose 13%, annualized recurring revenue reached $4.42 billion and ARPU rose 8.7%. Applications & Commerce revenue grew 11%. Management had reduced fully diluted shares by 7% gross year to date through July 29. Net debt of $2.7 billion is meaningful but only about 1.5x the 2026 FCF target. ([Q2 filing and release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1609711/000160971126000087/gddyex991-20260630xq2earni.htm))

**Mispricing thesis.** The market appears to be discounting mature domain/hosting growth and the risk that AI platforms weaken the traditional website funnel. Current ARPU, margin and cash-flow trends instead suggest GoDaddy can monetize its installed base and retire shares faster than the core business grows.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Customer growth is nearly flat; Core revenue is expected to grow only in the low single digits; $2.7 billion of net debt reduces flexibility; and AI-native discovery or commerce tools could bypass domains and standalone websites. The thesis weakens if ARR/ARPU growth stalls or buybacks are funded with rising leverage.

### 2. Arch Capital Group (ACGL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At roughly 7.5x trailing earnings and about 1.40x June 30 book value of $68.04 per share, the price does not demand heroic assumptions for an insurer generating a 15.3% annualized operating ROE.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2's consolidated combined ratio was 83.5%; even excluding catastrophe activity and prior-year development, it was 82.5%. Book value per share rose 2.8% sequentially, and the company repurchased $1.2 billion of shares. Its insurance, reinsurance and mortgage platforms diversify the underwriting engine. ([Q2 results](https://ir.archgroup.com/news/news-details/2026/Arch-Capital-Group-Ltd--Reports-2026-Second-Quarter-Results/default.aspx); [Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/947484/000094748426000124/acgl-20260630.htm))

**Mispricing thesis.** Investors appear to be capitalizing current earnings as if specialty-insurance pricing and reserve releases are near a sharp peak. Arch's underwriting discipline and diversified franchise should allow acceptable book-value compounding even as the cycle normalizes.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Premiums and underwriting income declined year over year, favorable reserve development helped results, and catastrophe losses can overwhelm a quarter. The thesis weakens if the normalized combined ratio moves persistently toward the mid-90s or book-value growth stalls. For an insurer, P/B and through-cycle ROE deserve more weight than FCF.

### 3. Leidos (LDOS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The shares trade around 11.3x trailing earnings and about 9.9x the midpoint of management's $12.20-$12.50 adjusted 2026 EPS guidance. Guided operating cash flow of approximately $1.85 billion equals about 12.1% of the current market capitalization before capital spending.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue grew 7%; the company produced $761 million of quarterly FCF; total backlog was $48.7 billion; funded backlog rose 44% year over year; and trailing book-to-bill was 1.1x. Government mission, defense, intelligence and health contracts provide unusually high revenue visibility. ([Q2 results and guidance](https://investors.leidos.com/news-releases/news-release-details/leidos-delivers-strong-second-quarter-and-enhances-full-year))

**Mispricing thesis.** The stock seems priced as a no-growth government contractor despite growing backlog and credible defense, cyber and energy-infrastructure demand. Modest execution plus debt reduction could produce an adequate return without multiple expansion.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Debt was $6.0 billion after acquisition activity; federal budget shifts, contract losses or shutdowns can delay awards and cash; Q2 cash conversion benefited from working-capital timing; and adjusted EPS excludes acquisition and restructuring items. The thesis weakens if book-to-bill falls below 1.0 for several quarters or debt does not decline.

### 4. SkyWest (SKYW)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The stock trades around 9.6x trailing earnings. A September quality-value screen estimated a 23.2% FCF yield, but aircraft deliveries and financing make any single-year airline cash-flow yield unusually noisy; the earnings multiple is the more conservative anchor. ([Cash-yield screen](https://intrinsiqq.com/blog/best-value-stocks))

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Unlike a conventional airline, most capacity-purchase flying is compensated at contracted rates based on block hours, departures and aircraft, while major-airline partners control fares and seat inventory. Q2 revenue grew 7%, production rose 5%, the company secured 11 additional E175s for American, and the board added $250 million to repurchase authorization. ([Q2 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/793733/000110465926086251/skyw-20260723xex99d1.htm); [Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/793733/000110465926086641/skyw-20260630x10q.htm))

**Mispricing thesis.** The market applies an airline discount even though contracted flying shifts much of the fare risk to larger partners. Higher utilization and new E175 placements can support earnings and cash generation.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** The customer base is concentrated, aircraft ownership is capital-intensive, pilot supply and maintenance can constrain production, and prorate routes retain fuel and fare risk. Q2 net income declined year over year. The thesis weakens if contract economics deteriorate or new-aircraft capex fails to earn adequate returns.

### 5. lululemon athletica (LULU)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At about $94.50, the stock is roughly 9.8x the midpoint of 2026 EPS guidance of $9.48-$9.73, or about 10.8x after subtracting the stated $0.86 tariff-refund benefit. That valuation embeds a substantial permanent impairment to a once-premium brand.

**Why it still qualifies, with lower confidence.** The company retained $1.4 billion of cash, inventory dollars fell 1% and units fell 7%, and it repurchased $330 million of stock in Q2. The brand still supports a high reported gross margin and a global store base, while a new CEO and leadership structure create a concrete reset path. ([Q2 results](https://corporate.lululemon.com/newsroom/press-releases/2026/09-03-2026-210528733); [Oct. 7 leadership update](https://corporate.lululemon.com/newsroom/press-releases))

**Mispricing thesis.** The price assumes recent Americas weakness and product fatigue will persist. Even partial stabilization, supported by clean inventory and disciplined repurchases, could justify a materially higher normalized multiple.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** This is the closest name to a value trap: Q2 revenue fell 4%, comparable sales fell 9%, Americas revenue fell 8%, and management expects Q3 revenue down 10%-11%. Q2 EPS included a non-recurring tariff benefit. Confidence should rise only after sequential sales and underlying margin stabilization.

## Conclusion

**Strongest current candidates: GoDaddy and Arch Capital.** GoDaddy offers the clearest combination of recurring revenue, improving margins, a double-digit FCF yield and aggressive per-share value creation. Arch offers strong through-cycle underwriting economics at a restrained earnings and book-value multiple. **Leidos** is the best lower-volatility follow-up, supported by backlog and government demand. **SkyWest** is attractive but more capital-cycle sensitive. **lululemon** has the widest apparent upside and the weakest evidence of stabilization, so it belongs in a smaller, watch-for-confirmation category.

No estimate here is a certainty about intrinsic value. Prices, guidance and operating conditions can change quickly; each thesis includes explicit evidence that would invalidate it.
