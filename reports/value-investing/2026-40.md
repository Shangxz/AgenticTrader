## Market context

A broad U.S. screen covering roughly 5,600 listed stocks was used to surface profitable companies with reasonable earnings or free-cash-flow valuations, durable returns, and manageable financial risk. Candidates were then checked against current market prices and recent company filings or earnings releases.

The market backdrop calls for selectivity. Morningstar estimated the U.S. market at a 9% discount to its aggregate fair values at August 31, but said that discount was highly concentrated: excluding seven mega-cap companies, the remaining coverage was near fair value. It also flagged rising long-term rates and a weakening macro backdrop. That favors companies where the valuation case rests on current cash economics rather than a distant terminal value. [Source](https://www.morningstar.com.au/markets/september-us-stock-market-outlook-how-position-your-portfolio-during-risky-stage)

Prices and market capitalizations below are a September 28, 2026 snapshot. Multiples and yields are approximate and should be refreshed before acting.

### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $235.47, Adobe trades at about 13.1 times trailing GAAP earnings despite double-digit growth. Q3 operating cash flow was $2.52 billion against only $85 million of capital expenditure, implying excellent cash conversion. The current market capitalization is only about ten times a conservatively annualized recent free-cash-flow run rate.

**Why it is still a quality business.** Q3 revenue rose 13% to $6.76 billion, subscription revenue rose 14%, GAAP operating margin was about 35%, ending ARR reached $27.5 billion, and AI-first ARR grew more than 150%. Adobe raised FY26 revenue and EPS targets while repurchasing 9.5 million shares in the quarter. Its entrenched creative and document workflows, high recurring revenue, and low capital intensity remain unusually strong. [Adobe Q3 FY26 release](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/01906202/au56y4ter.pdf)

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** Generative AI could erode pricing power or shift creation toward competing tools. Stock-based compensation is meaningful, the CEO transition adds execution risk, and recent acquisitions must earn an adequate return. The thesis weakens if ARR growth slips into the mid-single digits without offsetting margin or buyback gains.

### 2. Broadridge Financial Solutions (BR)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Broadridge trades at 17.5 times trailing earnings. FY26 free cash flow was $1.233 billion, a 6.4% yield on the current market cap, with 110% conversion of adjusted net earnings.

**Why it is still a quality business.** Broadridge occupies deeply embedded infrastructure roles in investor communications and capital-markets operations. FY26 recurring revenue grew 8%, adjusted EPS grew 12%, and management guided FY27 to 6%–8% constant-currency recurring-revenue growth, 8%–12% adjusted EPS growth, and FCF conversion above 100%. The dividend increased for a twentieth consecutive year. [Broadridge FY26 results](https://www.broadridge-ir.com/news/news-details/2026/Broadridge-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2026-Results/default.aspx)

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** Acquisitions add integration and leverage risk; a small group of large clients matters; lower market activity can affect event-driven revenue. The thesis would weaken if organic recurring growth falls below the mid-single digits or FCF conversion deteriorates.

### 3. PTC (PTC)

**Why it looks undervalued.** FY26 free-cash-flow guidance of about $850 million equates to a 5.4% yield on the current market value. That is attractive for durable engineering software growing recurring revenue near 9%, though reported EPS is distorted by a divestiture gain and should not anchor the valuation.

**Why it is still a quality business.** PTC's CAD and product-lifecycle systems sit inside long-lived industrial workflows with meaningful switching costs. Q3 constant-currency ARR excluding divested businesses grew 9.1%; Q3 FCF grew 3% to $249 million; and management raised ARR guidance to 9%–9.5% while repurchasing about $525 million of stock in the quarter. [PTC Q3 FY26 results](https://investor.ptc.com/resources/news/news-details/2026/PTC-ANNOUNCES-THIRD-FISCAL-QUARTER-2026-RESULTS/default.aspx)

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** Q3 reported revenue fell 7% and operating margin contracted, partly reflecting portfolio changes and ASC 606 timing. FY26 cash flow includes divestiture-related items. The thesis needs clean ARR retention and normalized FCF growth after those effects roll off.

### 4. Otis Worldwide (OTIS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Otis trades around 17 times trailing earnings. Its $1.50–$1.55 billion 2026 adjusted FCF outlook implies roughly a 6.0% yield before considering additional growth.

**Why it is still a quality business.** The installed base creates recurring, safety-critical service demand: Otis maintains about 2.5 million units globally. Q2 organic sales grew 6%, organic service sales grew 9%, modernization orders rose 9%, and modernization backlog rose 26% at constant currency. First-half adjusted FCF reached $562 million while the company repurchased about $800 million of stock. [Otis Q2 2026 results](https://www.otisinvestors.com/news/news-details/2026/OTIS-REPORTS-SECOND-QUARTER-2026-RESULTS/default.aspx)

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** New-equipment weakness, especially in China, can pressure mix and margins; Q2 adjusted EPS fell 4%. Otis also carries substantial debt and negative book equity, so the equity is less balance-sheet-resilient than its service franchise appears.

### 5. Constellation Brands (STZ)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The shares trade at 10.8 times trailing earnings. Management's $1.6–$1.7 billion FY27 FCF target implies an 8.2%–8.7% yield on the current market cap, providing a substantial starting return if cash flow proves durable.

**Why it is still a quality business.** Modelo and Corona give Constellation a differentiated U.S. imported-beer portfolio with pricing power and high margins. FY27 guidance calls for a 32%–33% enterprise operating margin and 37%–38% beer operating margin. Morningstar also included STZ among its ten most undervalued wide-moat names on September 21. [Constellation FY26/FY27 outlook](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/16918/000001691826000008/stzex991_22820268kearnings.htm) · [Wide-moat screen](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/10-wide-moat-stocks-buy-that-look-cheap-today)

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** U.S. beer demand is structurally soft, fiscal 2027 organic sales guidance is only negative 1% to positive 1%, the business is exposed to tariffs and Mexican production, and leverage reduces flexibility. The next earnings release is scheduled for October 6, making this a higher-event-risk idea.

### 6. Nike (NKE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $35.75, Nike trades at about 17 times FY26 GAAP EPS of $2.10 and holds $9.0 billion in cash and short-term investments. Morningstar ranked Nike the most undervalued constituent in its wide-moat focus list, at a stated 62% discount to fair value on September 21.

**Why it is still a quality business.** Nike remains a globally scaled athletic brand with unmatched sports-marketing reach and broad wholesale distribution. FY26 revenue held flat on a reported basis, wholesale revenue grew 6%, gross margin edged up to 42.9%, and North America partly offset weakness elsewhere. [Nike FY26 results](https://investors.nikeinc.com/investors/news-events-and-reports/investor-news/investor-news-details/2026/NIKE-Inc--Reports-Fiscal-2026-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-Results/default.aspx) · [Wide-moat screen](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/10-wide-moat-stocks-buy-that-look-cheap-today)

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** This is a turnaround, not a clean compounder today: currency-neutral revenue fell 2%, Nike Direct fell 8%, digital fell 12%, and Converse fell 31%. Q4 margin and EPS also benefited heavily from a one-time tariff recovery. Evidence of renewed full-price demand, digital stabilization, and normalized margin recovery is required before raising confidence.

Q2 capex of roughly $44.9 billion exceeded operating cash flow, and trailing FCF was only about $53.3 billion versus a $4.2 trillion market value. It remains high quality, but the present owner-earnings yield is too low for this list. [Q2 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000066/googexhibit991q22026.htm)

U.S. companion-animal revenue fell 11% in Q2 amid weaker clinic demand and more competition. Keep on watch until demand and guidance stabilize. ## Conclusion

**Best current balance of quality and value:** Adobe. Its roughly 13x GAAP multiple, near-10% cash yield, double-digit subscription growth, high margins, and aggressive share retirement provide the clearest combination of present economics and durable franchise quality.

**Best steadier compounder:** Broadridge. Its valuation discount is smaller, but recurring revenue, cash conversion, and forward growth guidance make the underwriting cleaner.

**Best industrial-software and installed-base alternatives:** PTC and Otis. Both offer defensible recurring revenue at mid-single-digit cash yields, with company-specific issues that are visible and monitorable.

Constellation and Nike offer larger perceived discounts but require more confidence in consumer-demand stabilization. Position sizing, a long horizon, and confirmation from future results matter more for those two.
