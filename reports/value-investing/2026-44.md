## 1. Accenture (NYSE: ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $249.37 and a $153.5 billion market capitalization, Accenture trades at about 19.9x trailing earnings. Fiscal 2026 free cash flow was $11.6 billion, implying a 7.6% equity FCF yield. Management's fiscal 2027 outlook calls for 3%–6% local-currency revenue growth and GAAP EPS of $14.39–$14.81; the midpoint implies roughly 17.1x forward earnings. This is a reasonable price for a durable, cash-generative franchise rather than deep value.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Fiscal 2026 revenue reached $74.2 billion, local-currency growth was 5%, bookings were $84.5 billion, adjusted operating margin was 15.8%, and adjusted EPS increased 8%. Cash of $12.8 billion exceeded current borrowings plus long-term debt of about $10.1 billion. The inference is that Accenture's deep client relationships, delivery scale, and ability to retrain and acquire talent give it a stronger adaptation path than smaller consultancies as AI changes the work mix. [FY2026 annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1467373/000146737326000049/acn-20260831.htm) [FY2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1467373/000146737326000037/q4fy26earnings8-kexhibit.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** AI may automate labor-intensive consulting faster than Accenture can replace it with higher-value work; pricing, utilization, and organic growth deserve close attention. Debt doubled during fiscal 2026 to fund acquisitions and capital returns, although cash still covers total debt. The thesis weakens if bookings fail to become mid-single-digit organic growth or if margins contract despite restructuring and AI demand.

## 2. Booking Holdings (Nasdaq: BKNG)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $155.83 and 16.6x trailing earnings, Booking trades near a 52-week low despite positive operating growth. In Q2, free cash flow increased 16% to $3.6 billion and adjusted EPS increased 15%. The company produced $6.75 billion of free cash flow in the first half, though seasonality means that figure should not simply be doubled.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 room nights increased 5% to 325 million, gross bookings increased 9% to $51.0 billion, and revenue increased 8% to $7.4 billion. More than half of room nights over the trailing four quarters came through the direct channel, supporting brand strength and lower dependence on paid acquisition. The scale of supply, demand, reviews, payments, and direct traffic supports a network-effect inference. Cash of $17.2 billion nearly offset $20.2 billion of short- and long-term debt at June 30. [Q2 results and balance sheet](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1075531/000107553126000036/q2-26bkngearningsrelease.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Q2 room-night growth slowed, management guided Q3 growth to 3%–5%, rental-car days declined 6.5%, and geopolitical or energy-price shocks can hit discretionary travel quickly. Google, AI travel agents, and hotel direct-booking efforts could raise acquisition costs or weaken the platform. The thesis weakens if direct mix declines, marketing intensity rises persistently, or room-night growth trails global travel.

## 3. Uber Technologies (NYSE: UBER)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $66.70, Uber's $136.8 billion market value is about 13.7x trailing free cash flow above $10 billion, equivalent to a yield above 7.3%. The 14.6x trailing P/E is less reliable because reported earnings include investment revaluations, so cash flow and operating income are better anchors.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 trips increased 18% to 3.9 billion, gross bookings increased 24% to $58.0 billion, GAAP operating income increased 30% to $1.9 billion, and non-GAAP operating income increased 40%. The inference is that liquidity on both sides of the marketplace, cross-use between mobility and delivery, and global scale create a meaningful network advantage. [Q2 results](https://investor.uber.com/news-events/news/press-release-details/2026/Uber-Announces-Results-for-Second-Quarter-2026/default.aspx)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** The pending $14.8 billion Delivery Hero offer is the largest issue: Uber had $12.6 billion of debt at June 30 and arranged a €14.2 billion bridge facility, so leverage and integration risk could rise sharply. Autonomous vehicles may strengthen Uber's aggregation role, but they could also shift bargaining power to fleet or technology owners. Regulation, insurance reserves, stock compensation, and competition remain material. The thesis weakens if the acquisition dilutes per-share FCF, core booking growth drops below the high teens, or margin gains stall. [Delivery Hero transaction disclosure](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1543151/000154315126000032/uber-20260630.htm)

## 4. Constellation Brands (NYSE: STZ)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $138.68, Constellation trades at about 12.4x trailing earnings. Fiscal 2027 FCF guidance of $1.6–$1.7 billion implies a 6.8%–7.2% yield on the $23.7 billion equity value. That is attractive for a branded consumer franchise, but leverage deserves a valuation discount.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 beer net sales increased 5% and the segment produced a 39.0% comparable operating margin. Consolidated net sales increased 6%, and first-half FCF increased 4% to $1.1 billion. The inference is that Modelo, Corona, and Pacifico retain pricing power and distribution strength in the U.S. [Q2 fiscal 2027 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/16918/000001691826000040/stz-20260831.htm) [Earnings recap](https://www.cbrands.com/blogs/stories/earnings-recap-constellation-brands-second-quarter-fiscal-2027-earnings-results)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Beer depletions declined 0.6% even as shipments rose 5.5%, suggesting some Q2 growth came from distributor restocking rather than end demand. Beer margin fell from 40.6% to 39.0%. Debt was $10.2 billion against only $98.5 million of cash, with management targeting roughly 3x comparable net leverage. Tariffs, Hispanic-consumer pressure, and category weakness could impair both volume and margins. The thesis weakens if depletions remain negative after inventory normalizes or leverage does not fall.

## 5. Zoetis (NYSE: ZTS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $81.03, Zoetis trades at 13.2x trailing earnings and about 13.1x the midpoint of management's reduced 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $6.15–$6.25. First-half free cash flow was approximately $829 million, calculated as $1.056 billion of operating cash flow less $227 million of capital spending.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Zoetis remains the world's largest animal-health company. Its portfolio spans medicines, vaccines, diagnostics, companion animals, and livestock; Q2 international revenue grew 8% reported, and international gross margin was 71.0%. These economics, veterinary relationships, and a broad R&D portfolio support the quality case even during a weak U.S. cycle. [Q2 results](https://investor.zoetis.com/news/news-details/2026/Zoetis-Announces-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx) [Q2 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1555280/000155528026000040/zts-20260630.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** This is the least settled thesis. Q2 revenue was flat, organic operational revenue declined 1%, adjusted net income declined 2%, and management cut full-year guidance. U.S. revenue declined 7% in the first half amid greater pet-owner price sensitivity, generic pressure on Cerenia and Convenia, and softer Librela sales. Net debt was roughly $7.4 billion. The thesis weakens if U.S. companion-animal share and volume do not stabilize, international growth fades, or product safety and competitive concerns spread.

## Conclusion

**Accenture and Booking Holdings are the strongest current quality-value candidates.** Both combine clear cash generation, defensible scale, positive operating evidence, and manageable balance-sheet risk at undemanding multiples.

**Uber offers the strongest growth at a value-like cash-flow yield**, but the Delivery Hero transaction materially lowers confidence. **Constellation offers a cheaper branded-franchise setup**, offset by leverage and a gap between shipments and end demand. **Zoetis has the widest apparent rerating potential but also the highest value-trap risk**, so stabilization evidence matters more than the low multiple.

The list is a research shortlist, not a claim of intrinsic-value certainty.
