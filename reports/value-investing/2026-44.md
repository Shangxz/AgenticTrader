## 1. Progressive (NYSE: PGR)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $229.25 and 11.5x trailing earnings, Progressive is priced below many slower-growing defensive franchises despite continuing policy growth and excellent underwriting. The multiple is not as optically cheap as catastrophe-sensitive peers, but it pays for a more repeatable underwriting record rather than a single benign-loss year.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Third-quarter net premiums earned grew 5%, policies in force grew 6%, and the combined ratio improved to 88.7 from 89.5. Agency auto policies grew 7% and direct auto grew 8%, showing that profitability has not required retreating from the market. Progressive's pricing data, direct distribution, claims execution, and disciplined combined-ratio target support a durable underwriting-advantage inference. [September and Q3 results](https://investors.progressive.com/financials/financial-news-releases/news-details/2026/Progressive-Reports-September-2026-Results/default.aspx)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Premium growth slowed to 4% in Q3, and August's combined ratio deteriorated year over year before September improved. Claims severity, regulation, reserve development, catastrophe losses, and investment-market volatility can move earnings sharply. The thesis weakens if policy growth stalls while the combined ratio rises persistently above the mid-90s.

## 2. Accenture (NYSE: ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $247.23, Accenture trades at about 17.7x FY2026 adjusted EPS of $13.97 and 16.9x the midpoint of FY2027 EPS guidance. Fiscal 2026 free cash flow of $11.6 billion implies a 7.6% equity yield on the current market value. This is attractive for a capital-light global leader, although it is reasonable value rather than deep value.

**Why it remains a quality business.** FY2026 revenue reached $74.2 billion, local-currency growth was 5%, bookings were $84.5 billion, adjusted operating margin was 15.8%, and adjusted EPS grew 8%. Management guides to 3%–6% local-currency growth, modest margin expansion, and $11.0–$11.8 billion of FY2027 FCF. Large-client relationships, delivery scale, and the ability to retrain or acquire specialized talent are meaningful advantages as AI changes consulting demand. [FY2026 results and FY2027 outlook](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1467373/000146737326000037/q4fy26earnings8-kexhibit.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** AI could automate labor-intensive work faster than Accenture replaces it with higher-value projects. Organic growth is only mid-single-digit, acquisition spending increased debt, and heavy buybacks could mask per-share stagnation. The thesis weakens if bookings fail to become revenue or margins contract despite restructuring.

## 3. Booking Holdings (Nasdaq: BKNG)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $153.14 and 16.3x trailing earnings, Booking trades only about 2% above its 52-week low even though operating results remain positive. Q2 free cash flow increased 16% to $3.6 billion and adjusted EPS increased 15%. The reported GAAP earnings surge included a euro-debt remeasurement gain, so adjusted earnings and cash flow are the better anchors.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 room nights increased 5% to 325 million, gross bookings increased 9% to $51.0 billion, and revenue increased 8% to $7.4 billion. More than half of room nights over the trailing four quarters came through the direct channel. Supply breadth, reviews, payments, and direct traffic reinforce one another, supporting a durable marketplace advantage. [Q2 results and outlook](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1075531/000107553126000036/q2-26bkngearningsrelease.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Room-night growth slowed and management guided Q3 growth to 3%–5%. Google, AI travel agents, and hotel direct-booking efforts could raise acquisition costs or weaken platform traffic. Geopolitics, energy prices, currencies, and recessions can quickly affect discretionary travel. The thesis weakens if direct mix falls or marketing intensity rises persistently.

## 4. Zoom Communications (Nasdaq: ZM)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The 9.1x headline P/E is flattered by gains on strategic investments, so it should not be the main valuation anchor. At $98.04, Zoom instead trades at about 16.1x the midpoint of FY2027 non-GAAP EPS guidance and offers a 6.1% guided FCF yield. Its $7.2 billion of cash and marketable securities equals roughly one-quarter of the market capitalization, making the enterprise-value cash yield still more attractive.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 revenue grew 4.9%, enterprise revenue grew 7.8%, GAAP operating margin was 24.6%, and management guides to $1.78–$1.82 billion of FY2027 FCF. Customers contributing more than $100,000 of trailing revenue grew 8.2%, while enterprise net dollar expansion improved to 99%. The installed base, product reliability, brand, and expanding contact-center and workplace suite give Zoom a credible path to modest durable growth. [Q2 FY2027 results and guidance](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1585521/000158552126000119/zm-20260731ex991.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Online revenue grew only 0.6%, enterprise expansion remains below 100%, and Microsoft, Google, Cisco, and specialized contact-center vendors are formidable competitors. Stock-based compensation means FCF overstates cash economically available to owners if dilution is not offset. The thesis weakens if enterprise growth slips, net retention deteriorates, or AI features fail to support pricing.

## 5. Micron Technology (Nasdaq: MU)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $903.70, Micron trades at about 12.2x FY2026 GAAP EPS of $74.33. FY2026 adjusted FCF of $62.3 billion implies a 6.0% equity yield. First-quarter FY2027 guidance calls for $61.5 billion of revenue and approximately $37.84 of GAAP EPS, indicating that near-term earnings have not yet rolled over.

**Why it remains a quality business.** FY2026 revenue reached $133.2 billion, operating cash flow was $89.7 billion, adjusted FCF was $62.3 billion, and year-end cash, marketable investments, and restricted cash totaled $73.5 billion. AI memory demand, process execution, and strategic customer agreements have transformed near-term economics; the screen also shows high ROE and low debt. [FY2026 results and FY2027 Q1 guidance](https://investors.micron.com/news/press-release/2026/Micron-Technology-Inc--Reports-Record-Fiscal-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2026-Results/default.aspx)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Fiscal 2026 revenue more than tripled and gross margins reached extraordinary levels, so current earnings are plainly above a normal memory-cycle baseline. New capacity, customer inventory changes, technology transitions, and competitor pricing can reverse profitability quickly. The thesis requires conservative normalized earnings; it weakens if supply growth catches demand or capital intensity rises enough to absorb the cash windfall.

It remains a candidate, but normalized earnings confidence is lower than for Progressive. ## Conclusion

**Progressive, Accenture, and Booking are today's strongest quality-value candidates.** Progressive offers the best current balance of multiple, underwriting quality, and organic policy growth. Accenture and Booking offer capital-light cash generation and durable scale at undemanding valuations.

**Zoom is the strongest cash-rich smaller-franchise candidate**, but its low headline P/E should be ignored in favor of FCF and normalized EPS. **Micron has the most powerful current earnings momentum**, yet also the greatest cycle-normalization risk.

This shortlist expresses relative research conviction, not certainty about intrinsic value or a recommendation tailored to any investor.
