## 1. Adobe (Nasdaq: ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At 13.9x trailing earnings and approximately a 10% operating-cash-flow yield, Adobe is priced as though AI will substantially impair its economics. That is possible, but current operating evidence still points the other way: fiscal Q3 revenue rose 13%, total ARR rose 11.2% to $27.50 billion, and operating cash flow reached $2.52 billion.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Adobe combines subscription revenue, professional workflow integration, file standards, distribution, and high incremental margins. AI-first ARR grew more than 150%, suggesting Adobe is participating in the transition even though the absolute contribution is not yet disclosed. [Adobe Q3 FY2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000147/adbeex991q326.htm) [Adobe Q3 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000156/adbe-20260828.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Canva, Figma, and AI-native creation tools may erode entry-level demand or pricing; AI-first ARR remains small relative to total ARR; and the December CEO transition adds execution risk. The thesis weakens if total ARR growth falls below high single digits, margins compress materially, or AI usage fails to convert into paid retention.

## 2. Intuit (Nasdaq: INTU)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The October 28 close is about 16.2x the midpoint of FY2027 GAAP EPS guidance of $20.12-$20.36, a modest multiple for a franchise embedded in tax, accounting, payroll, payments, and consumer-credit workflows. Management still guides to 9%-10% revenue growth and 22%-24% GAAP EPS growth.

**Why it remains a quality business.** FY2026 revenue grew 14% to $21.4 billion, GAAP operating income rose 20% to $5.9 billion, Global Business Solutions grew 16%, and QuickBooks Online Accounting grew 23%. The installed base, accountant ecosystem, proprietary data, and switching costs support durable returns. [FY2026 results](https://investors.intuit.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000896878-26-000029/fy26q4earningspressrelease.htm) [FY2027 guidance reaffirmation](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1323/intuit-hosts-investor-day-reaffirms-first-quarter-and-fiscal-2027-guidance)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** TurboTax units fell 2% in FY2026, Mailchimp is guided flat to slightly down, and AI-native accounting or government filing tools may weaken the moat. FY2026 cash flow benefited from unusually low cash taxes, while roughly $2 billion of annual stock compensation makes headline FCF less conservative than GAAP EPS. The thesis weakens if QuickBooks Online growth falls to single digits or repurchases stop offsetting dilution.

## 3. Deckers Outdoor (NYSE: DECK)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Deckers trades at about 10.3x the midpoint of raised FY2027 EPS guidance. Because that guidance includes an estimated $0.52 per-share benefit from tariff refunds, a more conservative multiple is approximately 11.0x. That still offers a substantial earnings yield for two globally scaled brands.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Fiscal Q2 revenue rose 3.5% to a record $1.48 billion; HOKA grew 6.8%, UGG grew 3.5%, international revenue grew 9%, and operating income rose to $389.5 million. Deckers held $1.21 billion of cash with no outstanding borrowings and repurchased $637.5 million of shares in the first half. [Deckers Q2 FY2027 results](https://ir.deckers.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000910521-26-000028/deckex991pressrelease-9302.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Domestic revenue slipped 0.4%, direct-to-consumer sales fell 1.8%, DTC comparable sales fell 6.4%, and the reported margin expansion included a 310-basis-point tariff-refund benefit. Fashion risk and concentrated dependence on HOKA and UGG remain material. The thesis weakens if DTC comps remain negative while inventory rises, or if normalized gross margin retreats without renewed unit growth.

## 4. Booking Holdings (Nasdaq: BKNG)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Booking trades at about 15.8x trailing earnings. FY2025 free cash flow of $9.1 billion equals roughly 8% of the current market value, and the company continues to retire shares aggressively. The discount reflects travel cyclicality and concern that AI assistants could weaken online travel agencies' customer-acquisition advantage.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 room nights grew 5%, gross bookings 9%, revenue 8%, adjusted EBITDA 9%, adjusted EPS 15%, and free cash flow 16%. Booking.com listed about 4.7 million properties at June 30, including more than 4.1 million alternative accommodations, creating a broad two-sided marketplace. [Booking Q2 2026 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1075531/000107553126000037/bkng-20260630.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Management guided Q3 room-night, revenue, gross-booking, and adjusted-EBITDA growth to only 3%-6%, travel is cyclical and geopolitical exposure is meaningful, and search or AI platforms could raise acquisition costs. Q3 results are due November 3, so conviction is explicitly conditional on those figures. The thesis weakens if direct traffic deteriorates, marketing intensity rises, or room-night growth turns negative. [Q3 reporting date](https://www.bookingholdings.com/press-releases/booking-holdings-to-webcast-third-quarter-2026-financial-results-on-november-3/)

## 5. AXIS Capital (NYSE: AXS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** AXIS trades at about 7.8x trailing earnings and 1.20x book value per share excluding AOCI. That valuation is attractive against a 15.7% quarterly operating ROE and 14.4% year-over-year growth in ex-AOCI book value per share.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q3 gross premiums rose 11%, the combined ratio was 94.0%, operating income was $229 million, and AXIS repurchased $109 million of shares. The 92.3% nine-month combined ratio confirms that earnings still include genuine underwriting profit rather than investment income alone. [AXIS Q3 2026 results](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/axis-capital-reports-third-quarter-net-income-available-common-shareholders-202)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** The Q3 combined ratio worsened from 89.4% to 94.0%, underwriting income fell 29%, and catastrophe, reserve, social-inflation, and pricing-cycle risks can overwhelm several quarters of profit. Today's release confirms the thesis but also lowers confidence versus yesterday. The thesis weakens if the combined ratio persists near or above 100 or ex-AOCI book-value growth stalls.

## 6. The Cigna Group (NYSE: CI)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At about 12.2x trailing GAAP earnings and 9.7x management's 2026 adjusted EPS floor of $30.45, Cigna trades at a wide discount to the market. The multiple provides some allowance for medical-cost uncertainty and PBM regulation.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 revenue rose 7%, adjusted income from operations rose 6% to $2.1 billion, and Cigna Healthcare adjusted pre-tax income rose 17%. The Healthcare segment's 10.9% pre-tax margin and Evernorth's scale in pharmacy and specialty care provide diversified earnings sources. [Cigna Q2 2026 results](https://newsroom.thecignagroup.com/2026-07-30-The-Cigna-Group-Reports-Strong-Second-Quarter-2026-Results,-Raises-2026-Outlook)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Evernorth Pharmacy Benefit Services income fell 27% in Q2, PBM economics face political scrutiny, and claims reserves can prove inadequate. Q3 results are due November 5. The thesis weakens if the medical-care ratio exceeds guidance or Evernorth margins continue to contract. [Q3 reporting date](https://newsroom.thecignagroup.com/2026-10-09-The-Cigna-Groups-Third-Quarter-2026-Earnings-Release-Details)

## Near misses and rejected headline bargains

- **Berkshire Hathaway (BRK.B):** Exceptional balance-sheet quality and diversified earnings, but the stock is close to a record high and is closer to 24x projected operating earnings than the screen's low headline P/E suggests. It lacks the margin of safety of today's top six.
- **Comcast (CMCSA):** The screen's 28.7% last-fiscal-year FCF yield is striking, but broadband revenue and subscribers are shrinking, leverage is meaningful, and buybacks are paused during the proposed separation. Cheap, but structurally weaker.
- **Lululemon (LULU):** The multiple is very low, yet double-digit guided Q3 revenue decline, negative comparable sales, and product/brand uncertainty make the recovery less supported than Deckers.
- **Regeneron (REGN):** Strong balance sheet, cash conversion, and an 18.6x screened P/E merit continued work, but Q3 results arrive October 30 and concentration around EYLEA creates a near-term information gap.
- **Fiserv, Charter, Corteva, Ford, and MGM:** High screened FCF yields are affected by deteriorating operations, leverage, cyclicality, or corporate-action/data-comparability issues. None clears the quality threshold today.
- **Micron:** Strong current profitability and screen scores, but a highly favorable memory cycle makes trailing earnings a poor normalized-value anchor.

## Changes from the October 28 update

Today's ranking was generated from the fresh screen and company review, then compared with the earlier weekly record.

- **Adobe and Intuit remain #1 and #2.** Both became slightly cheaper on October 28 without a change in the operating evidence.
- **Deckers enters at #3.** Its latest quarter, debt-free balance sheet, and roughly 11x earnings multiple excluding the tariff-refund benefit offer a cleaner quality-value case than Lululemon.
- **Booking returns at #4.** Its marketplace economics and cash generation outweigh slowing near-term guidance, though the November 3 earnings date caps conviction.
- **AXIS moves from #3 to #5.** Q3 confirmed a profitable 94.0% combined ratio and strong book-value growth, but underwriting income fell 29% and the combined ratio worsened 4.6 points.
- **Cigna moves from #4 to #6.** The valuation remains compelling; PBM margin pressure and the upcoming Q3 report keep it below the cleaner consumer-platform ideas.
- **Comcast and Lululemon leave the current six.** Their discounts remain large, but structural broadband erosion and brand/product deterioration, respectively, make the quality side less secure. Removal is not a sell call.

## Conclusion

**Adobe and Intuit are the strongest current quality-value candidates.** Both pair double-digit recent business growth with compressed multiples; Adobe offers the larger apparent cash yield, while Intuit's installed-base economics are especially durable but its stock-compensation-adjusted cash yield is less generous than the headline figure.

**Deckers is today's strongest addition.** The balance sheet is pristine and the multiple is low even after removing the one-time tariff-refund benefit, though weak DTC comps require close monitoring. **Booking is the best platform-style diversifier; AXIS and Cigna offer lower-multiple financial and healthcare exposure with more sector-specific risk.**

This ranking expresses relative research conviction under current public information. It is not a claim of certain intrinsic value and is not personal financial advice.
