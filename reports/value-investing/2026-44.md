## 1. Adobe (Nasdaq: ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Adobe trades at about 13.9x trailing earnings despite Q3 revenue growth of 13%, total ARR growth of 11.2% to $27.50 billion, and record quarterly operating cash flow of $2.52 billion. The price embeds a severe and lasting AI disruption scenario.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Subscription revenue, professional workflow integration, file standards, distribution, and high incremental margins remain powerful. AI-first ARR grew more than 150% year over year, while diluted shares have fallen from 424 million to 395 million over the past year. [Adobe Q3 FY2026 results](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/01906202/au56y4ter.pdf) [Adobe investor data sheet](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/01906202/b75st5a4rwea.pdf)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Canva, Figma, and AI-native tools can erode entry-level demand or pricing; AI-first ARR is growing from an undisclosed base; and the December CEO transition adds execution risk. The thesis weakens if total ARR growth drops below high single digits, margins compress materially, or AI adoption does not support paid retention.

## 2. Intuit (Nasdaq: INTU)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The current price is about 16.3x the midpoint of FY2027 GAAP EPS guidance of $20.12-$20.36, modest for a franchise embedded in tax, accounting, payroll, payments, and consumer-credit workflows. Management still guides to 9%-10% revenue growth and 22%-24% GAAP EPS growth.

**Why it remains a quality business.** FY2026 revenue grew 14% to $21.4 billion, GAAP operating income rose 20% to $5.9 billion, Global Business Solutions grew 16%, and QuickBooks Online Accounting grew 23%. The installed base, accountant ecosystem, proprietary data, and switching costs create durable economics. [FY2026 results](https://investors.intuit.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000896878-26-000029/fy26q4earningspressrelease.htm) [FY2027 guidance reaffirmation](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1323/intuit-hosts-investor-day-reaffirms-first-quarter-and-fiscal-2027-guidance)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** TurboTax units fell 2% in FY2026, Mailchimp is guided flat to slightly down, and AI-native accounting or government filing tools may weaken parts of the moat. FY2026 cash flow benefited from unusually low cash taxes, while stock compensation makes headline free cash flow less conservative than GAAP EPS.

## 3. Deckers Outdoor (NYSE: DECK)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Deckers trades at about 10.5x trailing earnings and roughly 11x the midpoint of guidance after removing the estimated $0.52-per-share tariff-refund benefit. That is a substantial earnings yield for a debt-free owner of two globally scaled brands.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Fiscal Q2 revenue reached a record $1.48 billion; HOKA grew 6.8%, UGG grew 3.5%, international revenue grew 9%, and operating income rose to $389.5 million. Deckers held $1.21 billion of cash, no borrowings, and repurchased $637.5 million of shares in the first half. [Deckers Q2 FY2027 results](https://ir.deckers.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000910521-26-000028/deckex991pressrelease-9302.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Domestic revenue slipped 0.4%, direct-to-consumer sales fell 1.8%, DTC comparable sales fell 6.4%, and reported margin expansion included a 310-basis-point tariff-refund benefit. Fashion risk and dependence on HOKA and UGG remain material.

## 4. Alphabet (Nasdaq: GOOGL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The shares were indicated near $283.82 before the open, down about 12.7%, at roughly 14.8x reported trailing earnings. Q3 revenue grew 25% to $127.6 billion and operating income grew 30% to $40.6 billion, while the $8.7 billion investment loss that reduced EPS is not a good proxy for the core business.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Search revenue grew 12%, Cloud revenue grew 103% to $30.7 billion, Cloud operating income more than tripled to $11.1 billion, and the balance sheet held $265.1 billion of cash and marketable securities. Search distribution, advertiser intent data, YouTube, Cloud, custom silicon, and the one-billion-user Gemini app provide several defensible platforms. [Alphabet Q3 2026 release](https://s206.q4cdn.com/479360582/files/doc_financials/2026/q3/2026q3-alphabet-earnings-release.pdf)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** This is no longer a conventional free-cash-flow bargain. Q3 operating cash flow of $42.3 billion was below $51.4 billion of capital expenditures; trailing 12-month free cash flow fell to $19.7 billion. Long-term debt reached $125.1 billion, Q3 included $28.7 billion of new debt issuance, and the company issued common stock. Search growth also missed market expectations. The thesis depends on exceptional returns from the AI buildout, not merely continued revenue growth, which caps conviction.

## 5. Booking Holdings (Nasdaq: BKNG)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Booking trades near 16.0x trailing earnings. FY2025 free cash flow of $9.1 billion represents roughly an 8% yield on the recent market value, and the company continues to retire shares aggressively.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 room nights grew 5%, gross bookings 9%, revenue 8%, adjusted EBITDA 9%, adjusted EPS 15%, and free cash flow 16%. Booking.com's approximately 4.7 million listed properties, including more than 4.1 million alternative accommodations, reinforce its two-sided marketplace. [Booking Q2 2026 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1075531/000107553126000037/bkng-20260630.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Management guided Q3 room-night, revenue, gross-booking, and adjusted-EBITDA growth to only 3%-6%. Travel is cyclical, geopolitical exposure is meaningful, and AI/search platforms could increase acquisition costs. Q3 results on November 3 are an important near-term check.

## 6. AXIS Capital (NYSE: AXS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** AXIS trades near 7.9x trailing earnings and about 1.2x book value per share excluding AOCI, attractive against a 15.7% quarterly operating ROE and 14.4% year-over-year growth in ex-AOCI book value per share.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q3 gross premiums rose 11%, the combined ratio remained profitable at 94.0%, operating income was $229 million, and AXIS repurchased $109 million of shares. The 92.3% nine-month combined ratio confirms genuine underwriting profit rather than dependence on investment income alone. [AXIS Q3 2026 results](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/axis-capital-reports-third-quarter-net-income-available-common-shareholders-202)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** The Q3 combined ratio worsened from 89.4% to 94.0%, underwriting income fell 29%, and catastrophe, reserve, social-inflation, and pricing-cycle risks can overwhelm several quarters of profit. Conviction is lower than the headline multiple suggests.

## Near misses and rejected headline bargains

- **The Cigna Group (CI):** About 12.3x trailing earnings and strong Healthcare results remain attractive, but PBM margin pressure, political scrutiny, and the November 5 earnings date leave it just outside today's six.
- **Bristol Myers Squibb (BMY):** Q3 revenue grew 7% and the Growth Portfolio grew 12%, while the shares trade around 13.6x trailing earnings with a roughly 4.1% forward yield. The 2027 Eliquis loss of exclusivity, generic erosion, acquisition-related charges, and product concentration keep it below the cleaner candidates. [BMS Q3 results](https://news.bms.com/news/corporate-financial/2026/Bristol-Myers-Squibb-Reports-Third-Quarter-Financial-Results-for-2026/default.aspx)
- **PepsiCo (PEP):** The shares trade around 16.8x earnings and the dividend yield is attractive, but Q3 organic growth was only 3.1%, management cut its profit-growth outlook, and $51.9 billion of short- and long-term debt limits balance-sheet flexibility. [PepsiCo Q3 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/77476/000007747626000048/pep-20260905.htm)
- **Berkshire Hathaway (BRK.B):** Exceptional balance-sheet quality, but the screen's 12.6x headline P/E is distorted by investment marks; normalized operating earnings imply a materially higher multiple, reducing the margin of safety.
- **Gilead, Merck, and other low-multiple pharma:** Several screen extremely well on recent earnings, but patent cliffs, acquired-IPRD charges, and pipeline concentration make normalized owner earnings less certain.
- **Comcast and HP:** High cash yields are offset by structural revenue pressure, leverage, or weak organic growth.
- **Micron and commodity producers:** Current profitability is too cycle-sensitive to treat trailing multiples as normalized value.

## Changes from the October 29 update

Today's list was selected independently, then compared with the existing weekly report.

- **Adobe, Intuit, and Deckers remain #1-#3.** Their latest prices and operating evidence still provide the best combination of valuation, cash economics, and defensible quality.
- **Alphabet enters at #4.** A roughly 12.7% premarket decline and 14.8x reported P/E create a major valuation reset after 25% revenue growth. It ranks below the top three because capex exceeded operating cash flow in Q3 and the AI investment program is increasingly debt- and equity-financed.
- **Booking moves from #4 to #5** solely because Alphabet offers a larger new dislocation; Booking's thesis is otherwise unchanged.
- **AXIS moves from #5 to #6.** Its underwriting remains profitable, but worsening Q3 underwriting trends limit conviction.
- **Cigna leaves the current six.** The valuation remains attractive; the change reflects stronger relative opportunities and an upcoming earnings report, not a sell conclusion.
- **Bristol Myers and PepsiCo receive explicit near-miss coverage** after current results confirmed both the discounts and the reasons those discounts exist.

## Conclusion

**Adobe and Intuit remain the strongest current quality-value candidates.** Adobe offers the larger apparent cash yield, while Intuit has especially durable workflow and data advantages. **Deckers is the strongest balance-sheet-backed consumer value, though weak DTC comps require monitoring.**

**Alphabet is today's most important new opportunity and the widest uncertainty band.** The operating franchises are excellent and the multiple is compressed, but the investment case now rests on future returns from an unprecedented AI capital program. Booking and AXIS round out the list with marketplace and insurance exposure at attractive multiples but clearer cyclical risks.

This ranking expresses relative research conviction under current public information. It is not a claim of certain intrinsic value and is not personal financial advice.
