## 1. Adobe (Nasdaq: ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At roughly 14.4x trailing earnings and a screened 9.8% FCF yield, Adobe is valued like a low-growth incumbent even though its latest quarter still delivered double-digit revenue and recurring-revenue growth. The discount appears to reflect fear that generative AI will commoditize creative tools, plus uncertainty around the December CEO transition.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Fiscal Q3 revenue rose 13% to $6.76 billion, total ARR reached $27.50 billion, operating cash flow was $2.52 billion, and Adobe repurchased about 9.5 million shares. Subscription revenue in the Creative & Marketing Professionals group grew 13%, while Business Professionals & Consumers grew 16%. This is evidence that Adobe's distribution, professional workflows, file standards, and enterprise integrations remain economically valuable even as AI changes how content is created. [Q3 FY2026 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000147/adbeex991q326.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Low-cost AI-native products could erode entry-level demand, pressure pricing, or force heavier R&D. AI-first ARR is growing rapidly but remains small relative to total ARR, so monetization is not yet proven at Adobe's scale. Anil Chakravarthy becomes CEO on December 1, adding execution risk. The thesis weakens if total ARR growth falls below high single digits, operating margins compress materially, or AI engagement fails to translate into paid retention. [Leadership transition](https://news.adobe.com/news/2026/09/adobe-announces-anil-chakravarthy-to-become-president-and-ceo)

## 2. Intuit (Nasdaq: INTU)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At about 16.8x the midpoint of FY2027 GAAP EPS guidance of $20.12-$20.36, Intuit trades at a modest multiple for a franchise with recurring tax, accounting, payroll, payments, and credit-data relationships. FY2026 operating cash flow was $8.8 billion against a roughly $94 billion closing market value, though lower cash taxes and $2.1 billion of stock compensation make the raw cash yield look better than normalized owner earnings.

**Why it remains a quality business.** FY2026 revenue rose 14% to $21.4 billion, GAAP operating income rose 20% to $5.9 billion, and Global Business Solutions revenue rose 16%. QuickBooks Online Accounting grew 23%. Cash and investments of $7.2 billion nearly matched $7.7 billion of debt, and buybacks more than offset dilution. The installed base, embedded financial data, accountant ecosystem, and switching costs support durable economics. [FY2026 results and FY2027 guidance](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1320/intuit-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-fiscal-2026-results-sets-fiscal-2027-guidance)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** FY2027 revenue guidance slows to 9%-10%; TurboTax unit volume declined 2% in FY2026; and Mailchimp is expected to be flat to down slightly. Government filing tools, AI-native accounting products, aggressive pricing, credit losses, and regulatory scrutiny could weaken growth or margins. The thesis weakens if QuickBooks Online growth drops into single digits or if repurchases cease to offset stock compensation.

## 3. AXIS Capital (NYSE: AXS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** AXIS trades at about 7.3x trailing earnings, one of the lowest multiples among companies that also scored well on growth and financial strength in the current market-wide screen. The low multiple partly reflects catastrophe exposure and the possibility that recent specialty-insurance pricing is near a cyclical peak.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 produced a 93.1% combined ratio, $143 million of underwriting income, a 14.3% annualized operating ROE, 6% gross-premium growth, and 15% year-over-year growth in book value per diluted share. Those figures indicate that current earnings are supported by underwriting profit rather than investment returns alone. [Q2 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1214816/000121481626000201/q22026pressrelease.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** A single catastrophe season, reserve deficiency, social inflation, or rapid pricing deterioration can overwhelm several quarters of underwriting profit. AXIS is scheduled to release Q3 results on October 28, after this report's evidence cutoff; conviction is therefore conditional on the new combined ratio, reserve development, and book-value trend. The thesis weakens if the combined ratio persists above 100 or book value per share reverses.

## 4. The Cigna Group (NYSE: CI)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At roughly 10.0x management's 2026 adjusted EPS floor of $30.45 and about 12.7x trailing GAAP earnings, Cigna offers a large discount to the broad market. The large-company screen estimates a 10.4% last-fiscal-year FCF yield, but insurance liabilities can make cash flow volatile, so normalized earnings are the more conservative anchor.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 revenue rose 7%, adjusted income from operations rose 6% to $2.1 billion, and Cigna Healthcare adjusted pre-tax income rose 17%. The Healthcare segment's 10.9% pre-tax margin and Evernorth's scale in pharmacy and specialty care provide diversified earnings sources. Management raised its 2026 adjusted EPS outlook. [Q2 2026 results](https://newsroom.thecignagroup.com/2026-07-30-The-Cigna-Group-Reports-Strong-Second-Quarter-2026-Results,-Raises-2026-Outlook)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Evernorth's Pharmacy Benefit Services income fell 27% in Q2, PBM economics face intense political and regulatory scrutiny, and medical-cost estimates can prove inadequate. Debt and the accounting effects of claims reserves also reduce the usefulness of simple cash-yield comparisons. The thesis weakens if Cigna Healthcare's medical-care ratio moves above guidance or Evernorth margins continue to contract.

## 5. Comcast (Nasdaq: CMCSA)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Comcast trades near 6.9x trailing earnings. Last fiscal year's $21.9 billion of FCF equates to a 28.1% screen yield at the October 27 market value, an unusually large discount even after allowing for corporate actions and cash-flow volatility. The market is pricing in a prolonged broadband decline, capital intensity, and separation risk.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q3 generated $4.2 billion of FCF and $9.1 billion of adjusted EBITDA. Pro forma revenue rose 0.5% and pro forma EBITDA rose 0.8%. Wireless service revenue grew 14.7%, Peacock produced a second consecutive profitable quarter with $261 million of adjusted EBITDA, and Business Services Connectivity retained a 55.9% EBITDA margin. These assets partly offset legacy cable erosion. [Q3 2026 results](https://cmcsa.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/comcast-reports-3rd-quarter-2026-results)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Domestic broadband revenue fell 4.7%, broadband customers declined by 144,000, quarterly FCF fell 14.8%, and debt plus continued network investment limit financial flexibility. Management paused buybacks during the proposed separation. The thesis weakens if broadband losses accelerate, wireless cannot offset price pressure, or the separation transfers too much debt to either company.

## 6. lululemon athletica (Nasdaq: LULU)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At about 10.7x the midpoint of reduced FY2026 EPS guidance and roughly 8.5x trailing earnings, Lululemon's multiple assumes a severe and lasting brand decline. Its $1.39 billion cash balance provides time for a product recovery, and inventory was slightly below the prior-year level rather than signaling an obvious balance-sheet crisis.

**Why it still qualifies, narrowly, as quality.** Even after deterioration, Q2 operating margin was 18.8%, the company remained profitable, and first-half operating cash flow was $589 million. The brand, direct distribution, customer community, and historical full-price selling model retain value. However, this is the least certain quality designation in the current top six. [Q2 2026 results](https://corporate.lululemon.com/newsroom/press-releases/2026/09-03-2026-210528733)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Q2 revenue fell 4%, Americas revenue fell 8%, total comparable sales fell 9%, operating income fell 13%, and Q3 revenue guidance calls for a 10%-11% decline. Competition, fashion misses, tariffs, discounting, and management turnover could make the low multiple a value trap. The thesis requires stabilization, not immediate growth; it weakens materially if inventories rise while comps remain deeply negative or operating margin falls into the low teens.

## Conclusion

**Adobe and Intuit are today's strongest quality-value candidates.** Both still show durable recurring revenue and double-digit recent growth, yet trade at multiples that leave room for execution disappointments. Adobe offers the larger apparent cash yield; Intuit has the clearer near-term growth profile but also a less conservative headline FCF figure.

**AXIS Capital and Cigna are the strongest lower-multiple diversifiers.** AXIS needs confirmation from today's pending results, while Cigna's regulatory and medical-cost risks justify its discount. **Comcast offers the deepest apparent cash yield but also clear structural decline. Lululemon is a watch-sized turnaround candidate, not a clean bargain.**

This ranking expresses relative research conviction under current public information. It is not a claim of certain intrinsic value and is not personal financial advice.
