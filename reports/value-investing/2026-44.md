## 1. Adobe (Nasdaq: ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At roughly 14x trailing earnings, Adobe is priced for substantial erosion even though Q3 revenue grew 13%, total ARR grew 11.2% to $27.5 billion, and quarterly operating cash flow reached a record $2.52 billion. The market is assigning little value to continued double-digit recurring-revenue growth.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Creative Cloud, Acrobat/PDF standards, enterprise workflow integration, distribution, and high incremental margins remain meaningful advantages. Nine-month operating cash flow rose 11% to $7.65 billion, and the company continues to retire shares. [Adobe Q3 results](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/01906202/au56y4ter.pdf) [Adobe Q3 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000156/adbe-20260828.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Canva, Figma, and AI-native creation tools could weaken entry-level demand, pricing, or paid-seat growth. Q3 operating margin declined year over year, and strong AI engagement is not yet proof of durable monetization. The thesis weakens if ARR growth falls below high single digits or AI delivery costs cause sustained margin compression.

## 2. Novo Nordisk (NYSE: NVO)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The ADR trades near 9.8x trailing earnings versus a much higher historical range. Current signals include a 9.8% earnings yield and 6.4% free-cash-flow yield, leaving room for a meaningful slowdown without requiring a heroic growth forecast.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Novo retains global scale in diabetes and obesity care, high manufacturing and regulatory barriers, a 39% trailing ROIC, and debt/EBITDA of only 0.8x. Q2 adjusted sales rose 7% and adjusted operating profit rose 11% at constant exchange rates; H1 free cash flow was DKK 42.5 billion. [Novo Q2 results](https://www.novonordisk.com/news-and-media/news-and-ir-materials/news-details.html?id=916589) [Novo Q2 presentation](https://www.novonordisk.com/content/dam/nncorp/global/en/investors/pdfs/financial-results/2026/Q2-2026-Full%20presentation.pdf)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Eli Lilly competition, U.S. pricing and reimbursement pressure, compounded alternatives, manufacturing execution, and pipeline failures could make the low multiple deserved. Full-year adjusted sales and operating-profit growth guidance remains between a 6% decline and flat at CER. Nine-month results due November 4 are an important near-term test.

## 3. Intuit (Nasdaq: INTU)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The headline P/E is higher than the rest of the list, but FY2026 free cash flow of $8.62 billion represents roughly a 9.2% yield on the October 30 market value. The current price is also about 16.8x the midpoint of FY2027 GAAP EPS guidance.

**Why it remains a quality business.** FY2026 revenue grew 14% to $21.4 billion and GAAP operating income grew 20%. QuickBooks, tax preparation, payroll, payments, credit data, and the accountant ecosystem create switching costs and proprietary data advantages. Cash and investments of $7.2 billion roughly offset $7.7 billion of debt. [Intuit FY2026 results](https://investors.intuit.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000896878-26-000029/fy26q4earningspressrelease.htm) [Intuit FY2026 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687826000037/intu-20260731.htm)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** TurboTax units declined 2%, Mailchimp is guided flat to slightly down, and AI-native accounting or government filing tools could weaken parts of the moat. FY2026 cash flow benefited from unusually low cash taxes, so the current FCF yield overstates normalized owner earnings.

## 4. Cognizant Technology Solutions (Nasdaq: CTSH)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Cognizant trades at about 11.9x trailing earnings and around 11.2x trailing free cash flow, a clear discount to its own longer-term cash-flow multiple and to higher-quality IT-services peers.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q3 revenue rose 4.6% to $5.7 billion, adjusted operating margin expanded 30 basis points to 16.3%, adjusted EPS grew 7.2%, and trailing bookings reached $28.7 billion, or about 1.3x revenue. Debt/EBITDA is approximately 0.5x, and the company generated $1.58 billion of free cash flow in the first nine months. [Cognizant Q3 results](https://news.cognizant.com/2026-10-29-Cognizant-Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Results)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Q3 bookings fell 7% year over year despite the healthy trailing book-to-bill. AI could reduce labor intensity and pricing before Cognizant captures enough AI implementation revenue. Wage inflation, execution on large fixed-price projects, and acquisition integration also matter.

## 5. Deckers Outdoor (NYSE: DECK)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Deckers trades at approximately 10.4x trailing earnings despite owning two scaled global brands and holding a net-cash balance sheet. The multiple provides a cushion if growth normalizes, provided the brands retain relevance.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Fiscal Q2 revenue reached a record $1.48 billion; HOKA revenue rose 6.8%, UGG rose 3.5%, and international revenue rose 9%. The company held $1.21 billion of cash, had no borrowings, and repurchased $637.5 million of shares in the first half. [Deckers Q2 FY2027 results](https://ir.deckers.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000910521-26-000028/deckex991pressrelease-9302.htm) [Deckers balance sheet](https://ir.deckers.com/financial-information/balance-sheet)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Domestic revenue slipped 0.4%, direct-to-consumer revenue fell 1.8%, and DTC comparable sales fell 6.4%. Reported margin benefited from a tariff refund. Fashion risk and dependence on HOKA and UGG make the earnings stream less durable than the software and healthcare leaders above it.

## 6. Accenture (NYSE: ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Accenture trades near 20.6x trailing earnings and offers a 7.3% FCF yield. That is not deep value, but it is reasonable for a capital-light leader still gaining share and converting earnings to cash unusually well.

**Why it remains a quality business.** FY2026 revenue reached $74 billion, up about 5% in local currency; bookings were $84.5 billion, adjusted margin expanded 20 basis points, and free cash flow rose 7% to $11.6 billion, or 1.4x net income. Current ROIC is about 17%. [Accenture FY2026 annual report](https://investor.accenture.com/~/media/Files/A/accenture-v4/investors/home/accenture-annual-report-2026.pdf) [Accenture FY2026 presentation](https://investor.accenture.com/~/media/Files/A/accenture-v4/investors/documents/accentures-fourth-quarter-fiscal-2026-earnings-presentation.pdf)

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Generative AI may automate billable work faster than new transformation demand appears. FY2027 local-currency revenue guidance of 3%-6% is solid rather than exceptional, and the labor-heavy model remains exposed to wage pressure, utilization, acquisition integration, and cybersecurity execution.

## Near misses and rejected headline bargains

- **lululemon athletica (LULU):** The 8.8x P/E, 11.4% FCF yield, 24% ROIC, and modest leverage are compelling. It misses the top six because management expects FY2026 revenue down 5%-7%, Q3 revenue down 10%-11%, and the company is navigating a leadership and product reset. [Lululemon Q2 results](https://corporate.lululemon.com/newsroom/press-releases/2026/09-03-2026-210528733)
- **Zoetis (ZTS):** A 13x P/E and roughly 7% FCF yield are unusual for the animal-health leader, but Q2 organic revenue declined 1% and full-year organic revenue guidance was cut to a 1%-3% decline. U.S. companion-animal weakness, competition, and roughly $7.4 billion of net debt lower conviction. [Zoetis Q2 results](https://investor.zoetis.com/news/news-details/2026/Zoetis-Announces-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)
- **Otis Worldwide (OTIS):** The installed service base and recurring maintenance economics are excellent, and Q3 organic sales rose 6%. At 18.8x earnings and more than 21x EV/free cash flow, however, the price offers less margin of safety than the current leaders; leverage and China exposure remain material. [Otis Q3 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1781335/000178133526000124/a2026-09x308xkerexhibit99.htm)
- **Alphabet (GOOGL):** The core franchises grew quickly, but Q3 free cash flow was negative $9.1 billion, full-year capex guidance is $195-$205 billion, and long-term debt rose sharply. The 14.8x reported P/E does not compensate enough for the uncertainty around normalized owner earnings.
- **Booking Holdings (BKNG) and AXIS Capital (AXS):** Both remain credible value ideas, but travel cyclicality and weakening insurance underwriting trends leave them below today's six.
- **Micron, energy, mining, and shipping:** Many dominate raw low-multiple screens, but peak-cycle earnings and commodity sensitivity make trailing valuation unreliable.

## Changes from the October 30 update

Today's list was selected independently and then compared with the existing weekly report.

- **Adobe remains #1.** Its combination of a 14x earnings multiple, double-digit recurring growth, and strong cash generation still leads the market-wide ranking.
- **Novo Nordisk enters at #2.** A sub-10x P/E, 39% ROIC, low leverage, and improving Q2 trends create the best new healthcare asymmetry, though November 4 results could change the view.
- **Intuit moves from #2 to #3.** The thesis remains strong; Novo offers a larger current valuation cushion.
- **Cognizant enters at #4** after fresh Q3 results showed revenue growth, margin expansion, healthy book-to-bill, and low leverage at less than 12x earnings.
- **Deckers moves from #3 to #5.** Its valuation and balance sheet remain attractive, but weak DTC comparisons keep conviction below the software and healthcare names.
- **Accenture returns at #6.** FY2026 cash flow and market-share gains support the quality case, while the price is less compelling than it was earlier in the week.
- **Alphabet, Booking, and AXIS leave the current six.** Alphabet's owner earnings are obscured by exceptional AI spending; Booking and AXIS remain viable but rank lower on current risk-adjusted quality.
- **lululemon and Zoetis improve to the top of the near-miss list** because their discounts widened, but current operating deterioration prevents higher conviction.

## Conclusion

**Adobe, Novo Nordisk, and Intuit are the strongest current quality-value candidates.** Adobe offers the cleanest blend of recurring growth and cash yield; Novo provides the largest multiple discount against still-exceptional capital returns; Intuit has the most deeply embedded workflow and data advantages.

**Cognizant is the best lower-multiple operational value, while Deckers and Accenture round out the list with stronger franchise quality than most headline bargains.** Lululemon and Zoetis offer larger apparent discounts, but their current revenue trends require evidence of stabilization before conviction rises.

This ranking expresses relative research conviction under current public information. It does not claim certainty about intrinsic value and is not personal financial advice.
