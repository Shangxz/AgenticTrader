### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $263.71, Adobe trades at roughly **14.7x** the midpoint of FY2026 GAAP EPS guidance of $17.90-$18.00. First-half operating cash flow was $5.12 billion and capital spending was only $95 million. A simple annualization implies about $10.1 billion of FCF, or approximately a **9.5% yield** on the current $106.1 billion market capitalization; seasonality means this is a valuation signal, not a forecast. Adobe repurchased $4.59 billion of stock in the first half.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Fiscal Q2 revenue rose **13%** to $6.62 billion, subscription revenue grew 14%, ARR reached $27.10 billion and remaining performance obligations rose 13%. AI-first ARR more than tripled and exceeded $500 million, evidence that AI is also a monetization opportunity rather than only a threat. Adobe's creative, document and marketing workflows retain substantial distribution and switching advantages. [Adobe Q2 FY2026 release](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/11606202/a5543arefgt.pdf) | [Adobe Q2 Form 10-Q](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/adbe-10q-q226-final.pdf)

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** Generative AI may reduce switching costs, commoditize parts of content creation or raise inference costs faster than pricing. Stock-based compensation and acquisitions dilute the apparent owner yield. The thesis weakens if ARR growth falls into single digits, AI products fail to support retention or pricing, or buybacks merely offset dilution.

### 2. Accenture (ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $179.82, Accenture trades for roughly **13.4x** the midpoint of FY2026 GAAP EPS guidance. Management still expects **$10.8-$11.5 billion of FCF**, equivalent to approximately a **9.8%-10.4% yield** on the current $110.7 billion market capitalization, while planning at least $9.5 billion of FY2026 capital returns.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Fiscal Q3 revenue rose 6% in US dollars to $18.72 billion, operating margin expanded 20 basis points to 17.0%, EPS rose 9% and quarterly FCF was $3.6 billion. Managed-services revenue grew 8%, and 104 bookings of at least $100 million year to date demonstrate the depth of its enterprise relationships. [Accenture Q3 FY2026 results](https://newsroom.accenture.com/content/3qfy26-earnings/accenture-reports-third-quarter-fiscal-2026-results.pdf)

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** Local-currency growth is only 3%-4%, bookings declined modestly and US federal work is a headwind. AI could reduce labor-intensive implementation demand before new transformation work fully replaces it. Persistent booking weakness, falling utilization or an FCF miss would weaken the case.

### 3. Comcast (CMCSA)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Comcast trades at about **8.2x trailing earnings**. A current public filing-based screen estimates a **21.4% trailing FCF yield**, broadly consistent with the company's unusually large cash output relative to its roughly $91 billion equity value. Q2 alone generated $4.60 billion of FCF. The planned tax-free separation of NBCUniversal and Sky from the connectivity business could expose asset value that the conglomerate multiple currently obscures.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 pro forma revenue rose 4.7%, wireless posted a record 448,000 line additions, Business Services EBITDA grew 5%, and Peacock reached quarterly profitability with 48 million paid subscribers. Comcast's network scale, broadband cash flows, media franchises, studios and parks are real assets even though the consolidated growth profile is weak. [Comcast Q2 2026 results](https://www.cmcsa.com/node/45906) | [Separation announcement](https://corporate.comcast.com/press/releases/comcast-announces-plans-to-separate-media-and-technology-businesses-into-two-leading-public-companies)

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** Residential connectivity revenue and EBITDA are declining, capital spending rose, the balance sheet carries substantial debt, and repurchases are paused during the separation. Execution costs or stranded overhead could consume part of the sum-of-parts upside. The thesis weakens if broadband losses reaccelerate, post-separation leverage is aggressive or consolidated FCF falls materially.

### 4. Arch Capital Group (ACGL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $98.26, Arch trades near **7.7x trailing earnings** and approximately **1.44x** June 30 book value of $68.04 per share. The earnings multiple must be normalized for the insurance cycle, but the book-value premium remains reasonable for a franchise generating mid-teens operating returns on equity.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 produced an 18.0% annualized net-income ROE, a 15.3% operating ROE, an 82.5% combined ratio excluding catastrophes and prior-year development, and 2.8% sequential book-value-per-share growth. Arch's insurance, reinsurance and mortgage platforms diversify its underwriting engine, while repurchases demonstrate active capital allocation. [Arch Capital Q2 2026 results](https://ir.archgroup.com/news/news-details/2026/Arch-Capital-Group-Ltd--Reports-2026-Second-Quarter-Results/default.aspx) | [Arch Q1 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/947484/000094748426000058/acgl-20260331.htm)

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** Net premiums earned and underwriting income declined year over year as pricing became more competitive. Catastrophes, reserve errors and softening rates can make trailing earnings a false anchor. Sustained combined ratios above the low 90s, reserve deterioration or falling book value would challenge the thesis.

### 5. Ameriprise Financial (AMP)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $562.98, Ameriprise trades at approximately **13.6x trailing earnings**, below the broad market despite a high-return, increasingly fee-based wealth franchise. Full-year 2025 adjusted operating EPS grew 14% to $39.29, and Q1 2026 adjusted operating EPS increased 19% to $11.26.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q1 assets under management, administration and advisement reached $1.7 trillion, pretax adjusted operating margin was 28%, and ROE excluding AOCI was 54%; the company returned 88% of operating earnings that quarter. Q2 assets rose to $1.8 trillion, up 14% year over year, while Advice & Wealth Management client assets grew 15%. Scale, advisor relationships and recurring asset-based fees support durable economics. [Ameriprise Q1 2026 results](https://www.ameriprise.com/newsroom/news-releases/ameriprise-financial-reports-first-quarter-2026-results) | [Ameriprise Q2 announcement](https://ir.ameriprise.com/news/news-details/2026/Ameriprise-Financial-Announces-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx) | [Reuters Q2 report](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/ameriprise-financial-quarterly-profit-rises-on-higher-fee-income-4809030)

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** Earnings are sensitive to equity markets, interest rates and client flows; Columbia Threadneedle has faced net-flow pressure, and insurance liabilities complicate the balance-sheet analysis. The thesis weakens if organic wealth flows stall, asset-management outflows worsen or capital return begins masking weak underlying growth.

## Changes from August 11

- **Adobe moves to rank 1** as its price fell about 3.4% while the latest operating evidence remained unchanged. The lower price lifts the simple annualized first-half FCF yield to roughly 9.5%.
- **Accenture remains a highest-confidence idea** but moves to rank 2. Its guided FCF yield is excellent; Adobe currently offers faster recurring-revenue growth at a comparable owner-earnings yield.
- **Comcast returns at rank 3.** The Q2 evidence and planned NBCUniversal/Sky separation provide a more concrete catalyst than yesterday's brief watchlist treatment reflected. This is still the highest-leverage and most structurally challenged nonfinancial name in the top five.
- **Arch Capital remains in the leading group** with essentially unchanged price and thesis.
- **Ameriprise is a new addition** because its 13.6x earnings multiple is paired with a 50%-plus ROE, strong asset growth and disciplined capital return.
- **Deckers moves to the watchlist.** Its balance sheet and brands remain attractive, but today's independent ranking gives the edge to Comcast's cash yield and catalyst and Ameriprise's capital efficiency. This is a relative change, not a broken-thesis call.

## Conclusion

**Adobe and Accenture are today's strongest quality-value combinations.** Both offer roughly double-digit owner-earnings yields or close proxies while retaining durable customer relationships and credible growth, though AI is central to both the opportunity and the risk. **Comcast** offers the largest apparent cash discount and a real separation catalyst, **Arch Capital** offers disciplined underwriting at a modest book-value premium, and **Ameriprise** pairs a below-market earnings multiple with exceptional capital efficiency.

No intrinsic-value estimate is certain. Treat this report as a research shortlist and test each thesis against its disconfirming factors rather than as a guarantee of investment returns.
