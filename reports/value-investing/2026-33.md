## Current market context

The broad US market is still expensive enough that "cheap versus the index" is a weak thesis by itself. An August 12 quality-value screen cited the S&P 500 at about **19.9x forward earnings**; dispersion beneath that aggregate remains wide. Today's work therefore emphasized normalized earnings, recurring cash economics, returns on capital, balance-sheet resilience and evidence that a discount reflects a temporary controversy rather than irreversible impairment. ### 1. Zoetis (ZTS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $75.84, Zoetis trades near **12.4x trailing EPS** and roughly **11.0x** the midpoint of its current $6.85-$7.00 adjusted 2026 EPS guidance. That is an unusually low entry multiple for the leading listed animal-health company. Morningstar's June wide-moat screen also identified Zoetis among the ten most discounted constituents, independently supporting the view that the price reset is material rather than merely market-relative. [Zoetis quarterly results and 2026 guidance](https://investor.zoetis.com/financials/quarterly-results/default.aspx) | [Morningstar June shortlist](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/10-undervalued-wide-moat-stocks-worth-buying-right-now)

**Why it remains a quality business.** Zoetis benefits from trusted brands, regulated products, a broad companion-animal and livestock portfolio, veterinarian relationships and global distribution. Those advantages have historically supported attractive margins and repeat demand. The current guidance still calls for positive organic growth despite a softer period, while animal-health spending has structural support from pet humanization and livestock productivity.

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** Current guidance is only 2%-5% organic revenue growth and 2%-6% organic adjusted-net-income growth, well below the company's historical premium-growth profile. Competition, product safety issues, channel inventory, weaker veterinary traffic, livestock cycles and approximately $8.6 billion of debt fair value at March 31 all matter. The thesis weakens if organic growth remains near zero, key franchises lose share, or leverage rises without a clear return. [Zoetis Q1 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1555280/000155528026000024/zts-20260331.htm)

### 2. Broadridge Financial Solutions (BR)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $172.24, Broadridge trades near **18.4x trailing EPS**. That is not statistically deep value, but it is attractive for a business with unusually sticky regulatory communications, governance and capital-markets infrastructure revenue. Morningstar ranked Broadridge among its most undervalued wide-moat companies in June and again among the highest-quality companies trading below assessed fair value. [Morningstar June wide-moat shortlist](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/10-undervalued-wide-moat-stocks-worth-buying-right-now) | [Morningstar Best Companies screen](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/10-best-companies-invest-now)

**Why it remains a quality business.** Broadridge sits inside mandatory investor communications and mission-critical brokerage workflows, where scale, regulation and switching costs support recurring revenue. Fiscal Q3 recurring revenue grew 6% in constant currency and adjusted EPS rose 11%; wealth and investment-management recurring revenue grew 13% in constant currency, including both organic and acquired growth. [Broadridge Q3 FY2026 results](https://www.broadridge-ir.com/news/news-details/2026/Broadridge-Reports-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2026-Results/default.aspx)

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** The valuation still assumes steady compounding. Acquisitions add integration and leverage risk, distribution revenue is lower quality than recurring revenue, event-driven activity is volatile, and large financial institutions can pressure pricing. Persistent organic recurring growth below the mid-single digits, margin compression or acquisition-led growth without per-share value creation would undermine the thesis.

### 3. The Cigna Group (CI)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $277.99, Cigna trades at about **9.1x** management's raised minimum 2026 adjusted EPS outlook of $30.45. Q2 adjusted EPS was $7.78, up 8.1% year over year. The multiple prices in substantial reimbursement and policy risk while assigning little value to continued mid-to-high-single-digit earnings growth. [Cigna Q2 results summary](https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/2965046/ci-q2-earnings-beat-estimates-on-cigna-healthcare-strength) | [Cigna investor relations](https://investors.thecignagroup.com/)

**Why it remains a quality business.** Cigna combines a scaled employer-health franchise with Evernorth's pharmacy, specialty and care services. Q2 revenue rose 7% to $71.7 billion, while Cigna Healthcare revenue rose 9.1% and adjusted operating income rose 17%. Scale, customer relationships and specialty-pharmacy capabilities support durable earnings, although those same advantages attract regulatory scrutiny.

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** PBM regulation, rebate reform, medical-cost inflation, customer concentration and political intervention can reset margins. Cash conversion should be monitored after first-half working-capital timing effects. The case weakens if Evernorth margins compress materially, medical costs outrun pricing or operating cash flow persistently trails adjusted income.

### 4. NXP Semiconductors (NXPI)

**Why it looks undervalued.** NXP appeared in the current broad value screen at **14.1x forward earnings** and a **0.73 PEG**, versus roughly 19.9x for the S&P 500. At $231.91, the equity value is also supported by trailing 12-month non-GAAP free cash flow of $2.81 billion, though the resulting yield is solid rather than extreme. [Current value screen](https://www.kiplinger.com/investing/stocks/the-best-value-stocks-to-buy) | [NXP Q2 2026 results](https://investors.nxp.com/news-releases/news-release-details/nxp-semiconductors-reports-second-quarter-2026-results/)

**Why it remains a quality business.** NXP holds strong positions in auto microcontrollers, secure connectivity and embedded processing, where qualification cycles and customer integration create switching costs. Q2 revenue rose 19% to $3.50 billion, GAAP gross margin was 57.3%, and free cash flow was $791 million, or 22.6% of revenue. The company also repaid $750 million of notes at maturity from available cash.

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** Auto and industrial semiconductor demand is cyclical, China exposure is meaningful, customers can reduce inventory abruptly, and capital allocation includes both debt and buybacks. A renewed inventory correction, gross margin falling materially below the high-50% range, or structurally weaker auto content growth would weaken the case.

### 5. Salesforce (CRM)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $201.37, Salesforce trades at about **15.3x** the midpoint of FY2027 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $13.11-$13.19. The stricter comparison is approximately **25.5x** midpoint GAAP guidance because stock compensation and acquired-intangible amortization are economically relevant. The non-GAAP multiple is compelling; the GAAP multiple says the margin of safety is moderate, not enormous. [Salesforce Q1 FY2027 results](https://investor.salesforce.com/news/news-details/2026/Salesforce-Delivers-Record-First-Quarter-Fiscal-2027-Results/default.aspx)

**Why it remains a quality business.** Fiscal Q1 revenue rose 13%, current remaining performance obligation rose 14%, GAAP operating margin reached 21.1%, and free cash flow was $6.56 billion. Subscription relationships, embedded workflows and a large installed base support retention and cross-selling.

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** Informatica contributed about four points to Q1 growth and roughly three points to full-year guidance. Salesforce also issued about $25 billion of debt to fund an accelerated repurchase, raising capital-allocation risk. AI may expand the platform or reduce seat growth and application switching costs. Slowing cRPO, stalled GAAP margins or debt-funded repurchases that fail to create per-share value would challenge the thesis.

## Changes from August 13

- **Zoetis enters at rank 1 from the earlier-week record.** Its approximately 11x guided adjusted EPS multiple is the strongest combination of franchise durability and valuation reset in today's independent comparison; slower near-term organic growth is the central concern.
- **Broadridge enters at rank 2.** Its valuation is less optically cheap than Arch or Cigna, but regulatory infrastructure, switching costs and recurring revenue give it a higher confidence quality profile.
- **Cigna remains in the top five at rank 3.** The roughly 9.1x guided adjusted earnings multiple remains compelling, though policy and PBM risk prevent a top-two ranking.
- **NXP is a new top-five entry.** A below-market forward multiple now pairs with 19% Q2 growth, high margins and 22.6% quarterly FCF conversion.
- **Salesforce remains in the top five but moves to rank 5.** The operating case is intact; Zoetis and Broadridge offer cleaner quality-value asymmetry, while debt-funded repurchases and the GAAP/non-GAAP gap cap conviction.
- **Arch Capital, Ameriprise and Comcast leave today's top five on relative ranking, not necessarily broken theses.** Arch's earnings require insurance-cycle normalization, Ameriprise is more exposed to buoyant asset markets, and Comcast retains the clearest structural value-trap risk.

## Watchlist and near misses

- **Arch Capital (ACGL):** disciplined underwriting and book-value compounding remain attractive, but current earnings benefit from the insurance cycle.
- **Ameriprise Financial (AMP):** high-return wealth economics and strong capital return, offset by market sensitivity and asset-management outflows.
- **Comcast (CMCSA):** exceptional current cash yield and separation optionality, but broadband losses and leverage keep confidence lower.
- **Adobe (ADBE):** excellent recurring revenue and cash generation; AI competition, stock compensation and acquisition economics require continued monitoring.
- **Accenture (ACN):** strong guided FCF yield and enterprise relationships; muted organic growth and AI's effect on labor-based delivery remain the key debate.
- **Nike (NKE):** the June moat screen showed an exceptionally large assessed discount, but current earnings erosion and turnaround execution make it lower confidence than today's five.
- **MarketAxess (MKTX):** strong margins and network economics, but the announced ICE transaction now makes merger terms and closing risk more relevant than a standalone value thesis.

## Conclusion

**Zoetis and Broadridge are today's strongest quality-value combinations.** Zoetis offers the week's most striking reset in a durable franchise, while Broadridge offers a cleaner recurring-revenue compounder at a reasonable rather than distressed multiple. **Cigna** provides the deepest guided earnings discount, **NXP** combines a below-market forward multiple with improving semiconductor fundamentals, and **Salesforce** remains attractive on non-GAAP earnings but requires disciplined treatment of stock compensation, acquisition effects and new debt.

No intrinsic-value estimate is certain. Treat this report as a research shortlist and test each thesis against its disconfirming factors rather than as a guarantee of investment returns.
