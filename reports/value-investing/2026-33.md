# Value Investing Weekly Report — 2026-W33

_Last updated: August 10, 2026 (prices approximately 8:00 a.m. ET). Research support only; not personal financial advice._

## Market context

The opportunity set is selective. The S&P 500 closed August 7 at 7,755.61, about **20.4x forward earnings** and **24.2x trailing earnings**, while the 10-year Treasury yielded roughly **4.65%**. That combination raises the hurdle rate for equity value and favors businesses with visible cash generation rather than merely low headline multiples. [StreetStats market valuation](https://streetstats.finance/valuation/market)

Today's ranking was produced independently from a fresh, cross-sector screen of US-listed equities. The screen emphasized earnings and free-cash-flow valuation, recurring revenue or defensible market position, returns and margins, balance-sheet risk, and evidence that current weakness is cyclical or fixable rather than structural. Reported P/E can be misleading: Alphabet, for example, was excluded because a large investment gain inflated reported earnings while second-quarter capital spending pushed quarterly free cash flow negative.

### 1. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $265.21, Adobe trades at roughly **14.8x** the midpoint of FY2026 GAAP EPS guidance of $17.90-$18.00. The market is valuing a still-growing subscription franchise close to a mature-software multiple. First-half operating cash flow was $5.12 billion, supporting a high-single-digit annualized cash-flow yield before modest capital spending.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Second-quarter revenue rose **13%** to a record $6.62 billion; subscription revenue grew **14%**; ARR reached $27.10 billion; and RPO was $22.27 billion. GAAP operating margin was about 34%, while the company raised full-year revenue and EPS targets. The creative and document ecosystems retain high switching costs, and the early AI-first ARR figure exceeded $500 million. [Adobe Q2 FY2026 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000109/adbeex991q226.htm)

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** Generative AI could lower switching costs or pressure pricing; reported cash flow should be judged after stock compensation; the Semrush acquisition and CFO transition add execution risk. The thesis weakens if ARR growth falls into single digits, net retention deteriorates, or AI products fail to monetize while inference costs rise.

### 2. The Cigna Group (CI)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The share price is about **9.3x** management's 2026 adjusted EPS floor of $30.35. Cigna also raised its operating outlook after the first quarter, and the low multiple leaves room for value even with modest long-term growth.

**Why it remains a quality business.** First-quarter revenue rose **5%** to $68.5 billion and adjusted income from operations rose **12%** to $2.06 billion. Evernorth provides scale in pharmacy, specialty care and services, while Cigna Healthcare adds a distinct earnings stream. Debt-to-capital improved to **42.3%** from 43.0% at year-end. [Cigna Q1 2026 results](https://newsroom.thecignagroup.com/2026-04-30-The-Cigna-Group-Reports-Strong-First-Quarter-2026-Results%2C-Raises-2026-Outlook)

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** PBM regulation, drug-pricing reform, client concentration and medical-cost inflation can impair earnings quality. Pharmacy customers declined 2% sequentially in Q1, and PBM pre-tax income fell 28%. Cigna reports Q2 on August 13, so today's confidence is capped pending that update.

### 3. Qualcomm (QCOM)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The trailing P/E near **19x** is below the broader market despite a high-margin licensing stream and credible non-handset growth. It is less statistically cheap than Adobe or Cigna, but its business mix is improving.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Fiscal Q3 revenue was $9.9 billion and non-GAAP EPS was $2.21. Automotive plus IoT revenue grew **28%** year over year, and automotive has now posted 23 consecutive quarters of double-digit growth. The patent portfolio and QTL royalty model remain difficult to replicate. [Qualcomm fiscal Q3 2026 results](https://www.qualcomm.com/news/releases/2026/07/qualcomm-announces-third-quarter-fiscal-2026-results)

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** Handsets remain cyclical and exposed to memory constraints; Apple modem insourcing, China exposure, licensing disputes and semiconductor competition are material. The thesis weakens if diversification fails to offset handset erosion or if acquisition spending dilutes free-cash-flow returns.

### 4. Zoetis (ZTS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $72.66, Zoetis trades around **11.9x trailing earnings** and roughly **10.4-10.6x** the May adjusted EPS range of $6.85-$7.00. That is an unusually low multiple for the global animal-health leader.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Zoetis has a broad companion-animal and livestock portfolio, trusted veterinary relationships, global distribution and historically high margins. In 2025 it produced $9.5 billion of revenue, 6% organic operational growth and adjusted EPS of $6.41. Its pipeline and category leadership create recovery potential if US veterinary demand stabilizes. [Zoetis 2025 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1555280/000155528026000008/exhibit9912-31x25q4.htm)

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** This is the least settled thesis in the top five. In Q1, price-sensitive pet owners reduced visits and premium-product demand; competition intensified in dermatology and parasiticides; and management cut revenue and EPS guidance. [Zoetis Q1 2026 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1555280/000155528026000021/exhibit99q12026.htm) The post-Q2 price weakness suggests investors still see estimate risk. Require evidence of stable product volumes and no further material guidance cuts before raising confidence.

### 5. Comcast (CMCSA)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Comcast trades near **8.2x trailing earnings**. First-half free cash flow was $8.51 billion against a market capitalization near $90 billion, an annualized yield approaching 19%; even allowing for working-capital seasonality and separation costs, the discount is large.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 free cash flow rose 2.3% to $4.60 billion. Business-services connectivity EBITDA rose 5% with a 56.7% margin; wireless additions hit a record; and Peacock reached quarterly profitability. The intended NBCUniversal/Sky separation may make the disparate assets easier to value. [Comcast Q2 2026 results](https://www.cmcsa.com/node/45906)

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** Residential broadband revenue and customers continue to decline, adjusted EPS fell 16.7% in Q2, and debt was about $90.4 billion versus $7.7 billion of cash. Capital spending is rising, buybacks are paused during the separation, and execution complexity is high. Comcast is cheap enough to qualify, but it is not a clean compounder.

## Watchlist and exclusions

- **Clorox (CLX):** strong brands and a roughly 17x trailing P/E, but weak year-to-date cash conversion and added leverage from the $2.25 billion GOJO/Purell acquisition keep it below the top five.
- **Alphabet (GOOGL):** excellent operating momentum, but reported P/E is distorted by investment gains and Q2 capex of roughly $44.9 billion exceeded operating cash flow. Reassess when normalized free-cash-flow visibility improves.
- **MarketAxess (MKTX):** passed the quality screen, but the announced acquisition by Intercontinental Exchange makes the risk/reward primarily merger-dependent rather than a clean standalone value case.
- **Campbell's (CPB):** the low multiple is offset by weak organic growth, leverage and limited evidence of durable reinvestment returns.
- **Nike (NKE):** a high-quality brand, but about 28x trailing earnings still prices in a meaningful turnaround; today's margin of safety is weaker than the leaders above.

## Conclusion

**Adobe is today's strongest all-around quality-value candidate:** the multiple has compressed while revenue, ARR, backlog and cash generation still grow at healthy rates. **Cigna** offers the deepest earnings valuation among the higher-confidence ideas, though the August 13 report is an important near-term check. **Qualcomm** ranks third as a less-cheap but high-quality diversification story. **Zoetis** has the largest apparent rerating potential but requires confirmation that its slowdown is stabilizing. **Comcast** is a high-cash-yield special situation whose structural and balance-sheet risks justify a smaller margin-of-safety allocation.

No intrinsic-value estimate is certain. These companies should be monitored against the explicit disconfirming factors above rather than treated as guaranteed winners.
