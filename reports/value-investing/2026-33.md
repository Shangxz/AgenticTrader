### 1. Arch Capital Group (ACGL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $97.29, Arch trades for about **7.6x trailing earnings** and **1.43x** June 30 book value of $68.04 per share. The trailing multiple is helped by a favorable insurance cycle and should not be capitalized blindly, but the book-value premium is modest for a franchise producing mid-teens operating returns on equity.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 produced an **82.5% combined ratio** excluding catastrophes and prior-year development, **15.3% annualized operating ROE**, 2.8% sequential book-value-per-share growth and $1.2 billion of repurchases. Arch's specialty insurance, reinsurance and mortgage-insurance platforms diversify the underwriting engine, while the long record of book-value compounding supports the inference that underwriting discipline is more than a one-cycle event. [Arch Q2 2026 results](https://ir.archgroup.com/news/news-details/2026/Arch-Capital-Group-Ltd--Reports-2026-Second-Quarter-Results/default.aspx) | [2026 proxy and long-term performance record](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/947484/000094748426000038/acgl-20260324.htm)

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** Net premiums earned fell 8.1% and underwriting income fell 19.7% year over year; the ex-catastrophe combined ratio also worsened 1.6 points. Catastrophes, reserve errors, mortgage stress and softening rates can make current earnings a false anchor. Persistent core combined-ratio deterioration or falling book value would weaken the case.

### 2. Ameriprise Financial (AMP)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $565.07, Ameriprise trades near **13.7x trailing earnings** and about **12.7x** annualized first-half adjusted operating EPS of $22.33. That is a below-market multiple for a high-return, increasingly fee-based wealth franchise, although the earnings base benefits from strong capital markets.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 assets under management, administration and advisement reached a record **$1.8 trillion**, up 14%; adjusted operating EPS rose 22%; adjusted ROE excluding AOCI was **55%**; and the company returned $932 million, or 91% of operating earnings, to shareholders. Advice & Wealth Management maintained a 28.9% margin and wrap net flows improved to $6.9 billion. [Ameriprise Q2 2026 earnings release](https://s205.q4cdn.com/618704162/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/AMP-Q2-2026-Earnings-Release.pdf)

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** Earnings are sensitive to equity markets and interest rates, and the asset-management arm still recorded $6.5 billion of quarterly net outflows. Insurance liabilities and market-risk benefits complicate simple balance-sheet comparisons. Weak organic wealth flows, accelerating asset-management redemptions or falling advisor productivity would challenge the thesis.

### 3. The Cigna Group (CI)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $277.41, Cigna trades at roughly **9.1x** management's raised 2026 adjusted EPS outlook of at least $30.45. Q2 adjusted EPS increased 8.1% to $7.78. The valuation leaves room for policy and reimbursement risk while assigning little value to continued mid-to-high-single-digit earnings growth.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Cigna combines a scaled employer-health franchise with Evernorth's pharmacy, specialty and care services. Q2 revenue rose 7% to $71.7 billion; Cigna Healthcare revenue rose 9.1% and adjusted operating income rose 17%, prompting a higher full-year earnings outlook. Scale, customer relationships and specialty-pharmacy capabilities support durable cash generation, though they also attract regulatory scrutiny. [Cigna investor relations](https://investors.thecignagroup.com/) | [Q2 results summary](https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/2965046/ci-q2-earnings-beat-estimates-on-cigna-healthcare-strength)

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** PBM regulation, rebate reform, medical-cost inflation, customer concentration and political pressure can reset margins. Q1 operating cash flow declined because of Inflation Reduction Act timing and working-capital items, making full-year cash conversion an important check. The case weakens if Evernorth margins compress materially or cash generation persistently trails adjusted income. [Cigna Q1 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1739940/000173994026000043/ci-20260331.htm)

### 4. Salesforce (CRM)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $193.32, Salesforce trades for about **13.7x** the midpoint of FY2027 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $14.06-$14.12. The more conservative comparison is **24.3x** midpoint GAAP EPS guidance because stock compensation and acquired-intangible amortization are economically relevant. Even on that stricter basis, the price is reasonable if recurring revenue and margin expansion endure.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Fiscal Q1 revenue rose 13%, current remaining performance obligation rose 14%, GAAP operating margin reached 21.1%, and non-GAAP margin reached 34.8%. Full-year revenue guidance implies about 11% growth, including roughly three points from Informatica, while subscription relationships and deeply embedded workflows support retention and pricing power. [Salesforce Q1 FY2027 results](https://investor.salesforce.com/news/news-details/2026/Salesforce-Delivers-Record-First-Quarter-Fiscal-2027-Results/default.aspx)

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** Organic growth is slower than the headline because Informatica contributes materially. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS excludes about $4.94 per share of stock compensation, and Salesforce issued $25 billion of debt to fund an accelerated buyback. AI may strengthen the platform or reduce seat growth and application switching costs. The thesis weakens if cRPO growth slips, GAAP margins stall or debt-funded repurchases fail to reduce dilution.

### 5. Comcast (CMCSA)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Comcast trades at about **8.2x trailing earnings** and screens with one of the highest filing-based FCF yields among large US companies. Q2 alone generated $4.60 billion of FCF. The intended tax-free separation of NBCUniversal and Sky may expose asset value obscured by the conglomerate structure, but the discount partly reflects genuine deterioration.

**Why it remains a quality business.** Q2 pro forma revenue rose 4.7%, wireless posted a record 448,000 line additions, Business Services EBITDA grew 5%, and Peacock reached quarterly profitability with 48 million subscribers. Comcast's broadband network, business connectivity, studios, media franchises and parks remain valuable assets with substantial scale. [Comcast Q2 2026 results](https://www.cmcsa.com/node/45906)

**Risks / disconfirming factors.** Residential broadband still lost 167,000 customers, reported revenue fell 1.2%, pro forma adjusted EBITDA fell 5.3%, debt is substantial and buybacks are paused during the separation. Fiber and fixed-wireless competition may turn a high FCF yield into a value trap. The case weakens if broadband losses reaccelerate, post-separation leverage is aggressive or consolidated FCF trends down materially.

## Changes from August 12

- **Arch Capital moves from rank 4 to rank 1.** Today's independent screen gives greater weight to its 15.3% operating ROE, 1.43x book valuation and active repurchases, while explicitly normalizing the favorable insurance cycle.
- **Ameriprise moves from rank 5 to rank 2.** The full Q2 release confirms 22% adjusted EPS growth, 55% adjusted ROE and 91% capital return, strengthening yesterday's shorter case.
- **Cigna enters at rank 3 from the watchlist.** Its raised $30.45 minimum adjusted EPS outlook and stronger Cigna Healthcare margins make the roughly 9.1x valuation compelling despite PBM policy risk.
- **Salesforce is a new top-five entry.** Margin expansion and 14% cRPO growth now pair with a 13.7x guided non-GAAP earnings multiple; GAAP valuation and debt-funded buybacks keep confidence below the top two.
- **Comcast remains in the top five but falls to rank 5.** Cash generation and the separation catalyst survive review, yet broadband losses, declining pro forma EBITDA and leverage make it today's highest-risk selection.
- **Adobe and Accenture move to the watchlist on relative ranking, not broken theses.** Today's screen found stronger valuation support in ACGL, AMP and CI and a more asymmetric, though riskier, setup in CRM. Prior picks did not influence candidate selection.

## Watchlist and near misses

- **Adobe (ADBE):** excellent recurring revenue and cash generation; AI competition, stock compensation and acquisition economics require continued monitoring.
- **Accenture (ACN):** strong guided FCF yield and enterprise relationships; muted local-currency growth and AI's effect on labor-based delivery remain the key debate.
- **EPAM Systems (EPAM):** approximately 13.6x trailing earnings and net liquidity are attractive, but negative first-half FCF, subdued organic growth and AI disruption fears keep it below the quality threshold today.
- **Deckers Outdoor (DECK):** premium brands and a clean balance sheet, offset by fashion concentration and margin pressure.
- **PayPal (PYPL):** inexpensive with healthy cash generation, but branded-checkout competition and uncertain pricing power remain unresolved.

## Conclusion

**Arch Capital and Ameriprise are today's strongest quality-value combinations.** Arch offers disciplined underwriting and book-value compounding at a modest premium to book; Ameriprise combines a scaled wealth franchise with exceptional capital efficiency. **Cigna** offers the deepest earnings discount among the higher-quality defensives, **Salesforce** offers recurring software economics at a reset multiple, and **Comcast** offers the greatest apparent cash discount but also the clearest value-trap risk.

No intrinsic-value estimate is certain. Treat this report as a research shortlist and test each thesis against its disconfirming factors rather than as a guarantee of investment returns.
