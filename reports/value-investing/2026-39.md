### Today's top ideas

#### 1. Adobe (Nasdaq: ADBE) - strongest overall candidate

**Why it looks undervalued**

- At **$240.69**, Adobe trades at **13.4x trailing earnings**, a large discount to the broad market despite double-digit growth.
- Fiscal Q3 revenue rose **13%** to $6.76 billion, operating cash flow reached a record **$2.52 billion**, and management raised full-year revenue and EPS targets.
- Adobe repurchased about **9.5 million shares** in the quarter, so the cash engine is translating into meaningful per-share support. ([Q3 FY2026 SEC earnings exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000147/adbeex991q326.htm))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- Subscription revenue rose 14%, total ARR reached **$27.50 billion**, and RPO was **$22.16 billion**.
- GAAP operating income of $2.35 billion equaled a roughly **35% operating margin**.
- Creative, document, and customer-experience products remain embedded in professional workflows, while Adobe is monetizing generative features inside the installed base.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- RPO growth of about 8% trails current revenue growth, so forward demand needs monitoring.
- Generative competitors could weaken workflow lock-in or pricing faster than Adobe converts free users to paid plans.
- The CEO transition scheduled for December adds execution risk. Sustained single-digit ARR growth, falling retention, or material margin compression would invalidate the thesis.

**Confidence:** High, with material technology-disruption risk.

#### 2. Deckers Brands (NYSE: DECK) - cleanest balance-sheet value

**Why it looks undervalued**

- The stock trades at **10.7x trailing earnings** and roughly the same multiple on the midpoint of FY2027 EPS guidance.
- June cash was **$1.60 billion**, nearly 15% of the market value, with no outstanding borrowings.
- Management expects FY2027 repurchases equal to about 80% of projected FCF, adding per-share upside if the shares remain depressed. ([Q1 FY2027 results](https://ir.deckers.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/661/deckers-brands-reports-first-quarter-fiscal-year-2027-financial-results))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- Fiscal 2026 revenue grew **9.8%**, diluted EPS grew **10.9%**, and operating margin remained an exceptional **23.1%**.
- HOKA and UGG supply two premium global franchises; international fiscal 2026 sales grew **26.8%**, broadening the growth base.
- Fiscal 2026 operating cash flow was **$1.18 billion**, while the company ended March with $1.91 billion of cash. ([FY2026 10-K](https://ir.deckers.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001628280-26-037664/deck-20260331.htm))

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- Q1 operating income declined as SG&A grew faster than revenue, and HOKA growth is moderating from a high base.
- Footwear brands can lose relevance quickly; tariffs, wholesale destocking, or discounting could compress the unusually high margins.
- Sustained low-single-digit brand growth or rising promotions would undermine the quality case.

**Confidence:** High-medium.

#### 3. Universal Health Services (NYSE: UHS) - cheapest durable operator

**Why it looks undervalued**

- At **$181.39**, UHS trades at **7.4x trailing earnings** and about **7.9x** the midpoint of revised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $22.28-$23.65.
- First-half operating cash flow was **$845 million** and capital expenditure was $445 million, leaving roughly $400 million of cash after capital investment.
- Debt to trailing adjusted EBITDA was **1.81x**, manageable for a business with recurring hospital demand. ([Q2 2026 SEC earnings exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/352915/000119312526318940/uhs-ex99_1.htm))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- Q2 net revenue increased **8.3%** to $4.64 billion and adjusted EBITDA increased **5.4%** to $678 million.
- Acute-care and behavioral-health networks benefit from local scale, licenses, payer relationships, and persistent demand that is difficult to recreate quickly.
- The behavioral-health segment adds a differentiated capacity base in an area with chronic access constraints.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- Management reduced the midpoint of 2026 EBITDA and adjusted EPS guidance; the quarter also included a favorable $100 million Florida Medicaid item.
- Labor costs, reimbursement changes, litigation, and self-insurance reserves can move results sharply.
- Cash conversion weakened year over year in the first half, and debt rose. Further guidance cuts or persistent volume weakness would turn the low multiple into a trap.

**Confidence:** Medium-high.

#### 4. PayPal (Nasdaq: PYPL) - high cash yield with a margin caveat

**Why it looks undervalued**

- At **$52.51**, PayPal trades at **9.9x trailing earnings**.
- Q2 generated **$1.78 billion** of FCF and $1.83 billion of adjusted FCF; annualizing one quarter is not a forecast, but it shows substantial cash capacity relative to the $46.3 billion market value.
- The company repurchased **$6.0 billion** of shares over the trailing 12 months, equivalent to roughly 13% of today's market value. ([Q2 2026 SEC earnings exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633917/000163391726000080/pypl2q-26earningsrelease.htm))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- Non-GAAP operating margin contracted **248 basis points**, non-GAAP operating income fell 8%, and active accounts were nearly flat.
- Checkout competition, lower-margin processing mix, and aggressive wallet ecosystems may keep pressure on economics.
- The thesis requires transaction-margin growth and branded-checkout stabilization; buybacks alone cannot repair a deteriorating franchise.

**Confidence:** Medium.

#### 5. Bristol Myers Squibb (NYSE: BMY) - low multiple with a credible portfolio transition

**Why it looks undervalued**

- June debt of about $43.1 billion versus $11.5 billion of cash and marketable securities leaves much less balance-sheet flexibility than Adobe or Deckers.
- The thesis fails if Growth Portfolio momentum slows before mature-brand erosion and leverage have declined.

**Confidence:** Medium.

### Near-misses and exclusions

- **PTC (PTC):** Recurring industrial software and 9% constant-currency ARR growth are high quality, but its roughly 5% guided FCF yield is less compelling than today's cash yields.
- **Comcast (CMCSA):** The cash-flow valuation remains low, but broadband losses, leverage, and separation execution make the business structurally weaker than today's five.
- **FIS (FIS):** Adjusted earnings and cash yields are optically excellent, but acquisition integration, heavy debt, and the gap between GAAP and adjusted economics prevent a higher rank.
- **Accenture (ACN):** Still a strong watchlist candidate, but weak bookings and modest organic growth leave less near-term earnings support than Adobe or Deckers.

### Changes from the September 23 update

Today's independently generated ranking retains **Adobe** and **Deckers** in the first two positions. **UHS, PayPal, and Bristol Myers Squibb** replace **Zoetis, PTC, and Comcast**.

- **Adobe** remains the strongest overall candidate after record Q3 cash generation and a still-low 13.4x trailing multiple.

### September 24 conclusion

**Adobe** remains the best overall quality-value candidate, and **Deckers** remains the strongest balance-sheet-backed alternative. **UHS** offers today's widest conventional earnings margin of safety among businesses with current revenue growth. **PayPal** is a cash-return thesis that still needs cleaner margin evidence. **Bristol Myers** is an inexpensive portfolio-transition case whose upside must be weighed against patent and leverage risk.

The highest-priority follow-up signals are Adobe ARR/RPO and AI monetization, Deckers HOKA/UGG demand and SG&A discipline, UHS reimbursement and cash conversion, PayPal transaction-margin growth, and BMY Growth Portfolio revenue versus legacy erosion.

- Management guides to **$6.15-$6.24** of 2026 adjusted EPS; at **$35.19**, that is only about **5.6-5.7x** adjusted earnings.
- Guided 2026 free cash flow excluding Worldpay sale taxes is **$2.15-$2.25 billion**, equal to an approximate **11.8%-12.4%** yield on the current equity value.
- Q2 FCF excluding sale taxes was **$525 million**, up 220%, and management raised the full-year cash-flow target by $100 million. ([Q2 2026 results](https://www.fisglobal.com/about-us/media-room/press-release/2026/fis-reports-second-quarter-2026-results))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- FIS supplies mission-critical core banking, payment, and capital-markets infrastructure; switching costs and operational risk support sticky recurring revenue.
- Pro forma organic economics remain positive: management expects **4.5%-5.0%** pro forma revenue growth and **5.9%-6.9%** adjusted EBITDA growth.
- The combined revenue table shows a large recurring base, not a one-time project business.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- Debt was **$21.2 billion** at June 30, exceeding the equity market value. Repurchases are curtailed until gross leverage reaches roughly 2.8x.
- The cheap P/E is not directly comparable with GAAP earnings because transaction effects, acquired intangibles, and adjustments are substantial.
- Management lowered its pro forma growth outlook after the acquisition. Integration misses, delayed deleveraging, or weak bank spending could make this a value trap.

**Confidence:** Medium-high; highest balance-sheet risk among the top five.

#### 3. Alphabet (Nasdaq: GOOGL) - highest-quality franchise, but normalize the multiple

**Why it looks undervalued**

- The displayed trailing P/E of **17.8x** is artificially low because Q2 included a **$98.0 billion net unrealized gain** on equity securities. It should not be treated as recurring earnings.
- Morningstar's normalized work estimates **$433** fair value and about **30x 2026 adjusted earnings**; versus today's $354.97 price, that is an approximate **18% discount**. ([current valuation assessment](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/an-undervalued-stock-buy-that-even-warren-buffett-likes))
- Even on a less optically cheap normalized basis, Q2 revenue grew **24%** to $119.8 billion and operating income grew **30%**, which is unusual growth for a company of this scale. ([Q2 2026 SEC exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000066/googexhibit991q22026.htm))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- Search, YouTube, Android, and Google Cloud reinforce a combination of data, distribution, network effects, switching costs, and scale.
- Google Services revenue rose 15%; Cloud revenue rose **82%** to $24.8 billion. Consolidated operating margin expanded two points to **34%**.
- The business produced $39.1 billion of quarterly operating cash flow before its unusually heavy infrastructure build.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- Q2 capex of roughly $44.9 billion exceeded operating cash flow, creating negative quarterly FCF; full-year capex guidance rose to about **$195-$205 billion**.
- The valuation depends on AI infrastructure earning attractive future returns while Search economics remain resilient.
- Antitrust remedies, AI-driven changes in user behavior, or continued capex escalation without commensurate monetization would undermine the thesis.

**Confidence:** High on business quality, medium on near-term valuation because normalized cash earnings are unusually capex-sensitive.

#### 4. United Parcel Service (NYSE: UPS) - recovery value with a real network moat

**Why it looks undervalued**

- At **$94.75**, UPS trades at about **13.1x** management's raised 2026 adjusted EPS guide of approximately **$7.22**, versus 17.6x trailing GAAP earnings.
- Management raised full-year revenue guidance to approximately **$91.2 billion** and adjusted operating profit guidance to about **$8.65 billion** after Q2.
- First-half FCF was **$1.57 billion**, up from $742 million a year earlier. ([Q2 2026 results](https://investors.ups.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/2164/ups-releases-2q-2026-earnings))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- UPS's integrated global pickup, sorting, air, ground, and last-mile network would be exceptionally expensive to replicate.
- U.S. Domestic revenue increased 6.0%, International revenue rose 12.5%, and Supply Chain Solutions revenue rose 7.8%.
- Management says the planned Amazon volume reduction and related network reconfiguration have been completed, reducing a major source of uncertainty.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- Q2 GAAP operating margin was only 4.1%; the 9.2% adjusted margin excludes substantial transformation costs.
- Confirmed 2026 dividend payments of about **$5.4 billion** are high relative to first-half FCF, limiting flexibility if the recovery stalls.
- Trade weakness, wage inflation, new customer losses, or failure to convert restructuring savings into GAAP margins would weaken the case.

**Confidence:** Medium.

#### 5. Mondelez International (Nasdaq: MDLZ) - defensive quality at a moderate discount

**Why it looks undervalued**

- At **$60.17** and 22.0x trailing earnings, Mondelez is not a deep-value stock. The case rests on paying a reasonable price for durable brands and improving volume, not on a very low multiple.
- Management expects approximately **$3.0 billion** of 2026 FCF, an equity yield of about **3.9%**; the annualized $2.08 dividend adds a roughly **3.5%** cash yield at the current price.
- Q2 organic revenue grew 2.2%, volume/mix turned positive at 0.7%, and management raised its organic-revenue outlook to at least 2%. ([Q2 2026 results](https://mondelezinternational.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/mondelez-international-reports-q2-2026-results))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- Global snack brands, broad distribution, retailer shelf presence, and marketing scale support pricing power and recurring demand.
- Emerging-markets strength and improving North American execution provide more than a pure cost-cutting story.
- Management returned $1.5 billion to shareholders in the first half and raised the quarterly dividend 4%.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- Q2 adjusted EPS fell 2.7% in constant currency, and the cash yield is not large enough to absorb a prolonged earnings decline.
- Cocoa and other input costs, consumer downtrading, tariffs, and currency movements can squeeze margins.
- Renewed negative volumes or failure to pass through commodity inflation would remove the modest valuation support.

**Confidence:** Medium; smallest margin of safety in the top five.

## Today's top ideas

### 1. Accenture (NYSE: ACN) — strongest overall quality-value balance

**Why it looks undervalued**

- At about **$181.29**, a **$111.6 billion** market value and roughly **14.5x trailing earnings**, the shares trade at a meaningful discount to the broad market.
- Management expects fiscal 2026 free cash flow of **$10.8-$11.5 billion**. Against the current market value, that implies an unusually high approximate **9.7%-10.3% free-cash-flow yield** for a capital-light, global services franchise.
- The price embeds a substantial slowdown even though third-quarter revenue rose 6% in U.S. dollars, adjusted EPS rose 9%, and operating margin expanded 20 basis points to 17.0%. ([Q3 FY2026 results](https://newsroom.accenture.com/news/2026/accenture-reports-third-quarter-fiscal-2026-results))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- Accenture has global scale, long client relationships, a broad delivery network and switching costs created by mission-critical transformation programs.
- Free-cash-flow conversion remains strong: third-quarter FCF was **$3.6 billion**, and management's full-year FCF guidance equates to about 1.3x expected net income.
- Balance-sheet risk appears manageable: the May 2026 10-Q reported **$5.0 billion** of long-term notes, modest relative to annual cash generation. ([May 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1467373/000146737326000032/acn-20260531.htm))

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- New bookings were **$19.3 billion**, down about 2% year over year, and management expects only 3%-4% local-currency revenue growth for fiscal 2026.
- Generative AI could automate labor-intensive work faster than Accenture can replace it with higher-value programs. Evidence of persistent booking contraction, material pricing pressure or falling cash conversion would undermine the thesis.
- The stock has already rebounded sharply from its mid-2026 low, so the margin of safety is smaller than it was several months ago.

**Confidence:** High relative to the shortlist, but dependent on bookings stabilizing.

### 2. GoDaddy (NYSE: GDDY) — exceptional cash yield with leverage and platform risk

**Why it looks undervalued**

- At about **$97.46**, a **$12.8 billion** market value and roughly **14.5x trailing earnings**, GoDaddy is priced like a no-growth utility despite recurring revenue and improving profitability.

- Year-to-date through July 29, the company repurchased **9.8 million shares** for $851.8 million, a **7% gross reduction** in fully diluted shares. ([Q2 2026 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1609711/000160971126000087/gddyex991-20260630xq2earni.htm))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- Q2 revenue rose 7%, bookings rose 6%, operating income rose 29%, and FCF rose 13% to $443.5 million.
- Annualized recurring revenue reached **$4.42 billion**. Domains, hosting, commerce and identity tools are embedded in small-business workflows and benefit from high renewal behavior.
- The higher-growth Applications & Commerce segment expanded revenue 11%, while Airo's annualized bookings run rate reached $50 million.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- Net debt was **$2.7 billion** at June 30. It is serviceable at current cash generation, but aggressive repurchases reduce balance-sheet flexibility.
- Customer growth is nearly flat, and full-year revenue growth is only about 6%. AI platforms may bundle domain discovery, web creation and commerce functions, weakening GoDaddy's position at the entry point for small businesses.
- A sustained drop in renewals, FCF below guidance or leverage rising while repurchases continue would negate the value case.

**Confidence:** Medium-high.

### 3. Leidos (NYSE: LDOS) — inexpensive cash generation backed by visible demand

**Why it looks undervalued**

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- Q2 adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 13.8% from 15.2% a year earlier. Further margin compression or book-to-bill persistently below 1.0 would be a warning.

**Confidence:** Medium-high.

### 4. Bristol Myers Squibb (NYSE: BMY) — cheap earnings with a credible growth portfolio, but no clean balance sheet

### 5. Exelixis (Nasdaq: EXEL) — profitable oncology growth and net cash, offset by concentration risk
