# Quality-Value Report — 2026 Week 39

**Update date:** 2026-09-21  
**Universe:** U.S.-listed public equities  
**Purpose:** Research support only; not personal financial advice.

## Market context

U.S. equities remain broadly expensive enough that security selection matters. At the September 18 close, the S&P 500 traded near **19.3x forward earnings** and **22.7x trailing earnings**, while the 10-year Treasury yield recently touched 5%. The index was still up roughly 11% year to date, but higher rates and oil prices had increased the hurdle rate for equities. ([market valuation](https://streetstats.finance/valuation/market), [market recap](https://apnews.com/article/8f72a301be85728018018735163f4dad))

This update began with a fresh, broad-market screen rather than prior picks. I used two current U.S.-market datasets as discovery tools: one ranks roughly 4,600 stocks across value, quality, accruals, issuance and other factors, and another screened U.S.-listed operating companies for at least an 8% free-cash-flow yield, a quality score of 65+, sub-18x earnings and no net share issuance. I then checked the finalists against company releases and SEC filings, using sector-appropriate measures rather than applying free cash flow mechanically to financial companies. ([broad factor screen](https://tickernerd.com/stock/best-value-stocks/), [cash-yield screen and methodology](https://intrinsiqq.com/blog/best-value-stocks))

Prices and market values below use the latest available quotes around the September 18 close / September 21 pre-market period. Multiples are approximate and should be refreshed before acting.

## Today's top ideas

### 1. Accenture (NYSE: ACN) — strongest overall quality-value balance

**Why it looks undervalued**

- At about **$181.29**, a **$111.6 billion** market value and roughly **14.5x trailing earnings**, the shares trade at a meaningful discount to the broad market.
- Management expects fiscal 2026 free cash flow of **$10.8-$11.5 billion**. Against the current market value, that implies an unusually high approximate **9.7%-10.3% free-cash-flow yield** for a capital-light, global services franchise.
- The price embeds a substantial slowdown even though third-quarter revenue rose 6% in U.S. dollars, adjusted EPS rose 9%, and operating margin expanded 20 basis points to 17.0%. ([Q3 FY2026 results](https://newsroom.accenture.com/news/2026/accenture-reports-third-quarter-fiscal-2026-results))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- Accenture has global scale, long client relationships, a broad delivery network and switching costs created by mission-critical transformation programs.
- Free-cash-flow conversion remains strong: third-quarter FCF was **$3.6 billion**, and management's full-year FCF guidance equates to about 1.3x expected net income.
- Balance-sheet risk appears manageable: the May 2026 10-Q reported **$5.0 billion** of long-term notes, modest relative to annual cash generation. ([May 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1467373/000146737326000032/acn-20260531.htm))

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- New bookings were **$19.3 billion**, down about 2% year over year, and management expects only 3%-4% local-currency revenue growth for fiscal 2026.
- Generative AI could automate labor-intensive work faster than Accenture can replace it with higher-value programs. Evidence of persistent booking contraction, material pricing pressure or falling cash conversion would undermine the thesis.
- The stock has already rebounded sharply from its mid-2026 low, so the margin of safety is smaller than it was several months ago.

**Confidence:** High relative to the shortlist, but dependent on bookings stabilizing.

### 2. GoDaddy (NYSE: GDDY) — exceptional cash yield with leverage and platform risk

**Why it looks undervalued**

- At about **$97.46**, a **$12.8 billion** market value and roughly **14.5x trailing earnings**, GoDaddy is priced like a no-growth utility despite recurring revenue and improving profitability.
- Management reaffirmed approximately **$1.8 billion** of 2026 free cash flow, implying an approximate **14% FCF yield** on the current equity value.
- Year-to-date through July 29, the company repurchased **9.8 million shares** for $851.8 million, a **7% gross reduction** in fully diluted shares. ([Q2 2026 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1609711/000160971126000087/gddyex991-20260630xq2earni.htm))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- Q2 revenue rose 7%, bookings rose 6%, operating income rose 29%, and FCF rose 13% to $443.5 million.
- Annualized recurring revenue reached **$4.42 billion**. Domains, hosting, commerce and identity tools are embedded in small-business workflows and benefit from high renewal behavior.
- The higher-growth Applications & Commerce segment expanded revenue 11%, while Airo's annualized bookings run rate reached $50 million.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- Net debt was **$2.7 billion** at June 30. It is serviceable at current cash generation, but aggressive repurchases reduce balance-sheet flexibility.
- Customer growth is nearly flat, and full-year revenue growth is only about 6%. AI platforms may bundle domain discovery, web creation and commerce functions, weakening GoDaddy's position at the entry point for small businesses.
- A sustained drop in renewals, FCF below guidance or leverage rising while repurchases continue would negate the value case.

**Confidence:** Medium-high.

### 3. Leidos (NYSE: LDOS) — inexpensive cash generation backed by visible demand

**Why it looks undervalued**

- At about **$127.69**, a **$16.0 billion** market value and roughly **11.9x trailing earnings**, Leidos is priced well below the market despite growing revenue and backlog.
- A current broad-market screen estimates a **12.9% FCF yield** and 12.4x earnings, with five-year share count down about 2% annually. ([cash-yield screen](https://intrinsiqq.com/blog/best-value-stocks))
- Management raised fiscal 2026 revenue, EPS and operating-cash-flow guidance; expected operating cash flow is now approximately **$1.85 billion**.

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- Q2 revenue increased 7% to $4.6 billion and non-GAAP FCF was **$761 million**.
- Backlog reached **$48.7 billion** with a 1.1x quarterly and trailing-twelve-month book-to-bill. Funded backlog grew 44% year over year, providing unusual visibility for the valuation.
- Long-lived government relationships, security clearances and specialized mission expertise create meaningful barriers to entry. ([Q2 2026 results](https://investors.leidos.com/news-releases/news-release-details/leidos-delivers-strong-second-quarter-and-enhances-full-year))

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- The balance sheet is less pristine than the headline cash yield suggests: cash was $748 million against **$6.0 billion of debt** at July 3, although the company paid down $300 million during Q2.
- Government budget changes, procurement delays and fixed-price contract overruns can impair margins and cash conversion.
- Q2 adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 13.8% from 15.2% a year earlier. Further margin compression or book-to-bill persistently below 1.0 would be a warning.

**Confidence:** Medium-high.

### 4. Bristol Myers Squibb (NYSE: BMY) — cheap earnings with a credible growth portfolio, but no clean balance sheet

**Why it looks undervalued**

- At about **$63.06**, a **$128.8 billion** market value and roughly **13.9x trailing earnings**, BMY trades below the market. The price is only about **9.2x** the midpoint of management's raised 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $6.75-$7.00.
- Q2 revenue rose 6% to $13.0 billion, and the Growth Portfolio grew 15% to $7.6 billion. Management raised full-year revenue guidance to **$49-$50 billion** from $46-$47.5 billion. ([Q2 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/14272/000001427226000018/a2026q2ex991-filing.htm))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- The company owns a diversified commercial portfolio, established oncology and cardiovascular franchises, deep clinical-development capability and global distribution.
- Eliquis demand was strong enough for management to raise expected 2026 worldwide revenue growth to 20%-25%.
- Cash provided by operations was $4.5 billion in the first half, and net debt fell by $2.4 billion.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- This is not a balance-sheet bargain: at June 30, cash and marketable securities were **$11.5 billion**, debt was **$43.1 billion**, and net debt was **$31.7 billion**. ([June 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/14272/000001427226000020/bmy-20260630.htm))
- Patent expirations and negotiated drug pricing can overwhelm growth-product momentum; pipeline outcomes are inherently uncertain.
- The thesis fails if the Growth Portfolio cannot offset mature-brand erosion while leverage remains elevated.

**Confidence:** Medium.

### 5. Exelixis (Nasdaq: EXEL) — profitable oncology growth and net cash, offset by concentration risk

**Why it looks undervalued**

- At about **$58.36**, a **$15.2 billion** market value and roughly **18.3x trailing earnings**, EXEL is not statistically the cheapest name here, but its balance sheet and earnings growth make the multiple more defensible than the market's.
- Q2 revenue rose to **$628.7 million** from $568.3 million, while GAAP diluted EPS increased to $0.82 from $0.65.
- Since March 2023, the company had repurchased **$2.9 billion** of stock and retired 93.3 million shares at an average $31.12 through Q2 2026. ([Q2 2026 results](https://ir.exelixis.com/node/27651))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- Cabozantinib has a durable commercial oncology franchise, very high gross economics and continued volume growth.
- The balance sheet had **$1.4 billion** of cash and marketable securities and no conventional funded debt at June 30; total liabilities were only $650 million.
- Zanzalintinib offers a plausible second-franchise pathway rather than relying solely on cost cutting. ([June 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/939767/000093976726000098/exel-20260703.htm))

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- Product concentration is extreme: most revenue still depends on cabozantinib, and 2026 product-revenue guidance was reduced modestly from the January range.
- Pipeline value is binary and regulatory outcomes can change the valuation quickly. R&D and SG&A both increased in Q2.
- Failure of zanzalintinib to establish a differentiated commercial profile, or an earlier-than-expected erosion in cabozantinib, would remove the quality-growth support.

**Confidence:** Medium; highest clinical-event risk in the top five.

## Near-misses

- **PayPal (PYPL):** The valuation is compelling at roughly 10x trailing earnings and the company repurchased $6.0 billion of stock over the last 12 months. However, Q2 GAAP operating income fell 5%, non-GAAP operating margin contracted 248 basis points, and active accounts were essentially flat. It stays on the watchlist until transaction-margin quality stabilizes. ([Q2 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633917/000163391726000080/pypl2q-26earningsrelease.htm))
- **SkyWest (SKYW):** Roughly 9.6x earnings and a screen-estimated 23% FCF yield are attractive, but customer concentration, aircraft financing, pilot constraints and capital intensity make it a weaker quality fit than the top five.
- **UnitedHealth (UNH):** The franchise remains high quality, but a roughly 24x trailing multiple does not provide enough compensation for medical-cost, regulatory and execution uncertainty in this specific quality-value ranking.

## Conclusion

**Accenture** is today's strongest overall candidate: its current valuation implies a roughly 10% cash yield despite global scale, solid margins and manageable leverage. **GoDaddy** offers the largest cash yield among the higher-quality finalists, but investors must explicitly underwrite debt and AI-driven distribution risk. **Leidos** is the best lower-multiple visibility play because backlog supports the earnings base. **Bristol Myers Squibb** and **Exelixis** add healthcare diversification, but their discounts should be sized against patent and clinical concentration rather than treated as certain intrinsic-value gaps.

The most important follow-up signals are Accenture bookings, GoDaddy renewals and leverage, Leidos margin/book-to-bill, BMY Growth Portfolio offset versus legacy erosion, and Exelixis pipeline/regulatory progress.
