## September 22 update

### Market context and method

This run started from a fresh cross-sector assessment and did not use the September 21 selections as inputs. Morningstar's latest U.S. market work estimates that its coverage universe was about **9% below fair value** at August 31, but says nearly all of that aggregate discount was concentrated in seven mega-cap stocks; excluding them, the rest of its coverage was around fair value. That argues for a selective quality-value approach rather than assuming the whole market is cheap. ([September market outlook](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/september-stock-market-outlook-how-position-your-portfolio-during-risky-stage))

The process combined broad current value/moat screens with a cross-sector candidate set, then re-ranked the finalists on valuation, recurring cash economics, competitive durability, leverage, and identifiable disconfirming evidence. Company releases and SEC filings were used for the operating facts below. Prices, market capitalizations, and trailing P/E ratios are intraday snapshots from September 22, 2026 and can move materially.

### Today's top ideas

#### 1. Adobe (Nasdaq: ADBE) - strongest overall candidate

**Why it looks undervalued**

- At **$249.52** and about **14.3x trailing earnings**, Adobe is priced well below the broader software peer group and below many slower-growing consumer franchises.
- Fiscal Q3 revenue increased **13%** to **$6.76 billion**, GAAP EPS was $4.62, and operating cash flow reached a record **$2.52 billion**. Management raised full-year revenue and EPS targets. ([Q3 FY2026 SEC exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000147/adbeex991q326.htm))
- Morningstar lowered its fair-value estimate after the latest results but still values the shares at **$344**, implying roughly **38% upside** from today's price rather than requiring a return to the prior, more optimistic estimate. ([post-earnings assessment](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/adobe-earnings-forward-looking-metrics-under-pressure-freemium-transition))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- Subscription revenue was **$6.56 billion**, up 14%, and total ARR reached **$27.50 billion**. Creative Cloud and Document Cloud remain deeply embedded in professional workflows.
- GAAP operating income was **$2.35 billion**, a roughly 35% margin, while the company repurchased 9.5 million shares in the quarter.
- AI-first ARR grew more than 150% year over year, evidence that generative AI is not only a substitution threat.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- Remaining performance obligations grew only about 8%, a sharp deceleration, as Adobe shifts toward a freemium acquisition model.
- Generative tools could weaken pricing and workflow lock-in faster than new AI products monetize.
- The thesis should be reconsidered if ARR growth falls into sustained single digits, RPO keeps decelerating, or margins contract materially.

**Confidence:** High, with above-average disruption risk.

#### 2. Fidelity National Information Services (NYSE: FIS) - deepest cash-flow discount

**Why it looks undervalued**

- Management guides to **$6.15-$6.24** of 2026 adjusted EPS; at **$35.19**, that is only about **5.6-5.7x** adjusted earnings.
- Guided 2026 free cash flow excluding Worldpay sale taxes is **$2.15-$2.25 billion**, equal to an approximate **11.8%-12.4%** yield on the current equity value.
- Q2 FCF excluding sale taxes was **$525 million**, up 220%, and management raised the full-year cash-flow target by $100 million. ([Q2 2026 results](https://www.fisglobal.com/about-us/media-room/press-release/2026/fis-reports-second-quarter-2026-results))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- FIS supplies mission-critical core banking, payment, and capital-markets infrastructure; switching costs and operational risk support sticky recurring revenue.
- Pro forma organic economics remain positive: management expects **4.5%-5.0%** pro forma revenue growth and **5.9%-6.9%** adjusted EBITDA growth.
- The combined revenue table shows a large recurring base, not a one-time project business.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- Debt was **$21.2 billion** at June 30, exceeding the equity market value. Repurchases are curtailed until gross leverage reaches roughly 2.8x.
- The cheap P/E is not directly comparable with GAAP earnings because transaction effects, acquired intangibles, and adjustments are substantial.
- Management lowered its pro forma growth outlook after the acquisition. Integration misses, delayed deleveraging, or weak bank spending could make this a value trap.

**Confidence:** Medium-high; highest balance-sheet risk among the top five.

#### 3. Alphabet (Nasdaq: GOOGL) - highest-quality franchise, but normalize the multiple

**Why it looks undervalued**

- The displayed trailing P/E of **17.8x** is artificially low because Q2 included a **$98.0 billion net unrealized gain** on equity securities. It should not be treated as recurring earnings.
- Morningstar's normalized work estimates **$433** fair value and about **30x 2026 adjusted earnings**; versus today's $354.97 price, that is an approximate **18% discount**. ([current valuation assessment](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/an-undervalued-stock-buy-that-even-warren-buffett-likes))
- Even on a less optically cheap normalized basis, Q2 revenue grew **24%** to $119.8 billion and operating income grew **30%**, which is unusual growth for a company of this scale. ([Q2 2026 SEC exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000066/googexhibit991q22026.htm))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- Search, YouTube, Android, and Google Cloud reinforce a combination of data, distribution, network effects, switching costs, and scale.
- Google Services revenue rose 15%; Cloud revenue rose **82%** to $24.8 billion. Consolidated operating margin expanded two points to **34%**.
- The business produced $39.1 billion of quarterly operating cash flow before its unusually heavy infrastructure build.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- Q2 capex of roughly $44.9 billion exceeded operating cash flow, creating negative quarterly FCF; full-year capex guidance rose to about **$195-$205 billion**.
- The valuation depends on AI infrastructure earning attractive future returns while Search economics remain resilient.
- Antitrust remedies, AI-driven changes in user behavior, or continued capex escalation without commensurate monetization would undermine the thesis.

**Confidence:** High on business quality, medium on near-term valuation because normalized cash earnings are unusually capex-sensitive.

#### 4. United Parcel Service (NYSE: UPS) - recovery value with a real network moat

**Why it looks undervalued**

- At **$94.75**, UPS trades at about **13.1x** management's raised 2026 adjusted EPS guide of approximately **$7.22**, versus 17.6x trailing GAAP earnings.
- Management raised full-year revenue guidance to approximately **$91.2 billion** and adjusted operating profit guidance to about **$8.65 billion** after Q2.
- First-half FCF was **$1.57 billion**, up from $742 million a year earlier. ([Q2 2026 results](https://investors.ups.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/2164/ups-releases-2q-2026-earnings))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- UPS's integrated global pickup, sorting, air, ground, and last-mile network would be exceptionally expensive to replicate.
- U.S. Domestic revenue increased 6.0%, International revenue rose 12.5%, and Supply Chain Solutions revenue rose 7.8%.
- Management says the planned Amazon volume reduction and related network reconfiguration have been completed, reducing a major source of uncertainty.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- Q2 GAAP operating margin was only 4.1%; the 9.2% adjusted margin excludes substantial transformation costs.
- Confirmed 2026 dividend payments of about **$5.4 billion** are high relative to first-half FCF, limiting flexibility if the recovery stalls.
- Trade weakness, wage inflation, new customer losses, or failure to convert restructuring savings into GAAP margins would weaken the case.

**Confidence:** Medium.

#### 5. Mondelez International (Nasdaq: MDLZ) - defensive quality at a moderate discount

**Why it looks undervalued**

- At **$60.17** and 22.0x trailing earnings, Mondelez is not a deep-value stock. The case rests on paying a reasonable price for durable brands and improving volume, not on a very low multiple.
- Management expects approximately **$3.0 billion** of 2026 FCF, an equity yield of about **3.9%**; the annualized $2.08 dividend adds a roughly **3.5%** cash yield at the current price.
- Q2 organic revenue grew 2.2%, volume/mix turned positive at 0.7%, and management raised its organic-revenue outlook to at least 2%. ([Q2 2026 results](https://mondelezinternational.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/mondelez-international-reports-q2-2026-results))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- Global snack brands, broad distribution, retailer shelf presence, and marketing scale support pricing power and recurring demand.
- Emerging-markets strength and improving North American execution provide more than a pure cost-cutting story.
- Management returned $1.5 billion to shareholders in the first half and raised the quarterly dividend 4%.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- Q2 adjusted EPS fell 2.7% in constant currency, and the cash yield is not large enough to absorb a prolonged earnings decline.
- Cocoa and other input costs, consumer downtrading, tariffs, and currency movements can squeeze margins.
- Renewed negative volumes or failure to pass through commodity inflation would remove the modest valuation support.

**Confidence:** Medium; smallest margin of safety in the top five.

### Near-misses and exclusions

- **Comcast (CMCSA):** A roughly 7.4x trailing P/E is compelling, but secular video losses and uncertainty around broadband competition made it too close to a classic value trap for today's top five.
- **UnitedHealth (UNH):** The franchise remains formidable, and current outside fair-value work suggests upside, but roughly 24x trailing earnings does not offer enough compensation for medical-cost and regulatory uncertainty.
- **Nike (NKE):** Brand value is real, but a roughly 24x trailing multiple requires a cleaner operational recovery than current evidence supports.
- **Baxter (BAX):** Turnaround upside exists, but negative trailing earnings and execution risk do not meet today's quality threshold.
- **Jackson Financial (JXN):** Parent-company cash generation is attractive, but accounting volatility and liability complexity make the headline P/E unsuitable for a cross-sector rank.

### Changes from the September 21 update

Today's independently generated list is a **complete rotation** from September 21: Adobe, FIS, Alphabet, UPS, and Mondelez replace Accenture, GoDaddy, Leidos, Bristol Myers Squibb, and Exelixis.

This does **not** constitute a sell call on the prior group. The change reflects today's ranking inputs:

- **Adobe and FIS** offered stronger current price-to-cash-earnings gaps after their latest reported results.
- **Alphabet** entered on unusually strong operating growth and a current normalized fair-value discount, although its capex risk is explicitly higher.
- **UPS** entered after completing the Amazon volume reset and raising 2026 guidance.
- **Mondelez** entered to provide a defensive, brand-led counterweight to software, fintech, and cyclical transport exposure.
- **Accenture, GoDaddy, Leidos, Bristol Myers, and Exelixis** remain part of the week's research record but did not rank in today's top five. Their omission should be read as lower relative priority today, not as a reversal of every underlying thesis.

### September 22 conclusion

**Adobe** is today's strongest overall quality-value candidate: the recurring revenue base, margins, and cash generation provide tangible support at a mid-teens trailing multiple, while AI disruption remains the central debate. **FIS** offers the largest raw cash-flow discount but requires disciplined deleveraging and successful integration. **Alphabet** has the strongest competitive position, though its reported P/E overstates cheapness and its AI buildout is consuming near-term FCF. **UPS** is the clearest operating-recovery idea. **Mondelez** is the least statistically cheap, but offers the most defensive earnings profile.

The priority follow-up signals are Adobe's RPO/ARR trend, FIS leverage and pro forma organic growth, Alphabet capex returns, UPS GAAP margin and dividend coverage, and Mondelez volume/mix versus commodity inflation.

## Market context

U.S. equities remain broadly expensive enough that security selection matters. At the September 18 close, the S&P 500 traded near **19.3x forward earnings** and **22.7x trailing earnings**, while the 10-year Treasury yield recently touched 5%. The index was still up roughly 11% year to date, but higher rates and oil prices had increased the hurdle rate for equities. ([market valuation](https://streetstats.finance/valuation/market), [market recap](https://apnews.com/article/8f72a301be85728018018735163f4dad))

This update began with a fresh, broad-market screen rather than prior picks. I used two current U.S.-market datasets as discovery tools: one ranks roughly 4,600 stocks across value, quality, accruals, issuance and other factors, and another screened U.S.-listed operating companies for at least an 8% free-cash-flow yield, a quality score of 65+, sub-18x earnings and no net share issuance. I then checked the finalists against company releases and SEC filings, using sector-appropriate measures rather than applying free cash flow mechanically to financial companies. ([broad factor screen](https://tickernerd.com/stock/best-value-stocks/), [cash-yield screen and methodology](https://intrinsiqq.com/blog/best-value-stocks))

Prices and market values below use the latest available quotes around the September 18 close / September 21 pre-market period. Multiples are approximate and should be refreshed before acting.

## Today's top ideas

### 1. Accenture (NYSE: ACN) — strongest overall quality-value balance

**Why it looks undervalued**

- At about **$181.29**, a **$111.6 billion** market value and roughly **14.5x trailing earnings**, the shares trade at a meaningful discount to the broad market.
- Management expects fiscal 2026 free cash flow of **$10.8-$11.5 billion**. Against the current market value, that implies an unusually high approximate **9.7%-10.3% free-cash-flow yield** for a capital-light, global services franchise.
- The price embeds a substantial slowdown even though third-quarter revenue rose 6% in U.S. dollars, adjusted EPS rose 9%, and operating margin expanded 20 basis points to 17.0%. ([Q3 FY2026 results](https://newsroom.accenture.com/news/2026/accenture-reports-third-quarter-fiscal-2026-results))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- Accenture has global scale, long client relationships, a broad delivery network and switching costs created by mission-critical transformation programs.
- Free-cash-flow conversion remains strong: third-quarter FCF was **$3.6 billion**, and management's full-year FCF guidance equates to about 1.3x expected net income.
- Balance-sheet risk appears manageable: the May 2026 10-Q reported **$5.0 billion** of long-term notes, modest relative to annual cash generation. ([May 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1467373/000146737326000032/acn-20260531.htm))

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- New bookings were **$19.3 billion**, down about 2% year over year, and management expects only 3%-4% local-currency revenue growth for fiscal 2026.
- Generative AI could automate labor-intensive work faster than Accenture can replace it with higher-value programs. Evidence of persistent booking contraction, material pricing pressure or falling cash conversion would undermine the thesis.
- The stock has already rebounded sharply from its mid-2026 low, so the margin of safety is smaller than it was several months ago.

**Confidence:** High relative to the shortlist, but dependent on bookings stabilizing.

### 2. GoDaddy (NYSE: GDDY) — exceptional cash yield with leverage and platform risk

**Why it looks undervalued**

- At about **$97.46**, a **$12.8 billion** market value and roughly **14.5x trailing earnings**, GoDaddy is priced like a no-growth utility despite recurring revenue and improving profitability.
- Management reaffirmed approximately **$1.8 billion** of 2026 free cash flow, implying an approximate **14% FCF yield** on the current equity value.
- Year-to-date through July 29, the company repurchased **9.8 million shares** for $851.8 million, a **7% gross reduction** in fully diluted shares. ([Q2 2026 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1609711/000160971126000087/gddyex991-20260630xq2earni.htm))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- Q2 revenue rose 7%, bookings rose 6%, operating income rose 29%, and FCF rose 13% to $443.5 million.
- Annualized recurring revenue reached **$4.42 billion**. Domains, hosting, commerce and identity tools are embedded in small-business workflows and benefit from high renewal behavior.
- The higher-growth Applications & Commerce segment expanded revenue 11%, while Airo's annualized bookings run rate reached $50 million.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- Net debt was **$2.7 billion** at June 30. It is serviceable at current cash generation, but aggressive repurchases reduce balance-sheet flexibility.
- Customer growth is nearly flat, and full-year revenue growth is only about 6%. AI platforms may bundle domain discovery, web creation and commerce functions, weakening GoDaddy's position at the entry point for small businesses.
- A sustained drop in renewals, FCF below guidance or leverage rising while repurchases continue would negate the value case.

**Confidence:** Medium-high.

### 3. Leidos (NYSE: LDOS) — inexpensive cash generation backed by visible demand

**Why it looks undervalued**

- At about **$127.69**, a **$16.0 billion** market value and roughly **11.9x trailing earnings**, Leidos is priced well below the market despite growing revenue and backlog.
- A current broad-market screen estimates a **12.9% FCF yield** and 12.4x earnings, with five-year share count down about 2% annually. ([cash-yield screen](https://intrinsiqq.com/blog/best-value-stocks))
- Management raised fiscal 2026 revenue, EPS and operating-cash-flow guidance; expected operating cash flow is now approximately **$1.85 billion**.

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- Q2 revenue increased 7% to $4.6 billion and non-GAAP FCF was **$761 million**.
- Backlog reached **$48.7 billion** with a 1.1x quarterly and trailing-twelve-month book-to-bill. Funded backlog grew 44% year over year, providing unusual visibility for the valuation.
- Long-lived government relationships, security clearances and specialized mission expertise create meaningful barriers to entry. ([Q2 2026 results](https://investors.leidos.com/news-releases/news-release-details/leidos-delivers-strong-second-quarter-and-enhances-full-year))

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- The balance sheet is less pristine than the headline cash yield suggests: cash was $748 million against **$6.0 billion of debt** at July 3, although the company paid down $300 million during Q2.
- Government budget changes, procurement delays and fixed-price contract overruns can impair margins and cash conversion.
- Q2 adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 13.8% from 15.2% a year earlier. Further margin compression or book-to-bill persistently below 1.0 would be a warning.

**Confidence:** Medium-high.

### 4. Bristol Myers Squibb (NYSE: BMY) — cheap earnings with a credible growth portfolio, but no clean balance sheet

**Why it looks undervalued**

- At about **$63.06**, a **$128.8 billion** market value and roughly **13.9x trailing earnings**, BMY trades below the market. The price is only about **9.2x** the midpoint of management's raised 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $6.75-$7.00.
- Q2 revenue rose 6% to $13.0 billion, and the Growth Portfolio grew 15% to $7.6 billion. Management raised full-year revenue guidance to **$49-$50 billion** from $46-$47.5 billion. ([Q2 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/14272/000001427226000018/a2026q2ex991-filing.htm))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- The company owns a diversified commercial portfolio, established oncology and cardiovascular franchises, deep clinical-development capability and global distribution.
- Eliquis demand was strong enough for management to raise expected 2026 worldwide revenue growth to 20%-25%.
- Cash provided by operations was $4.5 billion in the first half, and net debt fell by $2.4 billion.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- This is not a balance-sheet bargain: at June 30, cash and marketable securities were **$11.5 billion**, debt was **$43.1 billion**, and net debt was **$31.7 billion**. ([June 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/14272/000001427226000020/bmy-20260630.htm))
- Patent expirations and negotiated drug pricing can overwhelm growth-product momentum; pipeline outcomes are inherently uncertain.
- The thesis fails if the Growth Portfolio cannot offset mature-brand erosion while leverage remains elevated.

**Confidence:** Medium.

### 5. Exelixis (Nasdaq: EXEL) — profitable oncology growth and net cash, offset by concentration risk

**Why it looks undervalued**

- At about **$58.36**, a **$15.2 billion** market value and roughly **18.3x trailing earnings**, EXEL is not statistically the cheapest name here, but its balance sheet and earnings growth make the multiple more defensible than the market's.
- Q2 revenue rose to **$628.7 million** from $568.3 million, while GAAP diluted EPS increased to $0.82 from $0.65.
- Since March 2023, the company had repurchased **$2.9 billion** of stock and retired 93.3 million shares at an average $31.12 through Q2 2026. ([Q2 2026 results](https://ir.exelixis.com/node/27651))

**Why it still qualifies as quality**

- Cabozantinib has a durable commercial oncology franchise, very high gross economics and continued volume growth.
- The balance sheet had **$1.4 billion** of cash and marketable securities and no conventional funded debt at June 30; total liabilities were only $650 million.
- Zanzalintinib offers a plausible second-franchise pathway rather than relying solely on cost cutting. ([June 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/939767/000093976726000098/exel-20260703.htm))

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence**

- Product concentration is extreme: most revenue still depends on cabozantinib, and 2026 product-revenue guidance was reduced modestly from the January range.
- Pipeline value is binary and regulatory outcomes can change the valuation quickly. R&D and SG&A both increased in Q2.
- Failure of zanzalintinib to establish a differentiated commercial profile, or an earlier-than-expected erosion in cabozantinib, would remove the quality-growth support.

**Confidence:** Medium; highest clinical-event risk in the top five.

## Near-misses

However, Q2 GAAP operating income fell 5%, non-GAAP operating margin contracted 248 basis points, and active accounts were essentially flat. It stays on the watchlist until transaction-margin quality stabilizes. ([Q2 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633917/000163391726000080/pypl2q-26earningsrelease.htm))
- **SkyWest (SKYW):** Roughly 9.6x earnings and a screen-estimated 23% FCF yield are attractive, but customer concentration, aircraft financing, pilot constraints and capital intensity make it a weaker quality fit than the top five.
- **UnitedHealth (UNH):** The franchise remains high quality, but a roughly 24x trailing multiple does not provide enough compensation for medical-cost, regulatory and execution uncertainty in this specific quality-value ranking.

## Conclusion

**Accenture** is today's strongest overall candidate: its current valuation implies a roughly 10% cash yield despite global scale, solid margins and manageable leverage. **GoDaddy** offers the largest cash yield among the higher-quality finalists, but investors must explicitly underwrite debt and AI-driven distribution risk. **Leidos** is the best lower-multiple visibility play because backlog supports the earnings base. **Bristol Myers Squibb** and **Exelixis** add healthcare diversification, but their discounts should be sized against patent and clinical concentration rather than treated as certain intrinsic-value gaps.

The most important follow-up signals are Accenture bookings, GoDaddy renewals and leverage, Leidos margin/book-to-bill, BMY Growth Portfolio offset versus legacy erosion, and Exelixis pipeline/regulatory progress.
