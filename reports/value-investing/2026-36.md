## 1. Everest Group (EG)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Everest trades below June book value of $398.83 per share and at roughly **6.5 times** annualized Q2 operating EPS of $14.85. The $14.8 billion equity value is also below reported shareholders' equity of $15.4 billion. Unlike many sub-book insurers, Everest is currently earning a mid-teens return on equity.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 operating ROE was 14.9%, year-to-date operating ROE was 15.8%, and annualized total shareholder return was 16.8%. Book value excluding unrealized fixed-income marks reached $407.67 per share, up from $379.70 at year-end. The company repurchased $395 million of stock in Q2 at a valuation below book, making the buybacks economically attractive if reserves are sound.

**Why the discount may close.** Continued book-value compounding and below-book repurchases can create per-share value without requiring a large re-rating. Higher reinvestment yields also support investment income.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Core gross written premiums fell 7.1%, Q2 operating ROE declined from 19.6%, and the Global Wholesale & Specialty combined ratio was 95.2%. Catastrophes, casualty reserve deterioration, and softer reinsurance pricing can erase apparent cheapness quickly. The thesis weakens if the core combined ratio moves persistently above the low 90s or adverse reserve development emerges.

Source: [Everest Q2 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1095073/000109507326000029/everest2q26earningsrelease.htm).

## 2. Universal Health Services (UHS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $169.21, UHS trades at roughly **7.4 times** the midpoint of revised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $22.28–$23.65. First-half operating cash flow of $845 million exceeded $445 million of capital spending by about $400 million, though that simple spread is not a full-year free-cash-flow forecast.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** First-half revenue rose 8.9% and adjusted EPS increased to $11.60 from $10.19. Same-facility Q2 revenue grew 8.2% in acute care and 7.4% in behavioral health, with positive volume in both. Debt to trailing adjusted EBITDA was a manageable 1.81 times, and the company repurchased $448 million of shares in the first half.

**Why the discount may close.** Durable demand for hospital and behavioral-health services, improved labor productivity, and repurchases at a low earnings multiple can support per-share growth even with modest margin expansion.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Management cut the midpoint of 2026 adjusted EPS guidance by 2.6%. Q2 included a $100 million pre-tax Florida Medicaid catch-up, operating cash flow declined year over year, days sales outstanding rose to 56 from 50, and the Talkspace acquisition adds execution risk. Reimbursement changes, wage inflation, malpractice reserves, and regulatory scrutiny are central risks. The thesis weakens if same-facility volumes turn negative or reimbursement fails to cover cost growth.

Source: [UHS Q2 2026 results and revised outlook](https://ir.uhs.com/static-files/ea42ffc0-9e4c-4918-89a9-894d460b977d).

## 3. Synchrony Financial (SYF)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At the September 3 close of $79.85, Synchrony trades at about **7.7 times** annualized Q2 EPS of $2.59 and 1.90 times tangible book value of $42.01. The valuation prices in a meaningful credit downturn despite current returns well above the cost of equity.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 return on equity was 21.4%, return on tangible common equity was 25.2%, and book value per share grew 10% year over year. Purchase volume rose 8%, loan receivables rose 2%, net interest margin expanded 30 basis points to 15.08%, and the 13.2% CET1 ratio provides a substantial capital cushion. Long partner relationships and closed-loop purchase data support underwriting and customer acquisition.

**Why the discount may close.** Credit normalization, funding-cost relief, modest receivables growth, and buybacks below a normalized earnings value can compound book value per share. Synchrony returned $950 million in Q2 and retained $5.7 billion of repurchase authorization.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** This is unsecured consumer credit: a recession can lift charge-offs rapidly. Q2 net earnings fell 8%, expenses rose 7%, and the provision benefited from a $163 million reserve release. The 5.43% net charge-off rate remains high in absolute terms even though it improved year over year. The thesis weakens if delinquencies rise materially, reserve releases reverse, or partner economics compress the margin.

Source: [Synchrony Q2 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1601712/000160171226000030/earningsrelease2q26.htm).

## 4. Arch Capital Group (ACGL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $99.83 and a $34.8 billion market value, Arch trades near **9.8 times** annualized Q2 after-tax operating income of $893 million and 1.47 times June book value of $68.04 per share. The premium to book appears reasonable for a business still producing mid-teens operating returns.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 annualized operating ROE was 15.3%, book value per share rose 15.0% year over year, and the mix of specialty insurance, reinsurance, and mortgage insurance diversifies underwriting profit. First-half repurchases totaled $1.9 billion.

**Why the discount may close.** Underwriting discipline, investment income, and per-share capital returns can keep book value compounding even as insurance pricing moderates.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Insurance pricing is cyclical; catastrophe losses and reserve errors can dominate results; mortgage insurance adds housing and credit exposure; and Q2 operating ROE declined from 18.2% a year earlier. Repurchases above book only add value if strong future ROE persists.

Source: [Arch Capital Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/947484/000094748426000124/acgl-20260630.htm).

## 5. Allstate (ALL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $263.11, Allstate is roughly **7.3 times** annualized Q2 adjusted EPS of $8.99. At about 2.13 times June book value, this is an earnings-normalization thesis rather than an asset-value bargain.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 adjusted net income rose 46% to $2.33 billion, trailing-12-month adjusted ROE reached 44.2%, and Property-Liability policies in force rose 2.6%. The recorded combined ratio was 86.6%, and Allstate returned $3.5 billion to shareholders over the prior year.

**Why the discount may close.** Pricing, telematics, expense discipline, and resumed policy growth could sustain underwriting returns above the market's implied normalized level.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** First-half results benefited from lower catastrophe losses and favorable prior-year reserve reestimates. Climate exposure, social inflation, repair costs, regulation, and reserve development can normalize earnings sharply. The stock's premium to book offers less protection if underwriting returns revert quickly.

Sources: [Allstate Q2 2026 earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/899051/000089905126000117/allcorp63026earningsreleas.htm), [Allstate Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/899051/000089905126000118/all-20260630.htm).

## 6. Global Payments (GPN)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $91.77, Global Payments trades at about **6.7 times** the midpoint of 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $13.60–$13.80. Its plan to return more than $2 billion in 2026 equals roughly 8% of the current equity value, although capital returns should not be confused with free cash flow.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Normalized Q2 adjusted revenue grew 4%, adjusted EPS rose 12%, and normalized adjusted operating margin expanded 70 basis points to 42.0%. The company processes trillions of dollars across more than 175 countries, giving it scale, distribution, and embedded merchant relationships.

**Why the discount may close.** Successful Worldpay integration, roughly 150 basis points of expected 2026 margin expansion, and execution of the multi-year capital-return plan could make the adjusted multiple look overly pessimistic.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Q2 GAAP EPS was only $0.05 versus adjusted EPS of $3.46, a very large reconciliation gap. Worldpay integration, acquisition debt, dilution, security and network risk, and a reduced 4%–5% normalized revenue-growth outlook justify a substantial discount. This is the lowest-confidence selection; the thesis weakens if GAAP cash economics do not converge toward adjusted earnings or integration disrupts organic growth.

Source: [Global Payments Q2 2026 results](https://investors.globalpayments.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/514/global-payments-reports-second-quarter-2026-results).

## Near misses

**Fidelity National Information Services (FIS).** The guided free-cash-flow yield is attractive, but $21.2 billion of debt, lowered growth ranges, transaction-tax exclusions, and a large GAAP-to-adjusted EPS gap keep it below Global Payments in today's relative ranking.

**Bristol Myers Squibb (BMY).** A sub-10-times adjusted EPS multiple and 15% Growth Portfolio revenue growth are compelling. Patent expirations, acquisition debt, and pipeline replacement risk lower confidence versus the selected names.

**Chubb (CB).** Chubb may have the most dependable underwriting franchise in the group, but at roughly 12 times trailing earnings and 1.7 times book it offers less valuation asymmetry than Everest and Arch.

**General Motors (GM), Fiserv (FI), and Omnicom (OMC).** All surfaced in the low-forward-P/E screen. GM's cyclical auto economics and current GAAP earnings distortion, Fiserv's recent data-quality inconsistencies across quote sources, and Omnicom's acquisition-accounting distortion prevented confident inclusion today.

## Conclusion

**Everest is today's strongest quality-value candidate.** It trades below book while producing a mid-teens operating ROE, and below-book repurchases can accelerate per-share compounding. The key caveat is that reinsurance reserves and pricing cycles can make apparent book value less dependable than it looks.

**UHS and Synchrony offer the best non-insurance alternatives.** UHS has resilient demand, solid growth, and manageable leverage, but reimbursement and labor costs require close monitoring. Synchrony offers the highest current return on equity relative to valuation, balanced by recession-sensitive unsecured credit risk.

**Arch and Allstate remain strong insurance candidates**, with Arch offering the cleaner quality profile and Allstate the larger current earnings yield. **Global Payments is the highest-risk entry** because the adjusted valuation is compelling but GAAP earnings and integration risk do not yet provide equal confirmation.

The next evidence to watch is core premium and reserve development at Everest; volumes, reimbursement, and cash conversion at UHS; delinquencies and charge-offs at Synchrony; normalized combined ratios and book-value growth at Arch and Allstate; and GAAP cash conversion plus Worldpay integration at Global Payments.
