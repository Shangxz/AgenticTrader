## Market context

The broad U.S. market remains demanding: the S&P 500 trades near 20 times forward earnings after a strong 2026 advance, while the most conspicuous discounts are concentrated in businesses facing a specific controversy rather than in the index as a whole. That makes balance-sheet strength, cash conversion, and evidence that the core franchise is still intact especially important.

Today’s ranking was rebuilt from scratch. The process combined market-wide valuation screens with a second-stage review of profitability, cash generation, leverage, competitive position, and current filings. Low multiples caused by structurally impaired economics were penalized. Prices and market-derived multiples below are snapshots from September 1, 2026 and will move.

## 1. Accenture (ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $189.76, the shares trade at 15.2 times trailing earnings. Management’s FY2026 free-cash-flow outlook of $10.8–$11.5 billion against a market capitalization near $117 billion implies a roughly 9% equity FCF yield. A separate quality screen placed price/FCF at 8.6 times and ROIC at 27.1%, reinforcing the unusually cheap cash economics for an asset-light professional-services franchise.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal Q3 revenue rose 6% in U.S. dollars, operating margin expanded 20 basis points to 17.0%, EPS rose 9%, and quarterly FCF was $3.6 billion. Accenture returned $2.2 billion through buybacks and dividends. Its breadth across industries, deeply embedded delivery relationships, and global talent base are meaningful advantages even if they do not create a software-like moat.

**Why the discount may close.** The market is treating generative AI chiefly as a threat to billable labor. If AI-related transformation work offsets automation pressure and bookings stabilize, even modest growth plus buybacks can support a higher multiple.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** New bookings declined to $19.3 billion from $19.7 billion; local-currency revenue growth was only 3%; U.S. federal work reduced the outlook; and AI could compress pricing or headcount faster than it creates advisory and implementation demand. The thesis weakens if bookings and revenue remain flat while margins contract.

Sources: [Accenture Q3 FY2026 results](https://www.investor.accenture.com/~/media/Files/A/accenture-v4/investors/earnings-reports/2026/accenture-3q-fy26-earnings-release.pdf), [September quality-value screen](https://www.forbes.com/sites/investor-hub/article/best-stocks-buy-now-september-2026/).

## 2. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The stock trades at 16.8 times trailing earnings. Adobe’s midpoint FY2026 GAAP EPS guidance of $17.95 equates to about 16.3 times guided earnings; the non-GAAP midpoint of $24.40 is about 12.0 times. The valuation requires neither the company’s former premium multiple nor heroic growth assumptions.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue reached $6.62 billion, up from $5.87 billion a year earlier. Management raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $26.50–$26.60 billion and guides ending ARR growth of 10.2%. Subscription revenue, workflow integration, switching costs, and high incremental margins continue to support durable cash generation. Adobe also repurchased about 8.1 million shares in Q1.

**Why the discount may close.** AI-first ARR more than tripled year over year in Q1. If Adobe demonstrates that Firefly and embedded AI features expand usage and monetization rather than merely defend the installed base, the current “ex-growth” multiple should prove too pessimistic.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Generative tools can lower creative-production barriers, pressure seat counts, and shift value toward model or distribution owners. The Semrush acquisition adds integration risk, and the CFO transition adds near-term uncertainty. The thesis fails if ARR growth falls materially below 10% or cash margins erode as AI costs rise.

Sources: [Adobe Q2 FY2026 results and outlook](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/11606202/a5543arefgt.pdf), [Adobe Q2 investor data sheet](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/11606202/bu45teegrf.pdf), [Adobe Q1 FY2026 results](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/21306202/ay45th643t5y46.pdf).

## 3. Intuit (INTU)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Fiscal 2026 operating cash flow was $8.84 billion and capital spending was $221 million, implying roughly $8.62 billion of simple owner FCF. Against a market capitalization near $99 billion, that is about an 8.7% FCF yield. The shares trade at 21.9 times trailing GAAP earnings and about 17.7 times the midpoint of FY2027 GAAP EPS guidance.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** FY2026 revenue rose 14% to $21.4 billion, GAAP operating income increased 20%, and GAAP EPS grew 20%. Online Ecosystem revenue rose 19%. TurboTax and QuickBooks sit inside high-stakes, recurring workflows where accuracy, data history, integrations, and customer trust matter.

**Why the discount may close.** Management guides FY2027 revenue growth of 9%–10%, GAAP operating-income growth of 26%–27%, and GAAP EPS growth of 22%–24%. Delivery near those targets would make today’s multiple look conservative.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** AI agents may commoditize tax preparation and bookkeeping, while free government filing options can pressure TurboTax. Mailchimp guidance is flat to down 1%. Stock-based compensation remains material, and the simple FCF figure should not be treated as fully distributable because working-capital and customer-fund movements can be noisy. The thesis weakens if customer growth stalls or management cannot convert AI adoption into pricing and retention.

Sources: [Intuit FY2026 results and FY2027 guidance](https://investors.intuit.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000896878-26-000029/fy26q4earningspressrelease.htm), [Intuit FY2026 cash-flow statement](https://investors.intuit.com/_assets/_64f3415e6601d7a94d9a083d8f6622dc/intuit/news/2026-08-25_Intuit_Reports_Fourth_Quarter_and_Full_Year_1320.pdf).

## 4. Uber Technologies (UBER)

**Why it looks undervalued.** Uber trades at 16.6 times trailing earnings. Q2 results put trailing-12-month FCF above $10 billion for the first time; against a market capitalization near $155 billion, that is approximately a 6.5% FCF yield for a business still growing gross bookings above 20%.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 gross bookings grew 22%, revenue rose 12% to $14.2 billion, and non-GAAP EPS increased 35%. The two-sided network, global liquidity, brand, and cross-use between mobility and delivery support improving unit economics and cash conversion.

**Why the discount may close.** Continued bookings growth, repurchases, and disciplined autonomous-vehicle partnerships could show that Uber remains the customer-acquisition and fleet-orchestration layer even as the vehicles change.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Autonomous fleets could shift economics to vehicle or model owners; regulation and driver classification remain persistent risks; insurance costs can pressure margins; and acquisitions or new-market investment could consume cash. The thesis fails if mobility growth decelerates sharply or autonomy partners bypass Uber’s network.

Source: [Uber Q2 2026 results](https://investor.uber.com/files/doc_earnings/2026/q2/earnings-result/Uber-Q2-26-Earnings-Press-Release.pdf).

## 5. Zoetis (ZTS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $77.10, Zoetis trades at 12.6 times trailing earnings and about 12.4 times the midpoint of revised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $6.15–$6.25. That is a striking reset for the global animal-health leader, but the discount is deserved in part.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Zoetis retains unmatched industry breadth across companion animals and livestock, a global commercial and regulatory platform, and a history of strong margins and innovation. Morningstar continues to characterize the franchise as wide-moat despite lowering its fair-value estimate.

**Why the discount may close.** Stabilization in veterinary visits, better pricing architecture, or evidence that the pipeline can replace pressure in major companion-animal categories would make a low-teens earnings multiple too severe.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Q2 organic revenue declined 1%, organic adjusted net income fell 2%, and management cut 2026 organic revenue guidance to a 1%–3% decline and adjusted-net-income guidance to a 5%–9% decline. Competition, pet-owner price sensitivity, lower clinic traffic, product-safety concerns, and leverage after buybacks make this the least certain selection. It should remain lower-weight until volume and market share stabilize.

Sources: [Zoetis Q2 2026 results](https://investor.zoetis.com/news/news-details/2026/Zoetis-Announces-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx), [Zoetis 2025 annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1555280/000130817926000228/zts015237-ars.pdf), [Morningstar moat assessment](https://www.morningstar.com/company-reports/1482475-zoetis-wide-moat-remains-intact-but-weve-lowered-our-fair-value-estimate).

## Near miss: Allstate (ALL)

Allstate screens exceptionally cheaply at 5.2 times trailing earnings and 5.4 times FCF, with a standardized 32.6% ROIC. It stays outside the top five because insurer earnings and cash flow are unusually sensitive to reserve development, catastrophe frequency, pricing cycles, and investment results. A normalized combined-ratio and book-value analysis is needed before treating the headline multiple as durable.

Sources: [Allstate Q2 2026 investor supplement](https://www.allstateinvestors.com/static-files/3829568a-6b46-432a-8c45-e2a0286dc02c), [September quality-value screen](https://www.forbes.com/sites/investor-hub/article/best-stocks-buy-now-september-2026/).

## Conclusion

**Accenture and Adobe are the strongest current candidates.** Both pair mid-teens earnings valuations with high cash generation and still-intact franchises; both also have clearly testable AI-disruption risks. **Intuit** ranks close behind because its cash conversion and FY2027 earnings outlook are strong, though the valuation and platform-disruption risk warrant a smaller margin of safety. **Uber** offers the best growth/value combination but has more ecosystem uncertainty. **Zoetis** has the largest apparent re-rating upside and the weakest current operating evidence, so it belongs in the shortlist only with lower confidence and tighter monitoring.

The next evidence to watch is bookings and margin direction at Accenture, ARR and AI monetization at Adobe, customer growth and Mailchimp stabilization at Intuit, autonomy economics at Uber, and companion-animal volume/share trends at Zoetis.
