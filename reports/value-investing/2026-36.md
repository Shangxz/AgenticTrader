## 1. Arch Capital Group (ACGL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $98.21 and a $34.3 billion market value, Arch trades at roughly **9.6 times** annualized Q2 after-tax operating income of $893 million. The stock is also about **1.44 times** June book value of $68.04 per share. That is a modest premium for a company still producing mid-teens operating returns on equity.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 annualized operating ROE was 15.3%, while book value per share rose 15.0% year over year and 2.8% sequentially. Arch combines specialty insurance, reinsurance, and mortgage insurance, and its 2025 operating ROE was 17.1%. The balance sheet supported $1.9 billion of first-half repurchases, equal to about 94% of first-half net income.

**Why the discount may close.** Continued underwriting discipline, investment income, and buybacks can compound book value even if insurance pricing becomes less favorable. The current valuation does not require peak ROE to persist.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Insurance and reinsurance pricing are cyclical; catastrophe losses and reserve errors can overwhelm a quarter; mortgage insurance adds housing and credit sensitivity; and Q2 operating ROE declined from 18.2% a year earlier. Repurchases above book value only help if future ROE remains strong. The thesis weakens if normalized combined ratios deteriorate or book-value growth falls below high single digits.

Source: [Arch Capital Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/947484/000094748426000124/acgl-20260630.htm).

## 2. Allstate (ALL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $259.34, Allstate is about **7.2 times** annualized Q2 adjusted EPS of $8.99. The stock trades at 2.10 times June book value of $123.38, so the value case depends on maintaining strong underwriting returns rather than on a low price-to-book ratio.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 adjusted net income rose 46% to $2.33 billion, trailing-12-month adjusted ROE reached 44.2%, and Property-Liability policies in force rose 2.6%. The Q2 recorded combined ratio improved to 86.6%, and the underlying combined ratio was 79.4%. Allstate returned $3.5 billion, or 6.7% of its market value, to shareholders over the prior year.

**Why the discount may close.** The company has moved from post-pandemic auto losses to strong underwriting while restarting policy growth. If better pricing, telematics, and expense discipline make even part of the improvement durable, current earnings are priced too pessimistically.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** First-half results benefited from lower catastrophe losses and large favorable prior-year reserve reestimates; management's adjusted auto underlying combined ratio was 90.0% in Q2 after updating loss estimates. Climate exposure, social inflation, repair costs, regulation, and reserve development can normalize earnings sharply. The thesis fails if underlying auto profitability returns above the mid-90s or book value stops compounding.

Sources: [Allstate Q2 2026 earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/899051/000089905126000117/allcorp63026earningsreleas.htm), [Allstate Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/899051/000089905126000118/all-20260630.htm).

## 3. T. Rowe Price (TROW)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $109.38, T. Rowe trades at roughly **10.6 times** annualized Q2 adjusted EPS of $2.57. June cash and investments totaled about $7.3 billion versus a $23.3 billion market value, making the operating franchise materially cheaper after allowing for required seed and corporate capital.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** AUM ended Q2 at a record $1.89 trillion. Adjusted operating income rose 15.4% year over year to $709 million, implying a roughly 37% adjusted operating margin. The firm generated $1.77 billion of operating cash attributable to T. Rowe in the first half and returned $441 million to shareholders in Q2. Its retirement distribution, brand, proprietary research, and balance sheet remain durable assets.

**Why the discount may close.** Q2 outflows slowed to $6.5 billion, and June turned positive. ETFs, separately managed accounts, fixed income, multi-asset, alternatives, and strategic distribution partnerships can reduce dependence on legacy active-equity mutual funds.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Fundamental active equity remains under secular fee and flow pressure. Q2 equity outflows were $13.5 billion, the effective fee rate declined 1.5 basis points year over year, and market appreciation rather than net inflows drove the AUM record. The thesis weakens if firmwide flows do not approach breakeven or fee compression outpaces expense discipline.

Source: [T. Rowe Price Q2 2026 earnings release](https://investors.troweprice.com/static-files/0f9b7f70-832d-4db8-af4d-da278a54a3be).

## 4. Fidelity National Information Services (FIS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $41.87, FIS trades at about **6.8 times** the midpoint of 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $6.15–$6.24. The $2.15–$2.25 billion FCF outlook, excluding Worldpay sale taxes, is roughly a **10% equity FCF yield** against the current market value.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 pro-forma revenue grew 5.3%, pro-forma adjusted EBITDA grew 7.4%, and adjusted EBITDA margin expanded 113 basis points to 41.7%. Banking Solutions and Capital Market Solutions are embedded in mission-critical customer workflows with recurring revenue and high switching costs.

**Why the discount may close.** Deleveraging, integration of Total Issuing Solutions, margin expansion, and resumption of meaningful buybacks after reaching the 2.8x gross-leverage target could convert the low multiple into attractive per-share returns.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Debt was $21.2 billion at June 30, nearly equal to the equity market value. Management lowered pro-forma revenue and EBITDA growth ranges, current GAAP EPS is far below adjusted EPS, and the FCF guidance excludes transaction taxes. Acquisition integration and prior Worldpay capital-allocation mistakes warrant a discount. The thesis fails if leverage does not decline or recurring organic growth slips below the low-single digits.

Source: [FIS Q2 2026 results and outlook](https://www.investor.fisglobal.com/news-releases/news-release-details/fis-reports-second-quarter-2026-results).

## 5. Bristol Myers Squibb (BMY)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $67.66, Bristol Myers trades at about **9.8 times** the midpoint of raised 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $6.75–$7.00. The valuation leaves room for patent-expiry pressure without assuming the pipeline fully succeeds.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue increased 6% to $13.0 billion. Growth Portfolio revenue rose 15% to $7.6 billion and now exceeds the $5.4 billion Legacy Portfolio. The company has a diversified commercial oncology, hematology, cardiovascular, immunology, and neuroscience platform and raised full-year revenue and EPS guidance.

**Why the discount may close.** Continued double-digit Growth Portfolio expansion can offset mature-drug erosion sooner than the current multiple implies. Deleveraging also helps: net debt fell to $31.7 billion at June 30 from $34.0 billion at year-end.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Legacy Portfolio revenue fell 7% in Q2, with steep declines in Revlimid, Pomalyst, Sprycel, and Abraxane. Drug development is binary, pricing regulation is material, and net debt remains substantial after acquisitions. Adjusted EPS excludes meaningful items. The thesis weakens if Growth Portfolio growth falls below low double digits or pipeline launches fail to replace exclusivity losses.

Source: [Bristol Myers Squibb Q2 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/14272/000001427226000018/a2026q2ex991-filing.htm).

## 6. Chubb (CB)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $339.42, Chubb trades at about **12.0 times** trailing earnings and 1.74 times Q2 book value of $195.45 per share. The multiple is higher than Arch's but still reasonable for Chubb's underwriting record and global diversification.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 core operating income rose 14.6% to $2.84 billion, core operating EPS rose 18.2% to $7.26, and P&C underwriting income rose 18.8%. The 83.8% combined ratio, 14.5% core operating ROE, 21.2% core operating return on tangible equity, and 12.3% year-over-year book-value-per-share growth show both underwriting discipline and investment-income strength.

**Why the discount may close.** Premium growth, higher reinvestment yields, favorable business mix, and continued capital returns can compound value without requiring substantial multiple expansion.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Catastrophe losses, casualty inflation, adverse reserve development, regulatory capital requirements, and softer commercial-property pricing can reduce returns. North America Commercial premiums fell 2.3% in Q2 because of underwriting actions. The thesis weakens if the combined ratio moves persistently above the low 90s or book-value growth stalls.

Source: [Chubb Q2 2026 results](https://investors.chubb.com/News--Events/news/news-details/2026/Chubb-Reports-Second-Quarter-Per-Share-Net-Income-of-7-30-and-Per-Share-Core-Operating-Income-of-7-26-Up-18-2-Consolidated-Net-Premiums-Written-of-14-7-Billion-Up-3-6-with-PC-and-Life-Insurance-Up-3-0-and-7-5-PC-Combined-Ratio-of-83-8/default.aspx).

## Conclusion

**Arch Capital is today's strongest quality-value candidate.** It combines a roughly 10x operating-earnings run rate with mid-teens operating ROE, diversified underwriting, strong book-value growth, and aggressive but supportable capital returns. **Allstate** offers the largest current earnings yield, though reserve and catastrophe normalization require a wider margin of safety. **T. Rowe Price** offers the cleanest balance sheet and attractive look-through valuation, offset by secular active-equity outflows.

**FIS and Bristol Myers are higher-risk value cases:** both offer single-digit adjusted earnings multiples, but leverage, integration, patent, and accounting-adjustment risks lower confidence. **Chubb** is the least statistically cheap selection but may offer the most dependable underwriting quality.

The next evidence to watch is normalized combined ratios and book-value growth at Arch, Allstate, and Chubb; firmwide flow stabilization at T. Rowe Price; leverage and organic recurring growth at FIS; and Growth Portfolio replacement of legacy erosion at Bristol Myers.
