## 1. Leidos Holdings (LDOS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At about $132, Leidos trades at 12.5 times trailing earnings and 10.5 times forward consensus earnings. Trailing free cash flow of $2.2 billion versus a $16.8 billion market value implies a roughly 13% cash-flow yield. The share price remains about one-third below its 52-week high despite operating progress.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 7% to $4.6 billion, non-GAAP free cash flow was $761 million, and management raised full-year revenue, earnings, and cash guidance. Total backlog reached $48.7 billion, including $10.2 billion funded, while the trailing book-to-bill ratio was 1.1. The business earns a 27.8% trailing ROE and has durable positions in defense, intelligence, health, cyber, and infrastructure.

**Why the discount may close.** Delivery on the enhanced 2026 outlook, conversion of funded backlog, and continued debt reduction could make the current earnings and cash-flow multiples look too pessimistic.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** Leidos depends heavily on U.S. government budgets, procurement timing, recompetes, and contract execution. It had $6.0 billion of debt and $748 million of cash after Q2, so the equity FCF yield overstates the yield on enterprise value. One strong working-capital quarter should not be annualized. The thesis weakens if book-to-bill drops below 1 for a sustained period, cash conversion normalizes sharply lower, or leverage stops declining.

Sources: [Leidos Q2 2026 results](https://investors.leidos.com/news-releases/news-release-details/leidos-delivers-strong-second-quarter-and-enhances-full-year), [current valuation and TTM figures](https://tickernerd.com/stock/ldos-forecast/).

## 2. Cirrus Logic (CRUS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At about $113, Cirrus trades at 13.8 times trailing earnings and 12.7 times forward consensus earnings. Trailing free cash flow of $571 million against a $5.7 billion market value implies a roughly 10% yield. Enterprise value of about $4.7 billion sits below market capitalization because of the cash-rich balance sheet.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Trailing revenue grew 12.9%, net margin was 21.0%, ROE was 20.9%, and ROA was 12.2%. Fiscal 2026 produced record revenue of $2.0 billion and a 32% non-GAAP FCF margin. At June 27, Cirrus held about $1.17 billion of cash and marketable securities, providing resilience and room for buybacks and product investment.

**Why the discount may close.** Continued content gains in smartphones, expansion in laptops and other power/audio applications, and repurchases at a double-digit cash yield could support per-share value even without multiple expansion.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** Customer concentration, especially exposure to Apple product cycles, is the central risk. Semiconductor inventory swings, component pricing, design losses, and weak adoption outside smartphones can make current margins less durable than they appear. Q1 free cash flow was only $48.6 million despite strong trailing cash flow, and stock-based compensation is a meaningful adjustment. The thesis weakens if diversification stalls or gross margin falls materially below the low-50% range.

Sources: [Cirrus Logic fiscal Q1 2027 results](https://investor.cirrus.com/news-and-events/investor-news/news-details/2026/Cirrus-Logic-Reports-Record-Fiscal-First-Quarter-Revenue-of-460-Million/default.aspx), [current valuation and TTM figures](https://tickernerd.com/stock/crus-forecast/).

## 3. Arch Capital Group (ACGL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At about $98, Arch trades at 7.7 times trailing earnings, 10.2 times forward consensus earnings, and roughly 1.5 times book value. The multiple is modest for an insurer that has recently earned a high-teens return on equity, although peak-cycle earnings require normalization.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Trailing ROE was 19.9%, operating margin was 26.6%, and the company has diversified earnings across specialty insurance, reinsurance, and mortgage insurance. Arch repurchased $3.5 billion of stock over the trailing year, and its underwriting culture and flexible capital allocation have historically been important advantages.

**Why the discount may close.** Book-value compounding, disciplined underwriting, and continued repurchases can drive per-share value even if premium growth slows.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** Q2 revenue fell about 10.5% year over year, softer property pricing is pressuring growth, and catastrophe losses or adverse reserve development can overwhelm a seemingly cheap P/E. Mortgage insurance adds housing and credit-cycle exposure. Cash-flow measures are less comparable for insurers, so book value and normalized underwriting returns matter more. The thesis weakens if operating ROE settles below the low teens or reserve development turns adverse.

Sources: [Arch Capital Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/947484/000094748426000124/acgl-20260630.htm), [current valuation and TTM figures](https://tickernerd.com/stock/acgl-forecast/).

## 4. Science Applications International (SAIC)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At about $127, SAIC trades near 11.9 times the midpoint of raised fiscal 2027 adjusted EPS guidance of $10.65-$10.75. Management reiterated free cash flow above $600 million, more than 11% of the current $5.3 billion equity value.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 6.3%, including 5.3% organic growth; operating margin improved to 8.1%; and free cash flow was $131 million. SAIC's mission-critical defense, intelligence, and civilian work is recurring and capital-light. Diluted shares fell about 9% year over year, and management raised revenue, EBITDA, margin, and adjusted-EPS guidance.

**Why the discount may close.** Execution against the raised outlook, conversion of the $22.1 billion backlog, and buybacks at a low cash-flow multiple can increase per-share value.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** Quarterly book-to-bill was only 0.6 and trailing book-to-bill 0.8. Full-year organic-growth guidance remains negative 2% to flat despite the strong quarter. Debt was about $2.5 billion versus $126 million of cash, while receivables increased. Government budget delays, recompete losses, contract mix, and use of the receivables facility can pressure cash conversion. The thesis weakens if bookings remain below revenue or leverage rises.

Source: [SAIC fiscal Q2 2027 results](https://investors.saic.com/news-releases/news-release-details/saic-announces-second-quarter-fiscal-year-2027-results).

## 5. Exelixis (EXEL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At about $59, Exelixis trades at 17.9 times trailing and 13.4 times forward consensus earnings. Trailing free cash flow of $1.2 billion versus a $14.1 billion market value implies an 8.5% yield; enterprise value is lower at $12.9 billion because the company has net cash.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Trailing net margin was 35.3%, ROE 44.4%, and ROA 24.0%. Q2 revenue rose 10.6% to $628.7 million, led by $573.0 million of U.S. cabozantinib-franchise product revenue. The company spent $1.1 billion on buybacks over the trailing year and is funding the zanzalintinib pipeline from internal cash generation.

**Why the discount may close.** Continued CABOMETYX growth, successful zanzalintinib regulatory progress, and repurchases can extend earnings power beyond the market's product-concentration discount.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** This is the least conventionally cheap selection and the shares are near a 52-week high after a strong run. CABOMETYX concentration, patent and generic challenges, trial failure, regulatory delays, reimbursement pressure, and rising R&D needs can impair value quickly. The current median analyst target is below the share price, underscoring limited obvious near-term upside. The thesis weakens if cabozantinib growth slows before a second franchise is de-risked.

Sources: [Exelixis Q2 2026 results](https://ir.exelixis.com/node/27651), [current valuation and TTM figures](https://tickernerd.com/stock/exel-forecast/).

## Near misses

**Bristol Myers Squibb (BMY).** It scored extremely well on the broad factor screen, trades near 10.4 times forward earnings, and generated $11.4 billion of trailing FCF. However, the stock is near its 52-week high after a roughly 50% one-year total return, while Eliquis and Opdivo patent exposure and acquisition debt leave less margin of safety than the selected names.

**Everest Group (EG).** The below-book valuation and mid-teens recent operating ROE remain attractive, but declining premiums, reinsurance pricing pressure, and weaker quality/cash-conversion scores make normalized earnings less dependable than Arch's today.

**Universal Health Services (UHS), Synchrony Financial (SYF), and Allstate (ALL).** All remain statistically inexpensive. UHS carries reimbursement and working-capital uncertainty, Synchrony is exposed to unsecured-consumer credit normalization, and Allstate's current earnings benefit from favorable catastrophe and reserve experience. They remain valid watchlist candidates rather than negative calls.

**Global Payments (GPN).** The adjusted multiple is very low, but the large gap between GAAP and adjusted earnings, Worldpay integration, acquisition debt, and reduced normalized growth still prevent a high-confidence quality rating.

## Conclusion

**Leidos is today's strongest quality-value candidate.** Its roughly 13% trailing equity FCF yield, high ROE, growing backlog, and raised outlook provide the best current blend of cash economics and business visibility. The main caveat is debt: the enterprise-value yield is lower, and government contract timing can make cash flow uneven.

**Cirrus Logic offers the cleanest balance sheet and a compelling 10% trailing FCF yield**, but its customer concentration demands a permanent discount. **Arch is the strongest financial-sector candidate**, provided underwriting returns remain above the low teens.

**SAIC and Exelixis are more conditional ideas.** SAIC needs bookings to recover and leverage to remain controlled; Exelixis needs a second franchise to reduce dependence on CABOMETYX. Across all five, the next evidence to watch is cash conversion, backlog or revenue durability, and whether management converts cheap valuations into per-share value without taking balance-sheet or execution risk too far.
