## Market context

The opportunity set is still selective rather than broadly cheap. The S&P 500 traded at about **19.7 times forward earnings** on September 1 versus a long-run average near 16 times, while the 10-year Treasury yield reached **4.79%** and U.S. oil closed above **$90** amid renewed geopolitical tension. Higher discount rates make cash generation and balance-sheet resilience more valuable and leave less room for multiple expansion to rescue a weak thesis.

Today’s ranking was rebuilt independently from a broad U.S.-market screen before the September 1 report was reviewed. The second stage emphasized current earnings or free-cash-flow yield, returns on capital, cash conversion, leverage, durable customer or brand advantages, and identifiable reasons for the discount. Cyclical peak earnings, accounting distortions, and structurally impaired business models were penalized. Prices and market capitalizations are September 2 morning snapshots and will move.

## 1. Deckers Outdoor (DECK)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $84.58, Deckers trades at about **11.4 times** the $7.425 midpoint of raised FY2027 diluted-EPS guidance and 11.5 times trailing earnings. Fiscal 2026 free cash flow exceeded $1 billion, implying an equity FCF yield above 8.5% against the current market capitalization. The balance sheet makes the enterprise valuation still cheaper: Deckers had $1.60 billion of cash and no outstanding borrowings at June 30.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q1 FY2027 sales rose 5.7%, HOKA grew 7.7%, UGG grew 4.9%, direct-to-consumer sales grew 13.0%, and gross margin expanded to 56.4%. Management still expects FY2027 sales of $5.86–$5.91 billion, HOKA growth in the low double digits, UGG growth in the mid-single digits, and operating margin slightly above 21.5%. Inventory fell year over year, and a large repurchase authorization provides useful capital-allocation flexibility.

**Why the discount may close.** The market appears to be pricing Deckers as a fading footwear cycle even though both core brands are growing, international sales rose 8.4%, DTC is gaining mix, and management raised EPS guidance. Continued full-price sell-through and disciplined buybacks could expose the gap between the current multiple and the underlying brand economics.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Operating income fell to $155.3 million from $165.3 million as SG&A grew faster than revenue. Footwear demand is fashion-sensitive; HOKA and UGG concentration is high; tariffs and Southeast Asian sourcing can pressure margins; and aggressive repurchases can destroy value if brand momentum deteriorates. The thesis weakens if HOKA growth falls below guidance, gross margin rolls over, or promotional activity rises materially.

Sources: [Deckers Q1 FY2027 results and outlook](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910521/000091052126000015/deckex991pressrelease-6302.htm), [Deckers FY2026 results](https://ir.deckers.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/656/deckers-brands-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-fiscal-year-2026-financial-results).

## 2. Adobe (ADBE)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $286.08, Adobe trades at about **15.9 times** the midpoint of FY2026 GAAP EPS guidance of $17.90–$18.00 and **11.7 times** the $24.35–$24.45 non-GAAP range. The market is assigning an ex-growth multiple despite current double-digit growth and substantial recurring cash generation.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 13% to $6.62 billion, subscription revenue grew 14%, ARR reached $27.10 billion, operating cash flow was $2.17 billion, and Adobe repurchased about 8.5 million shares. Creative and document workflows, installed user habits, file standards, distribution, and high-margin subscriptions remain meaningful advantages.

**Why the discount may close.** AI-first ARR more than tripled and exceeded $500 million, while management raised full-year revenue and non-GAAP EPS targets. If Firefly and embedded AI expand usage and monetization while ARR holds near the guided 10.2% growth rate, the current valuation should prove too pessimistic.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Generative tools can lower creative-production barriers, pressure seats, and shift economics toward model or distribution owners. The Semrush acquisition contributes to reported ARR and adds integration risk, while the CFO transition adds execution uncertainty. The thesis fails if organic ARR decelerates materially, AI inference costs compress margins, or new tools weaken pricing power.

Source: [Adobe Q2 FY2026 results and guidance](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000109/adbeex991q226.htm).

## 3. Intuit (INTU)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $344.93, Intuit trades at about **17.0 times** the midpoint of FY2027 GAAP EPS guidance of $20.12–$20.36 and 21.0 times trailing GAAP earnings. FY2026 operating cash flow of $8.84 billion less $221 million of capital spending produces roughly $8.62 billion of simple FCF, or about a 9% yield against the current market capitalization. That cash figure is directionally useful but not fully distributable owner earnings because working-capital movements and share-based compensation are material.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** FY2026 revenue rose 14% to $21.4 billion, GAAP operating income increased 20%, GAAP EPS grew 20%, and Online Ecosystem revenue rose 19%. QuickBooks and TurboTax sit inside recurring, high-stakes workflows where historical data, integrations, compliance, accuracy, and customer trust create switching friction.

**Why the discount may close.** Management guides FY2027 revenue growth of 9%–10%, GAAP operating-income growth of 26%–27%, and GAAP EPS growth of 22%–24%. Execution near those targets, continued share-count reduction, and credible AI monetization would make the current forward multiple look conservative.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Total TurboTax units declined 2% in FY2026, Mailchimp is guided down 1% to flat, and AI agents or government filing options could commoditize parts of tax preparation and bookkeeping. FY2027 guidance includes about $2.02 billion of share-based compensation in non-GAAP operating income, confirming that SBC is economically important. The thesis weakens if customer growth stalls, pricing drives churn, or Mailchimp remains structurally weak.

Source: [Intuit FY2026 results and FY2027 guidance](https://investors.intuit.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000896878-26-000029/fy26q4earningspressrelease.htm).

## 4. Accenture (ACN)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $188.09, Accenture trades at 15.0 times trailing earnings. Management’s unchanged FY2026 FCF outlook of $10.8–$11.5 billion against a market capitalization near $116 billion implies a midpoint FCF yield of roughly **9.6%**.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Fiscal Q3 revenue rose 6% in U.S. dollars, operating margin expanded 20 basis points to 17.0%, EPS rose 9%, and quarterly FCF was $3.6 billion. Accenture had $10.2 billion of cash against $5 billion of long-term notes at May 31. Its global delivery capacity, industry breadth, client relationships, and ability to fund capability-building acquisitions support the quality case.

**Why the discount may close.** The market is treating AI mainly as a threat to billable labor. More than 100 client bookings of at least $100 million year to date and emerging large-scale AI transformation programs provide an alternative path: automation may change the delivery model while creating new advisory, integration, data, security, and managed-services work.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Q3 new bookings declined 3% in local currency, total revenue grew only 3% in local currency, U.S. federal work reduced the outlook, and consulting revenue grew just 1% locally. AI could compress pricing or headcount faster than new work appears. The thesis weakens if bookings remain soft and operating margin contracts.

Source: [Accenture Q3 FY2026 results and outlook](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1467373/000146737326000031/q3fy26earnings8-kexhibit.htm).

## 5. Uber Technologies (UBER)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $75.24, Uber’s market capitalization is about $154 billion. Trailing-12-month FCF exceeded $10 billion for the first time, placing the stock below 15.5 times FCF and above a 6.5% FCF yield while bookings still grow around 20%.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 gross bookings grew 22% in constant currency, trips grew 18%, revenue rose 12%, GAAP operating income grew 30%, and non-GAAP operating income grew 40%. Quarterly FCF was $2.79 billion. The two-sided network, global liquidity, brand, and cross-use between mobility and delivery support improving utilization and cash conversion.

**Why the discount may close.** Continued bookings growth, operating leverage, falling share count, and disciplined autonomous-vehicle partnerships could show that Uber remains the demand-aggregation and fleet-orchestration layer even as the vehicles change.

**Main risks / disconfirming evidence.** Autonomous-fleet owners may bypass Uber or demand most of the economics; regulation, driver classification, and insurance costs remain persistent risks; and acquisitions or strategic investments could absorb cash. Reported earnings also include volatile investment gains. The thesis fails if mobility growth decelerates sharply, FCF conversion weakens, or autonomy partners disintermediate the network.

Source: [Uber Q2 2026 results](https://investor.uber.com/news-events/news/press-release-details/2026/Uber-Announces-Results-for-Second-Quarter-2026/default.aspx).

## Near misses

**Zoetis (ZTS).** The 12.6 times trailing P/E remains optically attractive, but the operating evidence is weaker than the five selected names: Q2 organic revenue declined 1%, organic adjusted net income fell 2%, and management cut 2026 organic revenue guidance to a 1%–3% decline. It remains a watchlist-quality franchise, not a top-five idea until companion-animal volume and share stabilize.

**Allstate (ALL).** A 5.2 times trailing P/E is unusually low, but insurer earnings require normalized reserve, catastrophe, combined-ratio, and book-value analysis. Headline earnings and FCF are too cycle-sensitive to rank above the selected companies without that work.

**EOG Resources (EOG).** EOG’s balance sheet, assets, and capital discipline are strong, and the stock trades near 11.5 times trailing earnings. It stays outside the top five because today’s oil-price spike makes current earnings a less reliable intrinsic-value anchor; the margin of safety should be tested against a normalized commodity deck.

## Conclusion

**Deckers and Adobe are the strongest current candidates.** Deckers offers the largest combination of current earnings yield, net cash, durable brand economics, and still-positive growth; Adobe offers the deepest discount among large recurring-revenue software franchises with double-digit growth still visible. **Intuit** follows because its workflow position and guided earnings growth offset unit pressure and SBC. **Accenture** remains inexpensive and cash-generative but needs bookings stabilization. **Uber** has the fastest operating momentum, though autonomy and ecosystem economics make its long-duration moat less certain.

The next evidence to watch is HOKA growth, full-price sell-through, and SG&A discipline at Deckers; organic ARR and AI monetization at Adobe; customer growth and Mailchimp stabilization at Intuit; bookings and margin direction at Accenture; and autonomy economics plus FCF conversion at Uber.
