## Market context

The U.S. equity market looks less broadly cheap than the headline index suggests. Morningstar estimated its covered U.S. market at a 9% discount to fair value on August 31, but said that discount was concentrated in seven mega-cap stocks; excluding them, the rest of its coverage was approximately fairly valued. With valuation support narrow and macro risks elevated, today's process emphasized normalized earnings, free-cash-flow durability, capital returns, balance-sheet strength, and identifiable reasons for a discount.

Today's ranking was completed independently before this week's earlier report entries were read. The first pass combined a factor model ranking 1,500 names across a roughly 4,600-stock U.S. universe, a separate quality/fair-value screen with more than 400 current U.S.-listed matches, and a 5,670-company market map. The second pass checked the finalists against their latest company releases or SEC filings and rejected candidates whose apparent cheapness depended mainly on leverage, peak-cycle margins, accounting gains, or weak cash conversion. Reference prices are the September 4 close and will move.

## 1. Bristol Myers Squibb (BMY)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $66.82, Bristol Myers trades at about 9.7 times the midpoint of raised 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $6.75-$7.00. It ranked first in the broad value-factor screen, with 99th-percentile value and 92nd-percentile quality scores. That is a modest multiple for a profitable global biopharma portfolio whose current operating momentum is better than the patent-cliff narrative implies.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 6% to $13.0 billion, while the growth portfolio increased 15% to $7.6 billion. GAAP EPS was $1.62 and non-GAAP EPS $2.04. Management lifted full-year revenue guidance to $49-$50 billion and non-GAAP EPS guidance to $6.75-$7.00, citing broad portfolio momentum. The established oncology, cardiovascular, immunology, and hematology franchises provide scale, distribution, and cash to fund the replacement pipeline.

**Why the discount may close.** Continued double-digit growth from newer products, successful pipeline readouts, and deleveraging could show that loss-of-exclusivity pressure is manageable rather than a permanent earnings collapse.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** Eliquis and Opdivo patent exposure is real, acquired pipeline assets can disappoint, drug pricing policy may pressure returns, and acquisition debt reduces financial flexibility. Non-GAAP measures exclude items that still consume shareholder capital. The thesis weakens if growth-portfolio sales fail to offset legacy declines, updated guidance reverses, or debt reduction stalls.

Sources: [Bristol Myers Q2 2026 results filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/14272/000001427226000018/a2026q2ex991-filing.htm), [September 4 market price and valuation](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/BMY/).

## 2. Leidos Holdings (LDOS)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $133.05, Leidos trades at 12.4 times trailing earnings and roughly 10.8 times the midpoint of 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $12.20-$12.50. It also scored in the top fraction of the market on both value and quality in the broad factor screen.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 7% to $4.6 billion, operating cash flow was $793 million, and non-GAAP free cash flow was $761 million. Management raised full-year revenue, earnings, and cash guidance. Backlog reached $48.7 billion, including $10.2 billion funded; total and funded backlog grew 5% and 44%, respectively, and trailing book-to-bill was 1.1. Mission-critical defense, intelligence, health, cyber, and infrastructure work supports recurring demand.

**Why the discount may close.** Converting the funded backlog, delivering the enhanced outlook, and using cash flow to reduce debt or repurchase shares could make the current multiple look too pessimistic.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** Government budget timing, procurement delays, recompetes, and fixed-price execution can disrupt results. Leidos had $6.0 billion of debt and $748 million of cash after Q2, and one working-capital-rich quarter should not be annualized. The thesis weakens if book-to-bill remains below 1, cash conversion normalizes sharply lower, or leverage stops declining.

Source: [Leidos Q2 2026 results](https://investors.leidos.com/news-releases/news-release-details/leidos-delivers-strong-second-quarter-and-enhances-full-year).

## 3. Cognizant Technology Solutions (CTSH)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $62.31, Cognizant trades at 13.4 times trailing earnings and about 10.8 times the midpoint of 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $5.70-$5.82. A separate multi-model screen ranked Cognizant among its strongest quality-adjusted valuation gaps, with a 76/100 quality score. The market appears to be pricing the company as an ex-growth outsourcer despite improving bookings and margins.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue increased 4.5% to $5.48 billion, GAAP operating margin expanded 30 basis points to 15.9%, and free cash flow was $459 million. Trailing-12-month bookings grew 5%, seven large deals were signed, and management guided to 4.4%-5.9% full-year revenue growth with 20-40 basis points of adjusted margin expansion. The business is capital-light and returned more than $1.3 billion through buybacks and dividends in Q2.

**Why the discount may close.** Sustained financial-services growth, large-deal conversion, margin discipline, and credible enterprise-AI delivery could shift investor focus from legacy outsourcing exposure to improving per-share economics.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** AI may automate billable work faster than it creates new revenue, clients can delay discretionary programs, wage and utilization pressure can compress margins, and acquisitions account for part of expected growth. Heavy buybacks create value only if the underlying earnings base holds. The thesis weakens if bookings turn negative, organic growth stalls, or margin expansion is bought through underinvestment.

Sources: [Cognizant Q2 2026 results](https://investors.cognizant.com/news-and-events/news/news-details/2026/Cognizant-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx), [Q2 presentation filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1058290/000105829026000030/exhibit9936302026.htm).

## 4. Exelixis (EXEL)

**Why it looks undervalued.** At $59.01 and 18.5 times trailing earnings, Exelixis is not the lowest-multiple name here, but it combines strong current profitability with net cash and a potential second franchise. It scored in the 99th percentile on value and 98th percentile on quality in the broad factor screen, suggesting the earnings, balance sheet, and growth mix remains unusual at this price.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** Q2 revenue rose 10.6% to $628.7 million, led by $573.0 million of U.S. cabozantinib-franchise product revenue, up from $520.0 million a year earlier. GAAP diluted EPS was $0.82. Low product cost, collaboration economics, and disciplined expenses support high margins, while internal cash generation funds zanzalintinib development and share repurchases.

**Why the discount may close.** Continued CABOMETYX volume growth, successful zanzalintinib regulatory progress, and repurchases can extend earnings power and reduce the single-product discount.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** CABOMETYX concentration, patent and generic litigation, trial failure, regulatory delay, reimbursement pressure, and rising R&D needs can impair value quickly. Management modestly reduced the top end of 2026 revenue guidance, and the shares are near a 52-week high. The thesis weakens if cabozantinib growth slows before a second franchise is de-risked.

Sources: [Exelixis Q2 2026 results](https://ir.exelixis.com/news-releases/news-release-details/exelixis-announces-second-quarter-2026-financial-results-and), [latest Exelixis investor updates](https://ir.exelixis.com/press-releases).

## 5. lululemon athletica (LULU)

**Why it looks undervalued.** The shares closed at $100.61 after falling 17.4% on September 4. The trailing P/E is about 8.3 times; against 2026 guidance stripped of the disclosed tariff-refund benefit, the stock trades near 11.6 times normalized earnings. A quality/fair-value screen placed Lululemon near the top of more than 400 U.S.-listed matches, but the unusually wide implied discount should be treated as a warning as well as an opportunity.

**Why it still qualifies as quality.** The brand still supports a 60.5% reported Q2 gross margin, the company ended the quarter with $1.4 billion in cash, inventories fell 1% in dollars and 7% in units, and 2.7 million shares were repurchased for $330 million. These are not the financial characteristics of a distressed retailer.

**Why the discount may close.** Better product execution under the incoming CEO, stabilization in the Americas, controlled inventory, and continued buybacks could support strong per-share returns even if prior growth rates never return.

**Main risks / disconfirming factors.** This is a turnaround, not a stable compounder at a temporary discount. Q2 revenue fell 4%, comparable sales declined 9%, operating income fell 13%, and management now expects Q3 revenue to decline 10%-11% and full-year revenue to decline 5%-7%. The headline gross margin benefited by 560 basis points from tariff refunds. Fashion relevance, competition, markdowns, leadership execution, and weakening international demand could make the low multiple deserved. The thesis weakens if comparable-sales declines persist into 2027 or inventory reductions require deeper promotions.

Source: [Lululemon Q2 fiscal 2026 results](https://corporate.lululemon.com/newsroom/press-releases/2026/09-03-2026-210528733).

However, AI infrastructure spending drove a sharp deterioration in free cash flow, while Morningstar's post-earnings valuation implies about 30 times adjusted 2026 earnings. It is moderately undervalued quality, but not a stronger current quality-value proposition than today's five. Sources: [Alphabet Q2 2026 SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000071/goog-20260630.htm), [Morningstar post-earnings valuation](https://global.morningstar.com/en-nd/stocks/after-earnings-is-alphabet-stock-buy-sell-or-fairly-valued-5).

**Cirrus Logic (CRUS).** Its cash-rich balance sheet, roughly 14 times trailing earnings, and excellent margins remain attractive. It missed today's five because extreme customer concentration and uneven quarterly cash conversion make normalized earnings less certain than the selected companies' current evidence supports.

**Merck (MRK).** The core business and pipeline remain high quality, but acquisition charges obscure current earnings and the normalized valuation is less obviously cheap than the screen's headline value score suggests. Keytruda concentration and the cost of replacing future patent losses keep it behind Bristol Myers today.

**Arch Capital (ACGL) and SAIC (SAIC).** Both remain credible value candidates. Arch's current insurer earnings may be above mid-cycle as pricing softens; SAIC's weak book-to-bill and leverage offset its guided cash yield. They remain on the weekly watchlist.

## Conclusion

**Bristol Myers is today's strongest quality-value candidate.** The roughly 9.7 times guided non-GAAP earnings multiple, improving growth portfolio, and raised outlook provide the clearest combination of present earnings support and a plausible reason the market is too pessimistic. Patent cliffs and acquisition debt prevent high certainty.

**Leidos and Cognizant offer the cleanest non-pharma alternatives.** Leidos has superior demand visibility but more leverage; Cognizant has a cleaner capital-light model but faces more disruption risk from AI and client spending cycles. **Exelixis offers strong economics with single-product risk. Lululemon offers the largest apparent discount and the weakest current operating trend.**

Across all five, the next evidence to watch is whether cash conversion matches adjusted earnings, whether management converts low valuations into per-share value, and whether each company's specific discount narrows without a deterioration in the underlying business.
